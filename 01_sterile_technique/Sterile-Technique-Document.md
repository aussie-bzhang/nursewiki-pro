**First Phase: Spanning thousands of years, derived from natural materials**
Silk Thread
Used for ligating blood vessels by Galen in 1800
Halsted played a significant role in establishing the position of silk thread in surgical sutures, which has not been surpassed to date
Widely used non-absorbable sutures
Catgut
The concept of "catgut" was proposed by Galen in 1800
Lister confirmed in 1867-1869 that the human body could absorb catgut, demonstrating the necessity of sterilization for implanted sutures and the clinical application of the first absorbable suture

**First Phase: After the 1970s, synthetic sutures were developed according to the requirements of surgical operations**
In 1970-1974, the American DG Company and DuPont Company successively launched chemical synthetic absorbable sutures

PGA (trade name Dexon) and PLA
In 1974, Ethicon Company combined the high strength of PLA and the flexibility of PGA to launch Polyglactin 910 suture (trade name Vicryl/Vicryl), which became the most widely used absorbable suture in the world

In 1980, Ethicon Company launched PDS suture, which has higher strength and better flexibility
In 2003, Ethicon Company launched antibacterial PGLA suture (antibacterial Vicryl/Plus), which initiated a new concept in suture

Physical Characteristics                            Strength
a.   The force required to break the suture at its cross-section
b.   Strength depends on the basic material composition and size
c.   Usually represented as N-0

Elasticity and tensile strength
a.   The inherent ability of the suture to recover its original state and length after being stretched
b.   Nylon thread has the greatest elasticity, while steel wire has the least
c.   Only high-elastic materials can be used for small ring ligatures or sutures

Biological characteristics
Tissue reaction
a.   Inflammatory response caused by foreign bodies
b.   The peak usually appears between 2-7 days postoperatively
c.   Excessive tissue reaction makes the patient susceptible to infection
d.   When selecting a suture, its tissue reactivity must be considered
e.   The higher the biological inertness of the suture material, the lower the tissue reactivity

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->

Dyeing
a.   Woven materials are often coated to reduce capillary action and are given a smooth surface
b.   Teflon and silicon are most commonly used for non-absorbable sutures
c.   Film coating is most commonly used for absorbable sutures
Coating

a.   To increase the visibility of the surgical field, most sutures are dyed

Role of sutures in surgical procedures

b.   Ligates blood vessels or tissues
c.   Permanently or temporarily fixes implants: valves, drainage tubes
d.   Reconstruction of internal structures: gastrointestinal tract, blood vessels

The medical surgical needle has evolved through three generations:
First Generation: Traditional eye needle
Second Generation: Grooved needle - Tail with groove, needle thread connected
Third Generation: Drilled needle - Tail with drilled hole, needle thread connected

Evaluation standards for qualified suture needles:

Strength, toughness, sharpness, stability
