Perioperative Hypothermia Nursing

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
Introduction
    Body temperature is an important vital sign, and maintaining normal body temperature is crucial for the proper functioning of various body functions. Clinically, a core body temperature between 34-36℃ is considered hypothermia. Hypothermia can lead to various complications such as slowed metabolism of anesthetic drugs, coagulation dysfunction, immune function suppression, increased postoperative bleeding and infection risks. Paying attention to perioperative hypothermia is crucial for patient safety.

Mechanism of Heat Loss in the Body

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Radiation: The body transfers heat to the external cooler substances in the form of thermal radiation. Under resting conditions, radiation heat loss accounts for 60% of total heat loss. The amount of radiation heat loss is related to factors such as the temperature difference between the skin and the environment and the effective radiation area of the body. The greater the temperature difference between the environment and the skin, or the larger the effective radiation area of the body, the more radiation heat loss there will be.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
Conduction: Conduction is the direct transfer of heat from the body's deep heat to the cooler object it contacts. The heat from the deep body is transferred to the surface skin of the body by conduction, and then directly transferred to the object it contacts.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
Convection: Convective heat loss is a way of exchanging heat through gases and liquids. There is always a thin layer of air in contact with the skin surrounding the human body, and the body's heat is transferred to this layer of air. Due to the continuous movement of the air (convection), the body heat is dispersed into the space.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
Evaporation Heat Loss: When the environmental temperature is equal to or higher than the skin temperature, the methods of radiation, conduction, and convection heat loss are not effective. At this time, evaporation becomes the only way of heat loss for the body.

Causes of Hypothermia

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Psychological Factors
    Patients may experience fluctuations in emotions such as fear, tension, and anxiety, which can cause blood redistribution, affecting return blood volume and microcirculation, and increase the risk of hypothermia during surgery.
    Patients may experience cold and damp extremities.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
Age Factors
    Adolescence: During the peak period of development, body temperature control is unstable.
    Elderly: Less subcutaneous fat, slow blood circulation, low metabolism, leading to low body temperature, poor sensitivity to temperature changes.
    Children: Due to the relatively large surface area to body weight ratio and incomplete body temperature regulation function, they are more susceptible to environmental influences.

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
Fasting and NPO
    Prolonged preoperative fasting and NPO time: Routine preoperative fasting for 12 hours and NPO for 4 hours.
    Early enema and administration of laxatives: This extends the patient's waiting time before surgery, increases hunger, anxiety, and discomfort, reduces the body's resistance, affects sleep, and can easily lead to hypotension during anesthetic induction and postoperative hypothermia.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
Inadequate Warming Measures
    At all stages:
        From the ward to the operating room
        From the operating room to the recovery room
        During anesthetic recovery
        From the recovery room back to the ward
        Inadequate warming measures

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
Environmental Factors
    Studies have confirmed that if the operating room temperature is below 21℃, patients often experience hypothermia.
    The operating room temperature is generally controlled between 22-25℃.
    The two factors of the conventional temperature in the laminar flow operating room and the rapid air flow in the room can increase the heat loss of the patient's body, making it easier for the patient's body temperature to drop.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
Anesthetic Factors
    General anesthesia
    Tracheal intubation
    Regional anesthesia
    Muscle relaxants

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
General Anesthesia
    The change in core body temperature after general anesthesia can increase from the original 0.2℃ to 4℃, up to 20 times the normal temperature regulation threshold.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Tracheal Intubation
    Tracheal intubation → direct connection of the trachea with the external air → loss of filtration, humidification, and warming → cold and dry air directly enters the lungs → carries away a large amount of body heat → body temperature drops

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
Regional Anesthesia
    After epidural block, the skin temperature in the blocked area increases significantly, while the temperature of the non-blocked skin decreases. The contraction of skeletal muscles through the unblocked area increases heat production and vasoconstriction reduces heat loss to maintain body temperature constant.
    This suggests that shivering and central body temperature decrease are closely related after epidural anesthesia.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
Muscle Relaxants
    Muscle relaxants keep the entire skeletal muscle in a relaxed state, eliminating muscle tension and muscle movement heat production, leading to a decrease in body temperature.

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
Factors Affecting Heat Loss
    Skin Disinfection:
        The low temperature of the disinfectant, after the disinfectant dries, can achieve the purpose of disinfection. The evaporation of the disinfectant carries away a large amount of heat, causing body temperature to drop.
        Long exposure time of the chest and abdominal cavities in major operations, using cold liquid to rinse the cavity, are important factors causing body temperature to drop.

<!-- Slide number: 23 -->
Effects of Intravenous Fluids and Blood Transfusion

Surgical Intraoperative Administration of Cold Fluids

Administering large volumes of cold fluids during surgery, particularly large amounts of stored blood, can significantly lower the body temperature.

Every 1,000 ml of fluid or 200 ml of 4℃ blood administered at room temperature can cause a decrease in body temperature of 0.25 to 0.5℃.

Foreign research shows that if the room temperature is set at 21-22℃ and patients in non-surgical areas are covered with a surgical drape and a blanket, approximately 33% of patients will still have an intraoperative core body temperature below 35℃ after the administered fluids or blood are warmed.

Effects of Hypothermia on the Body

<!-- Slide number: 25 -->
Increased Incidence of Shivering

A patient's core body temperature decreases by about 1℃ when shivering occurs.
Shivering caused by hypothermia can significantly increase oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production.
The incidence of shivering in patients without effective rewarming during the recovery from general anesthesia is about 40%.
Shivering increases patient discomfort and can cause wound pain, necessitating an increased dosage of analgesics.

<!-- Slide number: 26 -->

Increased Incidence of Surgical Wound Infection
Even mild temperature reduction can directly damage the body's immune function, particularly by inhibiting the oxidative killing activity of neutrophils.
Hypothermia can reduce blood and oxygen supply to the skin, inhibit tissue oxygen uptake.
Perioperative hypothermia is also associated with reduced protein and osteogenic protein synthesis.

<!-- Slide number: 27 -->

Delayed Awakening
During hypothermia, the production of catecholamines decreases, leading to a weakened stress response to external stimuli, thereby prolonging the time to consciousness and extubation.
Hypothermia reduces liver metabolic rate and inhibits liver function, delaying the effects of muscle relaxants and intravenous anesthetics, which can prolong the awakening period.

<!-- Slide number: 28 -->
Effects of Hypothermia on Coagulation Function

Mild temperature reduction can decrease the number of platelets in the circulating blood, reduce platelet function, lower the activity of coagulation factors, activate the fibrinolysis system, and thus prolong bleeding time.
Severe hypothermia can lead to disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC).
Perioperative temperature reduction significantly increases blood loss and the need for blood transfusions.
Hypothermia also leads to venous stasis and reduced local tissue oxygen supply, further causing deep vein thrombosis.

<!-- Slide number: 29 -->
Effects of Hypothermia on Cardiovascular Function

Shivering can increase oxygen consumption by 109% to 468%. A decrease in postoperative patient temperature of 0.3 to 1.2℃ can increase oxygen consumption by an average of 92%.
Moderate or severe hypothermia can cause a decrease in cardiac output, hypotension, and potentially fatal arrhythmias.
When the core body temperature is below 30℃, atrial fibrillation is common, and ventricular fibrillation can occur when the temperature is further reduced.

<!-- Slide number: 30 -->
Effects of Hypothermia on Respiration

Severe hypothermia can slow the respiratory rate due to inhibition of the medullary respiratory center.
Extremely low temperatures can cause bronchospasm, and in chronic conditions, pulmonary edema, increased bronchial secretions, and increased pulmonary blood volume can be seen.

<!-- Slide number: 31 -->
Effects of Hypothermia on the Gastrointestinal System

At 32℃, hypothermia reduces intestinal motility.
If the body temperature is below 26.7℃, gastric mucosal erosion, ulcers, and hemorrhagic pancreatitis can occur.

<!-- Slide number: 32 -->
Other Effects

Hypothermia can cause hyperglycemia.
Perioperative hypothermia may affect the long-term prognosis of cancer patients.
The mortality rate of hypothermic patients is higher than that of normothermic patients, especially in severe trauma cases...

Nursing Care for Hypothermia

<!-- Slide number: 34 -->

Psychological Care

1. Preoperative visit and comprehensive assessment of the patient
2. Psychological care to alleviate patient anxiety

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->

Coverings

1. Transporting patients—pay attention to insulation, add covers according to the season, avoid unnecessary exposure
2. During anesthesia and surgery—reduce the exposed area, cover non-surgical areas with gowns or surgical drapes, avoid wetting the covers, and keep the bed dry
3. Skin of the surgical site—application of skin protective film

<!-- Slide number: 36 -->

Room Temperature

1. Room temperature: 22-28℃
2. Operating room temperature controlled at 22-25℃, humidity at 40%-60%
3. Operating room temperature for newborns and premature infants maintained at 27-29℃.
4. The greatest heat loss for surgical patients occurs within the first hour after surgery; when the operating room temperature is above 22℃, maintaining the operating room temperature for 1 hour can reduce patient heat loss.

<!-- Slide number: 37 -->

Temperature Monitoring

1. There is a large difference in temperature between different body parts, with core temperature being relatively uniform.
2. Core temperature can be measured at the pulmonary artery, tympanic membrane, distal esophagus, nasopharynx, mouth, and rectum.
3. The rectum is a more ideal site for temperature measurement.
4. Surgical patients should routinely monitor core body temperature to detect and treat hypothermia early, preventing complications.

<!-- Slide number: 38 -->

Reducing Heat Loss

1. Ethanol can rapidly evaporate, absorb, and carry away a large amount of heat from the skin, causing a decrease in body temperature in a short period of time.
2. Do not use volatile disinfectants; iodophor is the best disinfectant.

<!-- Slide number: 39 -->

Warming Fluids

1. Maintaining effective core body temperature is an effective measure, especially when administering large volumes of intravenous fluids and blood.
2. Warming intravenous fluids with a thermostatic incubator is a convenient, quick, and effective method. Ensure that the incubator is stable and the temperature is moderate; do not overheat the fluids, do not exceed 39℃.
3. The fluids in the incubator should be used according to the time they were placed in the incubator. Do not put too much fluid in the incubator at once, as this may lead to prolonged storage at high temperatures, ideally not exceeding 6 hours.
4. Caution should be exercised when warming fluids for patients with bleeding disorders or high fever to prevent exacerbating the condition.
5. The fluid used for lavage can carry away a large amount of heat; the lavage fluid should be warmed to 40℃ as appropriate.

<!-- Slide number: 40 -->

Water-temperature Control Blanket
1. When the water temperature in the circulating water tank is set at 38℃, it can ensure that the patient's anal temperature remains within the normal range of 36.5℃ to 37.5℃.
2. The use of a circulating water blanket for warming helps to reduce the occurrence of hypothermia and shivering reactions.
3. Studies have confirmed that this method has limitations, as the contact area between the water pad and the patient is only 15%, the tissue at the contact surface is compressed by gravity, resulting in poor local blood circulation, and heat cannot be transferred to the body's interior.
4. Heat and pressure can cause tissue damage, and some may cause severe burns due to the failure of the temperature control device.

<!-- Slide number: 41 -->

Inflatable Warming Device
1. Inflatable warming is a relatively stable and effective method, and is currently considered the most effective and feasible method.
2. The use of a high-convection warming device allows for a contact area of 35% on the upper body and 36% on the lower body, resulting in good warming effects.
3. The inflatable warming blanket, due to reasonable settings, can maintain the set temperature continuously, and will not cause burns or insufficient temperature to affect the effectiveness, etc.

<!-- Slide number: 42 -->

Humidification and Heat Exchange Device

1. This is an artificial nose that keeps the patient's inhaled air at a certain humidity and temperature, but it can only increase humidity by 50% and cannot prevent the drop in body temperature.
