**I: Introduction**
         Patients with acute left heart failure often suffer from respiratory failure, which poses a serious threat to life. Acute pulmonary edema is the main manifestation of acute left heart failure. Studies have shown that early use of BiPAP non-invasive ventilators has a total effective rate of 93.5%, which is significantly higher than the total effective rate of 82.6% with conventional drug treatment [1].

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
**Mechanism of Non-Invasive Positive Pressure Ventilation**

**Mechanism**

Increase expiratory end lung volume, expand collapsed alveoli, and improve lung compliance through positive pressure breathing
Improve ventilation and blood flow ratio, shift central blood volume into peripheral venous system, reduce systemic venous return, and alleviate cardiac preload and afterload
Increase alveolar capillary pressure around the alveoli, promote reabsorption of alveolar and interstitial fluid into the vascular cavity, reduce pulmonary exudation and pulmonary edema
Reduce respiratory muscle work, relieve respiratory muscle fatigue, and decrease oxygen consumption

# Continuity of Care
| Exercise Section |
| --- |
| 1. Guide the patient to perform pursed-lip breathing training for 10-15 minutes each time, twice a day. The heart rate should increase by less than 20/min or the breathing rate by less than 5/min compared to the resting heart rate [9]. Perform diaphragmatic breathing training with a breathing ratio of 2:1 or 3:1, for 10-20 minutes each time, twice or three times a day. |
| 2. Monitor the patient's heart rate changes during exercise, for example, whether there is shortness of breath, and the target heart rate for rehabilitation exercise for patients with stable angina pectoris can be 94.3-117.4 beats/min; the resting heart rate of 34.9+2.7 beats/min can be used as an estimate for HRpk (peak heart rate at the end of exercise). Wear a sports watch during exercise for heart rate monitoring. |
| 3. Pay attention to safety during exercise, do not exercise in the morning, and it is better to exercise in the evening, and it is best to go out with others. |

# Continuity of Care
| Medication Section |
| --- |
| 1. Guide the patient to take medication on time and correctly, and do not reduce or discontinue medication on their own. |
| 2. Pay attention to the adverse reactions of drugs, such as hypotension caused by antihypertensive drugs, which can easily cause dizziness in patients, leading to falls. Therefore, family members should accompany the patient when going out. |
| 3. Guide the patient to set a medication alarm clock to remind them to take medication, to avoid missing or taking the wrong dose. |
| 4. Guide the patient to purchase a medication box, place the medication for the week, and take it at the designated time. |

# Experience
       Nursing is not an accessory to treatment but should be a catalyst to enhance the effectiveness of treatment. This requires nurses to genuinely stand in the patient's shoes and provide individualized care, rather than a one-size-fits-all approach! At the same time, providing psychological care is also a long-term responsibility for us!
