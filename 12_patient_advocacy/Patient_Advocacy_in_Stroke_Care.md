Nursing Issue 1 — Hypoglycemia

| Date | Nursing Rationale | Nursing Goal | Nursing Measures | Nursing Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11/11 | Patient has a history of diabetes, post-stroke with dysphagia, reduced food intake, several episodes of hypoglycemia symptoms occurred within three days of admission | No hypoglycemia symptoms during hospitalization | 11/11 1. Informed the patient and family about the risk factors of hypoglycemia, discussed the causes of the patient's hypoglycemia with the patient and family together 2. Strengthened blood glucose monitoring during high-risk times for hypoglycemia (especially at night) 3. Guided food intake to prevent hypoglycemia; understood the patient's previous dietary habits, including preferred foods, flavors, and meal times 4. Try to choose food suitable for the patient's taste 5. Assisted the patient to sit up, raise the head of the bed to a semi-sitting position 6. Do not rush the patient during meals, allow the patient to eat slowly, and take a break during eating. | Hypoglycemia symptoms still occurred during the evening meal and at night on 13/11, blood glucose maintained relatively stable on 15/11, no hypoglycemia occurred during the transfer period on 24/11 | 14/11 Consulted the nutrition department, adjusted special dietary for diabetic patients, intermittent (gastric tube) feeding, swallowing function exercise, passive exercise on the bed | |

Nursing Issue 2 — Risk of Aspiration

| Date | Nursing Rationale | Nursing Goal | Nursing Measures | Nursing Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11/11 | Patient developed brain infarction, with dysphagia, Wada drinking test level Ⅴ, swallowing paste test not passed, refused to have a gastric tube inserted | (1) Patient remains stable with vital signs during hospitalization, no aspiration occurred (2) Patient's swallowing function recovers to level Ⅰ-Ⅱ, able to eat independently | 11/11 Patient refused to have a gastric tube inserted, suggested intermittent gastric tube, patient still refused 1. Clear oral secretions before eating; feeding position: try to take a sitting or semi-recumbent position, neck slightly flexed 2. Pause feeding if the patient shows nausea or vomiting, use an aspirator if necessary to remove 3. Swallowing function training started when the condition is stable: ① Basic training: guide the patient to perform phonation, cheek bulging, and tongue protrusion exercises daily, 10 repetitions per action, 3-5 times a day; ② Tongue training: guide the patient to use the tongue to舔 lips and corners in a clockwise and counterclockwise direction, then perform tongue curling exercises. 10 repetitions per action, 3 times a day in the morning, afternoon, and evening; ③ Empty swallowing training, 10 repetitions per action, twice a day in the morning and evening; ④ Oral pharyngeal ice stimulation: twice a day ⑤ Abdominal breathing, pursed-lip breathing, simple breathing exercises, blowing up balloons, etc., to exercise respiratory function | 13/11 Swallowing paste still caused coughing, continue training |

Nursing Issue 3—Deficiency in Self-Care Ability

| Date | Nursing Rationale | Nursing Goals | Nursing Measures | Nursing Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11/11 | BADL score 35 (20-40 points require significant assistance) Unable to eat, wash, bathe, or use the toilet independently | To master self-care skills such as washing, eating, using the toilet, dressing, and transferring, and improve self-care ability | 1. Assist with daily living care, maintain cleanliness and hygiene, make the patient comfortable, and keep a pleasant mood 2. Guide the patient to wear loose, easy-to-dress clothes, avoid wearing turtleneck sweaters, teach the patient dressing and undressing techniques, and recommend wearing non-lace slippers 3. Provide a commode or assist the patient to go to the toilet according to the patient's toilet time, teach the patient to use toilet grab bars when entering the toilet, and place toilet paper within easy reach 4. Guide the patient to eat independently with the right hand: choose appropriate utensils, assume a sitting or lying position, rinse the mouth after eating, and maintain oral hygiene 5. Guide the patient to perform self-care activities such as brushing teeth, washing face, bathing, combing hair, wearing pants and shoes with the right hand 6. Teach the patient transfer skills: from bed to chair, from chair to standing with support, 3-4 times a day, exercise with a companion present to prevent falls | 24/11 BADL score 55 (40-60 points require assistance) Master the skill of eating with the right hand, master the skill of brushing teeth, washing face, bathing, eating, dressing pants and shoes, and transferring with slight assistance |

Nursing Issue 4—Depression

| Date | Nursing Rationale | Nursing Goals | Nursing Measures | Nursing Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11/11 | SDS score 66 (63-72 points indicate moderate depression), related to the feeling that individual health is threatened; mood depression, difficulty falling asleep, easy to wake up, often cry, do not communicate with others, even feel that there is no meaning to living | Improve depression, enhance confidence in rehabilitation, improve sleep quality | (1) Use the nursing process to develop an individualized nursing plan. First, assess the patient's psychological state, understand the patient's needs through communication, identify the key issues, and develop an individualized psychological nursing plan with the patient, taking different psychological nursing strategies. (2) Communicate with the family to establish emotional and social support systems. Make them provide more care, concern, and assistance to the patient in life and psychologically. (3) Guide the patient to relax their tense psychology. Use music to regulate emotions, guide the patient to perform relaxation exercises such as lying flat with eyes closed and deep breathing. (4) Behavioral comprehensive therapy. Assist and guide the patient and family to start early active and passive movement of the hemiplegic limb, affirm the patient's progress,辅以必要的良性暗示, stimulate the patient's enthusiasm, and continuously enhance the confidence in rehabilitation. (5) Regularly evaluate the patient's effectiveness, and timely adjust the individualized psychological nursing plan. | 14/11 Emotions gradually stabilize, willing to try new treatment plans 16/11 Emotions significantly improve, mental state improves, sleep quality improves, and there is confidence in rehabilitation 24/11 Has confidence in rehabilitation, very willing to cooperate with rehabilitation treatment, SDS score 44 (standard score <50 indicates no depression) |

Nursing Issue 5—Risk of Falling

| Date | Nursing Rationale | Nursing Goals | Nursing Measures | Nursing Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11/11 | Associated with left hemiparesis; left upper limb muscle strength 1 grade, left lower limb muscle strength 1 grade, MORSE score 70 | No falls occur during hospitalization | a. Inform the patient and family about the risk of falling and the hazards of falling; b. Explain the measures to prevent falls and the methods of dealing with falls during hospitalization; c. Instruct the patient on the requirements for clothing and shoes before getting out of bed; educate on the use of environmental aids (use of bathroom call bells, corridor railings); d. Encourage the patient, give them courage, tell them: You can do it; inform the patient's family not to be anxious, encourage the patient together; 1. Teach the correct positioning of the limbs to maintain joint function; 2. Teach the patient the "ten-finger handshake" and "bridge exercise" training, 3-4 times a day, 5-10 minutes each time; 3. Teach the family and patient to perform passive movement exercises on the right limb: hip, knee, ankle, shoulder, elbow, wrist joint extension and flexion, 3-4 times a day, 15-20 minutes each time; 4. Guide walking training: first practice standing with support, leg forward and backward movement, stepping (flexing knees, extending hips, and alternating forward and backward steps with further training of right leg balance), 3-4 times a day, 15 minutes each time | 16/11 Left upper limb muscle strength 1 grade, left lower limb muscle strength 1+ grade, MORSE score 70; patient can walk independently with one person's company |

Summary
Reflections
Through the systematic and holistic nursing model, the integration of theory and practice in case nursing has been achieved, mastering the methods, skills, and techniques of elderly assessment and nursing, improving self-clinical practice ability, improving the doctor-patient relationship, and increasing patient satisfaction

The aging society is becoming increasingly severe, but specialized geriatric nursing in our hospital is at a very weak stage.
