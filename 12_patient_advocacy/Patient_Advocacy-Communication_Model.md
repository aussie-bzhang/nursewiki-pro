Background
Research has found that asthma and anxiety can be mutually causal and influential. Anxiety can affect the control level and performance of asthma control, thereby increasing the burden of asthma disease.

Risk factors for asthma include host factors (genetic factors, obesity, and gender) and environmental factors (allergens, infection, tobacco smoke, air pollution, and diet, etc.).

Asthma is a disease characterized by eosinophilic and mast cell-mediated airway allergic inflammation and airway hyperreactivity, with the main clinical manifestations being recurrent attacks of wheezing and expiratory dyspnea.

The WHO estimates that the number of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) due to asthma worldwide is approximately 15 million per year, accounting for about 1% of the global disease burden. There are about 30 million asthma patients in China.

Untreated or不规范 treatment of asthma can lead to COPD, and even be fatal. However, standardized treatment and current treatment methods can control the disease in nearly 80% of asthma patients, allowing them to work and live without being affected by the disease.

Discharge Instructions
1. Establish a family medical record, continue to write a good asthma diary including date, weather, exercise, coughing, wheezing time and location, medication use, and adverse reactions.
2. Actively participate in asthma home activities and exchange experiences with other asthma patients. Report medication use, any special diet, and contact with special chemicals. Self-detect and evaluate changes in the condition, providing detailed information for the doctor as a reference for treatment.
3. Actively strengthen physical activity: bridge exercise, twice a day, for ten minutes each time, bed-side cycling, twice a day, for ten minutes each time, or can be combined with Tai Chi and walking. Try climbing stairs when the condition allows.
4. Abdominal breathing: practice three times a day, for 3-6 minutes each time, and use abdominal breathing when shortness of breath is significant.
5. Asthma Attack Management: During the attack, consume fluid or semi-liquid, light, and non-cold food. Eat less and more often, do not overeat. In addition, when an asthma attack occurs, consider supplementing fluids, with daily water intake reaching 2000 milliliters or more. Avoid eating fishy and spicy foods and foods known to trigger asthma attacks.
6. Regular follow-up appointments.
