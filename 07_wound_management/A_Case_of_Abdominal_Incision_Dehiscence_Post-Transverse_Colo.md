# A Case of Abdominal Incision Dehiscence Post-Transverse Colon Ostomy: Nursing Care
xxxxxxxxxxxxxx

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# Introduction
Abdominal incision dehiscence refers to the complete or partial rupture of the abdominal fascia layer after abdominal surgery, accompanied by or without intestinal extrusion outside the abdominal cavity. It is one of the serious complications after abdominal surgery, with an incidence rate of 0.4% to 3.5%.

Traditional dressing methods can achieve secondary healing, but often take a long time. How to promote the wound healing of patients as soon as possible to alleviate their psychological and economic burden is a concern for ostomy therapists.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Case Introduction
Patient: Mr. Mei, male, 72 years old, married, retired.
Diagnosis: Intestinal obstruction, extensive metastasis of rectal cancer.
Underwent a bilateral transverse colon stoma under general anesthesia on October 14, 2015.
On the third day after surgery, the abdominal incision dehisced by 2.5 cm with a small amount of exudate. Dressing was applied, and gauze was covered.
On October 19, 2015, the doctor requested a consultation with the ostomy therapist.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Nursing Assessment—Overall Assessment
Age: Elderly
Disease: Advanced rectal cancer, cough;
Diet: Liquid diet, poor appetite;
Psychological Status: Anxiety (Hamilton Anxiety Rating 15 points);
Knowledge Level: Deficient;
Laboratory Tests: Albumin 17.0g/L, Hemoglobin 108.0g/L, Total White Blood Cell Count 13.87×109/L

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Nursing Assessment—Local Assessment
Type: Surgical Incision
Location: Midline of the abdomen
Size: 2.5*0.6*1.6cm, no sinus tract
Color: 75% yellow tissue, 25% red tissue, visible absorbable sutures
Exudate: Small amount, pale red serous, no odor
Edges: No inversion
Periincisional Skin: No maceration, no rash
Pain Assessment: No pain at rest, 4 points during dressing change (Facial Expression Scale)

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Nursing Issues
Non-healing incision: Related to incision infection and cough;
Pain: Related to incision infection;
Nutritional Deficiency, Below the Body's Requirements: Related to increased consumption and insufficient nutritional intake;
Anxiety: Related to concern about the non-healing of the incision;
Knowledge Deficiency: Lack of knowledge about ostomy care and promoting wound healing.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Nursing Challenges
Bilateral transverse colon stoma with incision dehiscence
Difficult to apply pouch, prone to leakage

Ostomy located above the incision
Easy to contaminate the incision

Malnutrition—Low albumin
Difficult wound healing

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Nursing Goals
Prepare the wound bed for secondary suture
Alleviate pain
Improve nutritional status
Reduce anxiety
Patient and family understand ostomy care and promote wound healing knowledge

# Comparison of Pre- and Post-Effect

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# Nursing Evaluation
Patient's anxiety reduced, Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale 10 points.
Basal red tissue from <25% growth to >75%, potential tract from 4 o'clock to 7 o'clock disappeared, wound size reduced from 6.5cm*2.0cm*2.5cm to 5.5cm*2.3cm*1.3cm. The attending physician sutured the wound.
Pain reduced, reported mild pain during dressing change, facial expression scale score 4 points.
Low protein improved, albumin from 17.0g/L to 30.4g/L.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
# Summary

1. This case used the theory of moist wound healing and simple negative pressure technology, resulting in significant improvement of the wound in a short period of time.
2. As a stoma therapist, it is essential to fully assess the factors affecting patient wound healing and choose appropriate dressings.
3. Strengthen team collaboration.
4. Shortcomings: The collection of imaging data was not standardized; communication skills need to be improved; a blockage phenomenon occurred during negative pressure treatment, which may be caused by the application of silver ion alginate, and silver ion oil gauze dressing can be chosen for use.
