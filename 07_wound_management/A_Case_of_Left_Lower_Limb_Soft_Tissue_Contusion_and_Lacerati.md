# A Case of Left Lower Limb Soft Tissue Contusion and Laceration Nursing

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# Introduction
Soft tissue contusion and laceration: Generally caused by external mechanical stress, which induces injury when it reaches a certain intensity. It can cause contusion or (and) laceration of local soft tissue (including skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscle, and containing nerves, blood vessels, and lymphatic tissue).
Skin and soft tissue contusion and laceration is one of the most common accompanying symptoms of trauma, often seen in traffic accidents and industrial machinery accidents.
Skin and soft tissue contusion and laceration often cause extensive damage to small blood vessels in the subcutaneous tissue, and primary implantation and suture are often unable to survive.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Clinical Data
Patient: Ding, female, 49 years old, married, Han nationality.

On October 6, 2015, due to a traffic accident, she sustained a left lower limb soft tissue contusion and laceration. She underwent debridement and suture surgery at Jiangsu Provincial People's Hospital, and the operation was uneventful.

After surgery, the color of the traumatic flap gradually turned black, and consultation with the plastic surgery department was requested. The medical advice was to perform flap transplantation treatment, but the patient refused. The medical advice was to perform conservative debridement with a scalpel to remove necrotic tissue to promote wound healing.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# General Assessment

Nutritional Status: Good
Medical History: None
Current History: None
Psychological Status: Anxiety (Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale score 12), patient is insomnia, afraid to take a bath, repeatedly asks how long it will take to heal, whether the wound has improved compared to the last dressing change, etc.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Local Wound Assessment
Wound Type: Soft tissue contusion and laceration
Wound Location: Anterior compartment of left lower limb
Wound Size: 2.0*4.8cm, no sinus tract, no sinus
Wound Color: 75% red tissue, 25% yellow tissue
Wound Drainage: Moderate, pale red serous, no odor
Wound Edges: No rolling edges, no protuberances
Surrounding Skin: No maceration, no rash
Pain: Pain score 6 during dressing change (Facial Expression Pain Scale)

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Main Nursing Issues

Delayed wound healing: Related to the necrosis of the patient's traumatic flap and the presence of viable tissue in the wound

Pain: Related to contact with the wound during dressing change

Anxiety: Related to the patient's concerns about wound healing and pain during dressing change

Knowledge Deficiency: Lack of health education knowledge related to promoting wound healing.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Nursing Goals
Wound care, promote wound healing

Promote comfort, reduce pain during dressing change

Reduce anxiety

Health education, explain health education knowledge related to promoting wound healing

1. Wound Care

Saline irrigation of the wound

Conservative scalpel debridement

Non-adhesive foam dressing

# Nursing Measures
2. Pain Management

Use non-irritating saline to irrigate the wound

Perform conservative scalpel debridement

Choose non-adhesive foam dressing to reduce pain during dressing removal

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Nursing Measures
3. Psychological Care

Patience in listening to the patient's complaints, showing more concern and comfort to the patient

Explain the factors affecting wound healing and the current methods and effects of treatment

Inform the patient that wound healing is a process and not to worry, to be patient, to cooperate with treatment, and to show the patient the photos of the wound before and after the second dressing change to increase confidence in wound healing.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Nursing Measures
4. Health Education

Diet: Encourage the intake of light, high-protein, high-vitamin, and high-calorie foods, avoid spicy and刺激性 foods.

Activity and Rest: Adequate rest, elevate the affected limb during rest, avoid strenuous exercise.

Personal hygiene: Pay attention to personal hygiene, wrap the dressing with a plastic film during bathing to prevent the dressing from getting wet, or take a bath before dressing change.

Summary

Wound healing is a continuous and dynamic process, and treatment plans should be adjusted in accordance with the patient's overall condition.

Debridement of traumatic wounds using conservative sharp instruments has shown significant effects.

New dressing (foam dressing): Provides a moist healing environment, promoting wound healing.

Shortcomings: Short case time,不明显 wound healing.
