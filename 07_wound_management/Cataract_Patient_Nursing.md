# Characteristics of the Lens
1. Transparent
2. Elastic
3. Avascular
4. Biconvex
The lens is one of the important refractive media in the eye, presenting a biconvex lens shape, located behind the iris and in front of the vitreous body. The lens, like the lens of a camera, has a refractive effect on light and can also filter out some ultraviolet light to protect the retina. However, its most important function is to change the refractive power through the contraction or relaxation of the ciliary muscle, so that the focal point of the eye's convergence can accurately fall on the retina when looking at distant or near objects. The lens has no blood vessels, and the nutrition it needs comes from the aqueous humor. If there is a problem with the metabolism of the aqueous humor or if the lens capsule is damaged, the lens may become cloudy due to a lack of nutrition. The originally transparent lens becomes milky white and opaque, ultimately affecting vision, which is cataract.

   Senile Cataract:
1. Ageing, the lens gradually becomes cloudy, which is senile cataract, generally starting after 40-45 years old.
2. Etiology: The result of the combined effects of environment, nutrition, metabolism, and heredity. Excessive ultraviolet light exposure, excessive alcohol consumption, smoking, multiple deliveries in women, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, mental illness, and other factors are related to the formation of cataracts.
3. Oxidative damage causes the earliest changes in cataracts.

Iris Shadow
Iris shadow: In the immature stage of cataract, the cortex under the anterior capsule of the lens has not become completely cloudy, and there is still a layer of transparency. When using the oblique illumination method for examination, a crescent-shaped shadow can be seen on the side illuminated by the light, known as iris shadow.

# Treatment Methods

1. Medication: There are no drugs with proven efficacy. Early cataracts can be treated with some drugs to delay the progression of the disease, such as: Visible Bright, Catarrhine, Sha P'ai Shi eye drops, etc.

2. Surgery: For moderate to advanced cataracts, there is no effective drug that can restore the transparency of the cloudy lens, so surgery remains the main method of treatment for cataracts.

# Phacoemulsification and Intraocular Lens Implantation for Cataracts
Method: Phacoemulsification only requires a 3mm incision, using a phacoemulsification instrument to emulsify and aspirate the cloudy lens, and then implanting an artificial lens to replace the diseased lens.

# Advantages of Phacoemulsification and Intraocular Lens Implantation for Cataracts

1. Small surgical incision, no suture, postoperative astigmatism is small, recovery is fast, and the effect is good.

2. It is less likely to tear the posterior capsule during surgery, and the incidence of postoperative complications such as macular edema and retinal detachment is low.

3. The timing of surgery can be advanced to the immature stage, even the initial stage. As long as the cataract causes your vision to drop below 0.5, affecting daily life and work, surgery can be performed, and there is no need to wait until the vision drops to only light perception or manual, shortening the process of blurred vision caused by cataracts.

# Preoperative Nursing
Apply mydriatics as prescribed and monitor for any changes in intraocular pressure.
For patients with hypertension or diabetes, blood pressure and blood glucose levels should be controlled preoperatively.
   Blood pressure should be below 150/90 mm Hg, and fasting blood glucose should be below 8.3 mmol/L.
Three days before surgery, follow the doctor's instructions to instill antibiotic eye drops in both eyes, flush the lacrimal ducts and conjunctival sacs. Discontinue anticoagulant and blood circulation drugs, such as enteric-coated aspirin and Danshen tablets.
Focus on preventing respiratory diseases, as coughing during surgery may cause vitreous detachment and lead to surgical failure. Instruct patients on the correct techniques for coughing and sneezing.
On the day of surgery, clean the face, flush the lacrimal ducts and conjunctival sacs, and cover with sterile gauze.

# Preoperative Nursing

Instruct patients to practice covering their nose and mouth with a towel to help them adapt to the sterile drape covering during surgery.
In the morning on the day of surgery, flush the conjunctival sacs of both eyes with 250 ml of 0.9% sodium chloride solution + 80,000 units of gentamicin and cover the surgical eye. Change clothes, remove dentures and jewelry.
Mydriasis: Follow the doctor's instructions for mydriasis before surgery. Ideally, the pupil should be dilated to 6mm. The drug of choice is compound tropicamide, administered at 5-10 drops every 30 minutes before surgery, for a total of 3-5 times. For certain special patients, such as those with diabetes, due to the impact of the disease, the pupil may not dilate easily, so mydriasis should be started early and the number of mydriasis applications increased.

# Postoperative Nursing
1. General care: On the day after surgery, instruct patients to lie quietly in a supine or healthy side-lying position, minimize head movement, avoid bending over, straining during bowel movements,剧烈咳嗽, sneezing, etc. Prohibit patients from suddenly turning over or sitting up, and prohibit loud talking to prevent complications such as lens displacement, intraocular hemorrhage, sudden increase in intraocular pressure leading to wound dehiscence, and iris prolapse.

2. Close observation of the patient's condition, monitoring vital signs and overall health. Observe the surgical eye closely, check for the presence of dressing detachment or displacement, bleeding or exudation at the incision. Instruct patients not to remove the dressing themselves. Pay attention to and understand the postoperative pain situation. If the surgical eye suddenly becomes painful, it may indicate wound dehiscence or bleeding, and the doctor should be reported immediately.

# Postoperative Nursing Complications
**(I) Prevention of Postoperative Endophthalmitis**
1. Closely observe the local condition of the surgical eye, pay attention to the amount of secretion, the healing of the incision, and the transparency of the cornea.
2. Follow the doctor's instructions to instill antibiotic eye drops and hormone treatment in the surgical eye.
3. Local care: Assist the doctor in performing aseptic dressing changes daily. Cover the surgical eye with an eye shield if necessary, to prevent pressure or injury to the surgical eye.
4. Keep the dressing clean and dry. Generally, there will be no pain after surgery, but there may be eye blurring and mild foreign body sensation, which are normal. If there is significant eye pain, nausea, vomiting, sudden decrease in vision, or other discomfort, report to the doctor immediately.
5. Avoid dirty water entering the eye for the first two weeks after surgery, do not apply pressure to the surgical eye, and prevent trauma.

**(II) Prevention of Intraocular Lens Displacement**
1. Instruct patients to rest in bed as much as possible on the day of surgery, avoid vigorous activity, avoid bending over and straining, do not bend down to pick up objects, do not rub your eyes; speak softly, control coughing and sneezing. For those with coughing or vomiting, take cough suppressants or antiemetics to prevent lens displacement.
2. Avoid carrying heavy loads, long-term低头bending, and avoid excessive head movement. Get up slowly when eating, defecating, or urinating. Avoid overexertion of the eyes.
3. Do not smoke or drink alcohol after surgery; instruct patients to eat high-fiber foods such as fresh fruits and vegetables. Prevent constipation to avoid excessive straining and lens displacement. If there is no bowel movement or difficulty defecating for two days, a laxative can be administered.
4. Remove obstacles from the patient's frequently used areas, waterproof and anti-slip the ground, to prevent falls and lens displacement caused by the patient falling.

# Health Education
1. Schedule regular outpatient follow-up at 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months after discharge. If there are any abnormal conditions such as sudden redness, burning, excessive secretion, or vision loss in the eyes, seek medical attention promptly.
2. Administer eye drops as prescribed, with an interval of 5-10 minutes between two types of eye drops, 3 times a day, and continue for a month. Do not rub your eyes to prevent eye injuries. Rest your eyes, avoid prolonged viewing of television, reading, or using computers, avoid smoke and fumes, and maintain eye hygiene.
3. Avoid heavy physical activity for the first three months after surgery, and can engage in activities such as walking, slow walking, and Tai Chi.
4. Diet-wise, quit smoking and alcohol, eat more vegetables and fruits, and maintain smooth bowel movements. Diabetic patients should pay attention to diet and blood glucose control.
5. Maintain a positive mood, pay attention to rest and warmth, and prevent colds. For patients with poor refractive adjustment after intraocular lens implantation, if they need to wear glasses, they can go to the ophthalmology clinic for glasses after 3 months to improve vision.

# Eye Drop Administration Technique

Operation Steps: Wash hands, verify the patient's name, eye, drug name, concentration, and observe for any discoloration or precipitation in water-based preparations. The patient should assume a sitting or supine position, tilting the head slightly backward and towards the affected side. Use a cotton swab to wipe away the secretion from the affected eye. With the left hand, use the index finger or a cotton swab to pull down the lower eyelid, and with the right hand, hold the dropper or eye drops and place a drop into the conjunctival sac of the lower fornix. Use a finger to gently lift the upper eyelid to allow the medication to disperse in the conjunctival sac. Wipe away any excess medication that drips out, and instruct the patient to keep their eyes closed for 1-2 minutes.

# Precautions for Eye Drop Administration

. When administering eye drops, keep the dropper tip or bottle opening 2-3 cm away from the eye, avoid touching the eyelid margin, eyelashes, and fingers to prevent contamination;
. Do not press on the globe during administration, especially for patients with corneal ulcers or wounds;
. When administering atropine-like drugs, apply pressure to the lacrimal sac for 2-3 minutes to prevent absorption of the medication through the nasal mucosa and potential中毒.
Pay special attention to not mixing mydriatics with miotics, corrosive drugs, as this can lead to serious consequences. When administering multiple types of eye drops, start with the less irritating medication and then the more irritating one.
When using both eye drops and ointments, apply the eye drops first, then apply the ointment. There should be a 5-10 minute interval between each type of medication.
