# Concept
Laparoscopic Surgery:
   Several small incisions, 5-12mm in diameter, are made at different parts of the abdomen;
   Camera lenses and surgical instruments are inserted through these small incisions;
   The images captured by the camera are transmitted to a television screen;
   The surgeon operates on the outside using surgical instruments while observing the images.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Application of Laparoscopic Surgery in Gynecology
Laparoscopic surgery for ectopic pregnancy
Laparoscopic tubal ligation
Laparoscopic ovarian cystectomy
Laparoscopic myomectomy
Laparoscopic subtotal (total) hysterectomy
Laparoscopic-assisted vaginal hysterectomy
Surgery for infertility (tubal obstruction, hydrosalpinx, adhesions)
Major gynecological surgeries (endometrial cancer, cervical cancer, etc.)

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Advantages
Small incisions, quick recovery
Less interference with the abdomen
Less intraoperative bleeding
Mild postoperative pain
Short hospital stay
Small scars, aesthetic

# Preoperative Preparation (Part 1)
1. Skin preparation
Skin preparation range: from the xiphoid process upwards, to the upper 1/3 of the thigh, including the perineum
on both sides to the midaxillary line.

Especially pay attention to the cleanliness of the umbilicus
Umbilical cleanliness - Johnson's baby oil to remove dirt + PVP iodine swab for cleaning and disinfection

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Preoperative Preparation (Part 2)
2. Bowel preparation:
   Avoid eating gas-forming legumes and dairy products the day before surgery;
   Fast for 12 hours before surgery and 8 hours before drinking;
   Clean enema is given on the evening before surgery and in the morning of the day of surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Preoperative Preparation (Part 3)
3. Vaginal preparation:
     Routine vaginal examination is required before surgery;
      Vaginal irrigation should be performed twice or more;
      Patients with vaginal inflammation should be cured before considering surgery;

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Preoperative Preparation (Part 4)
4. Indwelling catheter:
  Urinary catheter is placed before laparoscopic surgery.

5. Preoperative examination:
  Laboratory tests, electrocardiogram, imaging, pulmonary function, etc.

# Preoperative Preparation (Part 5)
6. Psychological nursing
Patient concerns:
What will be removed, what will be left behind
What impact will the surgery have on the general functions of the body
Will the surgery affect sexual life
Will the surgery affect fertility
Will scars be left behind
What should be paid attention to before and after the surgery
How long will the hospital stay be

Pay attention to patient privacy, relieve tension and fear

# Postoperative Nursing (Part 1)
1. General nursing

(1) Position and activity
Routine post-anesthesia care, lying flat for 6 hours, head turned to one side to prevent aspiration;
Take a comfortable position 6 hours after no special circumstances, encourage ambulation according to the condition.
Move the limbs more after becoming conscious to prevent venous thrombosis;
Strengthen the care of the lower limbs for patients who were in lithotomy position during surgery;
Do not use a hot water bottle to prevent burns and bleeding,

(2) Oxygen therapy
Continuous low-flow oxygen therapy for 4-6 hours after surgery;
Can significantly reduce the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting;
Accelerates the excretion of residual carbon dioxide after artificial pneumoperitoneum, corrects hypercapnia;

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Postoperative Nursing (Part 2)
2. Close monitoring of vital signs:
   Routine electrocardiogram monitoring, closely observe vital signs within 6 hours after surgery;
   Whether there is internal bleeding and tracheal obstruction;
   Whether there is fever and delayed internal bleeding.

3. Abdominal incision care:
   Keep the dressing at the puncture site dry, check for bleeding or exudate;
   Minor bleeding at the puncture site can be stopped by pressing with a sandbag or abdominal binder;
   When vomiting or coughing, press the abdomen with both hands or use an abdominal binder to reduce abdominal pressure and decrease incision tension.

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Postoperative Nursing (Part 3)
4. Care of tubes

(1) Urinary catheter care:
Keep the perineum dry and clean;
Pay attention to keeping the urinary catheter unobstructed after surgery;
Generally, the catheter can be removed the day after surgery;
Postoperative patients with cervical cancer and others need to keep the catheter in place for 1-2 weeks;

(2) Drainage tube care:
Securely fix the catheter, keep the drainage tube unobstructed;
Record the amount and nature of the drainage fluid, change the drainage bag twice a week;

Abdominal bleeding - fresh red > 100ml/H
Ureteral fistula - increased drainage volume, pale yellow

Removal of pelvic and abdominal drainage tube - drainage fluid < 20ml, normal body temperature

# Complications Care (Part 1)
1. Bleeding
(1) Vascular injury:
   A serious complication with poor prognosis, immediate laparotomy is required for hemostasis if it occurs;
(2) Intraoperative bleeding:
   A common complication, requires the surgeon to have skilled techniques and actively prevent it during surgery;
(3) Incision bleeding:
   Observe whether the surgical incision dressing has渗血, and whether there are skin ecchymoses or masses;

Bleeding often occurs within 24 hours after surgery, mild cases are mostly puncture site bleeding, severe cases can lead to hemorrhagic shock.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
# Complications Care (Part 2)
2. Shoulder and Back Pain

Due to the accumulation of CO2 gas residue under the diaphragm stimulating the nerve reflex, it can last for several hours or several days and can disappear spontaneously without special treatment.

For those with obvious symptoms:

   Assist with turning over, move more;
   Appropriate massage and hot compress or gently apply pressure to the abdominal wall to expel the gas.
   Take the knee-chest position or the head-down, feet-up position to reduce the stimulation of the intercostal nerve, phrenic nerve, and diaphragm,
   Use analgesics as prescribed to relieve pain.
   Give low-flow oxygen therapy for 6 hours to accelerate the excretion of CO2.
   Oxygen therapy or the use of dexamethasone to promote the diffusion of CO2 in the body, reduce pain

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# Complications Care (Part 3)
3. Subcutaneous Emphysema

A unique complication of laparoscopy,
Common in obese patients, presenting as local fluffiness,
It can be absorbed spontaneously within 24 hours and does not require special treatment.
Large gas emphysema requires puncture and decompression.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Complications Care (Part 4)
4. Urological Injury

   Due to the close anatomical relationship between the ureter, bladder, and uterine appendages;
   Gynecological surgery is prone to injury to the bladder and ureters.

Postoperative Observation: Urine output and color;
If the following symptoms occur: reduced urine output, hematuria, pain over the pubic symphysis, and a feeling of fullness,
a high suspicion of bladder and ureteral injury should be entertained, and immediate report to the doctor is required.

Ureteral fistula -- Increase in drainage volume, appearing pale yellow.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# Complications Nursing (V)
5. Hypercapnia:
     During surgery, the CO2 pneumoperitoneum increases abdominal pressure, reduces chest and lung volume, leading to difficulty breathing, decreased blood pressure, and increased heart rate.

     Postoperative respiratory function training should be conducted;
     Continuous monitoring of blood oxygen saturation;
     Oxygen therapy should be administered to increase blood oxygen saturation and reduce the absorption of carbon dioxide.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
# Complications Nursing (VI)
6. Neurological Injury

Laparoscopic surgery requires the patient to be in a Trendelenburg position, and prolonged surgery time, improper placement of the shoulder support, can lead to brachial plexus palsy, resulting in varying degrees of shoulder pain and sensory loss;
The patient's upper limb abduction is less than 90°, or fixed to the side.

During the lithotomy position for bladder surgery, elevation and abduction of the lower limbs may result in sciatic or peroneal nerve palsy, manifested as progressive worsening of lower limb motor and sensory loss.
Proper fixation and support of the lower limbs during surgery should be maintained to avoid excessive external rotation.

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
# Complications Nursing (VII)
7. Lower Extremity Venous Thrombosis

Due to the laparoscopic surgery performed under three-port pneumoperitoneum, lithotomy position, general anesthesia, long surgery time, and slow venous blood flow, a vortex is formed in the valve sinus, which is prone to iliac-femoral venous thrombosis, especially in the left lower limb.
If the patient's lower limb swelling, pain, and tenderness, superficial vein dilation, increased skin temperature, and a positive Homans sign are found postoperatively, timely management should be initiated.
Instruct the patient to elevate the affected limb, apply a 50% magnesium sulfate compress, and simultaneously perform thrombolytic, anticoagulant, and anti-aggregation treatment to prevent the occurrence of venous gangrene and pulmonary embolism.
