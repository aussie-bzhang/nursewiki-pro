Cholecystectomy Surgery Overview

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
Indications for Surgery

Gallstones
Gallbladder polyps
Acute cholecystitis
Recurrent biliary colic or cholecystitis caused by gallstones is a common indication for cholecystectomy. If gallbladder polyps enlarge or show signs of malignancy, surgical removal of the gallbladder may be necessary to prevent cancer. If acute cholecystitis does not respond to medication, an emergency cholecystectomy may be required to control the condition.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
Contraindications for Surgery

Acute cholecystitis
During an acute cholecystitis attack, the surgical risk is high, and inflammation must be controlled first before cholecystectomy can be performed.

Coagulopathy
Patients with coagulopathies, such as haemophilia or severe liver disease, are one of the contraindications for cholecystectomy surgery.

02
Preoperative Preparation

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Patient Assessment

Assess patient health status
Determine surgical indications
Assess the patient's overall health through blood tests, electrocardiograms, and other methods to ensure surgical safety. Determine whether cholecystectomy is necessary based on the patient's condition and gallbladder function.

Assess surgical risks
Psychological assessment and support
Consider the patient's age, past medical history, and other factors to assess the risks and complications associated with surgery. Conduct a psychological assessment of the patient and provide preoperative psychological counseling to help alleviate anxiety and fear.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
Preoperative Examinations

Blood and urine analysis
Routine preoperative tests include blood and urine analysis to assess the patient's coagulation and renal function.

Imaging studies
Through ultrasound or CT scans, doctors can examine the gallbladder to determine the extent of the lesion and the difficulty of surgery.

Cardiopulmonary function assessment
Electrocardiograms and pulmonary function tests are conducted to ensure that the patient can withstand the physiological changes during surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
Preoperative Education and Preparation

Psychological counseling
Provide psychological support to the patient, alleviate surgical fear, explain the surgical process and expected outcomes, and enhance confidence.

Dietary adjustment guidance
Instruct the patient to adjust their dietary habits before surgery, avoid fatty foods, and ensure the best physical condition before surgery.

03
Surgical Process

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
Surgical Steps

Establishing surgical access
Removing the gallbladder
Checking and suturing

Postoperative recovery instructions
Through laparoscopic techniques, several small incisions are made in the abdomen to provide access for surgical instruments and a camera to enter the abdominal cavity. The doctor uses specialized surgical instruments to carefully separate the gallbladder from surrounding tissues and then completely remove the gallbladder. After surgery, the doctor will check the surgical area to ensure there is no bleeding or missed areas, and then suture the incisions layer by layer. The patient must follow medical instructions for recovery, including dietary adjustments, activity restrictions, and regular follow-up appointments.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Surgical Techniques

Precise anatomical dissection
Prevent bile duct injury
Use of ultrasonic knife
Precise anatomical dissection of the gallbladder surrounding tissues is crucial during cholecystectomy to avoid injury to adjacent organs. Careful manipulation during surgery is necessary to ensure that the bile duct is not damaged, preventing complications such as bile leakage after surgery. The use of ultrasonic knives can reduce intraoperative bleeding, improve surgical efficiency, and reduce the risk of postoperative infection.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
Surgical Equipment and Materials

Laparoscopic equipment
Laparoscopic surgery is a commonly used method for cholecystectomy, utilizing specialized cameras and surgical tools for minimally invasive surgery.

Haemostatic materials
Various haemostatic materials, such as coagulants and haemostatic sponges, are used during surgery to ensure safe hemostasis throughout the procedure.

04
Postoperative Nursing

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->

Postoperative Monitoring

Vital Signs Monitoring

Pain Assessment

Drainage Tube Management

Complication预警

Monitor the patient's vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, and respiratory rate to ensure they are within the normal range.

Regularly assess the patient's pain level and adjust the treatment plan promptly to alleviate discomfort.

Inspect and record the drainage status of the drainage tube, prevent infection, and ensure smooth drainage.

Closely observe for signs of postoperative complications such as fever and increased abdominal pain, and handle them promptly.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->

Pain Management

Medication

Non-pharmacological Therapy

The postoperative physician will prescribe analgesics based on the patient's condition to help control pain and promote recovery.

This includes methods such as cold and hot compresses, massage, etc., which can assist in pain relief and improve patient comfort.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
Diet and Activity Guidance

Postoperative Initial Diet Recommendations

Gradual Return to Daily Activities

Long-term Diet Adjustment

During the initial postoperative period, choose easily digestible, low-fat foods such as congee and noodles, and avoid greasy and spicy foods that may irritate the digestive system.

Postoperative patients should gradually increase their activity level based on their own recovery, such as walking, but avoid strenuous exercise.

To prevent digestive problems after cholecystectomy, patients need to adjust their dietary habits long-term, reducing the intake of high-fat foods.

05 Common Complications and Prevention

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Complication Overview

Biliary Duct Injury

Biliary duct injury is a serious complication of cholecystectomy, which can lead to bile leakage and biliary stricture.

Postoperative Infection

Postoperative infection includes incisional infection and abdominal infection, which need to be prevented through strict aseptic operation and postoperative care.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
Preventive Measures

Preoperative Assessment and Preparation

Early Postoperative Activity

Rational Diet Management

Conduct a comprehensive preoperative assessment, including liver function tests, to ensure that the patient's physical condition is suitable for surgery and reduce the risk of postoperative complications.

Encourage patients to engage in early postoperative activity such as turning in bed and walking, to prevent deep vein thrombosis.

Guide patients to adopt a low-fat diet and avoid greasy foods after surgery to reduce the occurrence of cholecystitis and biliary tract infection.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
Complication Management

Identification and Management of Biliary Duct Injury

Biliary duct injury is a serious complication of cholecystectomy that requires early detection through intraoperative cholangiography and prompt repair measures.

Prevention and Treatment of Postoperative Infection

Postoperative infection may include incisional infection or abdominal infection, which should be prevented through strict aseptic operation and appropriate antibiotic use.
