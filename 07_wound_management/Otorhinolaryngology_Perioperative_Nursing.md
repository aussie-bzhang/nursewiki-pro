## Postoperative Care for Ear Surgery Patients

After 6 hours of full anesthesia awakening, patients can start with semi-liquid or liquid diets. Three to five days later, as the condition improves, they can switch to regular food, preferably high in protein, vitamins, and light in taste.

After the patient is fully awake from anesthesia, consider feeding based on the patient's condition and medical advice.

## Postoperative Care for Nasal Surgery Patients

Avoid hot, spicy, hard, and irritating foods.

## Postoperative Care for Laryngeal Surgery Patients

After anesthesia awakening, patients can consume cold liquid foods such as cold milk, congee, soy milk, and fruit juice.

## Postoperative Care for Patients

Wound care: Do not remove the wound dressing yourself, keep the wound clean and dry.

## Common Symptoms

Fever: A normal postoperative fever is present for 3 days after surgery, generally not exceeding 38.5°C, and gradually returns to normal after 3 days.

5/11/2026

## Common Symptoms

Nausea and Vomiting: Early postoperative nausea and vomiting are often due to anesthetic reactions, which can stop after the anesthetic effect wears off. When vomiting, turn the head to the side to prevent aspiration.
Dry mouth: At this time, you can use a clean gauze dipped in a small amount of white water to moisten the lips and tongue to relieve discomfort, but remember not to drink water to avoid related complications.
5/11/2026

## Common Symptoms

Bleeding: Minor bleeding from the wound is a normal phenomenon.

## Postoperative Care for Thyroid Surgery

Difficulties in Breathing and Asphyxia

Special attention should be paid to postoperative monitoring: before the patient is fully awake from general anesthesia, observe the pupils, limb movements, coughing, and swallowing reflexes, frequently call the patient to determine the time of awakening; closely observe the condition, especially the color of the local skin after swelling, and judge whether there is bleeding; observe the color recovery of the lips and nail beds after pressing to judge whether there is a lack of oxygen, and blood oxygen saturation should reach above 95%, and arterial blood gas analysis should be performed if necessary.
5/11/2026

## Preventive Measures for Respiratory Obstruction

According to the cause and location of respiratory obstruction, various preventive measures should be taken:
1. Thoroughly aspirate sputum in a timely manner to keep the airway clear;
2. Provide continuous oxygen at a flow rate of 2-4L/min to increase blood oxygen saturation;
3. Nebulize with 100ml of physiological saline, 5mg of α-chymotrypsin, 5mg of dexamethasone, and 80,000U of gentamicin once every 4 hours to dilute sputum, humidify the airway, prevent sputum from drying and sticking in the nasal cavity and trachea, and block the airway;
4. Follow the doctor's orders to administer routine intravenous drip of corticosteroid drugs and hemostatic drugs for 3 days after surgery to prevent laryngeal edema and wound bleeding;
5. In case of widespread swelling of the neck and increased respiratory secretions after surgery, perform tracheotomy in a timely manner to ensure the patency of the respiratory tract.
5/11/2026

## Bleeding

Bleeding often occurs within 24 hours after surgery and is usually due to incomplete hemostasis during surgery. Closely observe the drainage situation, respiratory condition, and the color of the skin on the neck and upper chest to determine whether there is subcutaneous hematoma. Instruct the patient to reduce neck movement and protect the neck with a "V" hand gesture when coughing to prevent bleeding.
5/11/2026

## Injury to the Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve

Due to general anesthesia surgery, the patient's pronunciation and swallowing conditions cannot be tested during surgery. After the patient wakes up, a brief answer should be given to correctly assess the patient's voice. When eating, especially when drinking water, observe for aspiration and coughing to timely detect the presence of recurrent laryngeal nerve and superior laryngeal nerve injury.
5/11/2026

## Feeding Guidance for Patients Undergoing Laryngectomy

1. Assess the presence of vomiting or cough reflex and swallowing ability.
2. Inform the patient that coughing during postoperative oral feeding is common, and only through intensive training can eating be smooth.
3. Guide the patient on the correct method of eating: start with solid soft food training, and then train with liquid food after eating solid food without coughing.
4. When eating, tilt the head forward by 45 degrees, use the index finger to block the tube opening, breathe through the nose, and keep breathing and swallowing coordinated.
5. Eat slowly.
5/11/2026

## Postoperative Care for Craniotomy

After craniotomy, pay attention to the observation of consciousness and neurological signs: the focus is on the change of pupils, vital signs, and limb movement status, and pay attention to the presence of signs of intracranial hemorrhage. Postoperative respiratory and electrocardiographic monitoring should be provided, including blood oxygen saturation monitoring. Pay attention to the recovery of the patient's limb function after surgery, whether there is improvement or aggravation, and listen to the patient's complaints more often.
5/11/2026

## Care of Drainage Tubes

For patients with intracranial tumors, placing intracranial cavity drainage after surgery can prevent increased intracranial pressure caused by intracranial hematoma and effusion, and is also conducive to observing the condition, so special attention should be paid to the drainage tube. ① Pay attention to keeping the drainage tube unobstructed, avoiding twists and turns. ② Control the negative pressure suction when connecting negative pressure drainage. Pay attention to avoiding all factors that can cause increased intracranial pressure, such as coughing, nose blowing, and urinary retention.
5/11/2026

## Observation and Prevention of Complications After Craniotomy

1. Intracranial hematoma: Incomplete hemostasis during surgery is the main cause of intracranial hematoma. In addition, rapid decrease in intracranial pressure can also lead to intracranial hemorrhage. Therefore, close observation should be made within 48 hours after surgery. If unequal pupils, hemiparesis, or increased intracranial pressure occur during this period, CT examination should be performed in a timely manner.
2. Brain edema: Brain edema usually reaches its peak 48 hours after surgery and gradually subsides after 5-7 days. As long as sufficient oxygen is provided, regular diuresis and hormone treatment, and lowering body temperature, most patients can get through the brain edema period; if brain edema is severe and drug treatment cannot be controlled, surgery can be performed to decompress.
3. Observation and nursing of cerebrospinal fluid leakage: Craniotomy surgery may damage the dura mater or lead to cerebrospinal fluid leakage due to incomplete repair after resection of the dura mater due to the lesion. Cerebrospinal fluid leakage can lead to complications such as low intracranial pressure syndrome (dizziness, headache, nausea, delayed response, more obvious symptoms when sitting or standing), intracranial gas, cranial nerve injury, and intracranial infection. Pay attention to the identification and localization observation of cerebrospinal fluid and the fistula, and apply sterile cotton balls loosely in the nasal cavity in case of cerebrospinal fluid rhinorrhea, and change them in a timely manner after they are moistened. The nursing of cerebrospinal fluid leakage should focus on maintaining low intracranial pressure to promote the natural repair of the dura mater.

The patient should remain in bed, maintaining a semi-recumbent position, and avoid actions such as coughing, sneezing, and nose blowing to prevent constipation. Limit fluid and salt intake, and use antihypertensive drugs to reduce intracranial pressure as appropriate. Administer antibiotics to prevent intracranial infection. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) often leaks from the nose or ear on the day of surgery, usually stopping within 3-5 days. CSF leaks are difficult to heal in the short term and are highly susceptible to intracranial infection. Therefore, careful observation of the location, quantity, and timing of CSF leakage is necessary. In the event of leakage, active guidance and infection prevention for the patient should be implemented. Long-term non-healing cases may require repair surgery.

4. Neurological Injury: Neurological dysfunction is a common complication of craniobasal surgery. Among the neurological dysfunctions caused by craniobasal tumor surgery, the dysfunction resulting from posterior cranial nerve injury is particularly important. For patients with posterior cranial nerve injury causing hoarseness, coughing, and swallowing difficulties, nasogastric feeding should be provided to prevent aspiration pneumonia. For patients unable to recover effective spontaneous breathing, artificial respiration, synchronized assisted ventilation, or controlled ventilation may be considered. In cases of respiratory distress, tracheotomy should be performed. Oxygen therapy should be administered reasonably to maintain blood oxygen saturation above 97%. Low-flow continuous oxygen therapy is preferred.
5/11/2026

5. Intracranial Infection: The surgical resection range of intracranial and communicating tumors is extensive, sometimes involving non-sterile areas such as the paranasal sinuses, nasal cavity, and oral cavity. Craniotomy often causes tissue defects at the base of the skull. Therefore, intracranial infection is highly likely to occur. Postoperative combined use of effective antibiotics is recommended, and nurses should arrange the intervals for antibiotic administration reasonably to ensure effective blood drug concentrations. Strengthen oral care and nasal lavage. Monitor changes in body temperature and be alert for symptoms of meningeal irritation such as headache, vomiting, and neck stiffness to determine the presence of intracranial infection.
5/11/2026

Prevention of Other Complications: Common complications of craniobasal surgery include epilepsy, diabetes insipidus, gastrointestinal bleeding, intractable hiccups, oliguria or anuria, pulmonary edema, fever, electrolyte imbalance, and acid-base imbalance. Prevention and treatment should be carried out according to general routine procedures.
