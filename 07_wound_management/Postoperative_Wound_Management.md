3. Postoperative General Nursing:

⑥ Wound Care:
- Regularly inspect the incision site for signs of infection or complications.
- Keep the wound clean and dry.
- Apply appropriate dressings as per medical advice.
- Monitor for signs of wound dehiscence or necrosis.

⑦ Pain Management:
- Assess and manage pain effectively using appropriate analgesics.
- Educate the patient on pain management techniques and the importance of pain control.

⑧ Psychological Support:
- Provide emotional support to the patient and their family.
- Address any concerns or fears regarding the surgery and recovery process.
- Encourage open communication and provide resources for further support if needed.

⑨ Education and Discharge Planning:
- Educate the patient on postoperative care, including wound care, activity restrictions, and signs of complications.
- Provide discharge instructions and follow-up appointments as necessary.
- Ensure the patient understands the importance of adherence to the prescribed treatment plan.

⑥ Guidance on Functional Exercise
Consider both the whole body and local areas
Restore physiological functions
Active exercise as the main method, passive exercise as a supplement
Gradual progression
Phased exercise: initial phase, intermediate phase, and late phase

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Functional Exercise Methods
Initial phase: postoperative 1-2 weeks
Upper limb postoperative: as soon as possible to ambulate, shrug shoulders, and make a fist
Lower limb postoperative: ankle and toe joint flexion and extension exercises
Intermediate phase: 2 weeks after surgery to the removal of external fixation
Strengthen functional exercise, use simple equipment
Late phase: use equipment to strengthen functional exercise

Common Postoperative Complications

Incision infection
Decubitus pneumonia and pulmonary infection
Deep vein thrombosis of the lower limb
Urine tract infection
Pressure sores

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
Preventive Nursing Measures for Incision Infection
Preoperative skin preparation
Pay attention to the precision of surgical techniques, strict hemostasis, and avoid incision bleeding and hematoma
Improve the patient's nutritional status and enhance anti-infection ability
Keep the incision dressing clean, dry, and free from contamination
Correct and reasonable use of antibiotics;
Healthcare personnel should strictly follow hand hygiene before and after contacting patients, change dressings with strict aseptic technique, and prevent iatrogenic infection.

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
Preventive Nursing Measures for Pneumonia

Assist patients in effective coughing, expectoration, and deep breathing
For sputum that is thick and difficult to cough up, and for those who are unable to cough, sputum aspiration should be performed.

Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

Definition: Abnormal coagulation of blood in the deep veins of the lower limb, blocking the venous lumen, leading to venous return obstruction.
Clinical manifestations: Unilateral limb swelling and sudden onset of swelling, accompanied by pain. Especially in the area of the gastrocnemius muscle in the lower calf, the pain is worse when walking.

<!-- Slide number: 24 -->
Causes of DVT
Slowing of blood flow due to surgery
Vascular wall injury caused by trauma
Blood in a hypercoagulable state

<!-- Slide number: 25 -->
Preventive Nursing Measures for DVT
Encourage early activity after injury; for bedridden patients, active and passive movements should be performed.
For high-risk patients, use elastic bandages or elastic stockings on the lower limbs to promote blood return.
Perform air pressure pump massage on both lower limbs twice a day to increase blood flow speed.
Low molecular weight heparin sodium is injected subcutaneously daily for drug prevention.
Try to avoid the lower limbs, especially the left lower limb, when puncturing.

Third, Postoperative Complications and Nursing
Urine tract infection
Cause:
      Usually due to postoperative indwelling catheter or long-term bed rest
Manifestations: Chills, fever, renal area pain, frequent urination, urgency, and urine examination shows red blood cells and pus cells.
Treatment:
      Promptly handle urinary retention, keep the urine通畅, drink plenty of water, pay attention to aseptic technique when inserting a catheter, and use antibiotics.

Third, Postoperative Complications and Nursing
Pressure sores
Cause:
      Due to long-term bed rest, wheelchair use, splints, and plaster casts
Manifestations:
      Erythema, blisters, ulcers, and sores in the areas of skin under pressure
Treatment:
       Prevent pressure: flat and soft bedding, protective pads at bony prominences, and correct care of splints and casts
       Care for compressed skin: change positions, local massage, skin cleaning, and prevent injury
