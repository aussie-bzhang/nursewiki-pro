# Definition of Fast-Track Surgery (FTS)
Postoperative rapid recovery has always been the goal and research focus of medical personnel, and it is also the expectation of patients!
It refers to a series of effective measures taken during the perioperative period, combined and synergistic results, to reduce various stress factors, accelerate the rapid recovery of patients after surgery, and shorten the hospital stay. Among these measures, nursing plays a crucial role.

## Preoperative Nursing
1. Preoperative education
        The majority of patients requiring surgical treatment in the liver and gallbladder surgery department have tumors, and they may experience varying degrees of anxiety and fear, leading to symptoms such as insomnia and gastrointestinal dysfunction. Comprehensive preoperative education for patients is beneficial for alleviating stress and stabilizing emotions, accepting surgery with the best mindset, and actively cooperating, such as postoperative coughing and expectoration, early mobilization, etc.

## Preoperative Nursing
2. Preoperative nutrition:
      To reduce the stress response of surgery, it is very important to strengthen nutrition in preoperative care for patients with liver and gallbladder diseases, with special nutritional requirements. The principle is to eat light, easily digestible and absorbable, and nutritious food, with a diet of small and frequent meals, high in protein, high in vitamins, low in fat, and low in salt.

## Preoperative Nursing
3. Preoperative functional exercise
      For patients with weak physical condition and elderly age, preoperative exercise can increase cardiopulmonary function, alleviate the patient's tension, increase tolerance to surgery, and reduce the stress response of surgery. Preoperative guidance for patients to perform walking, climbing stairs, etc. Patients who can climb two flights of stairs without shortness of breath are considered to be able to tolerate surgery.

## Preoperative Nursing
4. Preoperative bowel preparation
    Liver and gallbladder surgery is abdominal surgery, and it is routine to perform bowel preparation before surgery to reduce the incidence of possible intra-abdominal contamination and anastomotic leakage during surgery. However, recent studies have shown that there is no need for strict mechanical bowel preparation before surgery. The method of bowel preparation using oral senna and mineral oil can meet the requirements of surgery while reducing discomfort and postoperative intestinal bloating caused by other bowel preparation methods, and the gastrointestinal function is quickly restored.

## Preoperative Nursing
5. Preoperative fasting

    Fasting for 12 hours before surgery is one of the "conventional" practices before surgery, but due to liver dysfunction, patients with liver and gallbladder diseases already have a problem of insufficient glycogen reserve. Early fasting before surgery is prone to hypoglycemia. Therefore, for elective surgery patients in the liver and gallbladder surgery department, if there are no factors affecting gastric emptying, it is recommended to start fasting at midnight and stop drinking 3 hours before surgery. Small and frequent clear liquid diets can be taken until entering the operating room, which can dilute gastric acid and reduce pH value, and stimulate gastric emptying.

## Intraoperative
1. Intraoperative warming:
      During hepatectomy, most cases require hepatic blood flow occlusion, and the residual liver may have ischemia-reperfusion injury, which cannot be restored immediately. Therefore, the metabolism is low, and in addition to the low temperature of the surgical environment, the patient's body temperature after surgery can be 1-3℃ lower than the normal body temperature. Intraoperative hypothermia can lead to a series of adverse consequences. Therefore, patients with liver and gallbladder surgery should take more active warming measures, such as: raising the room temperature, using warming blankets and warming covers, and all intravenous infusions and abdominal lavage fluids should be warmed. At the same time, excessive fluid resuscitation and blood transfusion should be reduced.

Three, Postoperative Nursing
3. Nasogastric and Urinary Catheters:
Traditional view: Routine placement of nasogastric tubes for gastrointestinal decompression before abdominal surgery.
Modern view: For minor hepatobiliary surgeries such as cholecystectomy, LCBDE, LC, etc., nasogastric tubes are not placed preoperatively.
   Long-term placement of nasogastric tubes postoperatively can cause discomfort, increased nausea and vomiting, making patients irritable, restless, and insomnia.
   Immediate removal or removal within 24 hours postoperatively.
   Why?
   Early removal of nasogastric tubes reduces patient discomfort and facilitates coughing up sputum, reducing complications such as pulmonary infections.

## Four. Nursing of Postoperative Complications in Hepatobiliary Surgery
1. Prevention of Biliary Fistula
   Biliary fistula can be caused by bile duct injury, lower end obstruction of the common bile duct, and poor T-tube drainage.
   **Strengthen Observation**: Fever, abdominal distension, abdominal pain, or yellow-green bile in the abdominal drainage fluid in the patient may indicate a biliary fistula.
   **Properly Secure Drainage Tubes**: Secure the abdominal drainage tube or T-tube to the abdomen with sutures or adhesive tape.
   **Maintain Uninterrupted Drainage**: Regularly squeeze the drainage tube from the proximal to the distal end to maintain unobstructed drainage.
