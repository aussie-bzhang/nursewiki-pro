# Nursing Measures
(I) Preoperative nursing
1. Routine abdominal surgery preoperative care
   (1) Preoperative examination
   (2) Preoperative guidance
   (3) Actively assist doctors in managing medical complications
   (4) Perform skin preparation, gastrointestinal preparation, drug allergy test, blood reservation, assist patients with bathing, changing clothes, and promoting sleep one day before surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Nursing Measures
   (5) Observe the patient's vital signs and condition changes, detect whether the surgery needs to be suspended in time, such as fever, high blood pressure, excessive fear, menstrual period, etc.
   (6) On the day of surgery, assist the patient to remove removable dentures, valuable items, etc. After the ward nurse carefully checks the patient's bed number, name, age, hospital number, operation name, and other medical records, the patient is sent to the operating room and the handover is carried out face-to-face with the operating room nurse.

# Nursing Measures
   (7) The ward nurse prepares the麻醉bed according to the type of surgery and anesthesia method, prepares the postoperative monitoring equipment and emergency supplies.
2. Key points of preoperative nursing for gynecological abdominal surgery
   (1) Psychological support
   (2) Skin preparation: the range is from the xiphoid process upwards, to the upper 1/3 of the two thighs, and to the midaxillary line on both sides, the pubic hair in the external genitalia should be completely removed.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Nursing Measures
   (3) Gastrointestinal preparation: generally enema 1-3 times one day before surgery, the selected enema agents include soap water, isotonic saline, or glycerin solution, etc., and oral laxatives can be taken first if necessary before enema. On the evening before surgery, easy-to-digest food can be eaten, and fasting for 6-8 hours before surgery, and fasting for 4-6 hours before surgery to reduce nausea and vomiting caused by traction of internal organs during surgery, and to facilitate the recovery of intestinal function after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
# Nursing Measures
   (4) Vaginal preparation: for total hysterectomy, vaginal preparation is required. ①Purpose: to disinfect the vagina and cervix, and avoid infection during surgery. ②Method: from 3 days before surgery, use 0.1%-0.5% iodophor to wipe the vagina and cervix, and place antibiotics such as metronidazole in the vaginal fornix, 1-2 times a day.
   (5) Bladder preparation: routine placement of a retained urinary catheter in the morning of surgery, and keep it unobstructed, to avoid bladder injury during surgery or complications such as urinary retention after surgery, and to facilitate full exposure of the surgical field during surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
# Nursing Measures
(II) Postoperative nursing
1. Routine abdominal surgery postoperative care
   (1) Bedside handover: after the surgery is completed, the patient is sent back to the ward, the on-duty nurse should carry out a detailed bedside handover with the operating room nurse and anesthesiologist, immediately measure the pulse, respiration, blood pressure, observe consciousness, various tubes, wound dressing, and record them.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# Nursing Measures
   (2) Position: after 6-8 hours of supine position, assist the patient to turn over, and take semi-recumbent position the next morning if there are no special circumstances, which is conducive to relaxation of abdominal muscles, reduction of abdominal incision tension, pain relief, and at the same time, it is conducive to deep breathing, increase lung capacity, reduce atelectasis, etc., and it is also conducive to abdominal drainage. After lumbar anesthesia, it should be supine for a period of time to prevent headache.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Nursing Measures
   (3) Observation of vital signs: usually, within 2 hours after surgery, blood pressure, pulse, and respiration are monitored every half hour and recorded, and then changed to every hour after stabilization. The body temperature may slightly increase for 1-3 days after surgery, generally not exceeding 38.5°, and the body temperature should be measured 3 times a day. If the body temperature remains high or increases after returning to normal, it indicates the possibility of infection.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# Nursing Measures
   (4) Wound and pain care: observe the wound every 2-4 hours after surgery, observe whether the wound dressing has bleeding or leakage, etc. Fully assess the patient's degree of wound pain and use analgesics as prescribed.

# Nursing Measures

② Maintain the patency of the urinary catheter: prevent the catheter from twisting, blocking, or falling off.
③ Observe urine volume and color and record: The urine output of postoperative patients should be at least 50ml per hour. If the urine output is less than 30ml per hour, accompanied by a decrease in blood pressure, a fine pulse, the patient is restless, thirsty, or complaints of anal坠胀感, actively cooperate with the doctor in handling abnormalities.
④ Prevent urinary tract infections: perform perineal care twice a day, and encourage patients to drink 2000-2500ml of water per day.

# Nursing Measures
  ⑤ Train bladder function to prevent urinary retention: For patients undergoing radical hysterectomy, start clamping the urinary catheter 3 days before catheter removal, opening it once every 2 hours. After catheter removal, encourage patients to urinate spontaneously and perform a bladder residual urine measurement. The normal bladder residual urine volume should be less than 100ml. If the bladder residual urine volume exceeds 100ml, reinsert a catheter.

# Nursing Measures
 (2) Negative pressure drain tube care: For surgeries with a wide range and large wound areas, such as radical hysterectomy and radical ovarian cancer surgery, there is often a lot of bleeding and exudation, and an abdominal drain tube is often required. Ward nurses should carefully check the placement, fixation, and patency of the drain tube, prevent it from falling off or folding, ensure the patency of the negative pressure drain tube, and observe the color and volume of the drain fluid. The negative pressure drain fluid after surgery should be pale red, 50-100ml/d, generally placed for 72 hours. If the color is bright red, the volume is high, and accompanied by a decrease in blood pressure, a fine pulse, the patient is restless, thirsty, or complaints of anal坠胀感, consider the possibility of intra-abdominal hemorrhage.

  3. Common postoperative complications and nursing points
 (1) Urinary retention
 (2) Abdominal distension: Patients usually recover intestinal motility and pass gas around 36 hours after surgery. Common causes of abdominal distension include: ① The intestinal tract is stretched and stimulated during surgery, and the suppression of anesthetic drugs on intestinal function can lead to weakened intestinal motility or intestinal paralysis; ② Postoperative patients may swallow a large amount of gas due to reasons such as groaning and holding their breath, which can worsen abdominal distension; ③ Postoperative reduction in activity due to anesthesia and pain can weaken intestinal motility; ④ Postoperative electrolyte imbalance can lead to hypokalemic intestinal paralysis; ⑤ Due to the formation of intestinal adhesions, intestinal obstruction may occur.

# Nursing Measures
 (3) Urinary tract infection
 (4) Deep vein thrombosis of the lower extremities: Obesity, hyperlipidemia, elderly females, etc., are prone to deep vein thrombosis. Postoperatively, guide and assist patients in turning over, moving their lower limbs, and soaking their feet in warm water.
