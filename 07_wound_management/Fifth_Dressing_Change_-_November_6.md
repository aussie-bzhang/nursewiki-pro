# Health Guidance[5]
Compression Therapy: Instruct the patient to wear medical compression stockings for at least 12 hours per day,
            wearing them upon waking and removing them before bedtime.
Skin Care: Avoid using cold water to wash feet, do not wear tight clothing or belts, protect the lower limbs, avoid injury, and wear appropriate footwear and socks.
Functional Exercise: Maintain appropriate physical exercise, instruct the patient to perform gastrocnemius contraction exercises to promote venous return. Elevate the limbs during rest above heart level, avoid prolonged standing.
Dietary Care: Quit smoking, reduce weight, eat a light and nutritious diet that is easy to digest.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# Nursing Experience
First: Compression therapy is the gold standard for the treatment of lower limb venous ulcers, but it is essential to rule out arterial problems before using compression therapy. Absolutely do not perform compression therapy if there are concurrent arterial issues.
Second: Pay attention to the care of the skin around the ulcer.
Third: The role of health education cannot be overlooked; health education runs throughout the treatment process.
Fourth: Improve patient compliance, ensuring that patients and their families accept and learn how to use compression stockings and bandages, understanding the importance of compression therapy.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Summary
    Lower limb venous ulcers are difficult to heal. The use of silver ion foam dressings can create a moist, low-oxygen environment, promoting epithelialization while preventing excessive granulation tissue formation above the wound. Paying attention to nutritional support, developing individualized health education prescriptions, strengthening pain management, and providing psychological care can promote wound healing.
