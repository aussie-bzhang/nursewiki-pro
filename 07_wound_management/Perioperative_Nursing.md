# Perioperative Nursing
Second Affiliated Hospital of Gan Medical University
Wang Qiulan

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# Overview
Perioperative period:
          The period before, during, and after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Section 1: Preoperative Nursing
(1) Preoperative period:
           The period from the patient's decision to undergo surgery to the patient being taken to the operating table.

(2) Nursing focus:
      Assess and correct psychological and physiological issues that may increase surgical risk, provide psychological support to the patient regarding surgery, and guide training for adapting to postoperative changes.

(3) Classification of surgery:
           1. By surgery timing: Emergency   Elective     Time-limited
           2. By extent of thoroughness: Radical   Palliative

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Nursing Assessment
(1) Health history (medical history and health status, surgical history, medication history, drug allergy history, personal history)

(2) Physical and psychological status:
           1. Physical condition (age, nutritional status, fluid balance status, infection, function of important organs)
           2. Psychological and social status
         (Psychological status, family and social status)

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Nursing Assessment
(3) Diagnostic tests
            1. Laboratory (blood, urine, stool routine, coagulation time, prothrombin, blood type, blood cross match test, blood electrolytes, liver and kidney function, blood glucose, urine glucose)
            2. Chest X-ray
            3. Electrocardiogram
            4. Pulmonary function, blood gas analysis

# Nursing Diagnosis/Issues     [Nursing Goals]
Anxiety, fear      Anxiety and fear are eliminated or reduced
Knowledge deficit    The patient understands the knowledge of cooperation before and after surgery
Nutritional disorder    Nutritional disorder is improved
Fluid deficit    The patient's fluid balance is maintained
Disordered sleep pattern    The patient has sufficient rest and sleep before surgery
Risk of infection    Infection has not occurred or infection is controlled

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Nursing Measures
(1) Psychological care (causes of fear and anxiety, explanation, communication and care, psychological support, explanation of the purpose, method, and precautions of surgery)
(2) Routine preoperative preparation
        1. Respiratory preparation (quit smoking 2 weeks before surgery, control pulmonary infection, postural expectoration, expectorant lozenges to thin sputum, asthma with hormone nebulization).

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Nursing Measures
   2. Gastrointestinal preparation
       Purpose: ① Reduce vomiting and aspiration caused by anesthesia;
                   ② Prevent complications such as pulmonary infection;
                   ③ Reduce postoperative bloating and gastrointestinal complications.
      Methods:
         ① General surgery: fasting for 12 hours, no drinking for 4 hours
     ② Gastrointestinal surgery: start with liquid diet 1-3 days before surgery, and place a gastric tube, lavage, or enema the night before surgery as needed.
     ③ Colorectal surgery: start taking oral intestinal antibiotics, vitamin K, and laxatives 3 days before surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Nursing Measures
        3. Skin preparation
         Purpose: Prevent incision infection
         Methods: General surgery (shaving, cleaning, changing clothes)
                    Special surgery (head, face, oral cavity, orthopedic, perineal)
     Time: One day before surgery, orthopedic for three consecutive days.
     Range: 15-20cm around the incision center

# Nursing Measures
   ◆ (3) Special patient preparation (correcting malnutrition, correcting dehydration and acidosis, diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, liver and kidney dysfunction, preoperative iodine for hyperthyroidism)
 ◆ (4) Morning nursing on the day of surgery
       ① Perform four measurements, change the date if fever or menses; ② Check for skin preparation, changing clothes, fasting, and no drinking; ③ Follow the doctor's instructions for enema or gastric tube insertion; ④ Empty the bladder or insert a urinary catheter; ⑤ Remove dentures or jewelry; ⑥ Preoperative medication; ⑦ Send the patient and materials to the operating room; ⑧ Prepare the bed unit.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Nursing Measures
(5) Emergency surgery preparation:
          1. Act quickly to establish an intravenous access, correct shock.
          2. Simply cover the wound.
          3. No eating or drinking, skin preparation, drug sensitivity test.
         4. Emergency blood and urine routine tests, coagulation time, blood type, blood cross match test.
         5. Preoperative medication, ask the patient to urinate, and send them to the operating room.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Evaluation
(1) Whether the anxiety and fear about surgery are eliminated or reduced.
(2) Whether the patient understands the disease and the knowledge of cooperation before and after surgery.
(3) Whether the nutritional status is improved.
(4) Whether fluid and electrolyte imbalance and acid-base imbalance are corrected.
(5) Whether the patient has sufficient rest and sleep before surgery.
(6) Whether the patient has not developed infection or infection has been controlled.

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Section 2: Postoperative Nursing
(1) Postoperative period: Refers to the nursing care of the patient from the time they return to the ward after surgery until discharge.

(2) Nursing focus:
          ① Restore normal physiological function as soon as possible
          ② Reduce physical and psychological pain and discomfort
          ③ Prevent the occurrence of complications.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Nursing Assessment
(1) General condition
            Understand the anesthetic method, surgical method, intraoperative management, intraoperative blood loss, fluid and blood transfusion volume, urine volume, and medication during surgery, the placement of drainage tubes and their locations, and functions.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
# Nursing Assessment
(Three) Surgical fever:
           Due to the body's response to surgical trauma, the body temperature of postoperative patients may slightly increase, generally not exceeding 38°C, and gradually return to normal within 1-2 days.

(Four) Anesthesia recovery
           Assess the patient's consciousness, respiratory and circulatory function, limb movement and sensation, and skin color, etc., to judge the awakening and degree of awakening of anesthesia.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
# Nursing Assessment
(Five) Incision and drainage situation
            Dressing fixation, secretion, bleeding, infection, whether the drainage is unobstructed, the nature, color, and amount of drainage fluid.

(Six) Emotional response
           Concerned about the surgical outcome and prognosis, anxiety and depression may occur, even negative and pessimistic disappointment.

# Nursing Diagnosis/Issues      [Nursing Goals]
(One) Inefficient respiratory pattern   Stable vital signs, improved breathing
(Two) Risk of fluid deficit   Maintenance of electrolyte balance
(Three) Change in comfort   No postoperative discomfort, able to rest
(Four) Nutritional imbalance   Maintenance and improvement of postoperative nutrition
(Five) Decreased activity tolerance   Increased activity tolerance
(Six) Knowledge deficit   Understanding postoperative rehabilitation knowledge and cooperation with treatment and nursing
(Seven) Anxiety and fear   Emotional stability
(Eight) Potential complications   No complications occur or are promptly identified and treated

# Nursing Measures
(One) Positioning and transfer
           1. Receiving the patient: Secure the drainage tube, minimize movement, and avoid postural hypotension and drainage tube detachment.
        2. Positioning: First, follow the position of anesthesia, then follow the position of surgery (general anesthesia, lumbar anesthesia, other anesthesia, cranial, neck and chest, abdominal, spinal and gluteal, extremities, shock)
    Advantages of semi-recumbent position: ① Beneficial for blood circulation and patient breathing, increasing lung ventilation; ② Relaxes the abdominal muscles, reducing the tension of the abdominal incision; ③ Allows inflammatory exudates to flow to the pelvic cavity, preventing the formation of subphrenic abscess.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# (Two) Maintaining respiratory and circulatory function
    1. Close observation of vital signs
             Measure every 15-30 minutes for patients under general anesthesia or major surgery, and change to 1-2 hours after the condition is stable, and record it well. It is best to enter the ICU for monitoring.
    2. Maintaining a clear airway
           Prevent tongue prolapse, encourage deep breathing, effective coughing to promote expectoration and lung expansion, frequent turning, nebulization for thick sputum, and suctioning.
    3. Oxygen therapy
    4. Preventing hypotension
             Administration of fluid; prevent postural hypotension when sitting up or standing up

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
# (Three) Maintaining gastrointestinal function
    1. Gastrointestinal decompression tube:
         Keep the gastric tube unobstructed and effective in decompression.
    2. Encourage movement:
       Turn over, bed exercises, and early ambulation to promote peristalsis
    3. Promoting peristalsis:
           If there is still no peristalsis 3-4 days after surgery, follow the doctor's instructions to give glycerin suppositories, rectal gas, or enema.
    4. Oral care:
       Provide good oral care for the patient, especially for unconscious patients. For dry lips, apply glycerin to protect and maintain oral hygiene.

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
# (Four) Supplementation of nutrition and water, electrolyte imbalance
  1. Fasting and eating:
           ① For non-abdominal surgery, local anesthesia, and minor surgery, eating can be resumed immediately after surgery.
           ② For spinal anesthesia, eating can be resumed 6 hours after surgery according to the patient's needs.
           ③ For patients under general anesthesia, wait until the anesthesia is清醒, without nausea and vomiting, first give a liquid diet, and then change to semi-liquid or regular food.

<!-- Slide number: 23 -->
# (Four) Supplementation of nutrition and water, electrolyte imbalance
    ④ For abdominal surgery and gastrointestinal surgery, fasting for 24-48 hours after surgery, on the 3rd to 4th day after the recovery of intestinal function and anal exhaust, start with a small amount of liquid diet, gradually increase to a full liquid diet, and start with semi-liquid diet on the 5th to 6th day, usually change to soft food on the 7th to 9th day, and start with regular food on the 10th to 12th day.
    ⑤ For those who can eat, encourage high-protein, high-calorie, and high-vitamin diets.

<!-- Slide number: 24 -->
# (Four) Supplementation of nutrition and water, electrolyte imbalance
 2. Fluid replacement:
        For those who cannot eat, fluid and nutrients should be supplemented intravenously.

 3. Record fluid intake and output: Record 24-hour fluid intake and output and monitor electrolytes to assess the status of water, electrolyte, and nutritional metabolism. If there are abnormalities, supplementation is provided.

<!-- Slide number: 25 -->
# (Five) Enhancing patient comfort
1. Pain care
         After anesthesia wears off, the patient may have incision pain, which is most severe within 24 hours and gradually subsides 2-3 days later.
       Management: Find the cause (compression, urinary retention, infection), corresponding treatment, explanation, symptomatic treatment

2. Nausea and vomiting care
      Commonly caused by anesthesia, water and electrolyte metabolism disorders, diabetic acidosis, uremia, increased intracranial pressure, acute gastric dilation, or intestinal obstruction.
      Management: Treatment of the cause, prevention of aspiration, sedation, and antiemetic.

<!-- Slide number: 26 -->
# (Five) Enhancing patient comfort
3. Abdominal distension care
       Often caused by anesthesia and gastrointestinal dysfunction due to abdominal surgery.
     Management:
   ① Encourage early ambulation, promote the recovery of gastrointestinal function;
   ② Consider fasting, continuous gastrointestinal decompression, and rectal gas;
   ③ For non-gastrointestinal surgery, neostigmine can be used intramuscularly;
   ④ For hypokalemia or peritonitis, appropriate treatment is given.

<!-- Slide number: 27 -->
# (Five) Enhancing patient comfort
4. Urinary retention care
       Often caused by anesthesia, not being accustomed to urinating in bed, and incision pain.
   Management:
    ① Reassure and encourage the patient, relieve anxiety and tension
    ② Change position; induce urination
    ③ Lower abdominal heat application, massage, intramuscular injection of carbachol;
    ④ If ineffective, perform catheterization.

# （Six）Wound Care
1. Protect the wound:
   ① Pay attention to aseptic operation and timely change of wound dressings;
   ② Prevent the patient from pulling off the dressing;
   ③ Follow the doctor's orders to use antibiotics;
   ④ When there are signs of infection at the incision, local hot compresses, physiotherapy, and other measures should be taken to promote the absorption of inflammation.
2. Classification of surgical incisions
         (Clean I, Contaminated II, Polluted III)
3. Classification of incision healing
          (Class A, Class B, Class C healing)
4. Incision suture removal (time, method) see page 78

# （Seven）Drainage Tube Care
        ① Be familiar with the function and direction of the drainage tube, do not connect it incorrectly;
        ② Securely fix it to prevent it from falling off or slipping into the body cavity;
        ③ Observe and record the color, characteristics, and quantity of the drainage fluid;
        ④ Keep the drainage unobstructed, avoid compression or twisting, and use negative pressure aspiration if necessary;
       ⑤ Keep the drainage device sterile to prevent contamination, change the drainage bag daily according to aseptic technique at the skin outlet of the drainage tube;
       ⑥ Master the indications, time, and method of removal for various types of drainage tubes.

# （Eight）Guidance for Early Activity
1. Advantages:
            Increase lung capacity, reduce pulmonary complications, improve systemic blood circulation, promote gastrointestinal function recovery and incision healing, prevent pressure sores, and reduce the formation of lower limb venous thrombosis, etc.
2. Principles:
       Early activity, strive to get out of bed and move around in a short period of time.
3. Attention:
            However, patients with shock, heart failure, severe infection, bleeding, extreme weakness, or patients who have undergone joint surgery and need to limit activity should not emphasize early activity.

# （Nine）Postoperative Complications and Nursing
1. Postoperative hemorrhage
   Reasons:
     ① Incomplete hemostasis during surgery, incomplete control of bleeding from the wound surface; ② Postoperative suture line loosening; ③ Original spastic artery ends relax; ④ Coagulation mechanism disorders, etc.
   Manifestations: External hemorrhage and internal hemorrhage
   Management: Prevention, sedation, pressure bandaging, hemostatic drugs, fluid resuscitation, blood transfusion, and reoperation exploration.

2. Incision infection
   Reasons:
            ① Inadequate aseptic operation during surgery; ② Incomplete hemostasis during surgery, incorrect suture technique, residual blood clots, dead spaces, or foreign bodies in the incision; ③ Poor nutrition or complications of diabetes, obesity, etc., leading to poor incision healing and increasing the risk of incision infection.
   Manifestations: Continued fever and wound pain for 3 to 4 days after surgery.
   Management: Strict aseptic operation, avoid excessive tissue damage, thorough hemostasis, suture without dead spaces, use antibiotics, enhance resistance, suture removal and drainage, and local dressing changes.

3. Incision dehiscence
   Reasons: ① Malnutrition; ② Poor suture; ③ Incision infection; ④ Sudden increase in intra-abdominal pressure.
   Manifestations: Commonly seen in abdominal incisions, sudden pain and relaxation of the incision when the patient strains, or hearing the sound of suture line rupture. A large amount of pale red fluid may be discharged from the incision, and in cases of full-thickness dehiscence, the bowel or omentum may be exposed.
   Management: For mild cases, butterfly gauze can be applied; for visceral prolapse, it should be returned under sterile conditions in the operating room (prohibition of on-site reinsertion) and tension-reducing suture performed, antibiotics, and enhanced nutrition.

4. Atelectasis
   Reasons: Increased respiratory secretions blocking the bronchus, postoperative pain, tight bandaging of the chest and abdomen.
   Manifestations: Fever, increased respiratory rate, dull or solid percussion, localized moist rales on auscultation, decreased or absent breath sounds, PaO2↓, PaCO2↑. In cases of secondary infection, T is significantly↑, and WBC and N↑.
   Management: In addition to preventing vomiting and over-tight bandaging, sputum aspiration, and if necessary, tracheotomy, encourage the patient to take deep breaths and effective coughs, and use antibiotics.

5. Urinary tract infection
   Reasons:
      Often due to postoperative indwelling urinary catheters or long-term bed rest
   Manifestations: Chills and fever, renal pain, frequent urination, urgency, and urine examination showing red blood cells and pus cells.
   Management: Promptly handle urinary retention, maintain urine通畅, drink plenty of water, pay attention to aseptic technique when inserting a urinary catheter, and use antibiotics.

6. Thrombophlebitis
   Reasons: Long-term bed rest, hypercoagulability, repeated puncture and catheter placement in the vessels, or infusion of drugs with strong刺激性, causing vascular endothelial injury.
   Manifestations: Pain and tightness in the calf gastrocnemius muscle, followed by the appearance of lower limb pitting edema; the superficial veins of the lower limb become red, hard, and have marked tenderness. Often accompanied by fever.
   Management: Stop intravenous infusion in the affected limb, elevate the limb and immobilize it, apply a 50% magnesium sulfate compress, and strictly prohibit massage (to prevent thrombus detachment leading to pulmonary embolism), intravenous infusion of low molecular weight dextran, compound salvia miltiorrhiza liquid, defibrinogenase, etc.

# 【Evaluation】
 (One) Whether the patient's postoperative vital signs are stable.
（Two）Whether the fluid balance is maintained and there is no electrolyte disturbance.
（Three）Whether the patient has no postoperative discomfort.
（Four）Whether the patient's nutritional status has improved.
（Five）Whether the patient's activity tolerance has increased.
（Six）Whether the patient can recite relevant postoperative health knowledge and actively cooperate with treatment and care.
（Seven）Whether the patient's mood is stable and actively cooperates with treatment and care.
（Eight）No complications have occurred, or complications are detected and handled in a timely manner.

# 【Health Guidance】
         1. Guide the patient on what to pay attention to in diet and activity after discharge;
         2. Guide the patient to master the methods of rehabilitation exercise;
         3. Inform the patient of the follow-up visit time and what situations require immediate hospital re-examination.

# 【Summary】
           The success or failure of surgery depends on whether the preoperative preparation is sufficient. Therefore, medical staff should attach great importance to preoperative preparation, which includes the preparation of medical personnel, patients (psychological and physiological), and surgical equipment. Postoperative observation and management are important measures for preventing and treating various postoperative complications, consolidating the efficacy of surgery, and promoting the early recovery of patients. Medical staff should be proficient in postoperative condition observation, timely detect and correctly handle postoperative discomfort, and prevent complications.
