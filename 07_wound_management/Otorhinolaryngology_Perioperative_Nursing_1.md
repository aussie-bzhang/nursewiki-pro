Nursing Care Before Surgery
Laryngectomy:
1. Inform the patient that they will not be able to speak after laryngectomy and cannot eat with a tube placed in their mouth.
2. Instruct the patient on the care of their tracheostomy tube after surgery.
3. Explain to the patient that postoperative wound pain is a normal phenomenon and will gradually improve and recover over time.
4. Include the family in the learning process as much as possible so that the patient can receive continuous support in the future.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Nursing Care Before Surgery
Thyroid Surgery:
    Begin to exercise the neck muscles and ligaments 3 days before thyroid surgery to allow the patient to adapt to the surgical position. Training method: have the patient flex and extend their neck, rotate left and right several times, relax the neck muscles, place a pillow under the patient's shoulders, and according to the patient's condition, position the head and neck in an extended position, for 30 minutes each time, 4 times a day.

Nursing Care After Surgery
Diet Care:
    After the patient is fully awake from anesthesia, consider eating according to the condition and medical advice.
    For patients with nasal surgery, avoid hot, spicy, hard, and irritating foods.
    For patients with ear surgery, they can have semi-liquid or liquid diets 6 hours after general anesthesia, and change to regular food 3-5 days later, preferably high in protein, high in vitamins, and light.
    For patients with laryngeal surgery, they can have cold liquid foods such as cold milk, congee, soy milk, and juice after anesthesia.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Nursing Care After Surgery
    Wound Care: Do not remove the wound dressing yourself and keep the wound clean and dry.

Nursing Care After Surgery
Common Symptoms:
1. Fever: A normal postoperative fever of up to 38.5℃ may occur within the first 3 days, which will gradually return to normal after 3 days.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
Nursing Care After Surgery
2. Nausea and Vomiting: Nausea and vomiting in the early postoperative period are often due to anesthesia and will stop as the anesthetic effect wears off. Turn the head to the side when vomiting to prevent aspiration.
3. Dry Mouth: At this time, use a clean gauze to moisten the lips and tongue with a small amount of water to relieve discomfort, but do not drink water to avoid related complications.

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
Nursing Care After Surgery
4. Bleeding: Minor bleeding from the wound is a normal phenomenon after surgery.

    In cases where there is oozing from the surgical wound in the nose or throat, it is essential to spit out the blood to observe the amount of bleeding and prevent gastrointestinal discomfort.

Postoperative Patient Care
Tonsillectomy Postoperative Care:
1. Bed rest: Patients under local anesthesia should be in a semi-recumbent position, while those under general anesthesia can be in a lateral position. Routine application of antibiotics for one week is recommended after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 24 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
2. Instruct patients to spit out the saliva in their mouth and not to swallow it. If there is continuous bleeding from the mouth, the wound should be checked, and hemostatic measures should be considered. In children under general anesthesia who continuously exhibit swallowing movements, there is a possibility of swallowing blood, and immediate examination and timely hemostasis should be performed.
3. Oral hygiene: Start with mouthwashes using boric acid solution 6 hours after surgery or the next day.
4. Observation of temperature, bleeding, and white membrane: A white membrane appears on the wound on the second day after surgery, which is a normal reaction and has a protective effect on the wound.
5. Diet: Start with cold liquid diet 6 hours after surgery, and switch to semi-liquid diet on the second day if the white membrane grows well.
6. Wound pain: Painkillers can be administered in small doses.

<!-- Slide number: 25 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
    The tonsil is located in the throat, which is the necessary passage for eating. Therefore, dietary care after tonsil surgery is crucial.
    The dietary care after tonsil surgery can be divided into three phases.

<!-- Slide number: 27 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
    The first phase is the dietary care within 24 hours after surgery:
Patients can eat cold drinks such as ice milk or pure ice cream without impurities 6 hours after surgery, and slowly swallow them after holding them in the mouth to help with pain and止血.
Fruits and fruit juices, due to their high content of fruit acids, may stimulate the wound and cause pain and affect wound healing, so it is advisable to eat them in small amounts or not at all.

<!-- Slide number: 28 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
    The second phase is the dietary care from 24 hours to 1 week after surgery:
Starting from the second day after surgery, pay attention to "three more" - more talking, more rinsing, and more eating. The food should be semi-liquid, such as congee or noodles, and should not be hard or fried. Similarly, attention should be paid to the temperature of the food.
At this time, patients often refuse to eat due to pain at the surgical site, so patience and encouragement are needed to persuade patients to eat small and frequent meals. Only by actively eating can wound healing be promoted.

<!-- Slide number: 29 -->

Postoperative Patient Care
    The third phase is from 7 to 10 days after surgery:
The first week after tonsillectomy is the period of secondary hemorrhage. At this time, improper eating can cause severe hemorrhage, so eating should not be taken lightly.
Continue with semi-liquid diet for 3 to 4 days, and only after the white membrane of the surgical wound is completely detached and the incision is healed can normal eating be resumed.

Postoperative Patient Care
1. Early postoperative care:
Preventive use of antibiotics;
Appropriate use of analgesics until the nasal packing is removed;
Remove nasal packing and simultaneously aspirate and clear secretions from the nasal cavity and sinuses within 28 to 48 hours after surgery;
On the fourth day after surgery, clean blood clots, scabs, and pseudomembranes, and simultaneously aspirate the secretions from the surgical cavity.

<!-- Slide number: 32 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
1. Wound care:
Handling of oozing and exudate:
If oozing or exudate from the wound flows into the mouth, do not swallow it. Tilt the head to one side and gently spit it out to avoid causing gastrointestinal discomfort.
Packings:
Do not pull on the packing in the nasal cavity. If it falls out, inform the doctor for handling and do not insert it back yourself. Avoid forceful blowing of the nose to prevent sneezing and coughing.

<!-- Slide number: 33 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
2. Situations that require immediate notification to the doctor:
Nasal packing falls out;
Nasal bleeding is bright red, dripping, and in large quantities, with blood vomiting from the mouth or a large amount of blood clots;
After the packing is removed, if there is a cool liquid discharge from the nose, which does not form a crust when dried and is severe when the head is tilted;
Swelling of the conjunctiva, ecchymosis around the orbit, blurred vision, and even transient blindness.

<!-- Slide number: 34 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
Due to the nasal packing, the patient's pain threshold is reduced and they may become irritable. If necessary, adjunctive sedative analgesics can be administered to ensure the patient's sleep, and encourage family members to communicate more with the patient, giving positive suggestions to distract the patient's attention from pain and discomfort, thereby reducing the severity of pain.

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
2. General care:
After general anesthesia, the patient should be placed in a supine position with the head turned to one side, and respiratory secretions should be cleared in a timely manner to maintain a clear airway. For patients who have recovered from general anesthesia and those under local anesthesia with normal vital signs, a semi-recumbent position should be maintained to facilitate breathing, drainage, and reduce congestion and swelling in the head and face.

<!-- Slide number: 36 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
Instruct patients not to squeeze the nasal cavity due to discomfort in the nasal and facial area. Cold compresses can be applied to the nasal and forehead area within 24 to 48 hours after surgery to reduce pain and swelling. Due to nasal packing, patients may need to breathe through the mouth, and a wet gauze can be placed over the mouth or patients can be guided to drink small amounts of water frequently to moisten the throat. Simple, nutritious, and easily digestible semi-liquid diets should be consumed, and the time for eating should be shortened, with attention paid to maintaining oral hygiene.

<!-- Slide number: 37 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
3. Close observation of vital signs: Closely observe the patient's consciousness, facial color, urine output, temperature, pulse, respiration, and blood pressure for changes. The dynamic changes in blood pressure and pulse are of great significance for observing nasal bleeding.

<!-- Slide number: 38 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
4. Observation and care for nasal bleeding:
For patients under general anesthesia, give a warm and cool semi-liquid diet 6 hours after awakening and for patients under local anesthesia after eating. Instruct patients to spit out the secretions in their mouth and not to swallow them, so as to observe the amount of bleeding and prevent mis-swallowing that may cause gastrointestinal discomfort. Due to accidental swallowing during surgery, some patients under general anesthesia may vomit a small amount of dark red old blood. A small amount of bleeding or blood-stained secretions may occur after surgery, which is a normal phenomenon and does not require alarm. If the patient repeatedly vomits blood or blood clots from the mouth, or if there is a continuous fresh blood drop from the anterior nares, an immediate report to the doctor should be made.

Tracheostomy Postoperative Care:
1. Maintaining the patency of the tracheostomy tube:
Keep the room clean and the air fresh;
Correct sputum aspiration to prevent infection;
Change the dressing on the surgical wound;

Postoperative Patient Care

Check daily to ensure the tube is securely fastened;
Maintain the patency of the inner tube (metallic tube).

2. Cleaning and disinfection of the tracheal tube:
Remove the inner tube;

Postoperative Patient Care
Post-thyroidectomy care:
1. Difficulty breathing and asphyxia.

<!-- Slide number: 47 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
2. Pay special attention to postoperative monitoring:
Before the patient regains consciousness from general anesthesia, observe the pupils, limb movement, coughing, and swallowing reflexes, frequently call the patient to determine their level of consciousness;
Closely monitor the condition, especially the color of the local skin after swelling, and determine whether there is bleeding;
Observe the color recovery of the lips and nail beds after pressing to judge the presence of hypoxia, with blood oxygen saturation levels reaching above 95%, and arterial blood gas analysis should be performed if necessary.

<!-- Slide number: 48 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
3. Take various preventive measures based on the cause and location of respiratory obstruction:
Remove sputum promptly and thoroughly to maintain airway patency;
Provide continuous oxygen at 2-4L/min to increase blood oxygen saturation;
Inhale nebulizer with 100ml normal saline, 5mg α-chymotrypsin, 5mg dexamethasone, and 80,000U gentamicin, 1 time/4h, to dilute sputum, humidify the airway, and prevent the drying and sticking of sputum in the nose and trachea, thus obstructing the airway;

<!-- Slide number: 49 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
Administer routine intravenous infusion of corticosteroid drugs and hemostatic agents for 3 days as prescribed by the physician to prevent laryngeal edema and wound bleeding;
In cases where the patient has widespread swelling in the neck and an increase in respiratory secretions after surgery, perform a tracheotomy promptly to ensure airway patency.

<!-- Slide number: 50 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
4. Hemorrhage
Hemorrhage often occurs within 24 hours postoperatively and is usually due to incomplete hemostasis during surgery. Monitor the drainage, respiratory status, and color of the skin on the neck and upper chest to determine the presence of subcutaneous hematoma. Instruct the patient to minimize neck movement and use a "V" hand gesture to protect the neck during coughing to prevent bleeding.

<!-- Slide number: 51 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
5. Injury to the recurrent laryngeal nerve
Due to general anesthesia, the patient's voice and swallowing cannot be tested during surgery. After the patient regains consciousness, they should provide a brief response to assess their voice correctly. During eating, especially when drinking water, observe for any occurrence of dysphagia or coughing to timely detect the presence of recurrent laryngeal nerve or superior laryngeal nerve injury.

<!-- Slide number: 52 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
Dietary guidance for laryngectomy patients:
Assess the presence of vomiting or cough reflex and swallowing ability.
Instruct the patient that coughing during oral feeding after surgery is common, and only through intensive training can they eat smoothly.

<!-- Slide number: 53 -->
Postoperative Patient Care
Dietary guidance for laryngectomy patients:
Guide the patient on the correct method of eating: start with solid soft foods, and only after eating solid foods without coughing should they practice with liquid foods.
When eating, tilt the head forward by 45 degrees, use the index finger to block the tube opening, breathe through the nose, and coordinate breathing and swallowing.
Eat slowly during meals.
