# Case Management of a Patient with Breast Cancer Postoperative Flap Necrosis
xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# Introduction
Breast cancer is the most common malignant tumor among women worldwide, and surgical resection is the most important method in the comprehensive treatment of breast cancer.
Electrocautery-induced burns of free flaps during surgery have become one of the common complications of this type [1].
Although flap necrosis does not directly threaten the patient's life, it increases hospital stay, affects the patient's postoperative chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and quality of life, and exacerbates the patient's psychological stress [2].

# Case Introduction
Patient Guo, female, 50 years old, height 158 cm, weight 62 kg.
Presented with "right breast mass for one week" and was diagnosed at a hospital in Jiangsu Province in August 2015.
Underwent "modified radical mastectomy for right breast cancer" under general anesthesia on August 21, 2015, and received chemotherapy for 2 cycles on September 11 and October 9.
The breast wound was treated with a common method at an external hospital (disinfected with Anruiodine and wrapped with gauze and cotton pad), but the effect was not satisfactory.
Visited the ostomy clinic at the Provincial Cancer Hospital on October 19, 2015.

# Comprehensive Assessment (Factors Affecting Healing)
Chemotherapy: Chemotherapy drugs are cytotoxic agents that can inhibit the division and proliferation of cells in the bone marrow, reduce the number of inflammatory cells and platelets, and insufficient related growth factors, thereby severely affecting wound healing [3].
Low white blood cell count: 2.75*10^10/L, impairs the function of macrophages, cannot guide normal inflammatory reactions, and is prone to infection [3].
Mild anemia: Hemoglobin 108g/L, red blood cells 3.5*10^12/L.
Social support: The patient is married, has one son and one daughter, the family economy is slightly poor, the husband cares and supports the patient, and participates in the New Rural Cooperative Medical Insurance.
No special medical history.

[3] Hu Ailing, Zheng Meichun, Li Weijuan. Modern Clinical Nursing Practice of Wounds and Intestinal Stomas [M]. Beijing: Peking Union Medical College Press, 2010, 10: 15-16

# Wound Management (October 19, First Dressing)
1. Wash the wound with normal saline, remove black eschar and necrotic tissue
2. Use hydrophilic silver fiber, cover with gauze and absorbent cotton pad
3. Elastic chest band compression bandaging

# Nursing Measures
Elevated White Blood Cells: Consulted with the attending physician and administered 300ug of Granocyte to the patient.
Subcutaneous injection, white blood cell count returned to normal after injection of the white blood cell-elevating medication.
Nutritional Support: Encourage the patient to consume a diet rich in nutrition.
Psychological Care: Establish a mutual trust between the patient and the nursing staff, listen patiently, encourage the patient to express their feelings and concerns, and provide psychological counseling before and after dressing changes.
Health Education: Guide the patient on the correct functional exercise methods, inform them of the method of applying compression bandaging with a chest belt, and maintain the cleanliness of the skin around the wound.

# Discussion
Traditional dressing change: Dry healing, requires frequent dressing changes, the wound is prone to adhere to and eschar, easy to damage and destroy the wound and new granulation tissue, leading to slow wound healing speed and increased patient discomfort [6].
Moist healing: In a moist environment, the wound can heal quickly and can prevent scar and eschar formation [7].

Experience

# Discussion
Debridement: Conservative sharp debridement + hydrogel autolytic debridement, accelerates debridement speed, the patient has no obvious pain during the procedure.
Hydrophilic fiber silver: Can absorb a large amount of exudate vertically, protect the surrounding skin, and at the same time has a broad-spectrum antibacterial effect.
Silver ion foam dressing: When the granulation tissue is completely red and fills the wound bed, it is selected to create a micro-humid, low-oxygen environment that is both antibacterial and promotes epithelialization, and prevents granulation tissue from being higher than the wound surface [3].

# Discussion
Thick cotton pad under the armpit + elastic chest band compression bandaging
The appropriate physical pressure provided by the elastic bandage keeps the flap in a relatively fixed state with the chest, which can eliminate the potential dead space.
It is conducive to the growth of capillaries, promoting the growth of granulation tissue.
The elastic bandage has strong elasticity, can play an even pressure role, and make the flap and chest wall fit more closely, accelerate wound healing [9].

[9] Feng Huan, Zheng Xinxin, Ma Xiuying, et al. The application of elastic bandage combined with silver-containing dressing in accelerating the healing of incisional healing after radical mastectomy for breast cancer. West China Medical Journal, 2014, 29(9): 1633-1634

# Summary

According to the dynamic changes of the wound, choose the dressing, in addition to meeting the needs of the wound, consider the economy and practicality, the method is simple.
Collecting a complete case requires patience and care.
Communication is very important, explain the knowledge of rehabilitation and the reasons for choosing the dressing to the patient's family, and it is very important to obtain their understanding and cooperation.
