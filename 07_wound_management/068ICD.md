Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD)
An ICD is a multi-functional electronic device with multiple programmable parameters designed to terminate life-threatening ventricular tachycardia. It senses ventricular tachycardia or ventricular fibrillation through endocardial electrodes and then delivers anti-tachycardia pacing (ATP) or a defibrillation energy of 20～30 J to terminate rapid ventricular tachycardia.

# Functions of ICD

1
Perception (perception of local atrial and ventricular electrocardiographic signals)
Detection (classification of perceived signals according to programmed heart rate intervals)
2
Termination of ventricular tachycardia (VT) or ventricular fibrillation (VF)
Pacing treatment for bradycardia (or) cardiac resynchronization therapy

### Notes:
Cardiac arrest due to ventricular fibrillation or ventricular tachycardia caused by non-acute or reversible causes; spontaneous sustained ventricular tachycardia associated with organic heart disease; syncope of unknown etiology, which can induce sustained ventricular tachycardia or ventricular fibrillation with significant hemodynamic clinical manifestations during electrophysiological examination, and is ineffective with drug treatment; non-sustained ventricular tachycardia associated with coronary artery disease, old myocardial infarction, and left ventricular dysfunction; spontaneous sustained ventricular tachycardia without organic heart disease, which is ineffective with other treatments.

Summary and Reflection

ICD implantation has improved the survival rate of patients with malignant arrhythmias, thereby improving the quality of life of patients. However, severe complications may occur postoperatively, so it is very important to do well in perioperative nursing and postoperative health education.

2. For this patient, although hospitalization improved the patient's symptoms, it is more important to focus on continued care after discharge, whether it is ICD or chronic heart failure. # Local Postoperative Nursing

1. Wear loose, soft clothing to avoid pressing or impacting the pacemaker pocket; do not rub the skin at the buried ICD and catheter site vigorously during bathing.

2. Avoid excessive extension and abduction of the surgical limb for the first 3 months postoperatively, as the electrode needs 2-3 months to become firmly attached to the myocardium; try not to use the left arm to hold the handle when taking a bus; pay attention to the wound condition.

### Notes:
Avoid excessive extension and abduction of the surgical limb within the first 3 months postoperatively, as the secure attachment of the electrode to the myocardium requires 2-3 months; try not to use the right arm to hold the handle when riding in a car; pay attention to the condition of the wound.

2.3.4 Regular Postoperative Follow-up
Schedule regular follow-up appointments at 1, 3, 6 months, and 1 year postoperatively, monitoring heart rate and rhythm; check immediately if there is any discomfort; perform pacemaker programming to understand the functioning of the ICD; whether any related complications have occurred; provide explanations regarding the patient's psychological response to ICD treatment and quality of life.
