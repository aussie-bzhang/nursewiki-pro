# Lymphedema

      Lymphedema is caused by the destruction of lymphatic vessels and the removal of lymph nodes during surgery, which obstructs the return of lymph fluid, leading to the accumulation of protein-rich fluid in the subcutaneous tissue spaces, resulting in swelling of the limbs or tissues.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Case Introduction

        Patient: Female, 79 years old. Attended the outpatient clinic for "right breast cancer modified radical resection with concurrent upper limb lymphedema for 10 years."
Diagnosis: Lymphedema Stage II

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

# Manual Lymphatic Drainage

       Manual Lymphatic Drainage (MLD) is a technique that uses manual massage to promote lymphatic circulation, draining lymph fluid to the lymph nodes, and accelerating the excretion of toxins.

# Nursing Experience
Breast cancer is one of the common malignant tumors in women, and radical mastectomy is still the main treatment method. The occurrence of lymphedema in the limbs after surgery is 30%. Currently, there is no unified standard for the treatment of lymphedema. The manual lymphatic drainage mentioned in this case study also has several schools of thought. Reducing the occurrence and progression of upper limb lymphedema, alleviating the pain of patients, and improving the quality of life are all topics that require further research.
