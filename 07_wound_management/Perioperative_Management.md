# Concept of Perioperative Period (Perioperative Period)

   Refers to the entire process from the time of clear diagnosis and determination of surgical treatment, to the execution of surgery, and until postoperative recovery.

   1981, 26th Edition of Doland's Medical Dictionary: "The time limit from the need for surgery and hospitalization to discharge."

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Perioperative Management is Related to Surgical Classification
   - Surgical Classification:
     ① Emergency Surgery: The condition is extremely urgent and requires immediate surgery. For example, traumatic intestinal rupture, respiratory tract obstruction, and rupture of major blood vessels in the thoracic and abdominal cavities.
     ② Time-Limited Surgery: For example, radical surgery for various malignant tumors, and surgery for gastric duodenal ulcer complicated by pyloric stenosis. Preoperative preparation should be completed as soon as possible.
     ③ Elective Surgery: For example, subtotal gastrectomy for gastric duodenal ulcer and inguinal hernia repair. Surgery can be performed after sufficient preoperative preparation.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->

Patient's tolerance to surgery

Good Tolerance:
The patient's overall condition is good, with no organic lesions in the important organs, or their functions are in a compensatory state.
General preparation can be carried out before surgery.

Poor Tolerance:
The patient's overall condition is poor, or there are organic lesions in the important organs, and their functions are near or have already reached a state of decompensation.
Active and detailed special preparation is required before surgery can be performed.

# (I) Special Preparation
1. Malnutrition and Decreased Immune Function

Malnutrition can significantly increase the mortality and complication rates after surgery. Hypoproteinemia may occur when albumin is <30g/L or serum transferrin is <1.5mg/L.
Some drugs such as corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, cytotoxic drugs, and long-term use of antibiotics reduce the patient's resistance.
Immunodeficiency diseases such as lymphoma and leukemia.

<!-- Slide number: 23 -->
# 2. Hypertension
     Blood pressure should be controlled below 160/100 mmHg (21.3/13.3 kPa) to avoid risks of complications such as cerebral vascular accidents and congestive heart failure during anesthesia and surgical stress.

<!-- Slide number: 24 -->
# 3. Cardiovascular Disease
  The mortality rate for patients with cardiovascular disease undergoing surgery is 2 to 3 times higher than that of those without cardiovascular disease.

  Patients with acute myocardial infarction should not undergo elective surgery within 6 months after onset;

  For patients with heart failure, it is best to perform surgery 3 to 4 weeks after the heart failure is controlled.

  Patients with long-term low-salt diet and diuretic use, and those with electrolyte imbalance, need to be corrected before surgery.

# Indications for Prophylactic Antibiotic Use
   (1) Class II clean to contaminated incisions and some Class III (contaminated) incisions, mainly surgeries entering the gastrointestinal tract (starting from the oropharynx), respiratory tract, and female reproductive tract; (2) surgeries using artificial materials or devices, such as heart valve replacement, artificial vascular grafting, artificial joint replacement, and abdominal wall incision hernia repair with large artificial materials; (3) clean major surgeries, long surgery duration, significant trauma, or involving important organs where infection has serious consequences, such as craniotomy, heart and great vessel surgery, portosystemic shunt or occlusion surgery, splenectomy, intraocular surgery, etc.; (4) patients with high-risk factors for infection such as advanced age (>70 years), diabetes, low immune function (especially in organ transplant recipients), malnutrition, etc.

Refers to a series of evidence-based perioperative management optimization measures to reduce the physiological and psychological trauma stress of surgical patients, aiming for rapid recovery. # Position and Ambulation
Patients under general anesthesia who have not yet regained consciousness should be placed in a flat lying position with the head turned to one side.
After regaining consciousness from general anesthesia, different surgeries and different patients should take different positions.
Patients should, in principle, start early bed activities.
Advantages: increase lung ventilation volume, reduce pulmonary complications;
           promote blood circulation, prevent venous thrombosis formation;
          promote intestinal peristalsis and bladder contraction function recovery, reduce bloating and urinary retention.

# Incision Healing Record
Surgical incisions are classified into three categories: 
- Category I: Clean incisions. 
- Category II: Potentially contaminated incisions. 
- Category III: Contaminated incisions.

Wound healing is categorized into three grades: 
- Grade A healing: Excellent healing with no adverse reactions. 
- Grade B healing: Inflammation present, such as redness, swelling, hard nodules, hematoma, effusion, etc., but no suppuration. 
- Grade C healing: Incision suppuration with incision drainage required.

Recording method:

# Incision Classification
 ① Clean incision (Category I incision), refers to aseptic incisions that are sutured, such as subtotal thyroidectomy.
 ② Potentially contaminated incision (Category II incision), refers to sutured incisions that may be contaminated during surgery, such as subtotal gastrectomy.
 ③ Contaminated incision (Category III incision), refers to incisions adjacent to an infected area or tissue directly exposed to infectious material, such as appendectomy for appendiceal perforation, surgery for intestinal obstruction and necrosis.

# Incision Healing Grades
 ① Grade A healing, represented by the character "甲", indicates excellent healing with no adverse reactions.
 ② Grade B healing, represented by the character "乙", indicates inflammation at the healing site, such as redness, swelling, hard nodules, hematoma, effusion, etc., but no suppuration.
 ③ Grade C healing, represented by the character "丙", indicates incision suppuration requiring incision and drainage.

# Recording Incision Healing Status
Using the above classification and grading methods, observe the incision healing status and make a record.
For example, if the healing after subtotal thyroidectomy is excellent, it is recorded as "Ⅰ/甲"; if there is a hematoma after subtotal gastrectomy, it is recorded as "Ⅱ/乙", and so on.

# Postoperative Hemorrhage
Causes of postoperative hemorrhage: Incomplete hemostasis, oozing, spasm of small artery segments.
Hemorrhage sites: Incision, hollow viscus, body cavity.
Observation and diagnosis of postoperative hemorrhage: Drainage tube blood >100ml/h,   dressing soaked through; hemorrhage manifestations
          Treatment: Reoperation for hemostasis
Prevention of postoperative hemorrhage is crucial, with active management and thorough hemostasis.

# Postoperative Fever and Hypothermia
1. Fever
   (1) Non-infectious fever - Symptomatic treatment, close observation, minimize triggering factors.
   (2) Infectious fever - Strict aseptic technique, meticulous hemostasis, no dead space, correct application of antibiotics, active prevention and treatment of pulmonary and urological complications, etc.
2. Hypothermia: Intraoperative temperature monitoring, use of warming devices, postoperative attention to warmth.

# Incision Infection
Causes of incision infection: Bacterial invasion, hematoma, foreign body, local tissue blood supply, body resistance.
Clinical manifestations: Time, report incision pain, fever, local wound manifestations.
Prevention focus: Aseptic concept, meticulous surgical technique, incision hemostasis, enhance patient resistance, closely observe wounds.
Pus formation should be drained promptly.

# Incision Dehiscence
Causes: Malnutrition, suture defects, increased abdominal pressure.
Manifestations and diagnosis: Sudden pain and loosening of the incision when abdominal pressure is increased, protrusion of intestines or omentum, large amounts of pale red fluid exuding.
Prevention and treatment: Tension-free suture, good anesthesia, manage abdominal distension, lie flat during coughing, apply pressure bandage; surgical suture and tension-free suture, gastrointestinal decompression.

# Urological Infection
Causes: Urinary retention, retrograde infection.
Manifestations and diagnosis: Frequency, urgency, dysuria, difficulty in urination, presence of red blood cells, pus cells, bacteria in urine, renal pain, urine culture.
Prevention and treatment: Prevent or promptly handle urinary retention; apply antibiotics, maintain adequate urine volume, keep urination unobstructed.
