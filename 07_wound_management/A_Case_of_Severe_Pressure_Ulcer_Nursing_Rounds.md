# 2016 NPUAP Update: Terminology and Staging System for Pressure Ulcers
The term "pressure ulcer" has been changed from "pressure sore" in the staging of pressure injuries (pressure ulcers) proposed by the American Pressure Ulcer Advisory Panel at the expert meeting held in Chicago on April 8-9, 2016. Regardless of whether the skin injury is intact or ulcerated, the term "pressure injury" more accurately describes the injury.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Background and Significance of the Update
In the previous staging system, stage Ⅰ pressure ulcers and deep tissue injuries were considered local tissue injuries, but with intact epidermis. The other stages were open ulcers. If each stage of pressure ulcer injury is defined as a pressure ulcer, it would lead to confusion in concepts.
In addition to the change in the term "pressure injury," the Arabic numerals (stage 1, 2, 3) used in the pressure ulcer staging system have replaced the Roman numerals (Ⅰ,Ⅱ,Ⅲ). The word "suspected" in the previous staging system's "suspected deep tissue injury" has been removed. At the meeting, it was agreed to include medical device-related pressure injuries and mucosal membrane pressure injuries in the category of pressure injuries.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Latest Definition of "Pressure Injury" (Pressure Ulcer)
Pressure injury: It is a localized injury to the skin and/or underlying subcutaneous soft tissue, usually occurring at bony prominences or related to medical or other medical equipment.
This pressure injury can manifest as local tissue damage with intact epidermis or open ulcer and may be accompanied by pain. Severe and/or prolonged pressure or pressure combined with shear force can lead to the occurrence of pressure injury. The tolerance of subcutaneous soft tissue to pressure and shear force is influenced by the environment, nutrition, perfusion, comorbidities, and the condition of the soft tissue.

Half a year ago, he discovered skin erosion at the sacral tail and changed the dressing at home, with pus and an unpleasant odor, and loss of appetite, admitted to the hospital.
Past medical history: No history of hypertension, diabetes, or heart disease; no infectious disease history; no family history of hereditary diseases or allergies.

# Negative Pressure Wound Therapy for Yang Dongqiong
Purpose
   Remove excessive wound exudate
   Promote angiogenesis and granulation tissue growth
   Provide a protective environment, reduce bacterial count in the wound, and decrease the risk of wound infection
Suitable for complex wounds or chronic wounds, does not require daily dressing changes, and reduces treatment costs and hospitalization days.
