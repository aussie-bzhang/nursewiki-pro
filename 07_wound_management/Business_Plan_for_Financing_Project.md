Anatomy of the Rectum:

The rectum is the terminal segment of the large intestine, approximately 15-16 cm in length, located within the pelvis. It continues from the sigmoid colon at the level of the third sacral vertebra, descending in front of the sacrum and coccyx,穿过盆膈，and terminates at the anus.
Concept of Colorectal Cancer:
Colorectal cancer refers to malignant tumors occurring above the anal verge to the rectum, up to the junction with the sigmoid colon.
Etiology of Colorectal Cancer:

The etiology of colorectal cancer is not fully understood at present, and its occurrence is related to social environment, dietary habits, and genetic factors.

Shape of the Rectum

The upper part of the rectum above the pelvic diaphragm is called the pelvic part of the rectum, and the lower segment of the pelvic cavity dilates to form the rectal ampulla. The part below the pelvic diaphragm narrows to form the anal canal or rectal anal part. The rectum has two bends: the upper segment curves backward, consistent with the curvature in front of the sacrum, forming the sacral bend, and the lower segment curves backward and downward around the tip of the coccyx, forming an anteriorly convex perineal bend.

Clinical Manifestations of Colorectal Cancer

Most early colorectal cancers are asymptomatic.
Growth of colorectal cancer: When it reaches a certain degree, it can cause changes in defecation habits, hematochezia, mucoid stools, tenesmus, constipation, diarrhea, etc.
Stool: Gradually becomes thinner, and in the late stage, there may be bowel obstruction, weight loss, and even cachexia.
Tumor: When it invades surrounding organs such as the bladder, urethra, and vagina, it can cause urinary tract irritation symptoms, vaginal discharge of feces, sacral and perineal pain, lower limb edema, etc.
Patients may experience varying degrees of fatigue and weight loss.

Surgical Treatment

There are two types: radical and palliative.
Radical surgery: 1. Abdominoperineal resection (Miles surgery)
Low anterior resection and extraperitoneal anastomosis in one stage
Rectal resection preserving the anal sphincter.
Palliative surgery: When the local infiltration of the tumor is severe or the metastasis is extensive and cannot be cured, palliative resection can be performed to relieve obstruction and reduce the patient's pain.

# Surgical Coordination for Circulating Nurses
Preoperative Preparation
Preoperative Visit:
One day before the surgery, the circulating nurse visits the patient in the ward to understand the patient's condition, communicate with the patient, first introduce themselves, explain the general process from entering the operating room to leaving the operating room to the patient, understand the patient's general condition and blood perfusion, inquire about allergies, check the surgical site and skin preparation, and introduce the operating room conditions and anesthesia methods, surgical position, and general process of the surgery, and inform the patient of the preparation before entering the operating room (including changing clothes, removing jewelry and dentures, not wearing makeup, and entrusting valuable items to family members for safekeeping). Introduce the purpose, process, necessity, and advantages and disadvantages of laparoscopic surgery to alleviate the patient's and family members' concerns and fear of surgery. And inform the patient of preoperative fasting and drinking restrictions.

Room Preparation:
The laminar airflow switch is turned on 60 minutes before the operation, and the room temperature is adjusted to 22℃-25℃, humidity 40%-60%.

Instrument Preparation:
One set of camera system, pneumoperitoneum machine, cold light source, ultrasonic knife, electrosurgical knife, carbon dioxide, aspirator, positioner, shoulder pad, etc.

High-consumption preparation:
Disposable anastomosis stapler, laparoscopic linear cutting and closing stapler, 60# staple cartridge, purse-string suture, 2-0 and 4-0 vicryl suture, long gauze, mucosal protector, Cefuroxime, Surfactant, Bioclip, etc.

Intraoperative Preparation

After the patient enters the room, the circulating nurse communicates with the patient actively, strictly implements the three checks and seven matches, and reconfirms the surgical site with the anesthesiologist. Establish a 20# indwelling needle venous access in the patient's left hand and a deep venous puncture channel by the anesthesiologist. Half an hour before the operation, follow the doctor's order to give antibiotics and assist the anesthesiologist in performing endotracheal intubation for general anesthesia. The patient takes a lithotomy position with the head lower and feet higher, the leg stand height not exceeding 30 cm, a crystal pad is placed at the popliteal fossa, and the knees are fixed on the leg stand with restraints. The angle between the patient's legs is 100 degrees to 110 degrees to ensure full exposure of the surgical field during the operation. The negative pole plate of the electrosurgical knife is placed on the muscle-rich part of the thigh.
Install all instruments, place the monitor on the lower left side of the patient, connect all instruments to the operating table, adjust the pneumoperitoneum machine, electrosurgical knife, and ultrasonic knife to the required size, adjust the cold light source to maintain appropriate brightness.
Pay attention to keep the catheter unobstructed and the connection tube sterile, and observe the urine volume and color at regular intervals during the operation.
Prepare 70℃-80℃ sterile normal saline to preheat the lens during the operation, to prevent the formation of condensation on the cold lens surface due to abdominal heat, causing blurred vision and affecting the operation. After all preparations are complete, the operation can begin.
Closely observe the patient's condition changes and the progress of the operation during the operation. Pay attention to check the performance of each instrument, and handle emergencies in a timely manner if any occur.

Routine preparation: The scrub nurse washes hands and goes on stage 30 minutes before the operation, checks the integrity of the instruments, and together with the circulating nurse, counts the number of instruments. Routine abdominal perineal skin preparation, and properly fix various catheters, lenses, and ultrasonic knives on the operating table, hand them to the circulating nurse to connect to the instruments, and check and adjust their clarity.

Surgical Procedure

Establish pneumoperitoneum: The scrub nurse hands over a No. 11 scalpel, making a small incision of about 1 cm above the patient's umbilicus. Hand over a 10mm trocar and insert it vertically into the peritoneal cavity in a rotating manner. Immediately connect the CO2 input tube, wait until the injected gas reaches 12-15 mmHg, and withdraw the trocar to insert the laparoscope. Under the direct vision of the laparoscope, use the scalpel to puncture and insert a 5mm trocar slightly below the midline on the left and right sides, and insert a 12mm trocar slightly to the right above the pubic symphysis for operation.

After routine exploration of the abdominal cavity under laparoscopy, hand over a 10cm bandage to the surgeon to ligate the mesentery of the sigmoid colon above the tumor and lift it for traction. Hand over an ultrasonic knife to separate the peritoneum, free the rectum, and the sigmoid colon, then free the inferior mesenteric vessels and clip them off at the root with titanium clips.

Separate the mesentery of the sigmoid colon and the posterior rectal space, and then perform the anterior rectal dissection. The scrub nurse must properly install the titanium clips to prevent bleeding when separating larger vessels. After freeing the intestinal segment, hand over a linear cutting and closing device to cut the rectum 5 cm below the tumor.

The scrub nurse must check the closure of the disposable cutting and closing device to ensure proper use. At this point, hand over a clamp to clamp the rectal stump to pause pneumoperitoneum, and hand over a scalpel to enlarge the right lower abdominal puncture site to 3-5 cm according to the size of the tumor. Place the distal end of the rectum and the tumor into a specimen bag and drag it out of the abdominal cavity. Outside the body, cut the descending colon or sigmoid colon within a certain range, and resect the diseased intestinal tract.

Use a purse-string clamp and suture to make a purse-string at the colon end, place the钉座 of a circular anastomosis device into the intestinal lumen, tighten the purse-string and tie it, and then insert it into the abdominal cavity. Suture the abdominal wall incision with silk thread, place the trocar, re-establish pneumoperitoneum, and insert the main body of the anastomosis device through the anus. Align the anastomosis device with the钉座, fire, and complete the end-to-end anastomosis of the colon and rectum.

Inject 200 mL of normal saline into the pelvic cavity to submerge the anastomosis, observe for any liquid leakage from the anal opening. After completing the anastomosis, flush the pelvic cavity with 1500-2000 mL of distilled water, remove the fluid, hand over the abdominal drain tube from the right trocar, and remove the trocar. Close the small incision.

Conduct preoperative visits carefully, as it is necessary to explain the advantages of the surgery to the patient in detail the day before the operation to alleviate their anxiety and fear.

Since the intraperitoneal inflation and head-down position can affect the patient's circulatory and respiratory function to varying degrees, it is necessary to closely monitor the patient's vital signs during the operation.

3 Pay attention to the patient's surgical position, avoid excessive abduction of the upper limbs, and avoid compressing the common peroneal nerve of the lower limbs to prevent paralysis.

Extended use of CO2 pneumoperitoneum during surgery can cause the patient's body temperature to drop, so attention should be paid to keeping the patient warm.
