# Introduction
Clinical pathways are treatment plans that are strictly ordered and have accurate time requirements for the monitoring, treatment, rehabilitation, and nursing of a specific disease. They can accelerate patient recovery, reduce resource waste, and ensure that service recipients receive the best medical and nursing care.

The "Knowledge, Attitude, Practice" (KAP) model is one of the models for changing human health-related behaviors, which divides the change in human behavior into three consecutive processes: acquiring knowledge, forming beliefs, and forming behavior [2]. In order to improve patients' awareness of stoma care and improve their quality of life, clinical pathway-based health education is adopted to implement nursing based on the KAP model.

Nursing Experience

　　After the patient undergoes intestinal stoma surgery, the change in defecation method imposes a great psychological burden on the patient. During the later process of ostomy bag replacement, both the patient and the family encountered significant difficulties in operation. Therefore, in the clinical management of such stoma patients, while alleviating the psychological burden of the patient, it is also important to focus on cultivating the patient's self-care ability to facilitate the patient's early return to normal life.
　　Through the application of pathway education, early complications such as stoma edema were observed and intervened in a timely manner, and it was realized that professional knowledge and attitude can gain the patient's trust, produce the belief to overcome the disease, and thus achieve behavior change. However, due to the first-time participation in stoma management, the application of clinical pathway health education did not fully grasp the progress of postoperative patient's physical recovery, and in the future learning and work, I will continue to explore the application impact of pathway health education on stoma patients, summarize the deficiencies, and better apply it to clinical nursing work.
