# Introduction
Extravasation: Refers to the infiltration of chemotherapy drugs into subcutaneous tissue, causing pain, necrosis, and/or tissue separation and decay.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Causes
Physiological factors: Elderly arteriosclerosis, venous return obstruction
Pharmacological factors: Extravasation volume, contact time
Injection site: Elbow pit extravasation is difficult to detect
Iatrogenic factors: Lack of experience

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Knowledge Link
From the perspective of damage to the body, chemotherapy drugs can be classified as follows:
Bulking agents: Can cause tissue necrosis or (and) vesicle formation
Irritants: Can cause pain in veins and along the venous route, with or without inflammatory reaction
Non-bulking agents: Non-irritant chemotherapy drugs

# Medical History
On September 20, the patient experienced pain in the left back after receiving R-CH0P chemotherapy drugs.

After the symptoms were relieved with 50% magnesium sulfate wet compress, the patient was discharged home for self-application.

On September 30, the skin tissue broke down, and the condition worsened after visiting a local hospital.

On October 20, the wound clinic was visited.

# Summary
Provide chemotherapy drug knowledge to patients and their families before chemotherapy
Correctly use dressings to alleviate the patient's pain in the shortest and most economical way
Take positive and effective preventive measures once chemotherapy drug extravasation occurs
Understand the contraindications and injection sites of chemotherapy drugs to alleviate the patient's pain
