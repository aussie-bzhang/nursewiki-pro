The appendix is located in the right iliac fossa, with a serpentine shape, measuring approximately 5~10cm in length and 0.5~0.7cm in diameter. It is a tubular organ, opening into the cecum at a point 2~3cm below the ileocecal valve. The appendix originates from the root of the cecum and is attached to the posterior medial wall of the cecum at the convergence point of the three colonic bands. Therefore, by tracing the three colonic bands of the cecum to the apex, the base of the appendix can be found.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

Mesentery: A triangular fold formed by two layers of peritoneum surrounding the appendix. The mesentery is short, and the appendix is coiled. The mesentery contains the appendicular artery and vein, which run behind the terminal part of the ileum along the free margin of the appendicular mesentery.

Artery: A branch of the ileocolic artery, a terminal artery. It is prone to cause appendiceal necrosis when blood supply is obstructed.
Vein: Accompanies the artery, draining into the portal vein. Embolism detachment can cause portal vein inflammation and bacterial liver abscess.

Lymphatics: Accompany the blood vessels

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->

Appendix Histological Structure
Appendix Mucosa: Composed of intestinal epithelium. Epithelial cells can secrete a small amount of mucus. The mucosa and submucosa contain a rich amount of lymphoid tissue, a lymphoid organ involved in the production and maturation of B lymphocytes. It begins to appear after birth and reaches its peak between 12~20 years of age, with more than 200 lymphoid follicles. It gradually decreases, with a significant reduction in follicles after 30 years, and completely disappears by 60 years. Therefore, the removal of the appendix in adults does not damage the body's immune function.

Acute Appendicitis - Symptoms

Abdominal Pain: Transitory right lower quadrant pain, 70%~80%
Gastrointestinal Symptoms: Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, etc., of mild degree
Systemic Symptoms: Early fatigue. Severe inflammation can cause systemic toxic symptoms

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->

Transferable right lower quadrant tenderness, peritoneal sign, right lower quadrant - pouch sign
Other auxiliary signs that can be considered:
1) Rovsing's sign: By pressing the descending colon of the left lower abdomen with one hand and repeatedly compressing the proximal colon with the other hand, pain in the right lower abdomen is considered positive; it can indirectly confirm the presence of appendicitis
2) Psoas sign: Positive suggests appendicitis is located posteriorly
3) Obturator sign: Positive suggests appendicitis is located lower

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->

Acute Appendicitis—Laboratory Tests
Complete blood count: Elevated white blood cell count and neutrophil percentage. In some patients, white blood cell count may not be significantly elevated, which is more common in simple appendicitis or in elderly patients.
Urine routine: Generally negative; the presence of a few red blood cells in the urine indicates that the inflamed appendix is close to the ureter or bladder. In women with a history of amenorrhea during the reproductive period, serum β-HCG should be checked to exclude gynecological conditions.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->

Acute Appendicitis—Imaging Studies

Abdominal X-ray: Cecal dilatation and fluid-air level, occasionally calcified fecal stones and foreign body shadows
Ultrasound: Sometimes, an enlarged appendix or abscess can be detected.
CT: Similar to ultrasound, particularly helpful for diagnosing pericecal abscesses.
Note: These special examinations are not essential for the diagnosis of acute appendicitis. They may be chosen for use when the diagnosis is uncertain!

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->

Acute Appendicitis—Treatment
Surgical treatment
Acute simple appendicitis: Excision, primary suture
Acute suppurative or gangrenous appendicitis: Excision, primary suture
Perforated appendicitis: Right lower quadrant transrectus sheath incision, excision, primary suture
Pericecal abscess: Puncture and drainage, incisional drainage
Non-surgical treatment: Applicable only to simple appendicitis and early stages of acute appendicitis. Treatment: Anti-inflammatory and fluid replacement
