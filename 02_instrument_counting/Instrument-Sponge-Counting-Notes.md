Indications for Cataract Phacoemulsification Surgery
1. Secondary cataracts:
     ① Severe myopia with cataracts, with mild fundus lesions.
     ② Glaucoma surgery with cataracts, without visual function impairment.
     ③ Uveitis with cataracts, inflammation resolved for more than three months.
     ④ Diabetes with cataracts, blood sugar controlled to near normal levels.
2. Traumatic cataracts, without lens dislocation.
3. Cataracts with glaucoma, intraocular pressure controlled for combined surgery.
4. Maturity or immature cataracts in the elderly, visual acuity below 0.4.
5. Congenital cataracts.
6. Patients who have had cataract extraction and require artificial lens implantation.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
Contraindications for Cataract Phacoemulsification Surgery
1. Lens dislocation or semi-dislocation.
2. Active uveitis.
3. Concurrent ocular infectious diseases such as chronic dacryocystitis, acute conjunctivitis, etc.
4. Congenital ocular abnormalities: such as microphthalmia, microcornea, congenital glaucoma, etc.
5. Diabetic iris redness.
6. Severe fundus disease: severe macular lesions, severe retinal detachment, extensive vitreous hemorrhage or opacity, optic nerve atrophy, etc.
7. Nystagmus, severe amblyopia, etc.
8. Advanced or absolute stage glaucoma.
9. Certain systemic diseases not effectively controlled. Such as: congestive heart failure, active pulmonary tuberculosis, pulmonary infection or asthma, severe diabetes with uncontrolled blood sugar, etc.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Selection of Cases for Cataract Phacoemulsification Surgery
■ Crystalline opacity with corrected visual acuity below 0.3.
■ Crystalline opacity with corrected visual acuity greater than 0.3, less than 0.5, but with a personal need for the patient.
■ Another type of patient has a very good best-corrected visual acuity, but the patient reports blurred vision in bright light. On examination, nuclear crystalline opacity is visible. In this case, contrast sensitivity and glare tests should be performed. If there is a significant decrease, surgery should be considered.
■ Preoperative examination of the patient should be thorough, including medical history, and attention should be paid to the presence of other ocular abnormalities. Such as lens dislocation, pupillary block, glaucoma, high myopia, etc.

Perioperative Nursing Care for Cataract Phacoemulsification Surgery
I. Preoperative Nursing
① Psychological care: eliminate concerns.
        Education and psychological care. Most patients are elderly, with poor psychological endurance and understanding ability, and are overly tense and excited, which can induce systemic diseases such as cardiovascular and cerebrovascular accidents. From a psychological perspective, it is required that patients have good psychological quality and stable emotions. Therefore, nurses should actively communicate with patients, promote health knowledge, introduce hospital regulations, observe and analyze the psychological condition of patients, understand the emotional reactions of patients. The nurse should also explain to the patient that the surgery uses topical anesthesia, the operation time is short, the incision is small, the pain is small, the postoperative reaction is light, and the recovery is fast, so as to eliminate the patient's fear of surgery, make the patient have full ideological preparation, increase the patient's trust in the nurse, and thus easily accept the psychological support and help from the nurse, which is very important for the smooth progress of the operation and the postoperative recovery.

Perioperative Nursing Care for Cataract Phacoemulsification Surgery
② Preoperative examination: perform routine ocular examination, especially corneal curvature, corneal thickness measurement, and ocular axis ultrasound examination, so that the implanted artificial lens power is more accurate.
③ Ocular preparation:
       Antibiotic eye drops: use tobramycin dexamethasone eye drops in the surgical eye for 3 days before surgery, 1 time every 2 hours;
       Palpebral margin massage: especially pay attention to MGD patients.
       Dacryocystorhinostomy: use normal saline to flush the lacrimal duct and conjunctival sac before surgery, check whether the patient has lacrimal duct obstruction or chronic dacryocystitis, etc., to avoid postoperative infection;
       Mydriasis: use compound tropicamide eye drops every 10 minutes before surgery, alternating points until surgery, so that the pupil remains dilated during the operation, which is convenient for the operation.

Cataract Ultra-Phacoemulsification Perioperative Nursing
Postoperative Care:
① Monitor vital signs, alleviate anxiety, and ensure adequate sleep.
② Postoperative pain management: Maintain a quiet ward, comfort the patient with patience, and use analgesics appropriately. Eye swelling and pain accompanied by a headache and corneal endothelial edema may be considered as transient high intraocular pressure. Instruct the patient not to be anxious, as it may resolve spontaneously within 48 hours. If necessary, administer 250ml of 20% mannitol intravenously at a rapid rate. ③ Routine systemic application of antibiotics and an appropriate amount of corticosteroids for 2-3 days postoperatively; 24 hours after surgery, start alternating典必舒 eye drops and q2h eye drops, continuing for one week.
④ Have the patient lie flat, relax the head, and avoid sudden movements such as sitting up, bending over, etc., to prevent severe activities, coughing, and sneezing to prevent eye hemorrhage.
⑤ Closely observe the eye condition and be aware of complications.
⑥ Instruct the patient to maintain eye hygiene and change the eye dressing as needed.

Cataract Ultra-Phacoemulsification Perioperative Nursing
Dietary Care:
      Provide easily digestible, high-fiber nutritional diet, consume more fruits and vegetables, drink less than 2000ml of water daily, and no more than 200ml per time to maintain bowel regularity.
       Avoid hard foods to prevent excessive chewing and vibration of the eye wound; avoid spicy and刺激性 foods, and eat less or no cilantro and seafood, as these foods can increase the body's stress response and exacerbate the degree of postoperative inflammation.

Cataract Ultra-Phacoemulsification Perioperative Nursing
Postoperative Recovery Guidance:
① Guide the patient to adjust their mindset at any time, avoid adverse mental stimulation and psychological burden;
② Rest in bed postoperatively, reduce eye movement, avoid forceful coughing, sneezing, and maintain smooth bowel movements; food can be consumed 2 hours after surgery, and mild activity can be performed 6 hours after surgery;
③ Strictly follow medical instructions, use eye drops correctly; 24 hours after surgery, start alternating典必舒 eye drops and sodium diclofenac eye drops q1h or as prescribed by the doctor.
④ Provide discharge instructions and develop good hygiene habits;
⑤ Guide the follow-up schedule and methods, routine follow-up is required 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month, 2 months, and 6 months postoperatively; if there are obvious eye pain, nausea, vomiting, vision loss, diplopia, etc., seek medical attention promptly.
