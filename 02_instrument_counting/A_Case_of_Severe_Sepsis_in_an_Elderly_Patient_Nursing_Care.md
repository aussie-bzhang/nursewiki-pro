## Introduction

According to statistics, the incidence rate of sepsis in our country is 0.3%, with a mortality rate of elderly patients reaching 30%-60%. Early diagnosis, concurrent treatment, and nursing interventions within the first few hours can improve prognosis and reduce mortality rates.

It is reported abroad that there are 750,000 new cases of sepsis in the United States each year, costing over 200 billion dollars, accounting for 5.2% of the total hospital costs, and rising at a rate of 1.5%-8.0% annually.

Sepsis refers to a systemic inflammatory response syndrome caused by various infectious factors.

### Definitions
- Sepsis: A systemic inflammatory response syndrome caused by infection.
- Sepsis Shock: Sepsis complicated by organ dysfunction or tissue hypoperfusion or hypotension.
- Severe Sepsis: Sepsis with persistent hypotension, increased lactic acid (＞2 mmol/L), or oliguria despite adequate fluid resuscitation.

Pathophysiology of Shock
- Sharp reduction in effective circulating blood volume
- Microcirculatory disorders
- Metabolic changes
- Secondary organ damage to visceral organs

# Summary and Nursing Experience

Patients with sepsis have a rapid disease progression, and elderly patients have more underlying diseases, therefore, there are more nursing issues. Early nursing intervention and psychological care play a positive role in the treatment of sepsis.

Collaborating with doctors, gaining a deep understanding of the disease, improving clinical thinking ability, and implementing targeted nursing measures have led to satisfactory outcomes of nursing intervention.

Strengthening specialized knowledge learning, accumulating more individual cases to learn and reflect, providing better quality services for patients, and improving patient satisfaction.
