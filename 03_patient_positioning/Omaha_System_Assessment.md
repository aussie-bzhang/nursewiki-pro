**Definition and Classification of Cerebral Infarction**
Cerebral infarction, also known as ischemic stroke, refers to various causes of cerebral blood supply disorders, leading to ischemic and hypoxic necrosis of brain tissue, resulting in corresponding neurological deficits.
Source for haemorhagic stroke (P. Fayad; Neurology 1998;51(Suppl 3);S69-S73)

**Reason for Selection of Topic One**
And 30% of patients in working age can recover to work one year after rehabilitation treatment, according to the survey results of the World Health Organization.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
**Evidence-based Medicine Studies**
Have shown that early rehabilitation is helpful to improve the impaired function of stroke patients, reduce the degree of disability, and improve their quality of life. It is generally advocated to start rehabilitation treatment 48 hours after the vital signs are stable, and there is no aggravation or improvement in the primary neurological disease.
The acute phase of stroke refers to the 1-2 weeks after onset, and the rehabilitation treatment in this period is the first-level rehabilitation. Its goal is to promote the recovery of muscle tension on the hemiplegic side and the emergence of active movement through passive activities and active participation, as well as the correct positioning of the limbs and the reduction of respiratory tract infections and other complications.

Nursing Experience
(1) Starting rehabilitation exercise within 48 hours after the stable condition of acute cerebral infarction is crucial for the patient's future self-care ability and directly affects the future quality of life. During the nursing process, due to the disease and the decline of the patient's own body function, many nursing problems will arise. It is very difficult to solve all the nursing problems, so it is necessary to develop short-term nursing goals and long-term nursing plans, taking the patient's feelings as the main focus, and solving the patient's urgent nursing problems is the key.
(2) Achieving the best quality of life for patients is a long and gradual process. Many patients still have rehabilitation issues when they are discharged. They should continue rehabilitation training in the community and at home. Therefore, how to do a good job of post-discharge continuing care and reduce the recurrence rate is a problem worth thinking about.
