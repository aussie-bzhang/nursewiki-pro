The continuous increase in the difficulty and precision of surgery, along with the extension of surgery time, has increased the incidence of pressure ulcers during surgery.

Literature reports:

1.  Surgical patients are at high risk for acute pressure ulcers. Reasons: the use of various drugs, auxiliary treatment equipment, anesthesia, and other factors during surgery.

2.  Acute pressure ulcers during surgery develop rapidly, and stage I and II pressure ulcers that appear postoperatively can rapidly progress to stage III and IV pressure ulcers within a few hours.

3.  The incidence of pressure ulcers during surgery is 4%-45%, of which 45% can be prevented if the risk of pressure ulcers is correctly assessed.

4.  Enhancing the awareness and preventive behavior of operating room nurses regarding intraoperative pressure ulcers is beneficial to reducing the incidence of intraoperative pressure ulcers.

**Patient Passive, Unperceived State**

The patient is in a forced position during surgery, with minimal space for position changes and no self-perception ability, making it easy to ignore the affected areas caused by changes in position during surgery.
**Patients with Pressure Ulcer Risk**

Common mechanical actions during surgery include vertical pressure (gravity), shearing force, and friction. When the body tissue is subjected to pressure of 70 mmHg or more for more than 2 hours, irreversible tissue damage can occur.

**Stress Response**

When the body is suddenly stimulated by surgery, blood loss, hypoxia, and other factors, the body reacts by secreting a large amount of corticotropin-releasing hormone through the hypothalamus, causing a redistribution of blood flow throughout the body, reducing blood flow to the skin and muscles. The body's pressure sensitivity increases, making it more susceptible to pressure ulcers.

Electrosurgical Safety Measures
1. Avoid contact between the patient's limbs and the metal parts of the operating table. There should be at least a 4cm thick insulating layer between the patient and the metal bed, and the patient's limbs should be wrapped in two layers of bed sheets to ensure that they do not come into contact with metal objects.
2. Select an ideal electrosurgical blade soft electrode plate with a moderate size. The conductive adhesive should be strong and easy to peel off. Check for electrode plate displacement or detachment promptly if the patient is restless.
3. Clean the skin at the site of electrode plate application before sticking to reduce resistance and ensure a tight bond with the skin; the electrode plate should be as close as possible to the surgical site, placed on areas rich in blood vessels or muscle, avoiding bony prominences, fatty tissue, scars, and areas lacking in blood vessels.
4. For patients using cardiac monitoring during surgery, the electrosurgical electrode plate should be at least 15cm away from the cardiac monitoring electrode. Caution should be exercised when using electrosurgery in patients with pacemakers.

5. Avoid overlapping and twisting when using large electrode plates, and use a spiral adhesive method. After the electrode plate is placed, check for any "tenting" phenomena to ensure effective contact area between the electrode plate and the skin.

Key 3 - Operating Room

1. Check the skin before anesthesia, combine it with the admissions skin check list, and change the decompression site.
2. Avoid pulling or tugging the patient when positioning the surgical position to prevent the generation of shearing force.
3. For high-risk patients, adjust the left or right side, head down or head up by 10°~15° every 2 hours, if it does not affect the operation.
4. Timely relieve the local pressure, shearing force, and moisture of the patient during the operation.
5. Timely decompression and handover after the operation.

<!-- Slide number: 48 -->

Key 4 - Postoperative

1. Handover for patients with skin reddening after surgery:
   ① For patients under general anesthesia, the circulating nurse and recovery room should make a handover for further intervention;
   ② For patients directly sent to the ward, make sure the measures for skin decompression during the operation are in place for further intervention in the ward.
2. The pressure ulcer work in the operating room is perfect
   ① Send the skin photo taken to the "Pressure Ulcer Group Members". The circulating nurse tracks the skin condition for 1 day and fills out a complete pressure ulcer form and hands it over to the pressure ulcer group.
   ② The pressure ulcer group members do further follow-up, analyze the causes, and summarize the data.
3. If the redness on the surgical patient develops into a pressure ulcer within 6 days after surgery, report the inevitable pressure ulcer in the operating room.

3. For patients with difficult to move during the operation, such as neurosurgery and cardiovascular surgery, high-elastic foam mattresses should be used as much as possible to reduce pressure.

Surgical Mattress

1. Patients in restrictive surgical positions under general anesthesia will inevitably develop positional pressure ulcers, and effective decompression during the operation is the main measure to slow down the formation of pressure ulcers.
2. Studies have shown that for patients with operation time > 4h, the incidence of pressure ulcers using ordinary mattresses is significantly higher than that using memory foam (reason: poor recovery of ordinary mattresses, the body's gravity causes the mattress to be particularly thin at the buttocks)
3. Long-term use of surgical mattresses will cause the thickness of the tailbone to become thin, from the normal 6cm to 2cm, far from meeting the requirements for preventing pressure ulcers.

Surgical Mattress

	 When in a sitting or supine position, the distance between the lowest point of the bony prominence and the lower surface of the mattress should be at least 5cm to achieve the decompression effect.
	 By increasing the contact area with the human body or changing the contact position and duration between the support surface and the body, the pressure on the skin contact surface can be reduced. The support surface has become one of the most commonly used protective devices for preventing pressure ulcers.
	 Avoid using annular or ring-shaped devices or water-filled gloves for patients with general or local edema.
	 Avoid using water-filled gloves for patients with excessive skin exudation or sweating.

Electric Heating Pads

Specialist team findings:
	 The use of electric heating pads for保温 measures in long-duration surgical patients results in a higher incidence of postoperative pressure ulcers than in patients undergoing surgery during the same time period.
	 Discussion: The electric heating wire, separated by a layer of bedsheet, is equivalent to a foreign body under the skin, and lying on it for a long time increases the pressure on the bony prominence skin.
	 Change measures: Use an air-filled heating pad for保温 measures, cover the patient with the heat air blanket or place it under the patient, choose a low temperature (38°C) as a conventional choice, and choose 42°C during massive hemorrhage.

	 Maintain moderate warming, avoid sweating during the operation. After half an hour of warming, touch the patient's covered body surface with your hand. If there is sweat or moisture, turn off the warming equipment or remove the cover immediately. Do not place the overheating device directly on the skin surface.
