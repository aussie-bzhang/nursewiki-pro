☆The largest and most complex joint in the human body, with a high risk of injury.

Medical History
Patient Zheng, female, 62 years old, admitted on 2015-8-18.
Chief Complaint: Left knee pain for 2 years, exacerbated with limited activity for 1 month.

Current History: One month ago, the pain worsened, especially when descending stairs or flexing the knee, with limited activity. X-ray showed: Osteophyte changes were visible at the marginal part of the left knee joint, with mild narrowing of the joint space.

Past Medical History: Generally healthy, no history of hepatitis B, no surgery history, no allergic history.

Surgery: On 8-20, the patient underwent "left total knee arthroplasty" under lumbar epidural anesthesia, the operation was uneventful, and the patient returned to the ward safely.
Postoperative: Cardiovascular monitoring, oxygen 2L/min, postoperative supine position, knee kept in extension, ice bag on the wound, sandbag, one negative pressure drainage tube left in place, given anti-inflammatory, fluid replacement, analgesic, and antithrombotic treatment. On the first postoperative day, the drainage volume was 620ml, and 600ml of suspension of less white erythrocytes and 350ml of plasma were administered as ordered. On the second postoperative day, the drainage tube was removed and a compression pump was applied twice a day. On the fourth postoperative day, a repeat X-ray showed that the artificial knee was well positioned. On the fifth postoperative day, the patient was instructed to stand and walk with the assistance of crutches. The patient is still receiving fluid replacement therapy. The suture was removed around the 12th postoperative day.

### Passive Exercise

Passive flexion of the knee joint: After the patient has been administered prophylactic pain medication, perform the following actions. Instruct the patient to relax and take a deep breath. With one hand supporting the ankle and the other hand supporting the popliteal fossa, gently flex the knee to 90°. Alternatively, have the patient sit on the edge of the bed and perform the same action to flex the knee.

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->
☞康复锻炼
主动训练

Active training includes:

1. **Active flexion and extension of the knee joint**: After the patient has been administered prophylactic pain medication, instruct the patient to actively flex and extend the knee joint. The patient should be encouraged to perform this exercise regularly to improve joint mobility.

2. **Strengthening exercises for the quadriceps and hamstrings**: These exercises help to strengthen the muscles around the knee joint, which is important for stability and function.

3. **Gait training**: As the patient's mobility improves, gait training can be introduced to help the patient regain their normal walking pattern.

4. **Balance training**: Balance exercises are important to prevent falls and improve overall mobility.

Ankle pump exercise: Begin foot and ankle joint flexion and extension activities after anesthesia recovery, for 2-3 minutes each time, 2-3 times per hour.

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->
☞Rehabilitation Exercise
Active Training
Straight leg raising exercise: Extend the lower limb straight, as if performing a quadriceps exercise, lift the leg off the bed surface by several centimeters, maintain for 5-10 seconds, and then slowly lower it. Repeat this action until the thigh feels tired. This exercise can also be performed in a sitting position, raising the straight leg by contracting the thigh muscles to straighten the knee joint and maintain for 5-10 seconds.

☞Rehabilitation Exercise
Active Training

   From postoperative week 2 to week 8, this stage should increase the practice of walking on flat ground and climbing stairs, gradually increasing the training volume from less to more, without causing pain or swelling in the affected knee. Strengthen muscle strength and gait practice, climbing stairs, to prepare for the recovery of the preoperative lifestyle role.

<!-- Slide number: 39 -->
☞Prosthetic Protection

① Do not squat or kneel, and avoid excessive twisting of the knee joint.

② Avoid vigorous exercise.

③ Choose suitable exercises, such as walking.

④ When necessary (such as long-distance walking), use a walking aid to reduce the chance of injury.

⑤ Avoid overloading to accelerate cartilage wear, and pay attention to controlling weight and load-bearing items.

<!-- Slide number: 40 -->
☞Avoid Some Risky Activities

Do not twist the knee joint; do not engage in high-intensity activities such as running, jumping, race walking, or playing basketball when turning without moving your feet.
