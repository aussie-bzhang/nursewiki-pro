## Introduction
# Decubitus ulcers refer to localized damage to the skin or / and subcutaneous tissue, usually located at bony prominences. This type of injury is generally caused by pressure or pressure combined with shearing forces. 
International NPUAP-EPUAP Definition
Diabetes is a metabolic disorder syndrome characterized by chronic hyperglycemia caused by various etiologies, accompanied by vascular, neurological, and skin lesions due to defective insulin secretion or utilization.

## Case Introduction
Personal Information: Male, 75 years old, married, retired
Medical History: Diabetes, hypertension, cerebral infarction, bilateral lower limb amputation
Current Medical History: The patient was admitted to the geriatric ward with a diagnosis of "pulmonary infection with secondary decubitus ulcer" due to recurrent intermittent fever.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
## Nursing Assessment - General
Age: 75 years
Braden Score: 10 points
Nutritional Status: Unable to eat independently, nasogastric feeding
Long-term Bed Rest: Bilateral lower limb amputation
Medical History: Diabetes, hypertension, cerebral infarction
Social Support: Unable to care for oneself, care worker assistance
Laboratory Indicators: As follows

## Nursing Assessment - Local
Type: Coccysgeal stage Ⅳ decubitus ulcer
Size: 4.5x3.5x1cm
              Undermining 1 to 4 points 2cm
Color: 25% red tissue
               75% yellow fascia
Odor: No odor
Surrounding Skin: Intact without breakage
Exudate: Large amount of exudate

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
## Nursing Issues
Skin Integrity Impaired: Related to long-term bed rest
Incontinence: Related to cerebral infarction and hemiparesis
Nutritional Deficiency: Below the body's requirement
Knowledge Deficiency: Family lacks knowledge of diabetes and decubitus ulcer care

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
## Nursing Objectives
Patient's wound healing, effective control of blood sugar
Effective management of patient's incontinence, no perianal skin breakdown
Improvement of patient's nutritional deficiency
Patient's family and care workers master relevant disease knowledge
