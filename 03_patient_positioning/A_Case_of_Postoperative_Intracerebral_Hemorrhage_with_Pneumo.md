## History Introduction
### Basic Information
  56 Bed, Wu Qi, Male, 59 years old, high school education, employee, admission number D01016749. Admission time: 14:00 on July 5, 2018. Admission method: wheelchair. Admission diagnosis: 1. Intracerebral hemorrhage (postoperative) 2. Hemiparesis (right) 3. Impaired cognitive function and consciousness-specific symptoms 4. Pneumonia.
   - Current medical history: The patient has cognitive dysfunction, right-sided limb dysfunction, balance dysfunction, and pneumonia.
   - Past medical history: Left temporal parietal hematoma removal + bone flap decompression surgery.
   - Patient and family expectations: Hope the patient can live independently.

## History of Illness

On April 26, 2018, the patient experienced weakness in the right limb, right hand motor impairment, unclear cause, and slurred speech, and was admitted to our department of neurology with a suspected diagnosis of "stroke."

On April 29, the patient vomited and became unresponsive. Repeated cranial CT showed increased intracerebral hemorrhage compared to the previous examination. On the same day, an emergency operation was performed for left temporal parietal hematoma removal and bone flap decompression. Postoperative treatment included hemostasis and fluid replacement.
On May 1, the patient had a lot of sputum, and a tracheotomy was performed.

## Nursing Measures
1. Vibration Sputum Removal Machine
   A non-pharmacological manual method, which uses the principle of physics to produce a specific force direction on the human body surface, with a strong penetrating force that can penetrate the skin, muscle, and tissue to the body fluid, loosen the sputum in the lung, liquefy the sputum, and make the sputum move horizontally along the direction of the force, from the small bronchus to the bronchus to the trachea, so as to achieve the effect of expectoration [5].

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
## Nursing Measures
### Vibration Sputum Removal Machine Precautions
   - Perform treatment 2 hours before or 2 hours after meals; adjust the speed of the treatment instrument to 20-60/s; give the patient a lateral position, one hand holding the impact handle, one hand pressing tightly on the impact head, so that the impact head is in close contact with the skin, from bottom to top, from outside to inside, from the periphery to the center towards the hilum, move the impact head uniformly, covering the entire lung field, avoiding the stomach and heart; the operation time is 10-15 minutes [1]. When using the sputum removal machine, adjust the frequency and intensity of vibration according to the patient's condition and physical condition, and pay attention to observe the patient's vital signs, cough, sputum, and whether there are symptoms such as cyanosis, chest tightness, and dyspnea [6]. It cannot be used during the fracture period [7].

# Nursing Measures
3. Manual Chest Percussion - This involves hand percussion to mobilize sputum, performed three times daily for 10-15 minutes each session. Method: The nurse supports the patient's shoulder with one hand, while the other hand is used with the fingers together and slightly curled, forming a concave palm with a natural angle of 120° to 150°. The palm and the pads of the fingers rest on the patient's back, using the wrist joint strength to tap at a rate of 40-50 times per minute, with a uniform rhythm, gently tapping from the bottom of the lungs outward and inward, and from the bottom up, avoiding important organs such as the spine, sternum, and kidneys [4].

# Positional Drainage
4. The principle of positional drainage is to place the affected area higher so that the opening of the draining bronchus is directed downward. Caution: Drainage of one area should be performed for about 5-10 minutes, and if multiple areas are involved, the total time should not exceed 30-45 minutes to prevent patient fatigue. Positional drainage should be arranged 1-2 hours after meals or one hour before meals in a head-down position. This prevents gastroesophageal reflux, nausea, and vomiting. Changes in the patient's vital signs should be monitored during the drainage process.

# Nursing Measures

5. Instruct the patient in effective coughing
In a sitting or lying position, the patient should first perform 5-6 deep breaths, inhale deeply and then hold the breath, followed by coughing. Continue coughing several times to bring the sputum to the vicinity of the throat, and then cough forcefully to expel the sputum.

# Nursing Experience
1. Elderly patients, due to the decline in organ function, often have multiple underlying diseases. Bedridden patients are more prone to recurrent respiratory tract inflammation, poor overall condition, tracheostomy, which weakens and reduces the function of the mucociliary movement of the airway, causing weak coughing and delayed response, making it difficult to expel sputum, which is not conducive to the treatment of respiratory tract infections. [3] Airway care is an important link in timely and effective clearance of respiratory secretions, prevention and control of pulmonary infections, and reducing mortality rates. [2]
