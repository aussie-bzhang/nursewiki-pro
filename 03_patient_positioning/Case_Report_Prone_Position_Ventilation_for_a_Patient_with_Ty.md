## Introduction
Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)
       refers to acute (within 1 week) diffuse inflammatory lung injury caused by various etiologies (including infection, trauma, shock, etc.). The pathological features include increased pulmonary capillary permeability, pulmonary edema, and hyaline membrane formation. The pathophysiological characteristics include reduced effective pulmonary volume, reduced lung compliance, and V/Q mismatch. The clinical manifestations are dyspnea and refractory hypoxemia.

1

Early enteral nutrition support is extremely important for the prognosis of critically ill patients, but many critically ill patients have gastrointestinal motility disorders, with suppressed gastrointestinal motility. In severe cases, "gastroparesis" can affect the effectiveness of nutritional support. The use of spiral nasogastric tubes can provide nutritional support, effectively reduce the probability of aspiration and reflux in critically ill patients, and reduce the incidence of aspiration pneumonia.

2

**Recommendation 12 of the "Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Lung Injury/Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (2007)"**:
For severe ARDS patients with ineffective conventional mechanical ventilation and no contraindications, prone position ventilation can be considered (recommendation level: D).

The 29th Congress of the Society of Critical Care Medicine in the United States mentioned:
Prone position ventilation for the treatment of ARDS patients with hypoxemia is still a hot topic. Approximately 50% to 70% of patients can improve arterial oxygen tension and reduce inspired oxygen concentration.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
## What is Prone Position Ventilation?
Prone position ventilation is achieved by using a翻身 bed, a turning device, or manual operation to place the patient in a prone position (prone position) for mechanical ventilation. It is mainly used to improve oxygenation in ARDS patients.

## Mechanism of Prone Position Ventilation in Improving Oxygenation
  1. Reduction of intrathoracic pressure gradient, improving V/Q mismatch
2. Reduction of ventilator-associated lung injury
3. Effective relief of compression
  4. Favorable position for drainage
Indications for prone position ventilation
Severe hypoxemia, conventional mechanical ventilation cannot correct
Promote re-expansion of collapsed alveoli, promote airway secretion drainage
Form new gravity-dependent areas, improve oxygenation

### Reflection

During prone ventilation, sufficient manpower should be arranged for the turning of the patient in prone position, with one person supporting the head and neck, and two people on each side, ensuring the normal status of all tubes. At the same time, lift the patient to one side for turning, and place soft pillows at the bony prominences, with the abdomen contacting the mattress to enhance patient comfort.

For critically ill patients, the use of gastrointestinal motility drugs and clockwise stomach massage did not improve the success rate of nasogastric tube placement.

Under the premise that the focus of infection and the cause were not clear, physical and chemical cooling measures were not effective in controlling body temperature.

### Notes:

### Summary

Assess the patient as a candidate for early use of prone ventilation regardless of the severity of hypoxemia, as prone ventilation can quickly and effectively improve hypoxemia.

Critically ill patients have gastrointestinal motility disorders, and the intake, absorption, and utilization of enteral nutrition are insufficient. Improving the patient's nutritional status is a direction that needs to be explored.

The patient presented with a simple and persistent state of high fever, and the focus of infection and the cause were not clear. Effective treatment measures could not be taken, resulting in the patient's death.
