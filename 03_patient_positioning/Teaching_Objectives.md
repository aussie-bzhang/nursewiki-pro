# II. Purpose and Scope of Application of Surgical Positioning
Purpose: To provide guiding opinions for the positioning of perioperative patients, standardize the operation of position care, and maximize the avoidance of surgical position injury.
Scope of Application: Can be applied in operating rooms, catheterization laboratories, endoscopy rooms, interventional rooms, and other departments that implement invasive treatments.

<!-- Slide number: 24 -->
# Definition: Standard Surgical Position
Standard surgical position (standardized patient position) is confirmed and implemented by the surgical doctor, anesthesiologist, and operating room nurse together. Based on physiological and anatomical knowledge, the correct position equipment and supplies are selected to fully expose the surgical field and ensure patient safety and comfort. Standard surgical positions include supine position, lateral position, prone position, and lithotomy position, and other surgical positions are derived from these standard positions.

# V. Methods of Positioning

1. Supine Position
Definition: This is a position where the patient's head is placed on a pillow, the arms are placed on the sides of the body or naturally extended, and the legs are naturally extended. Various special supine positions are arranged according to the surgical site and method, including neck and head extension supine position, head high and feet low supine position, head low and feet high supine position, and V-shaped leg separation supine position, etc. Special supine positions are derived from the standard supine position.
Applicable Surgery: Head and neck, facial, thoracic and abdominal, and limb surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 34 -->
# Methods of Placing the Supine Position:
The head is placed on the pillow and is in a neutral position, with the height of the pillow appropriate. The head and cervical spine are in a horizontal neutral position.
The upper limbs are placed with the palms facing the sides of the body, the elbows slightly flexed, and the limbs fixed with a cloth. The distal joints are slightly higher than the proximal joints, which is conducive to the relaxation of the muscles and tendons of the upper limbs and the venous return. The shoulder joint is abducted no more than 90° to avoid injury to the brachial plexus.
A knee pillow should be placed under the knees, and a heel pillow should be placed under the feet.
A retractor band is used to fix at 5 cm above the knee joint, with appropriate tightness, to accommodate one finger, to prevent injury to the common peroneal nerve.

# Important Points:
Protect bony prominences (occiput, scapula, coccyx, elbows, heels, etc.) with protective pads as needed to prevent local tissue compression.
Do not fix the upper limbs too tightly to prevent compartment syndrome.
Prevent excessive cervical twisting to avoid injury to the brachial plexus by traction.
In the late stages of pregnancy, pregnant women should alternate between supine and left lateral positions to prevent the occurrence of supine hypotensive syndrome.

# 2. Lateral Position
Definition: The patient is placed in a natural lateral position, with the head turned towards the healthy side, the lower limbs naturally flexed, and placed apart. The arms are naturally extended forward, the patient's spine is on the horizontal line, and the physiological curvature is maintained. On this basis, various special lateral positions are arranged according to the surgical site and method.
Applicable Surgery: Temporal, lung, esophagus, lateral chest wall, hip joint, etc.

<!-- Slide number: 49 -->
# Placement Method:
Take a healthy side lying position, place a pillow under the head, the height is equal to the lower side shoulder height, so that the cervical spine is in a horizontal position.
Place a chest pad 10cm from the axilla, at the level of the shoulder peak.
Place the affected upper limb in a curved position on the adjustable arm rest, with the distal joint slightly lower than the proximal joint.
The lower limb is abducted on the arm rest, with the distal joint higher than the proximal joint, maintaining the natural expansion of the chest.
Support the pubic symphysis with a fixation frame on the ventral side, and fix the coccyx or scapular area on the dorsal side with a fixation frame (at least 15cm away from the surgical field).
The lower limbs can be naturally flexed at 45° or the upper limbs flexed and the lower limbs extended according to the type of surgery. Use soft pillows or support pads between the legs. Fix the limbs with restraints, ensuring appropriate tightness.

# 3. Prone Position
Definition: Prone position (prone position) is a surgical position where the patient lies on the bed face down, with the face down and the back up, ensuring the maximum range of the chest and abdomen is not compressed, and the lower limbs are naturally flexed.

Applicable Surgery: Head and neck, back, posterior spine, posterior pelvic, and lateral extremity surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 59 -->
# Placement Method:
According to the surgical method and patient's body type, select appropriate position support items and place them on the operating bed at the corresponding position.
After successful anesthesia, with the cooperation of medical staff, use the axis翻身 method to place the patient on the prone position support items, properly restrain to prevent falls.
Check the head and face, adjust the width of the head support according to the patient's face shape, place the head on the headrest, maintain the neutral position of the cervical spine, and maintain the normal physiological curvature of the human body. Choose the forehead, cheeks, and chin as support points, avoiding compression of the orbital upper nerve, orbital upper artery, eye, zygomatic bone, nose, and lips.
Choose the anterior chest, ribs, iliac crest, and pubic symphysis as support points, keeping the chest and abdomen suspended, avoiding compression, and avoiding the axilla. Protect the perineum of male patients and the breast of female patients.

⑥ Place the upper limbs along the physiological rotation direction of the joints, naturally forward on both sides of the head or on the arm rest, at an appropriate height, avoiding the下垂 of the fingertips, and fix them with restraints. # 4. Lithotomy Position
**Definition:** The lithotomy position is when the patient lies on their back, the legs are placed on the leg holder, the buttocks are moved to the edge of the bed, and the perineum is maximally exposed, commonly used for anorectal surgery and gynecological surgery.

**Applicable Surgery:** Suitable for perineal and perineal abdominal combined surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 74 -->
**Method of Placement:**
The patient assumes a supine position, and the lithotomy leg holder is placed at the level of the hip joint.
If the arms need to be abducted, they are the same as in the supine position. The lower limbs are secured with restraints.
Lower the operating bed leg board, and if necessary, place a position pad under the buttocks to reduce local pressure, at the same time raising the buttocks for easier surgical manipulation. The legs are abducted < 90°, and the angle of the anterior flexion of the thighs should be changed according to the surgical needs.
④ When a head-down, foot-up position is required, a shoulder support can be added to prevent the patient from sliding towards the head.

# 5, Accidental Injuries
Accidental injuries: Cervical vertebral dislocation, tube detachment, limb sprains, etc.
Prevention - Do a good job of "one assessment and four preventions"
"One assessment" - Carefully inspect and assess the patient's skin before surgery
"Four preventions" - Prevent falls
              Prevent pressure sores
              Prevent accidental burns and scalds
              Prevent exposure keratitis, etc.

# Summary - Preventive Measures
Thorough preoperative assessment, careful observation during surgery, and meticulous postoperative examination, with three timely actions (timely reporting, timely handling, timely recording).
Familiarity with the business, understanding the pressure points and fulcrums of various positions; high sense of responsibility, observing the limb blood circulation, skin color, and movement of the position pads during surgery; attention to warmth, preventing hypothermia leading to poor peripheral blood circulation and increasing the possibility of pressure ulcers.

# Preventive Measures
Avoid dragging, pulling, or tugging patients during the process of positioning; place soft pads on bony prominences to prevent pressure injuries; use cotton pads on areas with high friction, especially for the elderly, weak, diabetics, and those with poor nutrition; avoid pressure on the perineum in male patients.

# Preventive Measures
Before placing the patient in prone or lateral position, inform the anesthesiologist, protect the patient's head and various tubes, and prevent tube dislodgment; coordinate three-party actions consistently, rotating the head, neck, and thoracic vertebrae at the same level to prevent cervical dislocation and other accidents.
After positioning the patient, reconfirm that the bedsheet is flat, clean, and dry, and that the patient's body is in point contact with the bed surface to prevent local pressure leading to pressure ulcers.

# Preventive Measures
After positioning the patient, check if the patient's body parts (e.g., contact between the thighs or knees), the patient's body and the operating bed, and the patient's body and metal items (trays, brackets) are in contact to prevent accidental burns.
Keep the patient's skin dry during surgery, prevent the bedsheet from being wet by disinfectants, exudates, irrigation fluids, and sweat, and replace the pad or change the bedsheet immediately if it gets wet to avoid pressure ulcers, burns, and prevent infection.

# Preventive Measures
When the patient is in a head-down position during surgery, elevate the head as much as possible to prevent eye complications from prolonged head-down position.
If it is known that the patient's position needs to be adjusted during surgery, there should be measures to prevent the body from sliding or moving before surgery, to prevent skin damage caused by friction or pressure due to position movement.

# Preventive Measures
If the surgery allows, adjust the position appropriately every 2 hours, such as tilting the operating bed 5-10° left or right, slightly raising or lowering the backboard of the operating bed, turning the head to the other side, and raising the heels, etc., to shorten the time of local tissue compression and prevent pressure ulcers.
After the operation is completed, check and evaluate the skin condition, hand over the patient carefully, and ensure the continuity of the patient's care.
When complications from the position occur, write down the possible causes, symptoms, and treatment measures on the surgical nursing record sheet, and have the circulating nurse and the surgeon sign to confirm.
