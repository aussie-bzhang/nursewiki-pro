# Case Report: Prone Position Ventilation for a Patient with Type I Respiratory Failure (ARDS)

## Introduction

### Notes:

## Introduction

Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) refers to acute (within 1 week) diffuse inflammatory lung injury caused by various etiologies (including infection, trauma, shock, etc.). The pathological features include increased pulmonary capillary permeability, pulmonary edema, and hyaline membrane formation. The pathophysiological characteristics include reduced effective pulmonary volume, reduced lung compliance, and V/Q mismatch. Clinical manifestations include respiratory distress and refractory hypoxemia.

### Notes:

Early enteral nutrition support is extremely important for the prognosis of critically ill patients, but many critically ill patients have gastrointestinal motility disorders, with suppressed gastrointestinal motility. In severe cases, "gastroparesis" can affect the effectiveness of nutritional support. The use of spiral nasogastric tubes can provide nutritional support, effectively reduce the probability of aspiration and reflux in critically ill patients, and reduce the incidence of aspiration pneumonia.

### Notes:

## Prone Position Ventilation: Recommendations from the "2007 Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Lung Injury/Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome"

For severely ill ARDS patients with ineffective conventional mechanical ventilation and no contraindications, prone position ventilation can be considered (recommendation level: D).

The 29th Congress of the American Society of Critical Care Medicine mentioned that prone position ventilation for ARDS patients with hypoxemia is still a hot topic. Approximately 50% to 70% of patients can improve arterial oxygen tension and reduce inspired oxygen concentration.

## What is Prone Position Ventilation?

Prone position ventilation is achieved by using a翻身 bed, a turning device, or manual manipulation to place the patient in a prone position (prone position) for mechanical ventilation. It is mainly used to improve oxygenation in ARDS patients.

## Mechanism of Prone Position Ventilation in Improving Oxygenation

1. Reduction of intrathoracic pressure gradient, improving V/Q mismatch
2. Reduction of ventilator-associated lung injury
3. Effective relief of compression
4. Position favorable for drainage

### Indications for Prone Position Ventilation

Severe hypoxemia, conventional mechanical ventilation cannot correct
Promote re-expansion of collapsed alveoli, promote airway secretion drainage
Form new gravity-dependent areas, improve oxygenation

### Reflection

During prone ventilation, sufficient manpower should be arranged for the turning of the patient in prone position, one person to support the head and neck, two on each side, ensuring the normal status of all tubes. At the same time, lift the patient to one side for turning, and place soft pillows at the bony prominences, with the abdomen contacting the mattress to enhance patient comfort.

For critically ill patients, the use of gastrointestinal motility drugs and clockwise stomach massage did not improve the success rate of nasogastric tube placement.

Under the premise that the focus of infection and the cause of the patient were not clear, physical and chemical cooling measures were not effective in controlling body temperature.

### Notes:

### Summary

Assess the patient as a candidate for early use of prone ventilation regardless of the severity of hypoxemia, as prone ventilation can quickly and effectively improve hypoxemia.

Critically ill patients have gastrointestinal motility disorders, and the intake, absorption, and utilization of enteral nutrition are insufficient. Improving the patient's nutritional status is a direction that needs to be explored.

The patient presented with a state of pure and persistent fever, and the focus of infection and the cause were not clear. Effective treatment measures could not be taken, resulting in the patient's death.
