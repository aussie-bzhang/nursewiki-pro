## I. Case Information

On June 15, the patient accidentally fell and injured her head, experiencing pain. She was immediately sent to the emergency department of an external hospital. Head CT showed: 1. Hemorrhage and contusion in the left frontal, temporal, and parietal lobes, and the right cerebellar hemisphere; cerebral hernia formation; subarachnoid hemorrhage; slight hemorrhage in the posterior horns of both lateral ventricles; fracture of the right occipital bone; 2. Inflammation and effusion in all paranasal sinuses, bilateral nasal cavity effusion. After receiving hemostasis, hypotensive, and other symptomatic treatments, she was sent to our hospital's outpatients department for treatment on June 21 and admitted to the neurosurgery department for treatment.

Medical History and Treatment Process
- Past Medical History: None
- Drug Allergy History: None

On June 21, general anesthesia was used for left frontal lobectomy + cranioplasty + cranioprobe placement.

On June 29, local anesthesia was used for percutaneous dilatational tracheostomy.

On July 10, transferred to the rehabilitation and physiotherapy department for further treatment.

# But……
From 4/8 to 5/8 nursing observation: The patient had intermittent severe coughing.

Analysis:
1. Pneumonia exacerbation?      Small possibility, the patient's sputum has decreased, thin, and infection indicators are not high.
2. Improper tracheal tube position?        Possible, especially when changing the tracheostomy dressing and changing positions.
3. Tracheal tube complications:气管内息肉、肉芽形成？      Highly possible! The tube was placed on 29/6, and the replacement time was long.

Feedback to the attending physician

On 10/8, polypectomy was performed under bronchoscopy
Consultation with the respiratory department

On 7/8, bronchoscopy showed a small polyp in the trachea

On 11/8, the tracheal tube was changed to a metal tube

# Preparation for Tracheal Extubation

Phased occlusion method: On 12/8 and 13/8, intermittent occlusion, and on 14/8, start continuous occlusion

Extubation criteria: After the cuff of the metal tube is occluded, the patient's vital signs are stable, they can breathe autonomously, have little or no sputum, normal body temperature, cough reflex exists, and there is no severe airway obstruction.

21/8 Patient re-examination with bronchoscopy showed no abnormalities. Tracheal tube was removed, and the patient breathed smoothly through the nose, with sputum able to be expectorated through the mouth.

Consider Tube Removal

# Four, Nursing Experience

Gains

Shortcomings
        1. The patient is elderly, there are many nursing issues, cannot control the patient's condition from a global perspective, early intervention is not possible;
        2. Insufficient assessment of swallowing function, failed to provide timely and safer feeding guidance, accelerated patient's recovery.

        1. Learned knowledge of airway management, for patients with tracheostomy, nursing intervention is particularly important, which can effectively prevent complications;
