### Notes:
Perioperative hyperglycemia patients include the following categories:
1 Known diabetic patients
2 Undiagnosed diabetic patients
3 Stress hyperglycemia: Surgical stress can cause a sharp increase in blood glucose levels. When the body is subjected to strong stimulation, a fasting blood glucose level of ≥ 7.0 mmol/L or random blood glucose level of ≥ 11.1 mmol/L is considered stress hyperglycemia
4 Hyperglycemia caused by treatment: Factors such as the use of exogenous corticosteroids, pressors, total parenteral nutrition, and enteral nutrition in treatment can all lead to increased perioperative blood glucose levels in patients

### Notes:
Anaesthesia, metabolic stress, and the disease itself can act on the body, leading to decreased insulin secretion, increased insulin resistance, increased secretion of glucagon-like hormones, promoting gluconeogenesis, and inhibiting glycogen synthesis. Multiple factors acting together lead to significant increases in blood glucose levels.
Subsequently, the high blood glucose levels in patients can cause a decrease in the phagocytic ability and chemotaxis of leukocytes and phagocytes, affect endothelial cell function and hinder collagen synthesis, accumulate large amounts of lactic acid, and affect cell function; increased expression of adhesion molecules, decreased complement function, decreased vascular dilation function, and impaired NO generation.
Ultimately, this leads to increased inflammatory response in patients, increased susceptibility to secondary infection, and multi-organ dysfunction.

# Summary

Hyperglycemia in diabetic patients during the perioperative period is higher than that in non-diabetic patients, with about 40% of patients having postoperative blood glucose levels > 7.8 mmol/l. The highest proportion of hyperglycemia occurs during the surgical process, and the blood glucose level during the surgical process is significantly higher than that before and after surgery.
Abnormal blood glucose levels during the perioperative period are one of the causes of poor postoperative outcomes, with other common causes including multiple complications, multiple drug combinations, incorrect protocols for switching from intravenous insulin injection to subcutaneous injection, perioperative infection, and lack of diabetes knowledge among nursing staff.

# Conclusion
Perioperative hyperglycemia significantly affects patient clinical outcomes. Adverse outcomes associated with perioperative hyperglycemia reported in studies include: increased perioperative complications and hospital stay, increased mortality and morbidity, increased incidence of infection, and increased risk of cognitive dysfunction. Therefore, reasonable blood glucose control should be implemented during the perioperative period.

# Summary
Hyperglycemia in diabetic patients during the peri-operative period is higher than in non-diabetic patients, with about 40% of patients having post-operative blood glucose > 7.8mmol/l
Hyperglycemia during the peri-operative period significantly affects the clinical outcomes of patients, and both hypoglycemia and blood glucose fluctuations also have adverse effects on clinical outcomes
Guidelines suggest that blood glucose control during the peri-operative period should not be too strict, and insulin is commonly used to control blood glucose, while blood glucose changes should be closely monitored
The use of insulin has a positive impact on improving the clinical outcomes of post-operative patients
