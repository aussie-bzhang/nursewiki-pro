## Background
COPD has become the fourth leading cause of death and the fifth leading cause of disease burden globally. There is currently no effective treatment, and clinical management focuses on improving symptoms. Continuity of care refers to a series of nursing measures designed not only to provide care during hospitalization but also to extend these measures to the patient's transfer to another department and discharge, ensuring the continuation of standardized nursing care.

## Treatment Process
- 2017-11-05: Admitted to the Department of Internal Medicine due to recurrent cough and sputum for 3 years, worsened with dyspnea for over 10 days
- 2017-11-06: Due to left-sided chest tightness that did not relieve, chest DR showed left-sided pneumothorax, left lung compression 60%, transferred to the Department of Surgery for closed chest tube drainage
- 2017-11-07: Left lung reexpanded, symptoms of COPD and pulmonary infection did not improve, transferred back to the Department of Internal Medicine
- 2017-11-08: Due to pain at the left chest incision, cough and sputum symptoms worsened, dyspnea, tachycardia, low oxygen saturation, transferred to ICU for intensive care treatment
- 2017-11-12: Condition stable, chest tube removed, transferred back to the Department of Internal Medicine
- 2017-11-19: Improved, discharged

# Nursing Measures
| Nursing Diagnosis | 1. Knowledge Deficiency 2. Anxiety 3. Activity Intolerance |
| --- | --- |
| Goals | To promptly observe changes in the patient's condition, provide symptomatic treatment, alleviate symptoms of dyspnea and chest tightness, and maintain vital signs within the normal range |
| Nursing Measures (5/11-8/11) | 1. Joint rounds with medical staff to clarify the patient's current treatment focus, administer low-flow oxygen therapy as prescribed, monitor vital signs, complete relevant examinations, and provide symptomatic supportive treatment. 2. Bed rest, pay attention to position management, provide semi-recumbent position, develop a dietary plan to ensure daily intake of 1250 kcal, maintain room ventilation twice a day, 20 minutes each time; perform oral care and daily living care; 3. On 11-06 at 8:10, the patient's symptoms of chest tightness and shortness of breath were not relieved. At 8:50, the radiology department reported an emergency value, chest DR examination showed left-sided pneumothorax, left lung compression of 60%, urgent consultation with surgery department, at 11:05 transferred to surgery for left-sided chest tube insertion, hand over the progress of condition and health education, perform chest tube care to prevent tube compression, kinking, and disconnection, encourage deep breathing and blowing up balloons to promote lung re-expansion; chest DR examination on 11-7 showed that the left lung had re-expanded; chronic bronchitis, emphysema, and bilateral lower lung infection; transferred to the internal medicine department for further treatment. 4. Monitor body temperature changes regularly, accurately record intake and output, communicate with the doctor in a timely manner when the patient sweats a lot and drinks less, to ensure effective blood volume and maintain stable blood pressure; 5. On 11-8, the patient's condition deteriorated, transferred to ICU for treatment, APACHE score 17, after transfer, complete chest X-ray, blood gas analysis, etc., administer medication as prescribed, administer anti-infection drugs regularly, perform nebulization inhalation, mechanical sputum removal, observe the effects of medication and adverse reactions; |
| Effect Evaluation (8/11) | 1. The patient's chest tightness symptoms were relieved, no bubbles were seen in the chest tube, dyspnea was grade 3 2. No fever, paroxysmal cough, expectoration of yellowish white mucous sputum, large amount 3. The patient had poor appetite, ate less, and insufficient calories |

1. In the management of chronic diseases, the poor compliance of elderly patients with medical advice is closely related to the severity of the disease, the level of education, and the attention of family members. Nurses not only need to have professional knowledge but also good communication skills during the patient's treatment process. Any form of knowledge education must be based on good communication, and gaining the trust of the patient and family is the first step in individual nursing care.
2. Continuity of care can continue to follow up on issues that have not been resolved before discharge, reduce the number of hospital visits, and it is hoped that this model can be promoted to more nursing sisters. In addition, it is necessary to strengthen the seamless connection between patients and hospitals, and between patients and the community.
