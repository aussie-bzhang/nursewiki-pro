## What is a Medical Error in Intravenous Infusions?

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
## Concept

Intravenous infusion refers to the process of introducing sterile liquid medication from a fluid bag through an infusion tube and needle tip into the vein. If medical personnel violate the technical operation specifications for intravenous infusion and have medical errors in their work, but these errors have not resulted in patient death, disability, organ damage, or severe dysfunction, they can be identified as medical errors.

## 1. Not properly understanding the indications or contraindications for intravenous infusion.

Before intravenous infusion, it is necessary to recognize the indications and contraindications. The indications include: administering medication to treat diseases; supplementing nutrition and providing energy; correcting water and electrolyte imbalances and maintaining acid-base balance; increasing blood volume and maintaining blood pressure; and promoting diuresis.

The contraindications include: drugs or fluids that cannot be administered via the intravenous route. Intravenous catheters are suitable for patients who require long-term infusion. The soft material of the intravenous catheter does not cause harm to the blood vessels; it protects the blood vessels, reduces the damage to the blood vessels caused by repeated punctures, and alleviates the psychological distress of the patients; and it provides an effective treatment channel for emergency rescue.

## 2. Inadequate preparation before infusion

For patients: Urinate before infusion to avoid going to the bathroom during infusion;
For nurses: Environment, personal, and supplies

If the above preparations are not made and infusion is carried out in a hurry, resulting in adverse consequences, it can be identified as a medical error.

## 3. Not strictly implementing the check-and-double-check system.

Before intravenous infusion, the pre-infusion check should be done. Carefully check the medication, inspect the bottle for cracks, and check if the medication is cloudy, precipitated, or has絮状物. Also, carry the supplies to the bedside, check the bed number and name, explain to the patient to obtain cooperation.
Carefully implement the "three checks and seven matches" to avoid infusing into the wrong patient; carefully check the quality of the medication and liquid, and prohibit the administration of substandard liquids.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
## 4. Not following the special technical requirements for long-term infusion or infusion in comatose patients or children

According to the condition, infusion volume, age, and cooperation status, choose the venipuncture site. For patients who require long-term infusion, attention should be paid to the protection and rational use of veins.
For comatose patients or children who do not cooperate, scalp veins can be chosen for infusion.
When infusing in the limbs, appropriate restraints such as splints can be used.
For patients with peripheral circulatory collapse and difficult venipuncture in the limbs, jugular external veins, subclavian veins, and femoral veins can be used for infusion.

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
## 5. Not strictly implementing aseptic operation

Before injection, it is required to use 2% iodine tincture and 70% ethanol or only use 3% complex iodine to disinfect the bottle stopper, add medication according to medical orders, and disinfect the skin at the injection site. Strictly implement aseptic operation
For patients who require continuous infusion for 24 hours, change the infusion bottle and infusion set daily.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
## 6. Not adhering to operational standards, leading to air embolism

Before intravenous infusion, exhaust the air. Check that all connections of the infusion set are tightly connected and not easily dislodged; replace the solution bottle in time before the medication runs out; do not leave the patient when pressure infusion is performed; and remove the needle or seal the catheter in time after infusion.

# 8. Rapid Infusion

       Attention must be paid to controlling the infusion rate.
       The infusion rate should be adjusted according to the patient's age, condition, and the nature of the drug. Older patients, the frail, infants, and children, as well as patients with cardiovascular and pulmonary diseases, should have a slower infusion rate; the infusion rate for hypertonic saline, potassium-containing drugs, and pressor drugs can be slightly faster. For intracranial venous infusion, the rate should be adjusted according to the condition and age, generally not exceeding 20 drops per minute.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
# 9. Failure to Observe Compatibility

       Intravenous infusion should be arranged according to the patient's condition needs with a planned sequence. If additional drugs are required, attention should be given to compatibility, mix thoroughly, check clarity, and only use drugs with strong刺激性 if the needle is confirmed to be in the vein.

# 10. Failure to Fulfill Informative and Assistance Obligations

       During intravenous infusion, medical staff should assist the patient in positioning, avoid exposing the injection limb in winter to prevent cold. Instruct the patient to report immediately if there is a drop in solution, swelling at the injection site, or general discomfort, so that it can be addressed.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# 11. Failure to Make Records as Required

       During intravenous infusion, nurses should record the time and drop rate of the infused fluid on the infusion card and sign it, hanging the card on the infusion stand.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# 12. Failure to Use Venous Catheters as Directed

         When using venous catheters, the following should be noted:
       The fixation must be secure, avoiding too loose or too tight
       Protect the limb with the catheter
       Check the puncture site and venous course for redness, swelling, and ask the patient about pain and discomfort before and after each infusion.
       If there are any abnormalities, the catheter can be removed promptly for local treatment. For those who still need infusion, a new limb should be used and a new puncture performed.
