Overview
Pheochromocytoma is an endocrine disease characterized by the continuous or intermittent release of large amounts of catecholamines from adrenal medulla, sympathetic ganglia, and other chromaffin tissues, leading to persistent or paroxysmal hypertension and causing multiple organ dysfunction and metabolic disorders.
Adrenal medullary pheochromocytoma originates from the abnormal development of neural crest cells during embryonic development, so pheochromocytoma can occur not only within the adrenal glands but also in other parts of the body rich in ganglion cells. It is most common around the kidneys and adrenal glands, on the sides of the abdominal aorta and branches of visceral arteries, such as near the celiac trunk, at the bifurcation of the abdominal aorta (Zuckerhandle gland), and at the end of the ureter entering the bladder wall. In addition, it can also be occasionally seen in the thoracic cavity, mediastinum, and intracranial regions. Due to the abnormal development of neural crest cells during embryonic development, the formation of pheochromocytoma may also affect the development of other endocrine gland cells, leading to concurrent tumors. The thyroid, parathyroid, pancreas, and pituitary are the most susceptible endocrine glands.

Hypertension
                    The most common and main clinical manifestation of this disease is hypertension, which is often the main symptom for patients to seek medical attention. Blood pressure can increase persistently, but the more typical and diagnostic is paroxysmal hypertension.
　　 1. Paroxysmal hypertension This type has characteristic features  Patients usually have normal blood pressure, but during an attack, blood pressure rises abruptly and is accompanied by a special clinical syndrome. It often occurs in female patients and can be triggered by sudden changes in body position, sudden increase in abdominal pressure due to compression of the tumor (such as laughing, coughing, urination, defecation, lifting heavy objects, etc.), smoking, anxiety, or invasive procedures, and certain medications.
　　During an attack, systolic blood pressure can rise above 200mmHg, and diastolic pressure also increases accordingly. The duration of the attack is generally 15-30 minutes, but it can also last for several hours. It is often accompanied by tachycardia and arrhythmia, which are particularly evident at the beginning of the sudden rise in blood pressure. About 20% of patients may experience bradycardia due to reflex vagal nerve excitement. The attack is caused by the release of a large amount of catecholamines into the blood circulation. After the attack ends, blood pressure returns to the original level, and some patients may even experience hypotension or shock, feeling extremely exhausted and weak. The frequency of attacks can range from 1-2 times a year to frequent attacks within a day.
      2. Persistent hypertension is caused by persistent secretion of catecholamines  About 3/5 to 2/3 of patients fall into this type. Patients with persistent hypertension have fluctuations in blood pressure, but the amplitude of fluctuations is not large, and most patients do not feel this change themselves. The accompanying special clinical syndrome is also not obvious. A few patients may have very severe hypertension, reaching above 200/140mmHg.

Hypertension
       Patients can develop from paroxysmal hypertension to persistent hypertension, or start with persistent hypertension. Some patients may present with persistent hypertension accompanied by further increases in paroxysmal blood pressure. Such patients are prone to vascular complications such as retinal hemorrhage and papilledema.
　　   A considerable number of patients have a rapid progression of the disease, presenting as malignant hypertension. The course of the disease progresses rapidly, with severe hypertension changes and progressive damage to the heart, kidneys, and brain. Patients may ultimately suffer from stroke or myocardial infarction.
　　   About 3/4 of patients with paroxysmal hypertension attacks experience a triad of symptoms, including pulsatile headache, palpitations, and diaphoresis. This is caused by the sudden release of a large amount of catecholamines into the blood. Patients often have pale complexion, tremors, nausea, weakness, and an indescribable sensation of constriction, starting from the lower abdomen and ascending to the neck and chest, and finally to the head.
　　   Patients may also experience anxiety and fear, and after the attack ends, vagal nerve excitation symptoms may appear, such as flushing of the cheeks and skin, general fever, drooling, and pupillary constriction. Sometimes, they may be misdiagnosed as a neurological disorder. It has been confirmed that the specificity of diagnosing pheochromocytoma based on hypertension accompanied by headache, palpitations, and sweating is 93.8%, and the sensitivity is 90.9%.

Nervous System
The symptoms of the nervous system mainly manifest as psychiatric symptoms, panic, and extreme anxiety, and can also present with symptoms of intracerebral hemorrhage and cerebral embolism. A few patients may experience intellectual decline, which may be related to the long-term excitation of the cerebral cortex by adrenaline entering the reticular structure.

Local Symptoms
Some patients with pheochromocytoma may have a large tumor without symptoms such as hypertension, referred to as "functional" or "inactive" pheochromocytoma. These patients do not show hypertension or related clinical syndromes. A few patients may experience lumbar and abdominal pain. The pain is usually dull and mild, sometimes almost imperceptible, and easily overlooked by patients. When the tumor ruptures and bleeds internally, severe colicky pain may occur, presenting with an acute abdomen similar to renal colic. The mass is generally difficult to palpate, and when the tumor is very large, with a diameter >10cm, a mass can be palpated in the costal region.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->

Digestive System
Some patients with pheochromocytoma may experience gastrointestinal symptoms, which include:
    ① Abdominal distension and dyspepsia.
    ② Constipation.
    ③ Gastrointestinal bleeding and acute abdomen: Norepinephrine can cause proliferative or obstructive endarteritis within the gastrointestinal wall vessels, leading to intestinal necrosis, bleeding, and ulcers. After the ulcer ruptures and perforates, it can cause peritonitis and severe abdominal pain.
    ④ Cholelithiasis: Patients with pheochromocytoma have a higher incidence of cholelithiasis, which may be related to the weakened contraction of the gallbladder by catecholamines and the increased tone of the sphincter of the gallbladder duct, leading to bile stasis.

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->

Metabolic Disorders
Hypercatecholamineemia can cause various metabolic abnormalities:
     1. Disordered glucose metabolism can lead to elevated blood sugar, reduced glucose tolerance, and even diabetes. This is due to the fact that adrenaline can promote glycogenolysis.
     2. Increased basal metabolic rate Catecholamines can promote basal metabolism and negative nitrogen balance. The increase in the patient's basal metabolic rate is often more than 20%, presenting with a thinning constitution.
     3. Electrolyte disorders Some patients may have a low serum potassium concentration, which can be below 3.0mmol/L. It should be differentiated from primary hyperaldosteronism. And be alert to the possibility of MEN-Ⅱ and malignant pheochromocytoma.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->

Cardiovascular System
1. Hypotension, shock Pheochromocytoma patients can not only experience hypertension but can also develop hypotension, even shock. The reasons may be: ① Hypercatecholamineemia causes persistent and strong vasoconstriction of peripheral vessels, tissue ischemia and hypoxia, increased capillary permeability, and decreased plasma volume. When the action of catecholamines is removed, the vessels dilate, and due to insufficient blood volume, blood pressure decreases. ② Heart failure or severe arrhythmia leads to reduced cardiac output. ③ Some patients have tumors that mainly secrete adrenaline, which excites the β-adrenergic receptor, promoting vasodilation.
2. Arrhythmias Commonly include premature beats, paroxysmal tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, and some patients may occasionally experience bradycardia.
3. Acute myocardial infarction Hypercatecholamineemia can affect coronary circulation and lead to myocardial ischemia. When a paroxysmal hypertension attack occurs, a large amount of catecholamines enter the blood, causing persistent contraction of the coronary arteries, leading to acute myocardial infarction.
4. Heart failure Mainly表现为 acute left heart failure and pulmonary edema. The causes of heart failure are: ① Hypertension, increased cardiac load. ② Hypercatecholamine cardiomyopathy. ③ Obstruction of coronary circulation, myocardial ischemia, and even infarction.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->

Urogenital System
         The main manifestation is renal dysfunction. In patients with long-standing and severe disease, renal vascular damage may occur, and renal failure may occur in the late stage. There are reports of renal artery stenosis caused by renal artery compression by a renal artery paraganglioma. Patients with intravesical pheochromocytoma may experience hematuria, and hypertension attacks may be induced during urination.
Blood System
         A few pheochromocytomas can secrete a red blood cell stimulating factor, which is a protein or polypeptide. The patient's red blood cells can reach 8.0×10^12/L, and hemoglobin can be above 210g/L. Some patients may have an increased white blood cell count, reaching 10.0×10^9/L to 26.0×10^9/L. This is due to the action of a large amount of catecholamines, which causes the redistribution of white blood cells in the body, leading to an increased white blood cell count in peripheral blood in adrenal disease. The release of a large amount of catecholamines can cause fever, and the patient may experience headache, followed by shock and leukocytosis, which is easily misdiagnosed as septic shock. If vasoconstrictive pressor drugs are used for treatment, the condition may worsen further.

Diagnosis
Physical Examination
        During the intercritical period, patients have no obvious signs or only slight enlargement of the heart border, and a few can palpate an abdominal mass. During the attack, blood pressure increases, and symptoms such as diaphoresis, pale complexion, tachycardia, arrhythmias, and diplopia may occur.
Auxiliary Examinations
      (1) Blood and urine catecholamines and their metabolites such as vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) and metanephrine (MN) and normetanephrine (NMN) are significantly elevated, and the diagnosis is more meaningful when the total (TMN) is more than twice the normal high limit.
       (2) Pancreozymin stimulation test can be performed during the non-attack period, and phentolamine blocking test can be performed during the attack.
       (3) Ultrasound, CT, MRI, isotope-labeled metaiodobenzylguanidine scanning, and venous catheter segmental blood sampling can be used to determine the location of the tumor.
Differential Diagnosis
       It should be differentiated from angina, unstable essential hypertension, menopausal syndrome, hyperthyroidism, brain tumors with paroxysmal hypertension, tabes dorsalis, acute porphyria, lead poisoning, and other conditions.

How to Draw Three Hypertension Tests
 The three hypertension tests refer to:
     Determination of plasma renin activity, including renin, angiotensin, and aldosterone. When collecting, the special anticoagulant tube should be placed in ice water in advance, 4ml of venous blood should be collected and mixed, immediately placed in ice water to cool, and immediately sent for testing. The collection needs to be done twice, and the position should be noted, such as morning 5 o'clock supine, morning 8 o'clock standing.

Treatment
           Surgical resection of the tumor is the only effective treatment method. Without treatment, patients will die from the disease. 90% of pheochromocytomas are benign tumors, and the treatment effect of surgery is good, but the risk is high, and the mortality rate of surgery without preoperative preparation can reach 50%.

BLOOD PRESSURE CONTROL
   Aggressive oral nifedipine and Goutelin vasodilation treatment for 2 weeks. Due to the intermittent or continuous secretion of excessive catecholamines by pheochromocytoma, the drugs taken preoperatively may cause symptoms such as orthostatic hypotension, etc., and it is necessary to inform the patient of drug-related matters.
Correction of arrhythmia
   In patients with pheochromocytoma who have tachycardia or arrhythmia and still have the aforementioned conditions after using α-adrenergic receptor blockers, it is advisable to add β-adrenergic receptor blockers, such as atenolol (atenolol), metoprolol (metoprolol), and esmolol (esmolol), which have strong anti-arrhythmic effects and do not cause heart failure or asthma, making them significantly superior to the previously commonly used propranolol (propranolol).

**Volume Expansion**
Volume expansion is a crucial measure. Pheochromocytoma secretes excessive CA, causing peripheral vascular constriction and a reduction in vascular bed volume, leading to absolute hypovolemia. Once the tumor is removed, CA levels decrease, vascular beds open, and volume insufficiency becomes the primary issue. Before surgery, under controlled blood pressure, preloading with a certain volume of blood is recommended, supplemented by intraoperative volume expansion. This not only ensures stable blood pressure during surgery but also prevents complications such as heart failure and pulmonary edema that may occur due to rapid and massive volume expansion due to insufficient blood volume.

<!-- Slide number: 30 -->

**Close Observation of Vital Signs**
High levels of CA can cause significant myocardial damage, leading to catecholamine cardiomyopathy, which should be given high attention. Clinical manifestations may include severe arrhythmias, heart failure, myocardial infarction, with a very high mortality rate. However, this cardiomyopathy can usually be reversed with α-receptor blockers and heart protective treatment. Such patients should be prepared for at least half a year before surgery, waiting for myocardial damage to recover to a better state before undergoing surgical treatment.
**Improving General Condition**
This includes correcting electrolyte imbalances, adjusting blood glucose levels, and preoperative psychological preparation.
Pheochromocytoma patients should not be administered atropine before surgery to avoid tachycardia.

**Vital Signs**
After tumor resection, the patient's blood pressure is very unstable. After lying flat for 6 hours, the patient can assume a semi-recumbent position, minimizing changes in position. Routine oxygen therapy is provided, and a multi-function electrocardiogram monitor is used for 24-hour electrocardiogram monitoring, monitoring blood pressure and heart rate, ensuring venous patency, and recording 24-hour intake and output.
The main risks after tumor resection are heart failure and hypotension. Routine blood pressure monitoring should be performed. Due to the relative deficiency of plasma catecholamines after surgery, the vessels dilate due to reduced tension, the vascular volume expands, and blood volume becomes relatively insufficient, resulting in reduced return blood volume and cardiac output, which is prone to hypotension or hypovolemic shock, and the occurrence of tachycardia.

<!-- Slide number: 33 -->
**Observation of Hypovolemic Shock**
After pheochromocytoma resection, the abrupt decrease in catecholamines in the blood circulation causes the long-term contracted vessels to open. Although preoperative volume expansion treatment has been performed, some patients still experience insufficient blood volume after surgery. Patients may experience varying degrees of blood pressure drop and oliguria, which are corrected through blood transfusion, fluid administration, and symptomatic treatment. Therefore, it is necessary to: 1) Maintain patency of two venous catheters, one for adjusting the drug with a micro-pump to control blood pressure and the other for replenishing blood volume and monitoring circulatory function. 2) Accurately record 24-hour urine output, providing reference for fluid administration for the physician. Pay attention to electrolyte imbalances and detect various biochemical indicators as per medical advice.

<!-- Slide number: 34 -->
**Adrenal Crisis**
After bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma resection, there may be varying degrees of ischemic injury to the adrenal cortex, leading to adrenal insufficiency and adrenal crisis. The main manifestations are postoperative blood pressure drop, limb pain, abdominal pain, and even drowsiness.

**Adrenal Cortex Function Insufficiency**
Adrenal cortex function insufficiency mainly manifests as a series of symptoms caused by a lack of adrenal cortical hormones, with both chronic and acute forms. When patients exhibit the following symptoms, it indicates acute adrenal cortex insufficiency: 1) Severe gastrointestinal symptoms such as anorexia, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain; 2) Neurological and psychiatric symptoms such as irritability, apathy, and confusion; 3) Blood pressure drop; 4) Severe dehydration; 5) Hypoglycemia. Laboratory tests show decreased blood sodium levels and increased blood potassium levels. Prompt and adequate supplementation of corticosteroids can improve the condition.

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->
**Hypoglycemia**
After tumor resection, the previously suppressed insulin is released in large quantities, which can cause hypoglycemia. In clinical practice, patients may experience symptoms of hypoglycemia such as dizziness, palpitations, and generalized weakness, as well as persistent hypotension. The hypotension caused by hypoglycemia is insensitive to drug and blood volume supplementation.
**Wound Condition**
Strengthen rounds, inform the doctor of any leakage and change dressings as needed, guide patients in effective coughing techniques, protect the wound from laceration, promptly assess pain, and provide analgesia.

<!-- Slide number: 36 -->
**Catheterization Status**
Observe the external scale of the deep venous catheter, whether it is patent, whether the drainage ball at the right lumbar incision is in a negative pressure state, and whether the catheter is patent. Provide double fixation to allow for turning, strengthen rounds, prevent infection and cross-infection.
**Reaction to Medication**
Closely observe patients for the absence of transfusion reactions after transfusion of leukocyte-reduced red blood cell concentrate, and for the absence of phlebitis symptoms after transfusion of amino acids and other high-molecular-weight drugs.

<!-- Slide number: 37 -->
**Skin Condition**
Strengthen skin care, turn frequently, clean frequently, change close-fitting clothing and bedding frequently, and pay attention to the skin of the sacrum and coccyx, as well as the coldness of the hands and feet.
**Postoperative Laboratory Reports**
On the first postoperative day, the blood routine showed white blood cells 10.3*10^9/L, neutrophils 7.6*10^9/L, red blood cells 4.71*10^12/L, and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein rapid quantification in whole blood 24.79mg/L. Due to elevated blood count and C-reactive protein, cefuroxime sodium 2.25g twice daily was continued for anti-inflammatory treatment. On the fifth postoperative day, the blood count was normal.

**Psychological Nursing**
- Strengthen communication with the patient, introduce successful surgical cases, patiently listen to the patient's complaints, alleviate tension, and help the patient build confidence in overcoming the disease.
**Wound Care**
- Increase rounds, inform the doctor of any leakage or other issues promptly for dressing changes, instruct the patient on effective coughing techniques to protect the wound from splitting, conduct timely pain assessments, and provide pain relief.
**Intravenous Fluid Nursing**

- Adjust the infusion rate according to the actual condition of the disease, and provide proper care for the insertion of central and peripheral venous catheters.

<!-- Slide number: 41 -->
**Drainage Tube Nursing**
- Ensure the patency of all tubes, apply double fixation, leave room for turning, increase rounds, prevent infection and cross-infection.
**Activity Guidance**
- Due to long-term metabolic disorder leading to weakened physical condition, in addition to general nursing care, guidance should be provided for daily living. For example, the area where the tumor is growing should be avoided from being touched or collided with, especially during sleep at night, protective measures should be taken, and body positions should be changed slowly to reduce the chance of sudden blood pressure rise. Generally, patients should rest in bed for about a week after surgery, and after a week, bed-side activities can be appropriately assisted based on the patient's actual condition.

<!-- Slide number: 42 -->
**Safety Nursing**
- Before surgery, fully introduce the patient to the ward, such as the use of bed lights and call bells, how to use bedrails, and promptly perform four high-risk assessments after surgery, implement relevant nursing measures based on the results of the assessment, and inform the patient accordingly.
**Dietary Guidance**
- Consume light diet three days before surgery, fast after surgery, and consume liquid diet such as congee or noodles after gastrointestinal motility recovery, eat in small portions and more frequently, avoid sweet foods, and after three days post-surgery, consume more fresh vegetables and fruits to maintain bowel regularity.

<!-- Slide number: 43 -->

**Complications**
1. Adrenal Crisis - Surgery may cause varying degrees of ischemia and injury to the adrenal cortex, or the normal adrenal tissue and tumor may be removed together during surgery, leading to adrenal insufficiency and subsequent adrenal crisis. The main manifestations are fever (can reach 40℃), nausea, vomiting, tachycardia, up to 160 beats per minute, and hypotension. It is necessary to strengthen monitoring for 48 hours after surgery, use corticosteroids and salt corticosteroids in a timely manner, and correct dehydration and electrolyte disorders.
2. Pulmonary Infection - Oxygen inhalation, when bed rest is required, perform deep breathing on the bed, turn and pat the back every 2 hours, and perform appropriate bed activities.

<!-- Slide number: 44 -->

**Complications**
3. Urinary Tract Infection - Perform perineal care with 2% chlorhexidine twice a day, change the drainage bag on Mondays and Thursdays, maintain cleanliness of the perineum, drink more water after gastrointestinal motility recovery to achieve natural flushing.
4. Intravascular Infection - The peripheral venous catheter should be retained for a maximum of 72 hours, and the central venous catheter should be removed within two weeks if the condition allows. Flush the catheter with heparin sodium before and after infusion, change the dressing every other day, disinfect the local skin, and replace the dressing to prevent intravascular infection. If local swelling and leakage are found, the catheter should be removed immediately.
5. Intra-abdominal Infection - Increase rounds, observe the amount of drainage from the negative pressure drainage ball, pour the drainage fluid in a sterile manner when necessary, and use a hemostat to clamp the drainage tube before pouring to prevent intra-abdominal infection.

<!-- Slide number: 45 -->

**Complications**
6. Hypovolemic Shock - Sufficient preoperative preparation is crucial to ensure the key to tumor resection. After tumor resection, the catecholamines in the blood急剧 decrease, although preoperative preparation has been made, it is still possible to have insufficient circulatory blood volume after surgery. When the patient shows varying degrees of hypotension and decreased urine output, immediate warming, blood transfusion, and rapid fluid infusion should be provided to raise the blood pressure.
7. Prevention of Decubitus Ulcers and Lower Extremity Venous Thrombosis - Due to the need for a longer period of bed rest after surgery, guidance should be provided for relevant bed activities based on the patient's actual condition. Encourage the patient to turn every 2 hours and massage the lower limbs appropriately, change the bedding and patient clothing in a timely manner to maintain cleanliness, and if the patient shows lower limb swelling and pain, immediate immobilization should be provided to prevent embolism.

**Discharge Instructions**

Self-observation: A small number of patients may still have high blood pressure, and attention should be paid to changes in blood pressure, and medical treatment should be sought promptly if the blood pressure is unstable.
Adhere to Medication: Patients who require adrenal hormone replacement therapy after surgery should follow the doctor's instructions for medication, gradually reduce the dose based on the recovery of adrenal function, and do not adjust the dose, discontinue medication, or miss doses on their own.
Regular Follow-up: Regularly follow up on blood and urine catecholamine levels after surgery to understand the changes in the condition.
Establish Good Living Habits: Reasonable diet, consume high-protein, high-vitamin, and high-calorie foods, eat more vegetables and fruits, and maintain bowel regularity.
