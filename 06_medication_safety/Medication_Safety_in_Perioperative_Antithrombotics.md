Is it necessary to discontinue antithrombotic treatment preoperatively?

   For patients requiring major surgery or large invasive procedures, discontinuing antithrombotic treatment can reduce the risk of perioperative bleeding. Continuing VKA or aspirin treatment during the perioperative period increases the risk of bleeding.

   For patients undergoing minor surgical procedures (such as dental, dermatological, or cataract surgery), it is recommended to continue antithrombotic treatment and add hemostatic agents as needed.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
If antithrombotic treatment is discontinued preoperatively, is bridging anticoagulation necessary?

Whether to perform bridging anticoagulation depends mainly on the patient's risk of thromboembolism:

   High-risk patients, the benefit of preventing thromboembolism far outweighs the risk of bleeding from anticoagulation.
   Moderate-risk patients, the benefits and risks of bridging anticoagulation should be weighed based on the patient's individual condition and the nature of the surgery.
   Low-risk patients, bridging anticoagulation may not be necessary.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
Thromboembolic Risk Stratification: VTE Patients
High Risk
   VTE occurring within 3 months
   Severe hypercoagulability (e.g., antiphospholipid antibody syndrome)
Moderate Risk
   VTE occurring between 3-12 months
   Non-severe hypercoagulability (e.g., factor V variant heterozygote)
   Recurrent VTE
   Active tumor (treated within the past 6 months or palliative treatment)
Low Risk
   VTE occurring more than 12 months ago with no other risk factors

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Thromboembolic Risk Stratification: Atrial Fibrillation Patients
High Risk
   CHADS2 score 5-6
   Stroke or TIA within the past 3 months
   Rheumatic heart valve disease
Moderate Risk
   CHADS2 score 3-4
Low Risk
   CHADS2 score 0-2 with no history of stroke or TIA
* CHADS2 score: Congestive heart failure, hypertension, age ≥75, diabetes, past history of stroke or TIA (except the last item is 2 points, the rest are 1 point)

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
Thromboembolic Risk Stratification: Mechanical Heart Valve Patients
High Risk
   Any mitral valve prosthesis
   Older-generation aortic valve prostheses (caged-ball or tilting disc)
   Stroke or TIA within the past 6 months
Moderate Risk
   Bileaflet aortic valve + one of the following: atrial fibrillation, history of stroke or TIA, hypertension, diabetes, congestive heart failure, age >75 years
Low Risk
   Bileaflet aortic valve without atrial fibrillation or other stroke risk factors

Bridging (Transitional Anticoagulant) Therapy
   For patients with basic recovery of hemostasis, warfarin can be continued 12-24 hours postoperatively. If there is a high risk of bleeding, LMWH treatment can be initiated 48-72 hours postoperatively, and warfarin can be discontinued after INR reaches the target.
   Warfarin should be discontinued 5 days before surgery, and INR should be adjusted to normal. If INR is >1.5 1-2 days before surgery, vitamin K 1-2mg can be taken orally.
   Surgery (for high-risk patients for TE)
   For general subcutaneous tissue surgery and interventional treatments, as well as patients with low to moderate risk of TE, bridging therapy is not required (VKA can also be discontinued 2-3 days before surgery or warfarin can be reduced, and INR adjusted to 1.3-1.5).
   During the period of warfarin discontinuation, UFH or LMWH anticoagulation is used. LMWH should be discontinued 24 hours before surgery, and UFH 4-6 hours before surgery.
   * The half-life of warfarin is 36-72 hours, and anticoagulant activity is essentially cleared after at least 5 days of discontinuation, with a low risk of bleeding when INR < 1.5; the half-life of UFH is 60-90 minutes, and anticoagulant activity is maintained for 3-4 hours; the half-life of LMWH is 4 hours.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
Perioperative Management of Antithrombotic Therapy
For patients receiving aspirin treatment with moderate to high risk of cardiovascular events, it is recommended to continue aspirin use for non-cardiac surgery (such as dental, dermatological, cataract surgery, coronary artery bypass grafting).
Aspirin and clopidogrel at maintenance doses can be restarted 24 hours postoperatively.

Surgery
   For patients with low-risk cardiovascular events undergoing non-cardiac surgery, or certain surgeries with high bleeding risk (such as spinal or hip joint surgery), it is recommended to discontinue aspirin 7-10 days before surgery. For patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting with dual antiplatelet therapy, clopidogrel should be discontinued 5 days before surgery.
   For patients who require surgery within 6 weeks after bare stent implantation or within 6 months after drug-eluting stent implantation, perioperative dual antiplatelet therapy is recommended.
   * Antithrombotic drugs irreversibly inhibit platelet function, and platelet function recovers by 10-14% per day after discontinuation, with complete recovery requiring 7-10 days.

Management of Novel Oral Anticoagulants (NOACs)
These drugs have a rapid onset of action and a rapid resolution of effect after discontinuation, and bridging anticoagulation may not be necessary.
   Half-life: Dabigatran 12-14 hours, Rivaroxaban 7-11 hours, Apixaban 12 hours.

Rivaroxaban: Renal function normal: 1 day; creatinine clearance 60-90 ml/min: 2 days; creatinine clearance 30-60 ml/min: 3 days; creatinine clearance 15-30 ml/min: 5 days; postoperative use can be resumed 1-2 days after surgery.
Bleeding complications: Mild: discontinue medication and wait for the drug to be cleared by itself; Severe: consider methods such as activated charcoal adsorption, dialysis, and transfusion of coagulation factors for reversal.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
VTE Prevention in Orthopedic Patients
For orthopedic major surgeries (THA, TKA, HFS), the choice of LWMH for thromboprophylaxis is superior to that of fondaparinux, unfractionated heparin, apixaban, dabigatran, and rivaroxaban, as well as warfarin and aspirin. The duration should be at least 10-14 days.

It is recommended to start prophylaxis at least 12 hours before surgery or 12 hours after surgery.

For orthopedic major surgery patients after discharge, it is recommended to extend the thromboprophylaxis duration to 35 days. (2 B)

For orthopedic major surgery patients, it is recommended to use anticoagulant drugs and compression therapy in combination to prevent VTE during hospitalization. If the risk of bleeding increases, it is recommended to use intermittent pneumatic compression devices for prevention or not to prevent. (2 C)

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
Case 2
A 54-year-old male with mechanical mitral valve replacement, long-term oral warfarin, preparing for total hip arthroplasty.

Question: When should warfarin be discontinued? Is bridging anticoagulation necessary?

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
Plan:

Discontinue VKA 5 days before surgery to transition to LMWH anticoagulation with therapeutic doses;
The last use of LMWH 24 hours before surgery;
Resume warfarin use 12-24 hours after surgery;
Resume LMWH 48-72 hours after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
Case 3
A 69-year-old male with chronic atrial fibrillation, hypertension, CHADS2 score = 1, long-term oral warfarin, preparing for radical gastrectomy.

Question: When should warfarin be discontinued? Is bridging anticoagulation necessary?

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Plan:

Discontinue VKA 5 days before surgery;
No bridging anticoagulation is necessary;
Resume VKA use 12-24 hours after surgery.

Note: After VKA discontinuation, INR recovery to normal generally requires 5 days, so LMWH bridging is not necessary; if the patient was previously using LMWH for VTE prevention, LMWH should be discontinued 24 hours before surgery.

Case 4
A 78-year-old female with a non-ST elevation myocardial infarction 2 years ago, preparing for inguinal hernia repair. Currently taking aspirin (ASA) 100mg qd.

Other cardiovascular risk factors: hypertension (170/92mmHg), hyperlipidemia, type 2 diabetes.

Question: Should ASA be discontinued? Is bridging anticoagulation necessary?

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
Plan:

Continue using ASA during the perioperative period;
No bridging anticoagulation is necessary.
