Basic Prophylaxis Measures
Operations around the venous walls of the limbs or pelvis should be gentle and precise to avoid injury to the venous endothelium.
When elevating the limbs postoperatively, do not place a pillow under the popliteal fossa or lower part of the calf alone, as this may affect the deep venous return of the calf.
Encourage patients to start frequent active movements of the feet and toes as soon as possible, and to perform deep breathing and coughing exercises.
Attempt to mobilize out of bed as early as possible, and wear graduated compression stockings on the lower limbs.

Mechanical Prophylaxis Measures
Including foot pump, intermittent pneumatic compression device, and graduated compression elastic stockings.
The efficacy of anticoagulant drugs is superior to non-pharmacological prophylaxis measures.
Used only in patients with high bleeding risk factors, or combined with anticoagulant drugs to enhance efficacy.

Pharmacological Prophylaxis Measures
Currently, there are three methods (choose one):
Subcutaneous administration of conventional dose low molecular weight heparin 12 hours before surgery or 12-24 hours after surgery (2-4 hours after epidural catheter removal), or starting with half the conventional dose 4-6 hours after surgery and increasing to the conventional dose the next day.
Fondaparinux: 2.5mg, started 6-8 hours after surgery (not yet available in China).
Start vitamin K antagonists on the evening before surgery or the night after surgery, with the dosage to be monitored, maintaining INR between 2.0 and 2.5, not exceeding 3.0.
The duration of any anticoagulation method should generally be not less than 7-10 days.

Pharmacological Prophylaxis Precautions
The combination of these drugs increases the risk of bleeding complications, and therefore, combined use is not recommended.
It is not recommended to use low-dose unfractionated heparin, aspirin, dextran, graduated compression elastic stockings, or foot pump prophylactically, nor is it recommended to prophylactically insert an inferior vena cava filter.
Vitamin K antagonists (INR 2.0-3.0) can also effectively prevent VTE, but the risk of bleeding is higher.
If the surgery is delayed, it is recommended to use low molecular weight heparin for thromboprophylaxis from the date of admission to the time of surgery. If anticoagulation medication has been used preoperatively, it is advisable to avoid epidural anesthesia. If a patient has a high bleeding risk and is contraindicated to anticoagulation, mechanical prophylaxis measures should be used. The duration of postoperative medication should be not less than 7-10 days.
It is recommended to weigh the anticoagulant efficacy and bleeding risk when deciding the timing of starting medication.
For patients undergoing orthopedic major surgery, anticoagulant treatment is generally stopped at discharge.
The coagulation pathway remains activated for up to 4 weeks after total hip arthroplasty, and the risk of VTE can last up to 3 months. The prophylactic duration for high-risk DVT patients after total hip arthroplasty and hip fracture surgery should be extended to 28-35 days.

Pharmacological Prophylaxis Precautions
Before taking various prophylactic and treatment measures, refer to the manufacturer's instructions or product information for drugs and medical devices.
For patients at high risk of DVT, a comprehensive approach using basic, mechanical, and pharmacological prophylaxis should be adopted. For patients with high bleeding risk, drug prophylaxis measures should be used with caution, with mechanical prophylaxis measures as the main method, supplemented by basic prophylaxis measures.
It is not recommended to use aspirin alone for DVT prophylaxis.
When determining the dosage of low molecular weight heparin, vitamin K antagonists, dextran, and other drugs, consider the patient's liver, kidney function, and platelet count.
If severe bleeding tendency occurs after the use of anticoagulant drugs, appropriate examinations should be performed according to the specific situation, and timely treatment should be provided.
Percutaneous epidural hematoma is rare but has serious consequences. Therefore, care should be taken or anticoagulants should be avoided in the short period after spinal canal procedures (such as surgery, puncture, etc.). The puncture or catheter should be performed before medication; the catheter or needle should be removed 2 hours before the next dose; and low molecular weight heparin should be administered 2 hours or more after the catheter or needle is removed.
The contraindications for low molecular weight heparin are thrombocytopenia and severe coagulation disorders.

| After bleeding risk reduction, if there is still a high VTE risk, it is recommended to replace mechanical prophylaxis with pharmacological prophylaxis |
For medical inpatients initiating VTE prophylaxis, it is recommended that VTE prophylaxis be performed only when immobilized or hospitalized.
Note: Critically ill patients
ACCP9 indicates that there is currently no VTE risk assessment model for critically ill patients.
For VTE prevention recommendations for critically ill patients:
1. Routine Doppler ultrasound screening for DVT is not recommended
2. Low molecular weight heparin or LDUH is recommended for VTE prevention
3. For patients with bleeding or high bleeding risk, mechanical prophylaxis: IPC or GCS. After bleeding risk reduction, it is recommended to replace mechanical prophylaxis with pharmacological prophylaxis

# Principles for Managing Patients on Long-term Oral Vitamin K Antagonist (Warfarin) Therapy Before Surgery
For patients on oral vitamin K antagonist therapy, if normal coagulation function is required during surgery, it is recommended to discontinue the medication 5 days in advance. Resume the medication 12-24 hours after surgery. If INR is still prolonged 1-2 days before surgery, oral small-dose vitamin K (1-2 mg) can be administered.
For high-risk patients with atrial fibrillation, artificial mechanical heart valves, artificial biological valve replacement, or those who have undergone mitral valvuloplasty within 3 months or have a history of venous thromboembolism, during the period of vitamin K antagonist discontinuation, it is recommended to administer subcutaneous low molecular weight heparin or intravenous unfractionated heparin as transitional therapy. Subcutaneous low molecular weight heparin is preferred. For moderate-risk patients, it is recommended to administer therapeutic doses of subcutaneous low molecular weight heparin or intravenous unfractionated heparin or low-dose low molecular weight heparin. For low-risk patients, only subcutaneous low-dose low molecular weight heparin or no transitional therapy is recommended.
For patients receiving therapeutic doses of low molecular weight heparin, the last preoperative injection should be only half the dose and should be administered 24 hours before surgery; for patients receiving therapeutic doses of unfractionated heparin, the last preoperative injection should be 4 hours before surgery. Continue to apply therapeutic doses of low molecular weight heparin or unfractionated heparin for 1-2 days postoperatively, or until INR reaches the therapeutic range.
For patients receiving transitional therapy, vitamin K antagonists can be resumed 12-24 hours after minor surgery; for patients with major surgery and high bleeding risk, the time for administering low molecular weight heparin or unfractionated heparin can be delayed to 72 hours or until the patient's coagulation status is stable.
Therapeutic dose: ① Low molecular weight heparin: 100 IU/kg, twice daily, or 200 IU/kg, three times daily; Enoxaparin (Clexane) 1 mg/kg, twice daily, or 1.5 mg/kg, three times daily. ② Heparin: Extend aPTT to 1.5-2.0 times the normal value.
Preventive dose: ① Low molecular weight heparin: 5000 IU, once daily; Enoxaparin 30 mg, twice daily or 40 mg, once daily. ② Heparin: 5000 IU, twice daily.

# Principles for Managing Patients on Preoperative Antiplatelet Drug Therapy
In general, for elective surgical patients, if aspirin or clopidogrel is taken before surgery, it is recommended to discontinue at least 5 days, preferably 10 days; if there are no obvious signs of bleeding postoperatively, the medication can be resumed 24 hours after surgery.
For patients at high risk of thrombotic events, it is recommended to continue aspirin until surgery; for clopidogrel users, it is recommended to discontinue at least 5 days, preferably 10 days.
For patients with coronary artery placement of bare metal stents, it is recommended to schedule elective surgery 6 weeks after stent placement, while continuing aspirin. For patients with drug-eluting stents, it is recommended to schedule elective surgery 6-12 months after stent placement, while continuing aspirin. If an urgent operation is required within 6 months after drug-eluting stent placement, it is recommended to continue aspirin and clopidogrel perioperatively; for severe bleeding, platelet transfusion or other hemostatic drugs (such as antifibrinolytic drugs, recombinant coagulation factor VII) can be administered. Whether intravenous administration of tirofiban can be used as a transitional prophylactic measure if clopidogrel needs to be discontinued still requires research. It is not recommended to use heparin or low molecular weight heparin to replace aspirin and clopidogrel for the prevention of subacute thrombosis within drug-eluting stents.
For patients with coronary artery stents who no longer need dual antiplatelet therapy, transitional therapy is not recommended.

# Emergency Management of Emergency Surgery
Routine coagulation function tests should be performed before surgery, generally INR < 1.5, most surgeries can be performed safely without special treatment.
For patients on oral clopidogrel or other drugs before surgery, if emergency surgery is required or massive bleeding occurs, platelet transfusion or other hemostatic drugs (such as antifibrinolytic drugs, recombinant coagulation factor) can be administered.
For patients on oral warfarin or other drugs before surgery, if emergency surgery is required and INR is significantly prolonged, fresh frozen plasma (5-8 mL/kg) or coagulation factor complex (factors II, VII, IX, and X concentrates, or factors II, IX, and X concentrates and factor VII concentrates) (50 U/kg) can be administered.
For patients on combined antiplatelet drugs such as aspirin and clopidogrel, platelet dynamic function (thrombelastography) and static function (platelet aggregation) can be measured. However, it needs to be emphasized that the test results are only for clinical reference and not as a basis for surgical decision-making.
Surgeons should carefully inquire about the patient's medical history and perform a physical examination before surgery to understand the patient's platelet and coagulation function, such as whether there is bleeding when brushing teeth, whether there are subcutaneous ecchymoses, and whether it is easier to stop bleeding after preoperative blood collection.
For special patients, in cases where antiplatelet therapy cannot be discontinued for a long time, it is recommended to prioritize tirofiban, which has a rapid onset of action, reaches 96% inhibition of platelet function within 5 minutes after administration; its half-life is short, only 2 hours. Platelet function can recover to 89% of the baseline value 2-4 hours after discontinuation, and bleeding time returns to normal. The administration method is to dissolve 50 mg of tirofiban in 100 mL of 0.9% saline or 5% glucose, with an initial loading dose of 0.4 μg/(kg·min) for the first 30 minutes, and then maintained at a rate of 1 μg/(kg·min).
