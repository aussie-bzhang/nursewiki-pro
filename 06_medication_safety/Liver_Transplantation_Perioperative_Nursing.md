# Postoperative Nursing for Liver Transplant Patients
Includes intensive care nursing and general ward nursing.

The purpose of intensive care nursing:
1. Maintain vital signs and the stability of the internal environment.
2. Monitor the overall condition of the patient and the function of the graft.
3. Detect and prevent postoperative complications.

1. Vital signs.
2. Mechanical ventilation.
3. Venous access and medication use.
4. Monitoring of drainage tubes.

# SAS (Sedation-Agitation Scale)
7th level (extremely dangerous agitation): Self-removal of tubes, overstepping/knocking on the bed rail, completely uncooperative.
6th level (very agitated): Repeated persuasion ineffective, requires restraint, biting on tracheal intubation.
5th level (agitated): Anxiety, trying to sit up, can remain quiet after persuasion.
4th level (quiet and cooperative): Quiet, easily aroused, can follow instructions to move.
3rd level (sedated): Shouting and slight shaking can arouse, but immediately fall asleep, can follow simple instructions.
2nd level (over-sedated): Strong stimulation can arouse, but cannot communicate or follow instructions, can move autonomously.
1st level (unarousable): No or only slight response to sound stimulation, cannot communicate or follow instructions.

# Postoperative Nursing
(Three) Temperature Monitoring

Due to prolonged exposure during surgery, large-volume fluid infusion, and low temperature perfusion of the donor liver, the patient's body temperature should be rewarmed if it is less than 35°C upon admission, so that the patient's body temperature rises to 36°C-38°C within 2-4 hours.

# Postoperative Nursing
(Four) Monitoring of the Circulatory System
Preventive points:
1. Control CVP < 10 cmH2O. (CVVH) / (CVVHD) treatment.
2. Antidysrhythmic therapy, maintain sinus rhythm.
3. On the basis of ensuring organ-level perfusion, control the patient's blood pressure at 140/80 mmHg or below.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# Postoperative Nursing
(Five) Monitoring of Respiratory Function
Preventive points:
1. Choose ventilation mode as much as possible, retain spontaneous breathing.
2. Detect and remove factors affecting extubation.
3. Strict airway management if delayed extubation is required.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Factors Affecting Extubation
Including:
Disorders of consciousness, circulatory dysfunction, increased abdominal pressure, diaphragmatic movement dysfunction, incomplete lung expansion, pain, weakness of respiratory muscles, etc.
Prolonged mechanical ventilation itself constitutes a high-risk factor for pulmonary infection.

# Postoperative Nursing
(Five) Renal Function Monitoring
AFR: Intraoperative hypovolemia and hypotension due to any cause are the most important pathogenic and risk factors, ultimately leading to renal failure.

Standards
| Preoperative normal renal function: Postoperative serum creatinine ≥132umolc/l and/or blood urea nitrogen ≥18mmol/l. |
| --- |
| Preoperative renal dysfunction: Postoperative serum creatinine and/or blood urea nitrogen increased by more than 50%. |

# Renal Function Grading
Based on creatinine clearance

Normal: Clearance >70ml/min (80~120ml/min).
Mild: Clearance 40—69.9ml/min.
Moderate: Clearance 20—39.9ml/min.
Severe: Clearance <20ml/min.

# Key Points for Renal Function Monitoring Prevention
1. Maintain mean arterial pressure (MAP) >65mmHg. Avoid dehydration.
2. Follow the principles of combined use of immunosuppressants and monitor their concentrations. CSA, FK506 should be administered orally.
3. Monitor and control systemic infection.
4. It is not recommended to use high-dose diuretics in oliguric patients. "Renal dose" dopamine has no protective effect on renal function and does not reduce the need for dialysis.
5. Maintain urine output >2ml/kg.h in the early postoperative period.

# Dopamine Dose Application
Low dose: <5ug/kg.min, has a mild vasodilatory effect.
Medium dose: 5-10ug/kg.min, increases myocardial contractility and heart rate.
High dose: 10-20ug/kg.min, has a significant vasodilatory effect.

# Postoperative Nursing
(Six) Coagulation Function Monitoring

| POSTOPERATIVE BLEEDING | Various factors such as coagulation factor deficiency, abnormal platelet count and quality, increased endogenous anticoagulant substances, and hyperfibrinolysis often occur during surgery. |
| --- |
| HYPERTHROMBOSIS | Large amounts of procoagulant substances, improved splenomegaly or splenectomy, recovery of liver function after transplantation, antithrombin III deficiency leading to heparin resistance, and the occurrence of hypercoagulability. |

# Key Points for Coagulation Function Monitoring Prevention
1. Coagulation function is poor in the early postoperative period. If there is no obvious clinical tendency of bleeding, coagulation substances can be temporarily omitted, and invasive procedures should be avoided at the same time.
2. Heparin anticoagulation therapy helps prevent vascular complications after liver transplantation and other thrombotic diseases.
3. Monitor infection and clinical medication.

# Postoperative Nursing
(Seven) Gastrointestinal Function Monitoring
Common symptoms: Gastrointestinal bleeding, gastric retention, bloating, diarrhea.
Causes of diarrhea?
In addition to infection factors, the use of lactulose and ACEI drugs can also cause diarrhea. Mycophenolate mofetil and sirolimus are more likely to cause diarrhea than CSA and FK506.

# Key Points for Gastrointestinal Monitoring Prevention
1. Use acid inhibitors to prevent and treat gastrointestinal bleeding postoperatively.
2. Monitor the blood drug concentration of immunosuppressants.
3. Provide enteral nutrition as soon as possible, control blood glucose <6mmol/l.
4. Avoid long-term broad-spectrum antibiotics leading to intestinal flora imbalance and disorder.
