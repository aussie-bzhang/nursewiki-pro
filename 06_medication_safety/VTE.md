# VTE, Venous Thromboembolism

VTE is the third most common cardiovascular disease in the United States, following ischemic heart disease and stroke1. DVT and PE are different manifestations of VTE at different locations and stages.

Thrombus
Migration
Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
Approximately 80% of PE cases have DVT2

Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)
Approximately 50% of proximal DVT cases are complicated by PE2
1. Carlo Giuntini, et al. Chest 1995; 107; 3S-9S.
2. Girard P, et al. Chest 1999; 116; 903-908.

### Notes:
Another manifestation of VTE, pulmonary embolism, is an important cause of death in orthopedic perioperative periods.
There is a close relationship between pulmonary embolism and DVT; 90% of pulmonary embolism thrombi originate from DVT;

# Surgery as a Risk Factor for VTE

## High Risk Factors
- Hip and leg fractures
- Hip and knee joint replacement surgery
- Major general surgery
- Severe trauma
- Spinal cord injury

## Medium Risk Factors
- Arthroscopic surgery
- Central venous catheterization
- Chemotherapy
- Congestive heart failure and respiratory failure
- Hormone replacement therapy
- Malignant tumors
- Oral contraceptives
- Paralysis and stroke
- Pregnancy/postpartum
- Venous thrombosis history
- Thrombophilia

## Low Risk Factors
- Bed rest >3 days
- Long-term sitting
- Ageing
- Laparoscopic surgery
- Pregnancy/prepartum
- Varicose veins

Anderson FA Jr,  et al. Circulation 2003;107:I9 –I16.

# VTE is Overt, with Severe Harm

DVT leads to various complications, severely affecting the quality of life of patients: Approximately 30,000 DVT patients die from PE annually (data from the UK in the early 2000s)1, 20-50% will develop PTS2.
In hospital deaths, 1 in every 10 cases is caused by PE3.
70%-80% of PE patients have no clinical symptoms at the onset, and the cause of death is determined only after death3.
The Silent Killer!

1. Second Report of the UK House of Commons Health Committee for the 2004-2005 Session. Prevention of VTE in Hospitalized Patients
2. Clive Kearon. Circulation 2003, 107:I-22-I-30
3. William H. Geerts, et al. Chest. 2004;126:338S-400S.

### Notes:
Factors associated with correct antemortem diagnosis of major pulmonary embolism
In stark contrast to the severe harm of VTE, the clinical symptoms of VTE are concealed and easily missed, so prevention should be emphasized!
Approximately 80% of DVTs are asymptomatic; 70% of fatal PE cases are discovered only post-mortem; 25% of cases may result in sudden death
Therefore, VTE is also known as the "Silent Killer"!
So, in order to protect patients and keep them away from VTE, we should pay attention to the prevention of VTE in patients.

# Approved Indications or Indications for Use

In surgical procedures, used for the prevention of venous thromboembolism in cases of moderate or high risk of venous thromboembolism.

# Preventive Treatment 1
Preventive Treatment
The following recommended content is commonly applicable to all patients undergoing surgery under general anesthesia.
For patients undergoing surgery under epidural anesthesia, due to the theoretical possibility of increased risk of epidural hematoma formation, whether to administer preoperatively should be considered on a case-by-case basis.
Frequency of Use: Once daily injection
Dose:
For surgery with moderate risk of thromboembolism formation and patients without evidence of severe thromboembolic risk, a daily injection of 2850IU (0.3ml) can effectively prevent. The first injection is usually given approximately 2 hours before surgery.
High-risk thromboembolism surgery: hip and knee surgery, the dose should be adjusted according to the patient's weight. The daily dose is: 38IU/kg (see table on the following page)
     Before surgery, for example, 12 hours before surgery
     After surgery, for example, 12 hours after surgery
     Continue daily use until the third day after surgery
     From the fourth day after surgery, the dose is adjusted to 57IU/kg.
Other conditions: For some surgeries with high risk of thromboembolism formation (especially tumors) and/or patients (especially those with a history of thromboembolic disease), 2850IU (0.3ml) of nadroparin is sufficient.

# Preventive Treatment 2
Treatment Duration:
The duration of anticoagulation treatment should be selected based on the risk of thromboembolism formation. For all cases, this treatment can be supplemented with other standard treatment methods such as graduated compression stockings for the lower limbs, until the patient can walk normally. For general surgery, the average duration of heparin use is less than 10 days.
For some orthopedic surgeries, especially hip surgery, if long-term anticoagulation treatment is required, heparin or subsequent oral anticoagulant drugs can be continued.
However, the relative risks associated with these treatments have not been evaluated to date.
Laboratory Monitoring:
Platelet count must be monitored throughout the heparin treatment process.

# Therapeutic Medication
Treatment for established deep vein thrombosis
If there is any suspicion of deep vein thrombosis, it should be confirmed as soon as possible with appropriate testing methods.
Frequency of Use: Twice daily injections, every 12 hours
Dose: Each injection dose is 85IU/kg
The dose can be administered every 12 hours according to the patient's weight range, at a rate of 0.1ml/10kg (see table on the following page)
Treatment Duration: The use of nadroparin should not exceed 10 days, including the time for balancing with antivitamin K agents. Oral anticoagulants should be used as soon as possible unless contraindicated.

# Contraindications and Precautions 1
Contraindications: Clexane is contraindicated in the following situations:
Allergic to Clexane or any excipients in the Clexane injection
History of thrombocytopenia with the use of Clexane
Active bleeding or increased risk of bleeding associated with hemostatic abnormalities, except for disseminated intravascular coagulation caused by heparin.
Organic injury that may cause bleeding (such as active peptic ulcer)
Hemorrhagic cerebrovascular accident.
Acute infective endocarditis.
Severe renal impairment in patients receiving treatment for thromboembolic disease, unstable angina, and non-Q wave myocardial infarction (creatinine clearance less than 30 ml/min).
Generally not suitable for use in the following conditions:
Severe renal impairment, hemorrhagic cerebrovascular accident, uncontrolled hypertension.
