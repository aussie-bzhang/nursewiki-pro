(1) Surgical Prophylactic Medication - Drug Selection

| 1. Penicillin allergy is common and severe, and prophylactic use is not recommended |
| --- |
| 2. Cephalosporins are the first choice, cephalosporin II (cefuroxime-balanced) has strong bactericidal activity against G+ cocci and G- bacilli, suitable for prophylaxis in clean-contaminated surgeries, but not recommended for 3rd and 4th generation cephalosporins for prophylaxis reasons: ◆ Less effective against staphylococci than 1st generation cephalosporins ◆ Lack of selective antibacterial spectrum (not primarily aimed at surgical infection bacteria) ◆ Widespread use leading to resistance ◆ Expensive |
| 3. Aminoglycosides have ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity, and prophylactic use is not recommended 4. Quinolone类药物 are generally not used, only for urinary tract surgery 5. Macrolides are bacteriostatic agents, generally not used as surgical prophylaxis |
| 6. Glycopeptides are generally not used as surgical prophylaxis, vancomycin or teicoplanin can be selected in hospitals with a high incidence of MRSA for artificial material implantation surgery 7. Carbenepenems are not suitable for surgical prophylaxis |

(2) Surgical Prophylactic Medication - Administration Timing

SSI Development Process: Bacterial (endogenous, exogenous) contamination: easily cleared in the early stage

Colonization: Bacteria adhere to the surface of tissue cells but do not reproduce in large numbers (difficult to clear)
▽ G-rod bacterium pili
▽ Phospholipids on the cell wall of G+ bacteria
▽ Glycoproteins and polysaccharide complexes on the surface of bacteria
▽ Polysaccharide filaments on the surface of tissue cells

Infection: Large-scale reproduction of bacteria causing inflammation

The timing of prophylactic medication is extremely critical:
▽ It should be done before contamination occurs, "be prepared for battle"
▽ Early administration is of no benefit, it is a shot in the dark
▽ Medication should begin 20-30 minutes before the start of surgery to ensure that the drug has reached an effective concentration in the serum and tissues before contamination occurs
▽ Medication should be administered in the operating room rather than summoned from the ward

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
(II) Preoperative Prophylactic Medication - Administration Timing

Administer the first dose 0.5-2 hours before surgery or at the start of anesthesia (For Class I incisions, it is preferable not to use postoperative medication if preoperative medication has not been used)

For surgeries lasting longer than 3 hours, a second dose may be administered intraoperatively (except for ceftriaxone)

Administer a second dose intraoperatively if blood loss exceeds 1500ml

Administer medication after clamping the umbilical cord in cesarean section

Antibiotics should be used to prepare the bowel for colorectal surgery, and should be administered 1 day before surgery; it is not recommended to use them for 3 consecutive days
