Fast Track Recovery Surgery Concept

The early advocate and practitioner of fast track recovery surgery is the Danish surgeon Kehlet. Fast track recovery surgery is a new service concept in nursing in recent years, mainly utilizing evidence-based medicine to take a series of nursing measures for patients, thereby reducing the stress response of surgery and lowering postoperative complications, promoting the rapid recovery of postoperative patients.

It is a synergistic result of a combination of effective measures, many of which have been applied in clinical practice, such as perioperative nutritional support, attention to oxygenation, non-conventional use of nasogastric tubes for decompression, early feeding, the use of growth hormone, minimally invasive surgery, and so on.

Meningioma

Meningioma is the second most common intracranial tumor after glioma, accounting for about 20% of intracranial tumors, most of which are benign, grow slowly, and are mostly located on the parietal sinus of the cerebral hemisphere, with hyperplasia or erosion signs near the skull. Severe cases can cause hemiplegia, aphasia, incontinence of urine and feces, and inability to take care of oneself.

Surgical treatment is the most direct and effective method of treatment for meningioma.

Radiotherapy, as an auxiliary treatment method that has been recognized by many scholars at home and abroad for reducing the recurrence of meningioma after surgery, has been widely used (firstly gamma knife treatment).

Selective vascular embolization therapy can be used as a single treatment for some patients who are not suitable for craniotomy.

Reference: Zhang Xinliang. Current Status and Progress of Diagnosis and Treatment of Meningioma[J]. Journal of Clinical Oncology, 2011, 16(7): 658-661.

Examinations and Surgery

Related Examinations:

Chest X-ray, electrocardiogram, three major blood routine, coagulation four items, blood type, immune three items, routine urine and stool examination were generally normal
The patient underwent a brain MRI after completing all the necessary examinations on July 12, 2017
On July 14, 2017, a total cerebral angiography was performed, combined with the MRI results, considering a left temporal meningioma.

Surgical Treatment
On July 18, 2017, a total craniotomy for complete resection of the left temporal meningioma was performed under general anesthesia. There was a lot of bleeding during the operation, and the patient was transferred to the NICU for dehydration, anti-infection, and symptomatic treatment such as nutrition of the brain nerves after surgery.
On July 21, the patient developed brain swelling, with a lot of bleeding visible in the ventricles, and an emergency right-sided lateral ventricular puncture and drainage was performed.

The patient was transferred to the neurosurgery department on August 7 after the condition stabilized.
