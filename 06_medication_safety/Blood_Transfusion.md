Common Intraoperative Blood Transfusion Adverse Reactions and Nursing Measures
Febrile Non-Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction:
Refers to the increase in body temperature of the recipient by 1℃ or more during the transfusion of whole blood or blood components, usually within 15 minutes to 2 hours after the start of transfusion, or 1 to 2 hours after the transfusion, excluding other causes that can lead to an increase in body temperature.

Allergic Transfusion Reaction:
Includes simple urticaria, angioneurotic edema, laryngeal edema, and in severe cases, respiratory obstruction, shock, and even death.

Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction:
Refers to transfusion adverse reactions caused by the abnormal destruction of red blood cells in the recipient's body due to immunological or non-immunological reasons.

<!-- Slide number: 25 -->
Common Intraoperative Blood Transfusion Adverse Reactions and Nursing Measures
In the event of a hemolytic reaction, report to the doctor immediately, stop the transfusion, change the transfusion set, and maintain the venous access with intravenous normal saline.

Prepare the necessary items for examination, treatment, and rescue, and make corresponding records.

Follow the doctor's orders to administer medication and cooperate with the rescue.

Strengthen temperature management and take appropriate warming measures.

Store the remaining blood and transfusion sets at low temperature and report to the Blood Transfusion Department and relevant departments.

# Clinical Blood Transfusion Technical Standards
In the event of an emergency rescue requiring urgent blood, fill out the "Emergency (Rescue) Blood Application Form".

In an emergency, the urgent rescue blood can be sent back to the operating room first, and the application form is filled out, signed by the doctor on the stage, and sealed by the Medical Affairs Department before being sent back to the blood bank.

# Clinical Blood Transfusion Technical Standards
Article 25

Both parties involved in blood collection and distribution must strictly comply with the "three checks, ten matches, and two observations," and sign together only when accurate and error-free.

# Clinical Blood Transfusion Technical Standards
Article 26
☆Blood bags with any of the following conditions must not be issued:
Label damage, unclear handwriting
Blood bag damage, leakage
Visible clots in the blood
Plasma appears milky or dark gray
Visible bubbles, flocculent matter, or coarse particles in the plasma
The interface between the plasma layer and red blood cells is unclear or the junction surface appears hemolysis when not shaken
Red blood cells appear purple-red
Expired or other situations that require verification.

# Precautions
Precautions for Blood Collection:

    Keep the blood collection hotline open 24 hours a day.

    Re-draw the blood for preparation if it is more than 3 days old.

    If cold clot, platelets are needed, contact the blood bank in advance.

    It is strictly prohibited for one medical staff to collect blood for two patients at the same time.

    For patients with Rh-negative blood type, rare blood types, pay attention to checking the doctor's orders and matching sheets, and communicate with the blood bank staff.

Precautions for Blood Transfusion

    For patients who have received multiple transfusions, check the original blood type before blood collection and transfusion.

# Precautions
Blood Transfusion Time Limit

    Start transfusion of whole blood or red blood cells within 30 minutes after removing from the refrigerator and complete the transfusion within 4 hours.

    Platelets should be transfused within 20 minutes.

    200ml of plasma should be transfused within 20 minutes, and 1u of cold clot within 10 minutes.

<!-- Slide number: 32 -->

# Precautions
Blood Transfusion Warming and Pressurization

   Generally, there is no need to warm the blood for transfusion. If the transfusion volume is large, the limbs can be warmed to eliminate venous spasm.

  ☆Blood warming should be carried out in a special blood warming device, and it should not be warmed in containers with hot water.

  During intraoperative pressurized transfusion, ensure that the transfusion channel is unobstructed and avoid excessive pressure that may damage the formed elements of the blood.
  Follow the instructions for use of the equipment when using blood transfusion pressurization or warming instruments.

<!-- Slide number: 33 -->

# Precautions
Blood Transfusion Set Usage Standards

     When exhaust gas is released during blood transfusion, it should be avoided to squeeze the Murphy's drip tube as much as possible to prevent a large amount of liquid from rapidly flowing into the Murphy's drip tube of the transfusion set, thereby producing a large number of bubbles mixed in the liquid.

     The transfusion set used for transfusing whole blood, component blood, or biological preparations should be changed every 4 hours. When different group cross-matching blood products are infused during surgery, the transfusion set should be changed.

Autologous Blood Transfusion
Autologous Blood Transfusion: Refers to the collection or collection of the patient's own blood or blood components, after appropriate preservation or processing, and then returned to the patient for transfusion to meet the needs of surgery or emergency situations, which is a clinical blood transfusion treatment technology.

Preserved Autologous Blood Transfusion (PABD)
Diluted Autologous Blood Transfusion (HAT)
Recovered Autologous Blood Transfusion (SBA)

Pressurized Blood Transfusion
Pressurized Blood Transfusion: Refers to pressurized blood transfusion that can be carried out if the intraoperative blood transfusion cannot establish more channels or the established channels cannot meet the requirements of fluid infusion and blood transfusion speed for rescue. Specialized pressurized blood transfusion sets or blood pumps should be used.

Venous Injection Needle: Not less than 20G for adults (not less than 22G for children)
Blood bag connected to the venous access, ensuring the unobstructed venous access
Apply pressure according to the needs of the condition, and the speed of pressurized blood transfusion can reach 50-100ml/min. (Slowly increase pressure, but not exceeding 300mmHg)
After the blood transfusion is completed, the transfusion set is changed to connect the venous injection normal saline to flush the transfusion set channel.
During intraoperative pressurized blood transfusion, the circulating nurse should monitor the patient's condition throughout the process, and stop the blood transfusion immediately if there are any abnormalities, change to normal saline for venous injection to maintain the venous access, and immediately report to the doctor for handling.
