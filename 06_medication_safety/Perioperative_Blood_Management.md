Perioperative Blood Transfusion Indication Score - Individualized Red Blood Cell Transfusion Strategy
Peri-Operative Transfusion Trigger Score (POTTS)
| Points | Required Epinephrine Infusion Rate to Maintain Baseline Cardiac Output | Required Inspired Oxygen Concentration to Maintain SpO2≥95% | Central Body Temperature | Angina Pectoris |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Not required | ≤35% | <38℃ | None |
| +1 | ≤0.05μg/kg.min | 36-50% | 38-40℃ | Occurs during exercise, physical labor, or excitement |
| +2 | ≥0.06μg/kg.min | ≥51% | >40℃ | Occurs during daily activities or at rest |
1. The total score of POTTS is the sum of the above four items plus 6 points, with a maximum score of 10 points. A score of ≥10 points is considered as 10 points.
2. Dynamic scoring, which needs to be scored before each preparation of allogeneic red blood cell suspension.
3. This score is an immediate score when assessing the normal or basic normal blood volume.

▷ Why administer 1.0g of tranexamic acid ivgtt to the patient before the operation?
Antifibrinolytic drugs
Lysine analogues: aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid. They can bind to plasma fibrinogen and inhibit its binding to fibrin, thereby interfering with the process of fibrinolysis.

   European Society of Anaesthesiology Guideline on the Management of Severe Perioperative Haemorrhage (2013) recommends the use of tranexamic acid for perioperative haemorrhage in major orthopaedic surgery
   Expert consensus on the application of antifibrinolytic drugs and sequential anticoagulants in perioperative period of hip and knee arthroplasty in China (2015) - the use of tranexamic acid in hip and knee arthroplasty

CS Indications
1. Expected blood loss >1000ml or >20% estimated blood volume.
2. Patient with low Hb or high risk of bleeding.
3. Patient with multiple antibodies or rare blood type.
4. Patient refusing allogeneic blood transfusion.

Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs related to autologous blood recovery (CS):
★ Database: Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), MEDLINE, EMBASE, and ISI Web of Knowledge.
★ Primary outcome: Number of patients receiving allogeneic and/or autologous transfusions, and amount of allogeneic and/or autologous transfusions.
★ Secondary outcomes: Reoperation due to bleeding, postoperative complications (thrombosis, infection, renal failure, non-fatal myocardial infarction), mortality, and hospital stay.
★ Results: 75 RCTs meeting the criteria were included, 36 in orthopaedic surgery, 33 in cardiac surgery, and 6 in vascular surgery. A total of 4755 patients were included in the meta-analysis, of whom 2377 were randomly assigned to the CS group.
★ Conclusion: Autologous blood reinfusion can effectively reduce the need for allogeneic blood transfusion in elective adult cardiac and orthopaedic surgery, and has no adverse effects on clinical indicators.

Carless PA, Henry DA, Moxey AJ, et al. Cell salvage for minimising perioperative allogeneic blood transfusion. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2010;14: CD001888.

Controlled Hypotension (Hypotensive Anesthesia)
Through medication or medical technology, controlled reduction of mean arterial pressure (MAP), usually reduced to about (base value - 30%), to reduce surgical blood loss and reduce blood transfusion requirements.
1. Controllability. The range of reduction can be controlled, the duration of reduction can be predicted, and the level of blood pressure before reduction can be controlled.
2. Individualization. The degree and duration of blood pressure reduction need to be determined according to the requirements of the surgery and the patient's cardiovascular and systemic conditions to avoid ischemia and hypoxia complications due to insufficient perfusion of important organs such as the heart and brain.

Controlled Hypotension Implementation Methods
1. General Anesthetic Drugs
General anesthesia hypotensive drug requirements: easy to administer, rapid onset, the hypotensive effect can quickly disappear when the drug is stopped or the drug concentration is reduced, without toxic metabolites and can be metabolized quickly.
Remifentanil, propofol, inhaled anesthetics such as desflurane, sevoflurane, etc. are all ideal for use in general anesthesia drugs.
2. Hypotensive Drugs
Used alone or in combination with sodium nitroprusside, magnesium sulfate, calcium channel blockers such as nicardipine, and beta-adrenergic receptor blockers such as esmolol.
Drug requirements: rapid onset, dose-dependent, duration predictable, and can be adjusted at any time to control the duration and degree of hypotension.
