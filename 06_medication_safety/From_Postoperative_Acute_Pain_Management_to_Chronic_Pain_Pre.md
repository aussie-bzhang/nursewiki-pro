# Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA)
PCA Tool: Analgesic Pump
PCA is a new type of analgesic administration device. The patient wears an infusion control device, and when they feel pain, they can inject a single dose of analgesic into their body through the controller to achieve self-controlled analgesia.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
# Advantages of PCA
Stable blood drug concentration
Individualized analgesic effect, on-demand administration
Reduction in waiting time for pain management, immediate analgesia
Reduction in postoperative complications
Increased patient satisfaction
Relief of workload for healthcare staff

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
# Common Terminology of PCA
Loading Dose: The initial dose of medication administered at the start of PCA to rapidly achieve the required blood drug concentration;
Single Dose: The dose of analgesic administered to the patient when they feel pain;
Lockout Time: The interval between two single doses in the PCA device, which is a safety protection measure;
Background Infusion Rate: The continuous infusion rate of the PCA device

# Four: New Concepts of Perioperative Analgesia

Perioperative analgesia ≠ Postoperative analgesia
   The purpose of perioperative analgesia is to alleviate pain caused by surgery and its adverse reactions, and more importantly, to prevent the occurrence of peripheral and central sensitization.
Preventive Analgesia (Preventive Analgesia)
    Adopting continuous, multimodal preventive analgesic measures to prevent the formation of a state of pain sensitivity, in order to achieve complete, long-lasting, and effective analgesia covering the entire perioperative period. (So-called "pre-emptive analgesia" refers to the administration of analgesics before the pain transmission occurs in the spinal cord, rather than simply before the incision is made.)
Multimodal Analgesia (Multimodal Analgesia)
    Opioids that weaken central nervous system pain signals and regional blocks
    Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) that mainly act on the periphery to inhibit the triggering of pain signals, while effectively inhibiting peripheral and central pain sensitization…

Preventive Analgesia
Preemptive Analgesia
Preventive Analgesia

The commonly understood definition of preemptive analgesia in clinical and experimental research studies refers to effective analgesic treatment administered before skin incision, which affects postoperative acute pain, analgesic drug requirements, and chronic pain, and this effect should exceed the duration of the analgesic treatment administered before skin incision.
The shift from preoperative analgesia emphasizing treatment time to the use of continuous, multimodal analgesia. Through the prevention of the formation of a hyperalgesic state, preemptive analgesia achieves a completely effective, long-lasting, preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative analgesic method, reducing the incidence of postoperative chronic pain.

Multimodal Analgesia
Multimodal Analgesia (multimodal analgesia)
       The combined use of analgesic drugs or analgesic methods with different mechanisms of action, due to the reduction of each drug dose, the side effects are correspondingly reduced, the analgesic effects are added or synergistic, achieving the maximum effect/toxicity ratio. Including:
Combination of analgesic drugs:
Opioids or tramadol, acetaminophen, NSAIDs; opioids combined with local anesthetics for PCEA; gabapentin, pregabalin, ketamine, clonidine, etc. can also be combined with opioids

Combination of analgesic methods
Local anesthetic infiltration of incisions (regional block or nerve root block)
+ Systemic analgesic drugs (NSAIDs or tramadol or opioids)

    COX-2 inhibitors with high concentrations in cerebrospinal fluid (such as parecoxib) started before surgery have anti-inflammatory, inhibitory effects on central and peripheral sensitization, and can reduce the incidence of postoperative pain transforming into chronic pain.

# Multimodal Analgesia -- Smart Analgesia
Multimodal analgesia, also known as balanced analgesia, is a new concept of analgesia
It involves the combined use of analgesics with different mechanisms of action and/or various analgesic methods
Acting on different phases and targets of the pathophysiological mechanism of pain
Achieving perfect analgesia
Reducing the impact of pain and drugs on the nervous, immune, and endocrine systems, maintaining relative stability of the internal environment, reducing complications, and influencing the outcome of the disease

<!-- Slide number: 34 -->
# Principles of Multimodal Analgesia
Complementary analgesic mechanisms (acting on different receptors or different sites related to analgesia)
Additive and synergistic analgesic effects
No additive or even reduced side effects
Not using opioids with overlapping action times and mechanisms
It is not recommended to use opioid agonists and antagonists simultaneously

# ASA Acute Pain Treatment Guidelines
It is recommended that anesthesiologists should use multimodal analgesia as much as possible.
All patients should continue to use NSAIDs, COX-2, or paracetamol unless contraindicated.
Regional analgesia should be considered.
The minimum dose of drugs that can achieve the optimal analgesic effect with the least adverse reactions should be used
The selection of drugs, dosage, route of administration, and duration of treatment should be individualized

<!-- Slide number: 37 -->
# Smart Analgesia
No single postoperative analgesic method is the best for all patients
Smart analgesia is not about lowering analgesic standards but about individualized treatment for specific patients, which aims to achieve maximum improvement in patient quality of life and satisfaction while ensuring adequate pain relief and avoiding intolerable or undesirable side effects
