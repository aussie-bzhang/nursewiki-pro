### Notes:
Preoperative anxiety, surgical trauma, anesthesia, and postoperative pain can cause an increase in insulin antagonistic hormones (such as catecholamines, cortisol, glucagon, growth hormone, etc.), exacerbating insulin secretion disorders and insulin resistance. At the same time, excessive release of inflammatory factors (such as interleukin 1, tumor necrosis factor, etc.) and increased levels of vasopressin, prolactin, etc., lead to increased glycogenolysis, increased liver glucose output, and enhanced gluconeogenesis, further exacerbating metabolic disorders of glucose.

Moreover, surgery stress, blood loss, anesthesia, and medication further exacerbate organ function failure, increasing the risk of death in diabetic patients. Anesthesia during surgery reduces the patient's responsiveness to hypoglycemia, and fasting, strict preoperative blood glucose control, and inappropriate adjustment of insulin dosage can all increase the incidence of hypoglycemia in diabetic patients.

### Notes:
In this study, 97 patients with type 2 diabetes were switched to glargine insulin combined with short-acting insulin subcutaneous injection (32 cases) or CSII therapy (65 cases) 5 to 7 days before surgery. The CSII group used an insulin pump to subcutaneously infuse short-acting human insulin, and pre-meal insulin injection was stopped on the day of surgery. After resuming eating, pre-meal insulin injection was appropriately given according to the amount of food intake. ### Notes:
In this study, 45 patients with type 2 diabetes scheduled for surgery were divided into two groups based on different perioperative blood glucose control regimens. The glargine insulin group received a subcutaneous injection of glargine insulin before bedtime, with an initial dose of 0.3IU/(kg·d). Acarbose or repaglinide was administered orally before each meal, or short-acting human insulin was administered subcutaneously 30 minutes before each meal. The CSII group used an insulin pump for continuous subcutaneous infusion of short-acting human insulin. During the fasting period before and after surgery, the dose of glargine insulin in the glargine insulin group and the basal dose in the CSII group remained unchanged, and pre-meal insulin injection and oral hypoglycemic drugs were stopped. When eating was resumed after surgery, pre-meal insulin injection and oral hypoglycemic drugs were restored to the preoperative level. Hypoglycemia was defined as blood glucose ≤2.9mmol/L or symptoms of hypoglycemia. The results showed that the blood glucose control effects of the two groups were comparable during the perioperative period, with hypoglycemia incidence rates of 10% and 8%, respectively, with no statistical difference, confirming that glargine insulin and CSII treatment are equally safe.

Glargine insulin has a faster onset than NPH, covers the entire 24-hour period, and can be combined with short-acting insulin to more effectively control blood sugar throughout the day; while NPH has a relatively short action time, only lasting 10 to 16 hours.

### Notes:
目前常用的控制围手术期血糖的治疗方案有NPH皮下注射配合短效胰岛素治疗、静脉泵入胰岛素、胰岛素泵持续皮下输注（CSII）和甘精胰岛素单次皮下注射配合短效胰岛素皮下注射或口服降糖药等几种。

NPH每日皮下注射配合短效胰岛素治疗是临床上最常用的方法，但患者往往血糖波动幅度较大，尤其是对术后禁食的患者应用不便。而且因为NPH作用持续时间相对较短，其作用峰值在凌晨1~3时，易引起夜间低血糖。

胰岛素泵能模拟人体生理胰岛素分泌，24小时内脉冲式地向人体输注微量的胰岛素，模拟基础胰岛素分泌，并且在进餐时可给予餐前胰岛素的泵入，模拟进餐时的胰岛素的分泌，故可较好的控制血糖，尤其是对术后禁食的患者，但在我国由于经济问题制约了它的广泛应用。

甘精胰岛素作为长效胰岛素类似物，因其平稳的胰岛素时间－作用曲线而具有替代基础胰岛素的效果。由于其在皮下吸收平稳，注射后1~2小时起效，每日注射1次，作用时间近24小时而无明显峰值出现，故可作为基础胰岛素使用，且低血糖发生率较低。甘精胰岛素剂量调节方便，可据空腹血糖适当增、减剂量，能在较短的时间内控制患者的空腹血糖，配合短效胰岛素或口服降糖药的使用可以良好的控制全天血糖。患者术后禁食期间可只注射甘精胰岛素作为基础胰岛素，即能达到良好的血糖控制。

In the study, 62 type 2 diabetic patients in the perioperative period were divided into an insulin pump group (CSII group) of 23 patients, an NPH + short-acting insulin group of 18 patients, and an insulin glargine group of 21 patients. The insulin glargine group was injected with insulin glargine once at bedtime (22:00 pm) and subcutaneous injections of short-acting human insulin before meals. The NPH group was administered short-acting human insulin subcutaneously 30 minutes before breakfast, lunch, and dinner, and insulin NPH was injected at bedtime (22:00 pm). The CSII group used an insulin pump for subcutaneous infusion of short-acting human insulin. All patients underwent elective surgery after stable blood glucose control. The results showed that the blood glucose levels in all groups were significantly lower after treatment than before treatment. The fasting and postprandial blood glucose levels in the insulin glargine group were significantly lower than those in the NPH group, while there was no significant difference compared to the CSII group. This further proves that insulin glargine has better efficacy and safety in blood glucose control than NPH.
