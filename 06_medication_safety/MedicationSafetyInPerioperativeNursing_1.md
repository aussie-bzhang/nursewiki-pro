   Perioperative Analgesia Regimens
Recommended for use:
Preoperative 1-3 days, oral tramadol 50-100mg, bid;
Postoperative, two regimens:
       1. Tramadol 100-200mg, bid (depending on the degree of postoperative pain) + short-acting analgesics (NSAIDs), (NSAIDs use not to exceed three days)
       2. Fentanyl transdermal patch 1-2 patches/7 days (depending on the degree of postoperative pain) + short-acting analgesics (NSAIDs), (NSAIDs use not to exceed three days)
Rehabilitation period, two regimens: (to avoid acute pain transforming into chronic pain)
      1. Continue to use tramadol 100-50mg, bid for 10-14 days (to avoid withdrawal symptoms)
      2. Fentanyl transdermal patch 1 patch/7 days

Buprenorphine Transdermal Patch - Drug Tolerance/Dependence
Buprenorphine does not lead to a reduction in the number and function of opioid receptors.

Opioid receptors can undergo desensitization, down-regulation, and internalization under the long-term action of agonists, which are the main reasons for the tolerance and dependence of opioids (such as morphine, fentanyl). Studies have shown that fentanyl and morphine can reduce the number of opioid receptors by 35% and 9% respectively, while an equivalent dose of buprenorphine can increase the number of opioid receptors by 10% (P<0.05).

Research indicates that buprenorphine can accelerate withdrawal symptoms in individuals currently using high-dose μ-receptor agonists (drug abusers), without the "euphoria" experienced by abusers, and can counteract the respiratory depression caused by the original drug. This is why buprenorphine is often used as a drug for treating addiction.

Zaki PA, et al. 2000. Ligand-induced changes in surface mu-opioid receptor number: relationship to G protein activation? J Pharmacol Exp Ther, 292:1127–34.
2. Rolley E. Johnson, Paul J. Fudala, Richard Payne. Buprenorphine: Considerations for Pain Management. Journal of Pain and Symptom Management 2005 Vol. 29 No. 3 March 297-326

### Notes:
Receptor desensitization: Refers to the phenomenon where the sensitivity and responsiveness of tissues or cells to an agonist decreases after long-term use of one agonist.
Receptor down-regulation: Long-term application of opioids can lead to down-regulation of μ-receptors in brain areas, which may be related to the acceleration of receptor degradation or a decrease in receptor generation.
Receptor phosphorylation: The interaction between opioids and opioid receptors causes phosphorylation of serine and threonine at the carboxyl end of the receptor, leading to a decrease in receptor sensitivity and tolerance.
Receptor internalization (internalization): The above phosphorylation causes receptors on the cell membrane to move into the cytoplasm, where they cannot bind to the drug.
There are at least four subtypes of opioid receptors in the central nervous system: μ, κ, δ, σ. The fourth member of the opioid receptor family, the opioid orphan receptor, also known as opio id recepo r like 1 (ORL-1), orphanin FQ (OFQ), or nociceptin, has a similar structure to traditional opioid receptors but significantly different functional characteristics. It can antagonize the analgesic effect of opioid receptor agonists.

The main enzymes for metabolizing drugs in the human body are the cytochrome P450 superfamily (Cytochrome P450 proteins, CYP), which are a class of monooxygenases mainly present in the liver and intestines, located in the endoplasmic reticulum of cells, and catalyze the metabolism of a variety of endogenous and exogenous substances (including most clinical drugs). Buprenorphine/Buprenorphine Transdermal Patch Regulation Level in Other Countries
In the United States, buprenorphine is classified as Schedule III, which is different from morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone, and other drugs classified as Schedule II. The management of prescription requirements, logistics, storage, and operating license is closer to that of ordinary drugs.

Buprenorphine transdermal patch is used with a general prescription in at least the following countries, with a regulation level (Schedule III) different from that of opioid drugs: Austria, Argentina, Denmark, Finland, Mexico, Spain, Sweden, the United Kingdom, New Zealand.

Pharmacological Summary of Buprenorphine Transdermal Patch
Less physical dependence than full agonists 1,2
Buprenorphine is a partial agonist
The risk of withdrawal symptoms is relatively low
Blood drug concentration decreases slowly (50% decrease after removing the patch for 12 hours) 1,3
If withdrawal syndrome occurs, it is usually mild, generally appearing within 2 days and may last up to 2 weeks 1,2
The patch contains a low dose of buprenorphine, has a slow onset of action, and the possibility of obtaining a "high" through the patch is very low
Maintenance dose during detoxification is approximately 16mg/day of buprenorphine sublingual, and it is impossible to obtain a substitute effect through the patch
Blood drug concentration reaches steady state in 48-72 hours, and "highs" cannot be obtained quickly
Compared with many opioid drugs, the potential for abuse is limited
May be similar to agonist-antagonist opioid drugs 3

### Notes:
In a long-term clinical trial, use of the buprenorphine transdermal patch (mean exposure: 234 days) in 385 patients with chronic pain showed no unexpected safety concerns and was well tolerated by most patients.

The buprenorphine transdermal patch had a favorable safety profile compared with oxycodone/paracetamol tablets when used over 84 days in patients with chronic back pain.

In patients with chronic moderate to severe OA pain of the hip and/or knee, buprenorphine transdermal patches were well-tolerated and non-inferior to prolonged-release tramadol tablets.

### Notes:
This slide summarizes some information about use in special patient populations.
Dose adjustments are not required in patients with renal impairment.
Buprenorphine is predominately metabolized by the liver. Therefore, the buprenorphine transdermal patch should be used with caution in patients with impaired liver function.
All patients with hepatic impairment should be carefully monitored during treatment.
No dose adjustment is needed for mild to moderate hepatic impairment, but an alternative therapy should be used for patients with severe hepatic impairment.
Dose adjustments are not required based on age, ethnicity or gender.
The buprenorphine transdermal patch is not recommended for use in patients under 18 years of age as no clinical data are available in this age group.
There are no data available on the use of the buprenorphine transdermal patch during pregnancy and lactation. Therefore, this medication is contraindicated during pregnancy and should be avoided during lactation.
Febrile patients and those with increased skin temperature due to other causes should be monitored carefully during treatment with the buprenorphine transdermal patch, because exposure to external heat sources and fever may increase the absorption of buprenorphine.

Use of Opioids in Patients with Hepatic and Renal Impairment:
In addition to buprenorphine, all opioids should be used with reduced dosages, increased dosing intervals, and monitoring of creatinine clearance in elderly patients with liver and renal impairment, especially in those with renal impairment.
