      Heart and coronary arteries
The heart is a strong and powerful pump that delivers blood through the arterial and venous networks to all important organs in the body.
The arteries that supply the heart itself are called "coronary arteries."

Coronary Arteries of the Heart

Left anterior descending branch: Tip of the heart, anterior wall of the left ventricle, papillary muscles of the left ventricle, anterior 2/3 of the interventricular septum, anterior 1/3 of the right ventricular anterior wall.
Left circumflex branch: Left ventricular lateral wall, left atrium, aortic root.
Right coronary artery: Right ventricular anterior 2/3, posterior wall of the right ventricle, posterior wall of the left ventricle, posterior 1/3 of the interventricular septum, atrioventricular node, sinoatrial node.

Coronary Heart Disease (CHD)

It is atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease of the coronary arteries.
Coronary artery atherosclerosis leads to narrowing or obstruction of the vascular lumen, causing myocardial ischemia, hypoxia, or necrosis, resulting in heart disease.

Main Clinical Manifestations of Coronary Heart Disease

(1) Chest tightness and chest pain, which can be relieved by rest and sublingual nitroglycerin;
(2) During the onset of pain, symptoms such as fainting, sweating, shortness of breath, anxiety, palpitations, nausea, or dizziness may occur.
(3) Sudden severe pain in the posterior chest or anterior chest, which persists for more than half an hour and cannot be relieved by rest or sublingual nitroglycerin;
(4) Shortness of breath, dizziness, nausea, sweating, and tachycardia;
(5) Skin moist, pale, and a serious appearance;
(6) About one-tenth of the patients have syncope or shock as the only manifestation.

Myocardial Infarction:

Partial myocardial cell necrosis due to severe and persistent ischemia and hypoxia.

Clinical manifestations of myocardial infarction: Persistent severe pain behind the sternum, fever, etc.

Chest tightness symptoms are more frequent, more severe in nature, longer in duration, and less effective with nitroglycerin than angina, with unknown triggers.

Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)

A treatment method that uses catheter techniques to dilate narrow or occluded coronary artery lumens, thereby improving myocardial blood perfusion.

1. Percutaneous Coronary Angioplasty (PTCA);
2. Coronary stent implantation;
3. Coronary thrombus aspiration;
4. Coronary rotational atherectomy;
5. Coronary plaque rotational ablation;
6. Coronary laser angioplasty, etc.

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
Percutaneous Coronary Angioplasty (PTCA)

It is a catheterization technique that uses a balloon catheter to dilate narrowed coronary arteries. The method involves percutaneous puncture of the artery, retrograde insertion of the balloon catheter into the narrowed segment of the coronary artery, followed by inflation of the balloon to dilate the lesion, thereby increasing the diameter of the vessel and improving myocardial perfusion.

2026/5/11

<!-- Slide number: 23 -->
Coronary Stent Implantation

Building on PTCA, a suitable specific model of metallic stent is implanted into the narrowed segment of the coronary artery to maintain continuous dilation of the coronary artery, ensuring smooth blood flow within the coronary artery and guaranteeing blood supply to the diseased myocardium. When coronary stenosis is >70%, the doctor may consider stent implantation for the patient.

Preoperative Nursing for PCI

Sign the surgical consent form;
Routine examinations (complete blood count, blood type, coagulation five, liver and kidney function);
Inquire about a history of iodine allergy;
Prepare the skin (inguinal, perineum, both upper arm wrist joints);
Insert a catheter needle in the left upper limb;
Orally take 300mg of Plavix and 300mg of aspirin as needed before surgery;
Take a sleeping pill the night before surgery, and discontinue low-molecular-weight heparin on the day of surgery.

General Postoperative Nursing

1. Observation of condition:
a. Provide the patient with electrocardiogram and blood pressure monitoring, closely observe changes in the patient's electrocardiogram and blood pressure;
b. Strictly observe for bleeding at the puncture site and the presence of radial or dorsalis pedis artery pulsation;
2. Education:
a. Bracing: If the patient only has a puncture in the radial artery, instruct the patient to immobilize the affected wrist joint for 6 hours; if the puncture site is in the femoral artery, the patient should immobilize the affected lower limb for 24 hours;
2026/5/11

<!-- Slide number: 32 -->
General Postoperative Nursing

b. Increase fluid intake: Instruct the patient to drink water in multiple large doses after surgery, with a daily fluid intake of 2000-3000ml, and inform the patient of the importance and necessity of drinking plenty of water;
c. Lateral limb activity: Instruct the patient to move the elbow and metacarpophalangeal joints more, perform more fist-clenching exercises to promote blood circulation, inform patients with puncture at the inguinal region to stay in bed for one day and not to sit up or get out of bed;
d. Diet: Inform the patient that they can eat light and nutritious food after returning to the ward, and maintain smooth defecation;
e. Medication care: Instruct the patient to take antiplatelet aggregation drugs.

Observation and Prevention of Arrhythmias

Arrhythmias
    Causes:
1) Coronary artery occlusion or prolonged ischemia due to catheter insertion or balloon and other instruments
2) Acute coronary occlusion or thrombosis
3) Reperfusion arrhythmias

<!-- Slide number: 37 -->
Observation and Prevention of Arrhythmias

Arrhythmias
    Management:
1) Strictly monitor the patient's vital signs, especially heart rate and rhythm changes
2) Pay attention to the patient's complaints, and pay attention to whether the patient has discomfort
3) Keep a defibrillator at the bedside

<!-- Slide number: 38 -->
Observation and Prevention of Hemorrhagic Complications

False aneurysm
    Causes:
1) Displacement of puncture site compression
2) The patient prematurely moves the surgical limb, causing the pressure device or elastic band to displace.

<!-- Slide number: 39 -->

    Observation and Prevention of Hemorrhagic Complications

Management
1) Emphasize the importance and necessity of immobilizing the surgical limb after surgery, in order to obtain the patient's attention and cooperation
2) Immobilize the surgical limb for 4-6 hours after surgery, or stay in bed for 24 hours.
3) Auscultate for vascular杂音, and use vascular ultrasound to check for active bleeding
4) Reapply the pressure device or elastic band and re-wrap for 24 hours, and extend the bed rest time.
5) Strictly observe the puncture site, and handle any abnormalities in a timely manner.

<!-- Slide number: 40 -->

    Observation and Prevention of Hemorrhagic Complications

Arteriovenous fistula
  Causes: Fistula formed by simultaneous puncture of artery or vein
   Management:
    1) Strictly observe the puncture site and the pulse of the surgical limb artery
    2) Locate the fistula orifice position under vascular ultrasound and reapply pressure.

<!-- Slide number: 41 -->

    Observation and Prevention of Hemorrhagic Complications

Hemorrhage or subcutaneous hematoma:
Causes:
1) Large heparin dosage during surgery or the patient's own coagulation dysfunction;
2) Inadequate hemostasis time after catheter removal, the patient prematurely moves the limb, or gets out of bed too early after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 42 -->

Observation and Prevention of Hemorrhagic Complications

Hemorrhage or Subcutaneous Hematoma:
Management:
1) Preoperative and postoperative education to obtain patient cooperation.
2) Close observation of the puncture site, with timely management of any abnormalities.
3) After catheter removal, extend the affected limb straight and immobilize for 4-6 hours, remain in bed for 24 hours, and closely observe the puncture site and arterial pulsation of the affected limb. Bruises and small hematomas do not require special treatment and can be absorbed spontaneously. For larger hematomas, reapply pressure bandaging.

<!-- Slide number: 43 -->
Prevention and Nursing of Contrast Medium-Induced Complications

Contrast Medium Nephropathy:
Renal insufficiency after PCI, except for a few cases caused by complications during PCI (shock, no-reflow, heart failure, arrhythmia), is mostly due to contrast medium. Generally, a serum creatinine increase of >25% or >0.5 mg/ml within 24-48 hours after contrast medium administration can be diagnosed as contrast medium nephropathy. The main pathological change is acute tubular necrosis.

<!-- Slide number: 44 -->
Prevention and Nursing of Contrast Medium-Induced Complications

Prevention and Nursing:
1) Preoperative health education: Introduce PCI-related knowledge to patients and their families to obtain their active cooperation.
2) Preoperative hydration therapy: For high-risk patients, intravenous infusion of Ringer's solution 4 hours before surgery, continuing until 24 hours after contrast administration.

Nursing of Catheter Insertion

The patient should maintain a supine position, extend the affected limb straight, and immobilize to prevent catheter twisting or breaking.
For patients who only need catheter insertion for 4-6 hours, heparin anticoagulation is not required.
For patients who need catheter insertion for 24 hours, 0.9% NS 50ml + heparin sodium 12500u is administered intravenously at a constant rate of 2ml/h as prescribed.
Regularly recheck the five coagulation tests.
Stop using heparin sodium 2-4 hours before catheter removal, and draw blood to measure ACT <160S before removal.

Psychological Nursing

Closely monitor blood pressure and heart rate, measuring blood pressure every 30 minutes.
Maintain a patent intravenous line, and administer Ringer's injection intravenously at a rapid rate.
Frequently inquire about the patient and observe changes in the condition.
Be aware of the occurrence of vagus nerve reflex.
Apply manual pressure for 15-20 minutes after catheter removal, followed by elastic bandage compression or direct use of a vascular occluder.
Keep a detailed nursing record.

Prevention of Complications - Observation and Prevention of Catheter Removal Syndrome

Catheter Removal Syndrome (Vagus Nerve Reflex):
Manifested as hypotension, bradycardia, pale complexion, profuse sweating, nausea, and vomiting; mostly a benign process that can recover quickly; however, there may be severe consequences even leading to death.
It often occurs during femoral artery puncture and after the removal of the arterial sheath, with an incidence rate of 3% to 5%. It is related to factors such as nervousness, pain, low blood volume, improper pressure causing lower limb ischemia, and exaggerated vagus nerve reflex.

<!-- Slide number: 51 -->

Prevention and Nursing of Catheter Removal Syndrome
1) Pay attention to explaining to the patient before catheter removal to alleviate their tension.
2) Administer local infiltration anesthesia with lidocaine injection during catheter removal.
3) Closely observe the patient's vital signs and mental state changes, and prepare emergency medications and supplies.

Discharge Instructions

(1) Diet:
a. Avoid greasy foods, and light diet is preferred.
b. Eat more fruits and vegetables to maintain bowel regularity (for patients with a history of constipation, inform them not to strain during defecation, and use suppositories such as glycerin if defecation is difficult).
c. Cooking methods: It is best to steam or boil, reducing the consumption of fried or baked foods.
d. Avoid overeating, and eat small meals more frequently.
e. Quit smoking and limit alcohol consumption.

<!-- Slide number: 55 -->
Discharge Instructions

(2) Rest and Exercise:
a. Avoid heavy physical labor after discharge and pay attention to a balance between work and rest.
b. Engage in moderate exercise daily, mainly aerobic exercises such as jogging, walking, Tai Chi, etc., with the amount of exercise not causing fatigue, aiming for 30 minutes per day.
c. Rest immediately if any discomfort is felt.
d. Carry self-help medications: such as nitroglycerin, and sublingually ingest one tablet of nitroglycerin if an acute discomfort is felt. If symptoms are not relieved within 5 minutes, continue to take more. If symptoms are not relieved after taking three tablets, seek medical attention immediately.

Discharge Instructions

(3) Medication Guidance:

Regular follow-up and timely medication adherence

Active antiplatelet therapy is a long-term guarantee for the success of PCI surgery,

and Aspirin 100mg (for life, except for contraindications).
