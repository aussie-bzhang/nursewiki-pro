## Definition

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is one of the terminal treatment methods for end-stage chronic renal failure and uremia. Its principle is to utilize the concentration difference and osmotic difference of solutes between the capillaries on both sides of the peritoneum and the dialysate in the peritoneal cavity to remove metabolic products and excess water from the plasma, and to supplement necessary substances to the blood from the dialysate. By continuously infusing fresh dialysate into the peritoneal cavity and allowing the dialysate to remain in the peritoneal cavity for a certain period of time and to exclude old dialysate, the treatment purpose of removing toxins, dehydrating, correcting acidosis, and correcting electrolyte disorders is achieved.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
## Definition

Peritoneal dialysis-related peritonitis refers specifically to peritoneal infection in patients with peritoneal dialysis. Clinical manifestations may include abdominal pain, cloudy dialysate, and fever.

Since the successful application of PD in clinical practice in 1932, peritoneal dialysis-related peritonitis has always been a major complication, and it is also an important cause of dialysis failure and an impact on the development of dialysis.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
## Diagnostic Criteria

Abdominal pain and/or fever
Diagnosis can be made if two or more of the following are present:

Abdominal dialysate turbidity
White blood cell count > 100/mm3
Neutrophil count > 50%

Detection of pathogenic bacteria

### Instructions for Discharge

1. On October 20, 2017, the patient and family were retrained in peritoneal dialysis operation until they passed the assessment.

2. Instruct the patient to take medication as prescribed by the doctor, do not discontinue medication arbitrarily. Regularly check blood pressure and blood sugar. Teach the patient to recognize hypoglycemia and the first aid for hypoglycemia.

3. Dietary guidance: Consume 1.2-1.5g/kg/d of protein, mainly from high-quality protein sources such as milk, eggs, lean meat, and fish; consume sufficient calories of 146.5kj/kg.d; supplement vitamins and trace elements. Avoid eating cold, stale, and unclean foods to prevent and reduce the occurrence of intestinal infections.

4. Guide appropriate exercise and physical activity to enhance the body's immunity and strengthen physical fitness. And guide the ball cloud hand exercise Bid to promote heart function rehabilitation training.

5. Provide telephone follow-up and WeChat follow-up, inquire about the patient's general condition, encourage the patient to recognize the disease process,疏导 the patient's emotions, and improve treatment compliance. Conduct a home follow-up in the second week after discharge to examine the peritoneal dialysis home environment and guide standardized peritoneal dialysis operation.

6. During the fourth-week follow-up visit, track biochemical indicators and provide targeted and continuous nursing guidance to the patient.

7. Invite patients to attend kidney friend meetings irregularly, allowing patients to communicate and encourage each other, reduce psychological pressure, and enhance confidence.

Continuity of Care Instructions
1. Continue maintenance peritoneal dialysis, take medication as prescribed, and administer subcutaneous injections of erythropoietin 10,000U once a week, and intravenous infusion of human albumin 10g once a month. Regular follow-up visits are required to monitor biochemical results.

2. Advise a low-sodium, high-quality protein, and diabetic diet. Consume less than 5g of salt per day, and 1.2-1.5g/kg of protein per day, primarily from high-quality proteins such as milk, eggs, lean meat, and fish. Minimize intake of legumes and other plant proteins. Consume low-phosphorus and potassium-rich foods such as celery; and take Calcium Gluconate one tablet daily. Daily water intake = 500ml + previous day urine output + previous day ultrafiltration volume. Provide a food composition reference table for reference.

3. Maintain moderate outdoor aerobic exercise, such as walking, ball holding cloud hand exercises, etc.

4. Follow-up visit after one month, and seek medical attention if symptoms persist.
