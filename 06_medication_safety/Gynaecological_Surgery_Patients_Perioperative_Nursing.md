# Definition
Perioperative period: Refers to the period from the day the patient is admitted and determined for surgery to the day of postoperative recovery and discharge.
Preoperative period: From the time the patient decides to undergo surgery to the time the patient is sent to the operating table, this period is called the preoperative period.

Gynaecological Surgery Classification
1. According to the urgency: Elective surgery, time-limited surgery, emergency surgery.
2. According to the surgical site: Abdominal surgery, vulvar, vaginal surgery.
3. Abdominal surgery: Laparoscopic exploration, abdominal exploration, salpingectomy, total hysterectomy, subtotal hysterectomy, total hysterectomy and salpingectomy, subtotal hysterectomy and salpingectomy, salpingectomy, etc.

Vulvar and vaginal surgery: Hysteroscopy, radical vulvar cancer surgery, incision and drainage of Bartholin's cyst, repair of third-degree perineal laceration, repair of anterior and posterior vaginal wall, repair of urinary fistula, vaginoplasty, vaginal hysterectomy, submucosal myoma resection.

Nursing Measures
5. Pain Relief
The most severe pain usually occurs within 24 hours after surgery, and it gradually diminishes after 2-3 days.
If the pain persists or worsens after it subsides, be alert to the possibility of incisional infection.
Over-the-counter analgesics (Fortral 30mg po) have a good effect on skin and muscular pain after minor surgery.
For major surgery, intramuscular injection of pethidine (50-100mg im) is often required for pain relief within 1-2 days after surgery, and analgesic pumps may be used if necessary.
