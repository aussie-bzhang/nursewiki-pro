# Case Study One
Liang Lady, from Gansu Province, came to Xi'an Jiaotong University First Affiliated Hospital for treatment due to cancer on December 7, 2016.
On December 30, during the surgery, medical staff administered 200cc of plasma to her. Subsequently, the patient showed signs of hematuria, and upon examination, it was found that Liang, who was originally O-negative blood type, was mistakenly administered AB-negative blood due to the negligence of medical staff.

# Pre-transfusion Preparation
Blood should not be subjected to violent vibration during transportation to prevent red blood cell destruction and hemolysis.
The blood should not be left at room temperature for too long to prevent contamination.
Cold storage blood should not be heated arbitrarily; if it is necessary to heat it, only a dedicated warming device can be used.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Blood Transfusion Verification
Strictly implement the blood transfusion verification system, and the anesthesiologist and circulating nurse must verify before blood transfusion, based on medical records, blood type card, blood transfusion record form, and blood bag, and carefully carry out "three checks and eight matches".
Three checks: check blood validity period, check blood quality, check whether the blood transfusion device is intact;
Eight matches: match bed number, name, admission number, blood bag number, blood dosage, blood type, and the contents of the cross-matching form. Sign on the blood transfusion record form after verification and prepare for blood transfusion.
During blood transfusion, two people confirm the identity of the recipient again, and perform "three checks and eight matches" again before using a blood transfusion set that meets national standards for blood transfusion.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Application of Blood Transfusion Sets
When transfusing two or more bags of blood, a small amount of normal saline should be infused between the two bags of blood to prevent blood coagulation.
When changing each bag of blood, the two-person verification system must be strictly adhered to. If a blood transfusion set is used continuously for more than 5 hours, a new set must be changed.
In addition to injecting normal saline, no drugs should be added to the blood before and during blood transfusion.

# Observation of Adverse Reactions to Blood Transfusion
Closely observe the patient's condition changes during blood transfusion and carefully fill out the "Blood Transfusion Observation Record Form".
During blood transfusion, follow the anesthesiologist's order and adjust the infusion rate according to the patient's condition and the needs of the surgery.
When there are no special requirements, follow the principle of slow to fast, with the initial infusion rate being slow, generally not exceeding 20 drops/minute. Observe for 15 minutes without adverse reactions, and then adjust the infusion rate according to the patient's condition and blood type, with the adult rate generally being 40-60 drops/minute, and reducing for the elderly, children, and those with poor heart function.

# Emergency Treatment for Adverse Reactions to Blood Transfusion
 - Acute Hemolytic Blood Transfusion Reaction
 Clinical manifestations: fever, hypotension, shock, bronchospasm, DIC, and renal failure, etc.
 Measures 1. Rapidly replenish blood volume
          2. Apply rapid-acting diuretics
          3. Apply dopamine
          4. Alkalinize urine
          5. Apply adrenal cortical hormones and high-dose immunoglobulins
          6. For severe cases, perform exchange transfusion or plasma exchange therapy
          7. For acute renal failure, perform dialysis treatment
          8. Prevention and treatment of DIC

# Emergency Treatment for Adverse Reactions to Blood Transfusion
 - Non-hemolytic fever reaction due to non-hemolytic reaction
 (1) Immediately stop blood transfusion and maintain venous access.
 (2) Symptomatic treatment, pay attention to keeping warm, antipyretic, and sedative.
 (3) Medical staff should closely observe the changes in the patient's condition.
 (4) Non-hemolytic fever reaction should be distinguished from bacterial contamination of blood transfusion reaction.
 (5) If non-hemolytic fever reaction occurs, stop blood transfusion immediately, maintain venous access, and after symptomatic treatment for 30 minutes to 2 hours, the symptoms are relieved; if the patient still needs blood transfusion, change to a low leukocyte red blood cell or washed red blood cell.
 (6) If the symptoms are not relieved or even worsen after stopping blood transfusion and symptomatic treatment for 2 hours, consider bacterial contamination of blood transfusion reaction, and timely rescue should be carried out.

# Emergency Management of Blood Transfusion Adverse Reactions
 Three, Urticaria Caused by Allergic Reactions
Clinical Manifestations: Red rash, urticaria, or pruritic papules may appear, with severe cases presenting anxiety, laryngeal spasm, facial edema, accompanied by dyspnea, nausea, vomiting, loss of consciousness, and even shock.
Measures: 1. For mild allergic reactions: slow down the blood transfusion rate, take oral or intramuscular antihistamines, closely observe, and continue transfusion if symptoms subside; 2. For severe allergic reactions: immediately stop the transfusion, maintain the venous access, and for those with bronchospasm, administer adrenaline subcutaneously; for severe or persistent cases, inject or infuse dexamethasone, aminophylline, and other medications; for those with laryngeal edema, perform immediate tracheal intubation or tracheotomy to prevent asphyxia; for those with anaphylactic shock, actively perform antishock treatment.

# Emergency Management of Blood Transfusion Adverse Reactions
Four, Hyperemia
1. Clinical Manifestations: Sudden shortness of breath, cyanosis, increased heart rate, cough with pink frothy sputum during blood transfusion.
2. Management Principles: Oxygen therapy and diuretics. Slowing down the transfusion rate and reducing the amount of blood transfused can prevent this condition.

# Emergency Management of Blood Transfusion Adverse Reactions
Five, Acute Lung Injury Related to Blood Transfusion
1. Clinical Manifestations: Dyspnea, fever, chest pain, cough, hypoxemia, and hypotension.
2. Management Principles: Oxygen therapy, mechanical ventilation, and the use of adrenal cortical hormones.

# Emergency Management of Blood Transfusion Adverse Reactions
Six, Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction
1. Clinical Manifestations: Fever, fatigue.
2. Management Principles: Mild cases require observation, severe cases are managed similarly to acute hemolysis.

# Post-Transfusion Thrombocytopenia
1. Clinical Manifestations: Platelet count rapidly decreases 7 to 10 days after blood transfusion.
2. Management Principles: Plasma exchange, intravenous immunoglobulin; platelet transfusion has no significant benefit.

# Diseases Transmitted by Blood Transfusion
Viral hepatitis, HIV, Human T-lymphotropic virus, Cytomegalovirus, Syphilis, and Malaria, etc., should be managed according to the relevant diseases and executed in accordance with the "Management Measures and Reporting System for Blood Transfusion-Transmitted Diseases".

# Post-Transfusion Safety
After blood transfusion is completed, the blood bag should be stored in the refrigerator for at least 24 hours before being disposed of as medical waste with a record.
The "Blood Transfusion Record Form" should be kept in the medical record.

# Typical Case Two
On May 7, 2008, Mrs. Zhao's mother, Zhang Xiurui, was admitted to Haigang Hospital for treatment due to bile duct stones. The hospital diagnosed her with choledocholithiasis. On May 16, the hospital performed a choledochotomy and stone extraction, and cholangioscopy under general anesthesia. Due to postoperative malnutrition, the doctor recommended the supplementation of plasma. Subsequently, the hospital transfused 2200 milliliters of b+ leukocyte-reduced frozen plasma to Zhang Xiurui for 11 consecutive days. After that, Zhang Xiurui's incision did not heal for more than 20 days, and symptoms such as splenomegaly, portal hypertension, and hemoglobinuria appeared. On June 6, Haigang Hospital notified Mrs. Zhao that the hospital's blood laboratory had made a mistake in testing Zhang Xiurui's blood type, which was actually O type but was mistakenly identified as B type. Haigang Hospital believed that the B type leukocyte-reduced fresh frozen plasma administered to the patient was incompatible with the patient's O type blood, would not cause acute hemolytic reaction, and the patient's chills and fever symptoms before the transfusion were due to biliary infection and had no causal relationship with the transfusion of B type plasma.

# Operating Room Resuscitation Protocol
At 8:00 AM on November 1, the operating room received a notification from the delivery room that a 28-year-old pregnant woman would undergo an emergency cesarean section surgery in five minutes. The patient was a 28-year-old woman at 35 weeks pregnant, who came to the emergency department with lower abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding. She had been in pain for 7 hours and had shown reduced fetal movements, with bright red small clots during vaginal bleeding. After a thorough physical examination by the doctor, it was found that there were irregular uterine contractions and the intercontraction period was not obvious, with increased uterine tension and tenderness in the anterior uterine body. The fetal heart rate was still within the normal range. The patient had not undergone any prenatal examinations, and there were no obvious precipitating factors such as multiple pregnancies, hypertension, or trauma in this illness. The color Doppler ultrasound doctor reported that "the anterior placental edge echo was abnormal, covering an area of 1/3." Combined with the patient's medical history, signs, and auxiliary examination reports, it was confirmed as "placental abruption."

# One, Organization and Division of Labor in Resuscitation Work
Resuscitation work must have a comprehensive and sound organizational division of labor. In cases of major resuscitation or involving medical disputes and legal issues, the Nursing Department should be reported.

# Two, Compliance with Laws and Regulations
Rescuers should strictly comply with relevant laws and regulations, implement various regulations and technical operation procedures, and strictly prevent errors and accidents and medical disputes from occurring.

# Three, Complete and Ready Resuscitation Equipment and Medications
Resuscitation equipment and medications should be complete and ready, with designated personnel in charge, fixed placement, and quantitative storage, with clear markings. Resuscitation items should not be borrowed out to ensure emergency use.

# Four, Immediate Notification in Case of Resuscitation During Surgery
If a patient requires resuscitation during surgery, the circulating nurse should immediately call the duty nurse, request support. The duty nurse will immediately notify the nurse in charge, who will coordinate personnel to form a resuscitation team. Rescuers should arrive immediately upon receiving the notification.

# Five, Composition of the Resuscitation Team and Corresponding Resuscitation Work Arrangements See "Operating Room Patient Resuscitation Work Flow."

# Six, Compliance with Resuscitation Protocols
Rescuers must adhere to their posts, follow commands, and work according to various resuscitation routine procedures. Nurses should promptly prepare resuscitation equipment, medications, and items, and execute resuscitation measures according to medical orders and patient conditions, such as oxygen therapy, suction, establishment of a venous access, artificial respiration, chest compressions, defibrillation, and active cooperation with surgical procedures.

# Seven, Close Observation of the Patient's Condition and Timely Implementation of Resuscitation Measures
Detailed resuscitation records should be made.

# Eight, Strict Implementation of the Check-Counter System
Nurses must repeat oral medical orders and verify the medication with the doctor before execution; the empty vials of the medication used should be verified by two people before disposal.

# Nine, No Handover During Resuscitation
Principally, no handover is allowed during the resuscitation of surgical patients. All resuscitation items and instruments should be cleaned, disinfected, replenished, and returned to their original places for reuse.

# Ten, Completion of Resuscitation Records and Orders
If there is no time to record during the resuscitation process, medical staff should complete the resuscitation records and write medical orders within 6 hours after the resuscitation, and summarize experiences to promote work.
