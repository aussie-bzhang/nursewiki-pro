## Introduction

Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA)
A common surgical method for treating diseases such as hip arthritis and avascular necrosis of the femoral head[1]

Periprosthetic Fracture of the Femur (PFF)
Is one of the serious complications of THA, with a clinical incidence of 5.9%
Currently, it has become the third most common cause of hip revision after prosthesis loosening and recurrent dislocation[2]

Characteristics of PFF
Combined with osteoporosis, elderly age
PFF after surgery is prone to complications such as malunion, dislocation, failure of internal fixation, prosthesis loosening, and recurrent fracture[3]

Risk Factors for PFF
Trauma: such as falling or twisting
Osteoporosis caused by various reasons
Prosthesis loosening[4]

## Classification of Periprosthetic Fracture of the Femoral End

Type A
Type B1
Type B2
Type B3
Type C
Vancouver Classification System for Periprosthetic Fractures:
Type A fracture is located in the proximal region of the trochanter
Type B fracture is around or just below the stem of the prosthesis; B1 type fracture is stable with sufficient bone mass; B2 type fracture is unstable but with sufficient bone mass; B3 type fracture is unstable with insufficient bone mass
Type C fracture is located in the distal segment of the diaphysis away from the prosthesis[3]

Discharge Instructions
One. Hip Joint Dislocation Prevention: 1. Hip joint flexion less than 90 degrees, no adduction beyond the midline, avoid excessive flexion, internal rotation, and adduction, avoid bad posture, and follow the 5 Nos: Do not cross your legs; Do not lie on the affected side (if lying on the healthy side, place a soft pillow between the knees); Do not sit on low sofas and low chairs; Do not bend over to pick up items; Do not do the lotus position, to prevent hip joint dislocation. 2. Hip joint restriction for 3 months, regular follow-up.
Two. Fall Prevention: 1. Prepare at home: a pair of non-slip slippers, if possible, a comfortable cushion for the chair, a comfortable seat, a safe backrest and armrest; 2. Keep the ground dry at home, use non-slip mats in the bathroom, raise the toilet (toilet pad or commode chair); 3. Walking and movement: Do "3 30s" in daily life, i.e., get up after 30s when waking up, stand up after 30s when getting up, walk after 30s when standing, have someone to assist with daily activities, and if dizziness or other discomfort occurs, do not get out of bed; call for family help when getting up at night to use the bathroom.
Three. Dietary Nursing: Guide the patient to eat high-protein, high-vitamin, and high-iron foods such as lean meat, pork liver, egg yolk, black fungus, beans, fresh vegetables, etc., and avoid spicy foods and high-sugar diets, eat in small and frequent meals.

Reflections
Hip fracture surgery is a major trauma to patients, and the assessment of the patient's condition and physical status during the perioperative period is very important, especially for elderly patients with hip fractures and diabetes mellitus. Elevated blood sugar levels can have a significant impact on the prognosis of elderly patients with hip fractures and diabetes mellitus. During this period, it is necessary to pay close attention to their blood sugar control and provide scientific and effective sugar control treatment and active dietary adjustment to help control the patient's blood sugar levels, which is important for reducing postoperative complications.

Patients with fractures and diabetes are more complex in treatment and nursing than those with normal fractures, and they are prone to joint contracture and dysfunction. Early rehabilitation training can effectively prevent soft tissue contracture, muscle atrophy, and joint stiffness in diabetic lower limb fracture patients, and restore the movement function of the affected limb.
