# Cerebral Stroke - The Most Frightening Complication
Aging: Elderly patients with multiple internal diseases undergoing surgery
Perioperative cerebral infarction mortality is 10 times that of non-cerebral infarction mortality
53% of cerebral infarction patients are transferred to other hospitals after discharge
Minor stroke, big trouble

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Risk Factors for Perioperative Stroke
Age > 70
Previous history of cerebrovascular disease:
Carotid artery stenosis: 3% perioperative stroke with 50-99% unilateral carotid artery stenosis, 5% perioperative stroke with 50-99% bilateral carotid artery stenosis, 7-11% perioperative stroke with carotid artery occlusion
Discontinuation of anticoagulant drugs
Atrial fibrillation: 1601 cases of perioperative cerebral infarction, 27% with atrial fibrillation (history of atrial fibrillation, new atrial fibrillation)
Intraoperative hypoperfusion: General anesthesia, deep anesthesia, decreased vascular compliance, decreased blood pressure regulation ability, causing insufficient cerebral tissue perfusion, weakened cerebral blood flow brushing of microemboli, leading to cerebral infarction. Intraoperative blood pressure drop for a long time >20mmHg, more than 20% decrease from preoperative, postoperative complications increase, about 50% of thrombotic stroke occur on the first day after surgery
Incidence of perioperative stroke in general surgery is 0.08-0.7%

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Management of Perioperative Stroke
Assessment of preoperative medication: discontinue reserpine one week before surgery
Use of antithrombotic drugs
Preoperative carotid ultrasound examination
Carotid revascularization: carotid stent implantation, endarterectomy
Perioperative statin treatment: lipid-lowering therapy to reduce the incidence of cerebral stroke
     Simvastatin 40mg∕d   Decrease in incidence of ischemic stroke by 28%
     Pravastatin reduces the incidence of stroke by 32%, the incidence of stroke in the elderly group decreases by 40%
Identification of perioperative stroke
Intervention for perioperative atrial fibrillation
Prevention is the main focus for stroke

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Precautions for Lipid-Lowering Treatment in Elderly Patients
Monitoring of safety indicators: (AST, ALT, CK) AST, ALT should be discontinued if the normal upper limit is exceeded by 3 times
Myopathy is a serious adverse reaction, which can lead to rhabdomyolysis, with symptoms such as muscle weakness and myalgia, which are difficult to differentiate from senile bone, joint, and muscle diseases. CK (creatine kinase) needs to be checked
Discontinue the drug if CK exceeds the normal upper limit by 5 times (rosuvastatin)

# Identification of Perioperative Stroke
Unilateral limb weakness or numbness (with or without facial involvement)
Unilateral facial numbness or facial droop
Difficulty speaking or understanding language
Bilateral gaze to one side
Unilateral or binocular vision loss or blur
Dizziness with vomiting
Rarely severe headache, vomiting
Disorientation or seizures

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Intervention for Perioperative Atrial Fibrillation
Peak occurrence: 2-4 days after surgery
New-onset atrial fibrillation after surgery: spontaneously resolves in 4-6 weeks
Inducing factors: electrolyte imbalance, volume depletion, heart failure, infection.
Measures: correct electrolyte and fluid balance,
        apply beta-blockers, amiodarone
Anticoagulation therapy for 30 days after recovery to sinus rhythm
Low-molecular-weight heparin treatment for high-risk patients

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
# Perioperative Anticoagulation: Warfarin
Half-life of warfarin is 36-42 hours, discontinue 7 days before surgery
Elimination of 95% (15 days) after 5 half-lives
Minimize the duration of discontinuation, use low-molecular-weight heparin as a bridge during discontinuation
Perioperative stroke 0.6% without heparin during warfarin discontinuation
Perioperative stroke 0% with heparin during warfarin discontinuation
For patients who still have an INR > 1.5 one to two days before surgery, recommend small doses of vitamin K to reduce INR to normal if the risk of delaying surgery is greater than hypertension.

# Management of Elderly Hypertension in Perioperative Period
Characteristics of elderly hypertension
Systolic pressure increase  80% above pure systolic hypertension
Large blood pressure fluctuations, decreased arterial elasticity, decreased compliance, decreased sensitivity of pressure receptors
Prone to orthostatic hypotension
Prone to heart failure, stroke

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# 2011 Chinese Expert Consensus on the Diagnosis and Treatment of Elderly Hypertension
# 2011 American Heart Association Expert Consensus on the Treatment of Elderly Hypertension
Target blood pressure for elderly patients without complications: 140/90mmHg
Target systolic pressure for patients aged 80 and over: 140-145mmHg
Blood pressure target for patients with diabetes, chronic kidney disease, coronary heart disease, carotid artery disease, peripheral artery disease, abdominal aortic aneurysm: <130/80mmHg
Blood pressure target for patients with left ventricular dysfunction: <120/80mmHg
For patients under 80 years old with good general condition and able to tolerate antihypertensive drugs, blood pressure should be closely monitored at 130/80mmHg
Target blood pressure for patients with stable cerebrovascular disease: 140/90mmHg
For patients with bilateral carotid artery stenosis ≥70%,
Antihypertensive treatment should be cautious, systolic pressure maintained at 150mmHg, 150-160mmHg

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Management of Hypertension in Perioperative Period
Preoperative assessment of blood pressure: reduce intraoperative bleeding, postoperative cerebrovascular accidents
Elective surgery: 130-150/80-90mmHg is appropriate, diastolic pressure >65mmHg, <100mmHg
Elective surgery BP >180/100mmHg should be canceled
Emergency surgery BP >180/110mmHg, if the risk of delaying surgery is greater than hypertension, closely monitor
Reduce the dose of beta-blockers during inhalation anesthesia to prevent cardiac depression
Prevent hypotension: insufficient perfusion of important organs
Preoperative sedation and anti-anxiety drugs
Relieve pain, prevent urinary retention, nausea and vomiting, excessive fluid infusion
Lifestyle changes can reduce the dose of medication
Quit smoking, lose weight, reduce mental stress, limit salt, limit alcohol, strengthen physical exercise

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Perioperative Hypertension's Most Dangerous Complications
Heart: Rupture of aneurysm, myocardial ischemia, left heart failure
Brain: Intracranial hemorrhage, hypertensive encephalopathy 15%
Kidney: Renal ischemia 10%

# Perioperative Cardiovascular Risk Management
Perioperative Cardiovascular Risk Assessment
Pay attention to patient complaints, actively complete examinations
Most myocardial infarction patients show chest pain symptoms 4 hours after the onset, and serum troponin begins to rise, serving as a unit index for the size of myocardial infarction and the degree of necrosis. Repeat measurements every 6 to 8 hours
Actively improve anemia after surgery
Prevent constipation
Patients who have received drug stent placement should delay non-cardiac surgery to 6 months postoperatively

# Postoperative Cognitive Dysfunction in Elderly Patients
Diagnostic criteria of the American Psychiatric Association
History of surgery and anesthesia
Decreased awareness of the environment with transfer, and decreased attention to maintaining environmental stimuli
Disorientation and memory loss
   At least two of the following: 1) Perceptual disturbances  2) Incoherent language
   3) Disruption of sleep-wake rhythm  4) Increased or decreased neurologic activity
Clinical manifestations persist for several hours, several days, and sometimes fluctuate within a day

# Clock Drawing Test
This test is simple to operate and has minimal interference from external factors
The sensitivity and specificity for detecting dementia patients is up to 90%
The commonly used method is the 4-point method, where the total score is 4 points, completing a closed circle is 1 point, correct time and position is 1 point, complete 12 numbers is 1 point, correct pointer position is 1 point, and normal value is >2 points.

# Blood Glucose Control in Elderly Orthopedic Patients During Perioperative Period
About 10-15% are diabetic patients
The mortality rate of diabetic patients undergoing surgery is 1.5 times higher than that of non-diabetic patients
Anxiety, anesthesia, surgery, fever all lead to stress response, increased insulin demand, and increased secretion of insulin antagonistic hormones

<!-- Slide number: 25 -->
# Poor Blood Glucose Control During Perioperative Period
Especially in elderly, long-term patients
Anesthesia accidents
Wound dehiscence, infection
Neuropathy: Gastrointestinal paresis 75% postoperatively, hypotension
Heart: Painless myocardial ischemia, heart failure, arrhythmia
Brain: Transient ischemic attack (TIA), cerebral infarction
Peripheral vascular: Deep vein thrombosis, arterial embolism

<!-- Slide number: 26 -->
# Blood Glucose Control Standards
Elective surgery:
  Control target: Fasting blood glucose 6-8mmol/L
                    Postprandial blood glucose 8-11mmol/L
Emergency surgery: Correction of diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar coma is necessary, Glu <14mmol/L

# Management of Perioperative Blood Glucose
Before returning to normal diet, intravenous infusion of glucose injection + insulin
After returning to eating, resume oral hypoglycemic agents, and pay attention to the timing
Generally adopt the treatment plan of short-acting insulin before meals and long-acting insulin at bedtime

# Precautions for Blood Glucose Control During Perioperative Period
Stop metformin 3 days before surgery (easier to cause lactic acidosis)
Stop sulfonylurea insulin secretagogues (increase the incidence of myocardial ischemia during anesthesia due to hypoglycemia)
It is not supported to strictly control blood glucose after surgery
Increase the risk of hypoglycemia, will not reduce infection and wound healing

<!-- Slide number: 29 -->
# Perioperative Respiratory Management
Quit smoking on admission
Treat chronic bronchitis
Preoperative pulmonary function assessment: chest X-ray, blood gas, pulmonary function
Rational oxygen therapy: elderly patients have a certain degree of hypoxemia
Respiratory function exercise: deep inhalation - hold breath - forceful cough three times a day, each time for more than 5 minutes
For patients with pulmonary interstitial fibrosis: low carbohydrate, high-quality protein diet
