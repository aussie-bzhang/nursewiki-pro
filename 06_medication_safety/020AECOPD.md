**Introduction**
Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) is a chronic lung disease characterized by incomplete reversibility of airflow limitation, with progressive airflow limitation and increased chronic inflammation of the airways and lungs in response to harmful particles or gases.

**COPD**
According to prevalence surveys, nearly 1 in 4 adults over 40 years of age have symptoms of airway obstruction. With age, the prevalence of COPD shows a significant increasing trend. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that COPD ranks first in the burden of disease in China, and has become a serious public health problem.

### Notes:

**Herpes Zoster**
Herpes zoster is a skin disease caused by varicella-zoster virus (VZV) infection, which only occurs in individuals who have had chickenpox or are asymptomatic carriers. It is more common in adults, especially the elderly.

Herpes zoster is a common skin disease, and its incidence increases with age. The incidence is less than 1 in 1000 in individuals under 45 years of age, and it increases to more than 4 times in individuals over 75 years of age.
**References**: R B Odell, W D James, T G. Berger. Andrews' Clinical Dermatology. 9th Edition. Beijing: Beijing Science and Technology Press, 2004. 475.

Case Introduction—Treatment Process
October 2

The patient was in poor spirits, with blood gas results indicating type II respiratory failure. Non-invasive ventilation was provided for respiratory support, and symptomatic treatment was administered for anti-infection, expectoration, and asthma, with local wound dressing changes.

October 4

The patient experienced respiratory muscle fatigue, unable to cooperate with non-invasive ventilation, increased sputum production, and thick sputum. Blood gas analysis showed decompensated state. Tracheal intubation via the nose was performed, and invasive ventilation was connected for respiratory support, with local wound dressing changes.

October 13

The patient's respiratory failure improved, with reduced sputum production. The tracheal tube was removed, invasive ventilation was discontinued, and non-invasive ventilation was resumed, with local wound dressing changes.

Non-invasive ventilation was discontinued, and nasal cannula low-flow oxygen therapy was initiated, with local wound dressing changes.

October 18
