# PCI Preoperative Risk Assessment
The perioperative risks of PCI include death, myocardial infarction, no/slow flow, thrombosis, malignant arrhythmias, contrast-induced nephropathy, and bleeding, etc.

It is necessary to assess early to maximize the avoidance of perioperative risks.

Interventional patients
Initial dose: 3000~5000U
Subsequent: 1000U追加 per hour to maintain ACT ≥225s or 300s
Postoperative: Discontinue immediately in the absence of complications; continuous use should not exceed 48h

# Standardized Use of LMWH
Simple to use: Standardized use for different varieties
During interventional surgery
Last dose before surgery <8h, no additional dose

# Standardized Application of Fondaparinux (Ando)
Anticoagulation during interventional surgery: Not recommended for use alone, must be combined with IIa factor inhibitors; Fondaparinux dose is:
Combined with 2b/3a: 2.5mg
Not combined with 2b/3a: 5mg

# Standardized Application of Bivalirudin
IV injection of 0.75mg/kg as a loading dose, then immediate IV infusion of 1.75mg/kg/h to maintain until the end of surgery; if clinically necessary, postoperative maintenance should not exceed 4 hours.

# Attention to Accumulation Effect in LMWH Use in Patients with Renal Insufficiency

Large molecular fragments have more negative charges and are mainly cleared through the reticuloendothelial system

In renal insufficiency, decreased GFR leads to LMWH being difficult to clear, resulting in "accumulation effect" in the body
The "accumulation effect" triggers safety issues of LMWH

# Secondary Prevention After PCI
Beta-blockers: Unless contraindicated, long-term use should be recommended for patients with MI, ACS, left ventricular dysfunction (with or without symptoms of heart failure)
ACEI: Unless contraindicated, all patients with LVEF≤40% and hypertension, diabetes, or chronic kidney disease should start and continue long-term ACEI; for those who cannot tolerate ACEI, angiotensin receptor blockers can be used
Aldosterone Antagonists: It is recommended for patients with MI without obvious renal dysfunction or hyperkalemia, who have already received therapeutic doses of ACEI and beta-blockers, LVEF≤40%, and have diabetes or heart failure

# Secondary Prevention After PCI
Antihypertensive Treatment: Initial treatment with beta-blockers and/or ACEI, and other antihypertensive drugs may be added if necessary, with blood pressure controlled at ≤140/90 mm Hg; for patients with chronic kidney disease or diabetes, blood pressure should be reduced more aggressively, with a target of <130/80 mm Hg
Lipid-Lowering Treatment: The treatment goal is to reduce LDL-C to <2.60 mmol/L (100mg/dl). For patients at very high risk (such as ACS, diabetes), LDL-C should be <1.8 mmol/L (70mg/dl)
Diabetes Treatment: Lifestyle adjustments and medication should be made to achieve HbA1c <6.5%

# Postoperative Follow-up for PCI Patients
The attending physician in the ward arranges the follow-up visit time and doctor for the patient before discharge

The ward nurse responsible for follow-up enters the patient's detailed information into the follow-up system

The interventional follow-up doctor schedules the next follow-up visit after the patient's first visit

If there is a change in the condition, the follow-up clinic and follow-up doctor are responsible for contacting the hospital

# PCI Postoperative Specialized, Personalized, Professional Follow-up
Specialized Clinic (Coronary Intervention Scheduling and Follow-up Clinic)

Each PCI patient is scheduled for a postoperative follow-up clinic once a week

Outpatient Registration Support: A dedicated person is responsible for scheduling follow-up and medical record management

Human Resources Allocation: Dedicated follow-up personnel (doctors and specialized nurses)

Systematic patient education: Strengthening patients' trust in doctors and improving treatment compliance through appropriate communication methods
