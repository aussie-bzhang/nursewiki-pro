# Perioperative Blood Pressure Control

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# I. Definition of Perioperative Period
The perioperative period encompasses the entire process surrounding surgery, from the patient's decision to undergo surgical treatment to the completion of basic recovery. This includes the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative phases, and is specifically defined as the time from the determination of surgical treatment to the basic completion of related treatments, approximately 5-7 days before surgery to 7-12 days after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Diagnosis and Grading of Hypertension
Ideal blood pressure: Systolic blood pressure (SBP) <120 mmHg, diastolic blood pressure (DBP) <80 mmHg;
Normal blood pressure: SBP <130 mmHg, DBP <85 mmHg;
Normal high limit: SBP 130-139 mmHg, DBP 85-89 mmHg;
Grade 1 hypertension: SBP 140-159 mmHg, DBP 90-99 mmHg (subgroup: critical hypertension SBP 140-149 mmHg, DBP 90-94 mmHg);
Grade 2 hypertension: SBP 160-179 mmHg, DBP 100-109 mmHg;
Grade 3 hypertension: SBP ≥180, DBP ≥110 mmHg;
Isolated systolic hypertension: SBP ≥140 mmHg, DBP <90 mmHg (subgroup: critical systolic hypertension SBP 140-149 mmHg, DBP <90 mmHg).
When SBP and DBP belong to different grades, the higher grade is used as the standard.

# II. Preoperative Control
7th Edition of General Surgery, page 128
Patients with hypertension should continue to take antihypertensive medications to avoid withdrawal syndrome.
Blood pressure below 160/100 mmHg does not require special preparation.
For patients with high blood pressure (＞180/100 mmHg), appropriate antihypertensive drugs should be selected preoperatively to maintain blood pressure at a stable level, but it is not necessary to reduce it to normal before surgery.
For patients with a history of hypertension who experience a sudden increase in blood pressure upon entering the operating room, the anesthesiologist should be involved in the management. The decision to perform or delay surgery should be made based on the patient's condition and the nature of the surgery.

# Blood pressure at which to consider delaying or canceling surgery
No unified guidelines
Dix and Howell's survey: Most physicians believe that blood pressure >160/90-100 mmHg should be delayed or canceled for anesthesia and elective surgery, especially in patients with coexisting clinical conditions
Elective surgery with blood pressure >180/100-110 mmHg
Patients with blood pressure >180/110 mmHg upon arrival at the operating room on the morning of surgery, if blood pressure <200/100 mmHg without brain or cardiovascular symptoms, the blood pressure can be reduced to an allowable level with medication before induction; if blood pressure >200/110 mmHg, the surgery should be delayed
Emergency surgery with blood pressure >180/110 mmHg, if the risk of delaying surgery exceeds that of hypertension, it is advisable to maintain blood pressure around 140/90 mmHg under close monitoring

# Blood pressure control target
For patients with stable blood pressure still above normal, in line with the WHO/ISH diagnostic criteria for primary hypertension and those with a history of hypertension, appropriate antihypertensive treatment before surgery is crucial for preventing severe blood pressure fluctuations and cardiovascular and pulmonary accidents during surgery. The blood pressure control target is to reduce it to normal or "ideal" levels. For middle-aged and young patients or those with diabetes, blood pressure should be reduced to 135/85 mmHg or within the normal range; for patients ≥60 years old, blood pressure should be reduced below 140/90 mmHg. The rate and method of blood pressure reduction should be based on the patient's general condition, the function of the main organs such as the heart, brain, and kidneys, and the urgency of the surgery, and reasonable antihypertensive measures should be selected. However, it is crucial to avoid a sharp drop in blood pressure, unless there are hypertensive emergencies such as hypertensive crisis, hypertensive encephalopathy, rapidly progressive hypertension, acute left heart failure, or severe hypertension during surgery, in which case intravenous antihypertensive drugs are generally not recommended.

# Principles of antihypertensive drug therapy
Currently, the World Health Organization (WHO) and the International Society of Hypertension (ISH) recommend six major classes of antihypertensive drugs, namely diuretics, β-blockers, calcium channel blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), and α1-adrenergic receptor blockers. The appropriate drug should be selected based on the patient's condition and comorbidities. The current principles of antihypertensive drug therapy are as follows: ① For grades 1 and 2 hypertension, treatment should start with a low dose of any drug to minimize side effects. ② Preferably use once-daily, long-acting drugs with a duration of action of 24 hours. ③ Rational combination therapy should be used to achieve the maximum antihypertensive effect with the least side effects. Generally, it is preferable to use a second drug of a non-similar class rather than increasing the dose of the first drug. ④ For patients who need to fast before surgery and must discontinue oral medications, β-blockers should be avoided as these drugs are prone to withdrawal syndrome.

# Sodium nitroprusside
It is the first-line drug for early hypertensive crisis and a short-acting intravenous antihypertensive drug. Its nitroso group decomposes to produce NO, causing vasodilation, which can simultaneously dilate arteries and veins.
It takes effect immediately, and the effect only lasts 3-5 minutes after discontinuation of infusion, with mild side effects.
Usage: Infusion: 25/50 mg sodium nitroprusside + 50 ml 5% GS, starting at 1 ml/h, which can be adjusted to 3-5 ml/h later.
Intravenous drip: 25 mg sodium nitroprusside + 250 ml 5% GS intravenous drip, rate: 8-12 drops/minute.
It should be noted that whether infusion or intravenous drip, the medication speed needs to be controlled precisely, and light protection should be observed.
Caution should be exercised in the presence of intracranial hypertension.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Nitroglycerin
It is most suitable for patients with pre-existing heart failure or those with acute myocardial infarction during hypertensive crisis. It can dilate coronary arteries, veins, and large arteries simultaneously, but its effect on small arteries is less than that of sodium nitroprusside.
Nitroglycerin has a good short-term effect when used for a short period, but it can cause tolerance when used for a long time. For difficult-to-control hypertensive crises, alternating infusion of nitroglycerin and sodium nitroprusside can be used.
Usage: Infusion: 10 mg nitroglycerin + 50 ml 5% GS, starting at 1 ml/h, which can be adjusted to 3-5 ml/h later.

# Furosemide (Lasix)
Indicated for hypertensive emergencies with acute left heart failure due to fluid overload, with rapid and strong antihypertensive effects, reducing both pre- and post-load on the heart, and excessive use can lead to hypokalemia and hypotension.
It should be noted that strong diuretics should not be used in the early stages, except for patients with congestive heart failure or fluid volume overload.
Patients with hypertensive emergencies have sympathetic nervous system activation and overactivation of the RAS system.
Peripheral vascular resistance is significantly increased, and there is insufficient intravascular volume, with a risk of hypotension and inadequate perfusion when diuretics are used.
Usage: 40-120 mg of Lasix + 10 ml of 5% GS for intravenous push.

# Urapidil (Yanningding)
Used for the treatment of hypertensive emergencies (such as sudden increase in blood pressure), severe and very severe hypertension, and refractory hypertension; used for controlling perioperative hypertension.
Usage: Initially, a slow intravenous injection of 10-50 mg, and monitor blood pressure changes.
The effect is displayed within 5 minutes and maintains antihypertensive effects: dilute 100 mg to 50 ml (NS, 5-10% GS, 5% fructose can be used), the maximum drug concentration is 4 mg/ml, the initial infusion rate is 2 mg/min, the maintenance dose is 9 mg/h, if 250 mg of urapidil is dissolved in 500 ml of fluid, then 1 mg of urapidil is equivalent to 44 drops or 2.2 ml of infusion fluid. 250 mg (50 ml) infused at 1.2 ml/h (100ug/min), 100 mg + 500 ml fluid infused at 1 ml/min (200ug/min).

# Labetalol
Indicated for hypertensive emergencies in patients with pregnancy or renal insufficiency.
Due to its reduction in RAS system activity, it can improve renal function. Less than 5% is excreted unchanged through the kidneys, suitable for patients with renal impairment.
It has both α and β dual resistance effects, reducing peripheral vascular resistance without the drawback of reflex tachycardia.
Usage: Intravenous push: 25-50 mg of labetalol + 20 ml of 10% GS, infused over 5-10 minutes.
Intravenous infusion: 100 ml of 10% GS + 100 mg of labetalol, infused over 0.5-1.5 hours, adjust the dose according to blood pressure.

# Phentolamine
A non-selective α-adrenergic antagonist, indicated for hypertensive emergencies caused by pheochromocytoma and hypertension due to excessive use of sympathomimetic drugs such as adrenaline.
It has a mild vasodilatory effect and a short duration of action.
Usage: 20 mg of phentolamine + 250 ml of 5% GS, initial infusion rate 25 drops/min, adjust according to blood pressure.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Clonidine
A central sympathetic nervous system inhibitor, which激动s peripheral sympathetic α2 receptors, reduces the release of norepinephrine from peripheral nerves, can reduce peripheral and renal vascular resistance, slow heart rate, and lower blood pressure.
The drug takes effect in 2 minutes and has a high efficacy rate in patients with moderate to severe hypertension.
It has central inhibitory effects, which can cause drowsiness in patients, and should be used with caution in patients with acute cerebral hemorrhage.
Usage: Oral initial dose 0.2 mg, followed by 0.1 mg per hour until diastolic pressure is controlled or the total dose reaches 0.7 mg, then use maintenance dose.

# Nicardipine
A dihydropyridine class calcium channel blocker with the action of dilating peripheral vessels, coronary arteries, renal arterioles, and cerebral arteries, with weak inhibitory effect on the heart.
主要用于合并脑血管疾病的高血压急症。Dilating blood vessels while reducing cerebral spasm, effectively improving cerebral blood flow, and avoiding intracranial hemorrhage.
Contraindicated in acute cerebral edema and increased intracranial pressure to avoid the formation of cerebral hernia.
Usage: Intravenous infusion: 100 ml of 0.9% NS or 5% GS + 12 ml of nicardipine, infusion rate 6-10 drops/min, adjust according to blood pressure.
