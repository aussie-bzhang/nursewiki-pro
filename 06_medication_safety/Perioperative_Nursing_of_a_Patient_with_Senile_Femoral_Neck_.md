## Introduction

Femoral neck fracture refers to a fracture between the femoral head and the greater trochanter, commonly occurring in the elderly and primarily caused by indirect violence, with a higher incidence in females than in males.

Causes:
Osteoporosis in the elderly makes the femoral neck fragile.
Atrophy of the hip muscle group, combined with minor external forces such as twisting, can lead to femoral neck fractures in the elderly.

Lu Jingbo. Orthopedic Nursing [M]. Beijing: China

## Introduction

Femoral neck fracture is a common disease in the elderly, and surgery is the main treatment method. Effective rehabilitation and exercise after surgery are important measures to ensure the effectiveness of the surgery.
Hip arthroplasty is a reparative surgery that uses an artificial femoral head to replace the original femoral head to restore hip joint function, which is a commonly used treatment method in orthopedics.

## Analysis of Delirium Causes

Age factor: For patients with hip fracture surgery, compared to patients aged 65-75, those over 75 have a 3-fold higher incidence of delirium. The main reason is that as patients age, their brain tissue degenerative changes become more obvious, and under the central nervous system neurotransmitter content decrease, delirium occurs.

Underlying diseases: Elderly patients have more comorbidities before surgery, such as hypoxemia, diabetes, and hypertension, etc. The more comorbidities, the higher the risk of delirium. The reason is that under comorbid diseases, elderly patients are more difficult to tolerate the surgical process and anesthesia, which can lead to brain dysfunction such as cerebral edema or cerebral hypoxia, increasing the likelihood of postoperative delirium, leading to delirium.

Stress response: Hip fracture patients admitted to the hospital are mostly due to acute trauma, and stress symptoms such as fear and pain are extremely obvious. In addition to postoperative discomfort, pain, and excessive mental tension, it is easy to cause delirium.

# Reflections and Shortcomings
How to conduct a comprehensive assessment of elderly patients with femoral neck fractures, considering the individualization and feasibility of nursing measures is very important.

Applying evidence-based thinking helps solve patient problems, guidance for discharged patients is important, but implementing it is indeed challenging, how to more comprehensively monitor the condition of patients after discharge needs to be improved.

As a specialist nurse, interdisciplinary collaboration and comprehensive intervention yield more significant results.

Through case tracking, a more comprehensive understanding of nursing care for patients with femoral neck fractures and diabetes has been gained.
