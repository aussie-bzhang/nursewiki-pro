## Treatment of Perioperative Arrhythmias
Principles of perioperative arrhythmia treatment
   1. Severe arrhythmias must be treated immediately, even in an emergency, such as ventricular fibrillation and flutter, ventricular tachycardia, polymorphic ventricular premature complexes, RonT phenomenon, and third-degree atrioventricular block.
   2. When arrhythmias have a significant impact on hemodynamics, they should also be treated promptly, while analyzing the cause and eliminating the trigger.
   3. If the hemodynamics can maintain relative stability during arrhythmias, analyze the cause, eliminate the trigger, and provide appropriate treatment.

## Electrical Treatment Methods for Arrhythmias
Pacing
(1) Supraventricular pacing to prevent arrhythmias
   Atrial or ventricular pacing at a frequency higher than the own heart rate, which is used to suppress any type of tachyarrhythmia and ventricular premature complexes (PVCs).

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
## Electrical Treatment Methods for Arrhythmias
Pacing
(2) Rapid atrial pacing to terminate tachyarrhythmias
Ｃ：Pacing at a rate 20 bpm faster than the own heart rate. After capture, pacing is immediately stopped. If it fails, increase the frequency by 10 bpm and repeat the above process. This method is ineffective when the frequency is increased beyond 100 bpm faster than the tachycardia rate, and it may induce atrial fibrillation.

## Electrical Treatment Methods for Arrhythmias
Direct Current (DC) Shock
(1) Method
Ａ：Synchronous (defibrillation): the discharge time is triggered by the QRS wave on the ECG, which avoids shock during the vulnerable period of the T wave.
Ｂ：Asynchronous (defibrillation): shock is not considered in relation to the ECG.

## Electrical Treatment Methods for Arrhythmias
Direct Current (DC) Shock
(2) Indications
　Ａ：Ventricular fibrillation requires defibrillation. When it is difficult to distinguish between a single QRS wave and the T wave (this type is similar to a sine wave), rapid ventricular tachycardia requires defibrillation.

## Electrical Treatment Methods for Arrhythmias
Direct Current (DC) Shock
(2) Indications
Ｂ：Atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, SVT, and VT with obvious QRS waves and hemodynamic stability require cardioversion. The decision to choose electrical cardioversion instead of medication is usually based on the severity of hemodynamic changes.

# Procedure
Connect the power, ground and limb lead lines (upper right red, left yellow, lower black green)

   Set the switch to AC or battery (if using battery, the battery indicator light must show normal voltage)

   Press the extracorporeal defibrillation and non-synchronous keys, charge (first 200J, then 300, 360J)

Apply salt water gauze evenly to the apex of the heart (left clavicle, midline, fourth intercostal space), right second intercostal space, sternal旁

After charging, discharge, the operator needs to press the electrode plate tightly against the patient's skin, wait for everyone to leave the bed before discharging, and observe the ECG waveform after discharging

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# Precautions
1 The defibrillator should be placed at a fixed location (1 bed), managed and maintained by a designated person, and checked at any time to ensure the normal function of the instrument

2 Charge regularly to ensure that the battery voltage is within the normal range, and be prepared for the need when the backup instrument is out of power

3 During defibrillation, the electrode plate should be tightly adhered to the patient's skin to prevent skin burns

4 If sinus rhythm is not restored after defibrillation, defibrillation can be repeated after 5 minutes of chest compressions

5 Maintain circulatory stability after recovery, correct blood gas and electrolyte disorders

# Diagnosis and Treatment of Arrhythmias
Sinus bradycardia:
（1）When hypotension or heart rate <40~50bpm persists, atropine 0.5~1mg intravenous injection, isoproterenol 1~5μg/min or a pacemaker can be used.
（2）When blood pressure is normal or heart rate >40~50bpm, strengthen monitoring and do not treat temporarily.
（3）Do not treat temporarily when hypothermia occurs.

# Diagnosis and Treatment of Arrhythmias
Atrial fibrillation:
　Originating from multiple reentry or existence of multiple foci of ectopic activation within the atrium. No P wave, replaced by irregularly sized and shaped f waves. The ventricular rhythm is absolutely irregular.

# Diagnosis and Treatment of Arrhythmias
Atrial fibrillation
	Appearance of atrial fibrillation during surgery: electrical cardioversion; improve cardiac function (dopamine, etc.).
	Appearance of atrial fibrillation after surgery: improve cardiac function, appropriately supplement volume.
	Pre-existing atrial fibrillation before surgery: return to sinus rhythm after recovery, supplement magnesium, improve cardiac function.

# Diagnosis and Treatment of Arrhythmias
Atrial, junctional premature beats
（1）Non-frequent cases do not require treatment

# Diagnosis and Treatment of Arrhythmias
Ventricular premature beat:
　Originating from spontaneous ventricular activation or reentry activation, QRS complex is wide, deformed and premature.
　Ventricular tachycardia: three or more PVCs occur consecutively, heart rate >100bpm.

# Diagnosis and Treatment of Arrhythmias
Ventricular premature beat:
（1）Occasional ventricular premature beats do not require treatment, pay attention to observation.
（2）Frequent or multifocal ventricular premature beats, lidocaine is首选 (single intravenous injection 1~1.5mg/kg, maintained with 4mg/min intravenous infusion after effective), and atropine (0.5~1.0mg) can be added if sinus bradycardia is present.

# Arrhythmia Diagnosis and Treatment
Ventricular Premature Complexes:
(3) If lidocaine is ineffective, amiodarone 150~300mg can be administered intravenously over 10 minutes. The injection should be stopped immediately after conversion, and then maintained with intravenous infusion, with a total dose of 1000~1200mg in the first 24 hours.

# Arrhythmia Diagnosis and Treatment
Ventricular Tachycardia:
(1) Lidocaine, procainamide, or bretylium can be used.
(2) If suspected of digoxin toxicity, phenytoin sodium 100mg can be administered intravenously, with 100mg given every 5 minutes, with a maximum dose of less than 1000mg.

# Arrhythmia Diagnosis and Treatment
Ventricular Tachycardia:
(3) If drug therapy is ineffective, direct current cardioversion can be used. If immediate cardioversion is not possible, percussing the patient's chest with a fist may also stop the episode.
(4) For ventricular tachycardia caused by digoxin overdose, electrical cardioversion should not be the first-line treatment. Electrical cardioversion is not suitable for torsade de pointes ventricular tachycardia.

Atrioventricular Block:

# Arrhythmia Diagnosis and Treatment
Atrioventricular Block:
(1) First-degree and second-degree atrioventricular block may not require treatment.
(2) Second-degree atrioventricular block with hemodynamic instability or third-degree atrioventricular block requires atropine, isoprenaline, or ventricular pacing.
