Diabetic patients, in addition to monitoring fasting, three meals after, and bedtime blood glucose profiles, are recommended to have preoperative HbA1C testing. ### Notes:
Targeting Mechanisms of Antidiabetic Drugs for Type 2 Diabetes

In recent years, many new antidiabetic drugs have been developed that can be used alone, in combination with multiple drugs, or in combination with insulin. These drugs have different mechanisms of action. These achievements provide doctors with more options for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and offer more choices for combined therapy.
The mechanism of action of biguanide drugs (e.g., metformin) primarily reduces the output of glucose from the liver by reducing liver glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis, in addition to increasing the uptake of glucose by muscle tissue.
Sulfonylurea drugs (e.g., glimepiride, glibenclamide) and meglitinide drugs (e.g., repaglinide, nateglinide) are insulin secretagogues that increase the secretion of insulin by β-cells in the pancreas. Sulfonylurea drugs bind to specific receptors on the surface of β-cells in the pancreas, causing a series of changes in the cell membrane and intracellular, leading to the release of insulin granules. Meglitinide drugs also act on specific receptors on the surface of β-cells in the pancreas, but cause a more rapid and shorter-lasting insulin secretion.
The action of α-glucosidase inhibitors is not targeted at any pathological basis of type 2 diabetes, but rather by inhibiting α-glucosidase and other enzymes on the brush border of small intestinal epithelial cells, thus blocking the process of oligosaccharides, disaccharides, and other substances being decomposed into monosaccharides and absorbed.
Thiazolidinediones (e.g., rosiglitazone, pioglitazone) are peripheral tissue insulin sensitizers. They bind to nuclear receptors in muscle and fat tissues to activate them, increasing the expression of certain genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism.
DPP-4 inhibitors can increase the levels of endogenous GLP-1 and GIP by selectively inhibiting DPP-4, thereby regulating blood glucose.

Application of Oral Antidiabetic Drugs Before Surgery

Sulfonylurea and meglitinide drugs may cause hypoglycemia, so they should be discontinued 24 hours before surgery;

Metformin should be discontinued 24-48 hours before surgery in patients with renal insufficiency or those using intravenous contrast agents;

Oral antidiabetic drugs can be retained for short, non-fasted, local anesthesia surgery.

Application of Insulin Before Surgery
Patients who have been using insulin before admission generally use a combined regimen of long-acting insulin to control basal blood glucose and short-acting insulin to control postprandial blood glucose.

Patients using subcutaneous insulin pumps should retain the insulin basal dose.

Consider indications for complete discontinuation of the original insulin regimen:
1) Necessary intravenous infusion of insulin
    1) Major surgery with a long duration and inability to eat on the day after surgery
    2) Complete dependence on subcutaneous short-acting insulin treatment before surgery
    3) Lack of professional personnel to manage subcutaneous insulin pumps in the hospital

Elderly patients have a high risk of surgery, slow postoperative wound healing, and a high incidence of complications.

Blood Glucose Control Goals During the Perioperative Period

It is recommended that patients with normal diet control preprandial blood glucose ≤7.8mmol/L, postprandial blood glucose and random blood glucose ≤10.0mmol/L. Blood glucose should not exceed 10.0mmol/L during fasting. It is not recommended to control blood glucose too strictly, and it is more appropriate to control blood glucose between 7.8mmol/L and 10.0mmol/L during surgery and after surgery. During the transition period in the PACU, blood glucose can be transferred to the ward when it reaches the range of 4.0mmol/L to 12.0mmol/L.
For critically ill patients with a hospital stay of ≥3 days in the ICU after surgery, a blood glucose target value of ≤8.4mmol/L is recommended.
For patients with long-term elevated blood glucose, it is not advisable to decrease blood glucose too quickly during the perioperative period. Compared with hyperglycemia, the risk of perioperative mortality is higher when blood glucose fluctuates. Establish individualized goals around the preoperative baseline. Patients with cerebrovascular diseases have poor tolerance to hypoglycemia, and the target value can be relaxed to ≤12.0mmol/L. The highest blood glucose should not exceed 13.9mmol/L.
For plastic surgery, it is recommended to reduce the blood glucose target to 6.0mmol/L to 8.0mmol/L to reduce the risk of postoperative wound infection.

      Precautions for the Use of Radiographic Contrast Media

AKI can be diagnosed if any of the following conditions are met:

# Typical Issues in Elderly Medication - Drug Interactions
Clopidogrel + PPIs (CYP2C19, 3A4)
    Avoid Omeprazole in PPI selection, and prefer Pantoprazole as much as possible.

### Notes:
Clopidogrel is a prodrug that is metabolized by CYP2C19 and 3A4 to become an active drug and exert antiplatelet aggregation function. PPIs are mainly metabolized by CYP2C19 and 3A4, and they competitively inhibit each other. In vitro experiments have shown that Omeprazole, Esomeprazole, Lansoprazole, Pantoprazole, and Rabeprazole all have competitive inhibitory effects on CYP2C19. Rabeprazole's main metabolic pathway is non-enzymatic degradation, with few isoenzymes involved, but its metabolite Rabeprazole thioether has a strong inhibitory effect on CYP2C19. Pantoprazole has a unique II phase metabolic pathway, and when the I phase metabolism is occupied, it can be metabolized through the II phase pathway, thus it is less likely to cause drug interactions, and therefore has the weakest inhibitory effect on CYP2C19.
The analysis of individual PPIs has found that concurrent administration of Pantoprazole is not associated with recurrence of cardiovascular events and rehospitalization, while concurrent administration of Omeprazole, Lansoprazole, or Rabeprazole increases the risk of cardiovascular recurrence events by 40%.

# Typical Issues in Elderly Medication - Adverse Drug Reactions

The American Geriatrics Society 2012 Beers Criteria Update Expert Panel. American Geriatrics Society Updated Beers Criteria for Potentially Inappropriate Medication Use in Older Adults[J]. J Am Geriatr Soc, 2012, 60(4):616-631.

Yan Xuelian. Newly Revised Beers Criteria. Chinese Journal of Geriatric Medicine 2012,31(7):636-638.

### Notes:
In 1991, based on a literature review, a panel of experts from the American Geriatrics Society, pharmacy, nursing, and psychopharmacology, among others, formed a consensus and established the Beers Criteria for judging potential inappropriate medication use in elderly patients. The Beers Criteria have been revised several times and are widely used in nursing homes, outpatient clinics, and inpatient settings worldwide for the medication use survey of elderly patients. It has played a positive role in identifying potential inappropriate medication issues, reducing the related problems and treatment costs caused by unreasonable medication use. Recently, AGS released the latest 2012 version of the Beers Criteria. The Beers Criteria list inappropriate medication use unrelated to disease conditions, inappropriate medication use in certain disease states, and drugs that the elderly should use with caution. Each suggestion is accompanied by evidence-based medicine strength and recommendation strength.
