**Definition of Femoral Neck Fracture**

Femoral neck fracture refers to a fracture between the subcapital region of the femoral head and the base of the femoral neck. It is more common in the elderly.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
## Clinical Manifestations

Pain
Deformity
Swelling
Dysfunction

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
## Treatment

**Conservative Treatment:**
Continuous skin traction: Traditional Chinese medicine application, fomentation therapy, TDP therapy, etc. Suitable for fractures with no obvious displacement and interposed fractures.

**Surgical Treatment:**
Artificial hip arthroplasty: Suitable for elderly patients over 60 years old with subcapital fractures that have obvious displacement or rotation.
Internal fixation of the femoral neck: Suitable for interlocked fractures and fractures with displacement.

## Preoperative Nursing Diagnosis

Pain——Related to muscle injury and swelling.
1. Assess the location, nature, time, frequency, accompanying symptoms, and measures taken to alleviate pain.
2. Administer analgesics as prescribed, such as tramadol, and closely observe for adverse reactions.
3. Provide psychological guidance, advise to relax, and perform appropriate massage to distract attention.

Nursing Evaluation: Patient's pain is relieved.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
## Preoperative Nursing Diagnosis

Risk of skin integrity impairment——Related to long-term bed rest and limb immobilization due to fracture.
1. Assess the local condition of the compressed skin, such as the range and location.
2. Provide water cushions to protect the locally compressed skin.
3. Keep the skin and bedding clean and dry.
4. Encourage the consumption of high-calorie, high-protein, and high-vitamin foods such as chicken, fish, meat, eggs, milk, and more fruits and vegetables.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
## Preoperative Nursing Diagnosis

Anxiety——Related to concerns about prognosis and unfamiliar environment
1. Greet patients warmly, introduce the hospital environment, roommates, and eliminate unfamiliarity.
2. Communicate actively with patients, understand their psychological state and reasons for anxiety, and provide targeted psychological counseling.
3. Introduce the technical level of the attending physician and successful cases, introduce the relevant matters of the surgery, and encourage them to establish confidence in overcoming the disease.

Nursing Evaluation: Patient's emotions are stable and they actively cooperate.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
## Preoperative Nursing Diagnosis

Knowledge Deficiency——Related to lack of knowledge about preoperative preparation
1. Explain the purpose and significance of preoperative preparation.
2. Introduce the method, time, and anesthesia method of the surgery.
3. Instruct to fast for 6 hours.
4. Instruct to remove metal accessories such as bracelets, watches, rings, and dentures.

Nursing Evaluation: Patient understands and cooperates.

## Postoperative Nursing

Fasting for 6 hours.
Monitor vital signs, continuous oxygen inhalation.
Closely observe the wound drainage volume and wound bleeding.
Administer cefuroxime sodium to prevent infection, ambroxol atomization inhalation to prevent airway obstruction by sputum, danhong huoxue huayu, and compound bone peptide to promote bone metabolism, TDP irradiation to improve blood circulation, low molecular weight heparin calcium, and pressure waves to prevent deep vein thrombosis.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
## Postoperative Nursing Issues

Pain——Related to surgical trauma
1. Assess the location, nature, degree, and duration of the patient's pain, encourage them to express their feelings, and provide emotional comfort.
2. Instruct the patient to perform deep breathing, distract attention, and relax all muscle groups.
3. Improve a quiet and comfortable environment to reduce external stimulation.
4. Observe the effectiveness of the analgesic pump. If necessary, analgesics can be administered as prescribed.

Nursing Evaluation: Patient's pain is relieved.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
## Postoperative Nursing Issues

Risk of joint dislocation
1. Explain the importance of preventing dislocation to the patient.
2. After surgery, maintain the affected limb in an external rotation of 30° neutral position, wear a figure-of-eight shoe on the affected side, and place a trapezoidal pillow between the two thighs to prevent external rotation and adduction of the affected limb.
3. Instruct the patient to lie flat and not to turn over by themselves.

Nursing Evaluation:
   No joint dislocation occurred during the patient's hospital stay.

# Postoperative Nursing Issues
Potential Complications: Deep vein thrombosis of the lower limb
1. Closely monitor the blood supply and swelling of the affected limb postoperatively.
2. Encourage the patient to actively move and get out of bed as soon as possible. Instruct the patient to strengthen the quadriceps and ankle pump exercises during bed rest.
3. Maintain adequate fluid intake and hydration postoperatively. Consume low-fat, high-fiber foods to maintain bowel regularity and avoid increased intra-abdominal pressure due to constipation, which may affect venous return in the lower limb.
4. Administer low-molecular-weight heparin calcium injection as prescribed. Undergo pressure wave therapy.

Nursing Evaluation:
   No deep vein thrombosis of the lower limb occurred during the patient's hospital stay.

<!-- Slide number: 23 -->
# Postoperative Nursing Issues
Potential Complications: Possible urinary tract infection
Maintain the patency of the urinary catheter.
Perform perineal cleansing daily to keep the urethral orifice clean.
Drink more than 1500ml of water daily to flush the urethra.
Remove the urinary catheter as soon as possible.

# Traditional Chinese Medicine Rehabilitation Guidance
Postoperatively, keep the affected limb in abduction and neutral position, wear "T" shoes, and begin isometric contraction exercises for all lower limb muscles.
  (1) Quadriceps isometric contraction exercise.
  (2) Ankle movement.
  (3) Gluteal contraction exercise.

Emotional Nursing: Maintain a positive mood, keep the mind at ease, and avoid extreme joy or sorrow.

# Nursing Experience
Femoral neck fractures are more common in the elderly. The most important thing after surgical treatment is to persist with rehabilitation. Confidence is key; rehabilitation training is arduous, especially at the beginning. Perseverance is also necessary, as long-term commitment is required, without pride or impatience. Most importantly, caution is paramount; safety comes first. Rehabilitation treatment should be carried out slowly and not be rushed, to avoid dislocation and secondary fractures. Functional exercises should be strengthened. Therefore, when providing health education, it is crucial to ensure that the education is thorough.
