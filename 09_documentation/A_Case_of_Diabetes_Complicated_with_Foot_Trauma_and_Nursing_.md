# Introduction
Patients with diabetes, due to long-term hyperglycemia, can lead to a series of endocrine and metabolic disorders, resulting in decreased immune function, obstruction of small and medium-sized blood vessels and microcirculation in the lower limbs, peripheral neuropathy, and reduced or absent sensation in the feet, making them more susceptible to trauma. Trauma can lead to foot ulcers, infection, gangrene, and even amputation. Diabetic wounds are more difficult to heal than general wounds, posing great difficulties to nursing care.

# Case Introduction
Patient: Male, 88 years old

Medical history: Admitted for treatment on 10-11 due to "elevated blood sugar for over 20 years and left foot trauma for 3 days."

Past medical history includes diabetes and a 50-year smoking history, 3 packs per week.

No history of allergies.

# Reflections
Wounds in patients with diabetes complicated with foot trauma are more difficult to heal than general wounds

In this patient, the wound base had a large area of black eschar, and the author achieved ideal results by using conservative debridement with sharp instruments and autolytic debridement with moist dressings

However, improper selection of dressings led to maceration of the wound

Wound treatment is a dynamic process
