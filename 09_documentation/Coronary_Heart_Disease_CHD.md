# Coronary Heart Disease (CHD)
Refers to coronary atherosclerosis causing narrowing or occlusion of the vascular lumen, and/or myocardial ischemia and hypoxia or necrosis due to functional changes (spasm) of the coronary arteries, collectively known as coronary artery disease, abbreviated as CHD. Also known as ischemic heart disease.

# The gold standard for diagnosing coronary heart disease - Coronary Angiography

Coronary Arteriography (CAG)

Coronary arteriography is a method of imaging that uses percutaneous puncture of peripheral arteries to deliver a coronary angiography catheter to the root of the aorta or the ostia of the left and right coronary arteries, injects contrast medium, and makes the coronary arteries visible, directly and reliably displaying the normal or abnormal morphological appearance of the coronary arteries, providing direct and reliable evidence for the diagnosis and treatment of clinical physicians.

# Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) involves doctors puncturing the artery through the skin, under X-ray guidance, using catheters and other instruments to treat coronary artery stenosis or occlusion, restoring the vascular lumen and re-establishing blood flow. Typically used are balloon angioplasty and stent implantation.

# Indications for Coronary Angiography and Coronary Intervention

1. Any suspected coronary artery lesions.
2. Coronary artery incomplete stenosis, stenosis degree of 75% or more.
3. Isolated, concentric, limited, and less than 15mm in length non-calcified lesions in a single or multiple coronary arteries.
4. Symptomatic restenosis after PTCA.
5. Recent complete occlusion of a single coronary artery.
6. Restenosis lesions in coronary artery bypass graft vessels.

### Notes:
In fact, coronary heart disease is caused by various risk factors such as smoking, high blood lipids, hypertension, diabetes, and obesity. With the change in most people's lifestyle, it is no longer uncommon for individuals in their thirties and forties to develop coronary heart disease. According to statistics, currently, patients under the age of 50 account for one-third of all cases. Therefore, the prevention of coronary heart disease must start with changing lifestyles and should begin in one's youth.

# PCI  Contraindications

1. Allergy to iodine.

2. Severe cardiorespiratory dysfunction.

3. Severe arrhythmias and complete atrioventricular block, etc.

4. Electrolyte imbalance.

5. Severe liver and kidney dysfunction.

6. Fever, infection, coagulation disorders

7. Severe three-vessel disease

Common complications of coronary artery bypass grafting:

1. Coronary artery intimal tear and dissection
2. Coronary artery spasm
3. Coronary artery occlusion
4. Coronary artery rupture or perforation
5. Ventricular fibrillation or paroxysmal ventricular tachycardia

2. Local puncture site: Check for bleeding or hematoma.
3. Blood flow: Observe changes in skin temperature, color, sensation, and tactile sensation of the operated limb, the condition of radial artery pulsation, whether there is severe pain, and whether the upper arm of the operated limb is swollen.
4. Limb immobilization time and exercise
It is best to elevate the affected limb within 1-2 hours after surgery to relieve swelling.

# Postoperative Nursing - 3. Adequate Water Intake and Mild Diet

1. Patient's own feelings, drink in small amounts, with the premise that it does not cause stomach discomfort. The urine output should be about 800ml within 3 hours after surgery.
2. For patients with preoperative renal dysfunction (especially creatinine clearance <30mL/min), continuous intravenous infusion of normal saline at a rate of 1-1.5mL/(kg.h) for 6-12 hours before surgery and 12 hours after surgery for hydration therapy.

3. Diet should not be overfull, and a mild diet
