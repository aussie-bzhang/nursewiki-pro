# Definition

     The posterior third of the annulus fibrosus is ruptured, and the
  internal nucleus pulposus tissue herniates along the rupture site,
  causing mechanical and chemical stimulation to the adjacent nerve roots
  or cauda equina, leading to lumbar and leg pain as the main clinical
  symptom complex

# Symptoms

Lumbago: the earliest symptom, due to the stimulation of the outer layer of the annulus fibrosus and the posterior longitudinal ligament by the herniated nucleus pulposus
Sciatica: radiating pain from the lower back to the buttocks, the back of the thigh, the lateral side of the calf, and down to the foot
The incidence increases with increased abdominal pressure: L4/5 > L5/S1 > L3/4
Compression of the马尾 nerve: urinary and fecal incontinence, sensory abnormalities in the saddle area

Non-surgical Treatment

Indications: Young individuals with a first-time attack or a short course of disease
       Symptoms can resolve spontaneously after rest
       No vertebral canal stenosis on imaging studies

Bed rest
Traction, physical therapy
Massage, manipulation
Medication
Intraspinal injection
Lumbar brace protection

<!-- Slide number: 28 -->
Surgical Treatment

       Indications: Non-surgical treatment is ineffective or recurrent, significantly affects work and life
                      Significant neurological symptoms, widespread, or even worsening
                  Central lumbar disc herniation with urinary and fecal incontinence
                  Often associated with significant lumbar spinal canal stenosis

Nucleus Pulposus Removal
Intervertebral Fusion
Disc Arthroscopy

Rehabilitation Exercises

<!-- Slide number: 33 -->
  Rehabilitation Exercises – Phase 1

Ankle Flexion and Extension Exercise – Promote recovery of postoperative walking function
　Postoperative bed exercises, 3 sets/day, 15-20 repetitions/set

<!-- Slide number: 34 -->
 Rehabilitation Exercises – Phase 1

Straight Leg Elevation Exercise – Prevent nerve root adhesion
　Start from the first day postoperatively, from passive to active, gradually progressive, alternating; 2-3 sets/day, 5-10 repetitions/set

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->
Rehabilitation Exercises – Phase 2

Lumbar and Back Muscle Exercises – 5-point Support Method
  Lie flat on a hard bed for 5-7 days postoperatively, use the head, feet, and elbows to support the 5 points, lift the buttocks as high as possible, maintain for more than 10 seconds, 20 repetitions/set, 2-3 sets/day

<!-- Slide number: 36 -->
Rehabilitation Exercises – Phase 2

Lumbar and Back Muscle Exercises – 3-point Support Method
  Lie flat on a hard bed for 7-9 days postoperatively, use the head and feet to support the 3 points, lift the buttocks as high as possible, maintain for more than 10 seconds, 20 repetitions/set, 2-3 sets/day

<!-- Slide number: 37 -->
Rehabilitation Exercises – Phase 2

Lumbar and Back Muscle Exercises – 4-point Support Method (Arch Support Method)
　Lie flat on a hard bed for 9-10 days postoperatively, use both hands and feet to support the entire body, forming an arch shape, maintain for more than 10 seconds, repeat 20 times/set, 2-3 sets/day

<!-- Slide number: 38 -->
Rehabilitation Exercises – Phase 2

Lumbar and Back Muscle Exercises – Flying Swallow Method
　Lie prone on a hard bed for 10-15 days postoperatively, extend the head, upper limbs, and lower limbs backward, the abdomen contacts the bed surface, forming a flying swallow shape, maintain for more than 10 seconds, repeat 20 times/set, 2-3 sets/day

<!-- Slide number: 39 -->
Discharge Instructions

Enhance nutrition, guide the patient to consume high-calorie, high-protein, and high-vitamin diet, promote wound healing.

Continue to wear lumbar support brace for 3 months postoperatively, persist in lumbar and back muscle exercises, gradually increase the amount of exercise, avoid overexertion of the waist.

Change sitting posture frequently, do not stand or sit for long periods of time.

Prohibit vigorous activities and lifting heavy objects for 3-6 months postoperatively, continue to lie on a hard bed, guide correct wearing of the lumbar brace to prevent spinal twisting caused by activity.

Schedule regular outpatient follow-up at 2 months and 6 months postoperatively, visit the clinic for follow-up at any time if there is discomfort.
