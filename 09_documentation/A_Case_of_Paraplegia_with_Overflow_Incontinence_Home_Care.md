# Patient Condition Introduction
  General Information
   Patient: Male, 31 years old, unmarried, unemployed
  Presented on October 22, 2015, for recurrent pressure ulcers
  13 years ago, traffic accident resulted in spinal cord injury, left lower limb comminuted fracture, underwent left lower limb amputation surgery, during hospitalization, had stage IV pressure ulcers at the sacral tail, underwent skin flap transfer surgery
   Chronic urinary incontinence
   Diagnosed with diabetes five years ago, denies allergy history, denies family history
   Economic source: Subsidy

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Patient Condition Introduction
Laboratory Examinations

Ultrasound showed no obvious abnormalities in the bilateral kidneys and ureters, with significant residual urine

Random blood glucose  9.6mmol/L

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Nursing Assessment  (October 22, 2015)

  Patient ambulates in a wheelchair, left leg amputated, right leg muscle atrophy

  Wears open pants, carries a urine壶, with noticeable urine odor

  Sacral tail and bilateral greater trochanter areas have healing scars of pressure ulcers

  Self-reported high fasting blood glucose in the past 20 days

First Visit

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Nursing Diagnoses
First Visit

Overflow incontinence  Related to neurogenic bladder

Risk of skin integrity impairment (Braden 12 points)  Related to paraplegia

Abnormal glucose metabolism  Related to diabetes

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Nursing Goals
First Visit
Relieve the patient's condition of urinary incontinence throughout the day
Teach the patient knowledge of preventing pressure ulcers and methods of position change
Control blood glucose within a reasonable range

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Nursing Measures

| Diagnosis | Measures |
| --- | --- |
| Urinary incontinence | Watch videos and explain key points, guide operation, instruct to perform 5 times a day according to time, draw charts to record patient's drinking and urination habits |
| Risk of skin integrity impairment | Inform the patient of the causes of pressure ulcers, guide the positioning of the body, instruct low-sugar diet |
| Abnormal glucose metabolism | Explain dietary, exercise, and medication precautions |
First Visit

# Nursing Assessment
Irregular water intake, insufficient water intake, 2 times of leakage per day
Sacral tail skin breakdown
Unstable blood glucose, fasting blood glucose above 10mmol/L

Second Visit

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
# Nursing Diagnoses
Second Visit

Overflow incontinence  Related to irregular water intake

Sacral tail stage II pressure ulcer  Related to prolonged sitting

Abnormal glucose metabolism  Related to diabetes

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Nursing Goals
Second Visit
Patient to have regular water intake and intermittent catheterization, reduce the number of leakage times

Promote healing of sacral tail pressure ulcer

Correctly monitor and record fasting and postprandial blood glucose

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Nursing Measures
Urinary incontinence  Develop a water intake plan and intermittent catheterization record sheet
Pressure ulcer  Debridement + silicone foam + dressing change guidance
Diabetes  Home blood glucose monitoring (fasting + postprandial)

Second Visit

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Nursing Assessment
Third Visit
Excessive urine output, greater than 2500ml, 2 or more times of leakage per day
Sacral tail stage II pressure ulcer, with obvious epithelial crawling signs
Fasting and postprandial blood glucose are both above normal values

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Nursing Diagnoses
Third Visit

Overflow incontinence  Related to excessive urine output

Sacral tail stage II pressure ulcer  Related to paraplegia

Abnormal glucose metabolism  Related to diabetes

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
# Nursing Goals
Third Visit
Daily leakage times ≤ 1

Sacral tail pressure ulcer healing

Blood glucose controlled within normal range

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
# Nursing Measures

Increase intermittent catheterization to six times a day

Debridement + silicone foam

Consultation with the endocrinology department

Third Visit

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# Effect Evaluation

Daily leakage ≤ 1 time

Sacral tail pressure ulcer improved, no new pressure ulcers

Blood Glucose Control Good

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Nursing Experience
Gains

Guidance and tracking of the first intermittent catheterization procedure

Good pressure sore control with significant improvement, no new pressure sores

Effective tracking of the patient's blood glucose changes, providing objective data for the patient's endocrinology department visit

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# Nursing Experience
Shortcomings

Incomplete consideration during the guidance of intermittent catheterization, with the patient experiencing increased urine leakage during the procedure

Not considering the patient's long-term sitting during the first visit, and not taking timely pressure sore prevention measures
