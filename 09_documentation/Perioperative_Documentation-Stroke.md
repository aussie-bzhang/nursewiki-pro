### Introduction

   Cerebral infarction, also known as ischemic stroke, is a serious, fatal, and disabling disease that poses a significant threat to human health. According to survey statistics, there are 2 million new cases of cerebrovascular diseases in China each year, with up to 1.5 million deaths. Cerebral infarction accounts for approximately 60%-80% of these cases. After an acute stroke, 44.7% of patients experience dysphagia. Additionally, about 3/4 of survivors have varying degrees of labor ability loss, with severe disability affecting about 40%, and 1/4-3/4 of patients have recurrences within 2-5 years.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->

Dysphagia mainly occurs during the pharyngeal phase, characterized by a very weak elicitation of the swallowing reflex, with no obstruction in the oral phase.

True bulbar palsy, also known as true bulbar paralysis, primarily damages the brainstem nerve nuclei, causing dysphagia.

Nursing Issue 3 - Lack of Professional Care Guidance for Caregivers
| Date | Evidence | Nursing Measures | Effect Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 24/7 4/8 | Family has no experience with nasogastric patients | 1. Train caregivers in nasogastric feeding techniques: Elevate the bed head >60 degrees or sit up in a semi-fowler position, with the head slightly forward. Withdraw gastric fluid to confirm it is in the stomach before slowly feeding, give 20ml of water before feeding, 30ml after feeding, and maintain the original position for 20-30 minutes. 2. Avoid back patting and turning over for 30 minutes after nasogastric feeding to maintain airway patency, reduce pharyngeal irritation, and prevent food reflux due to increased intragastric pressure. 3. Use spoons with rounded edges, thick handles, and a capacity of 3-5ml. Feed from the left corner of the mouth, ensuring the patient swallows before feeding the next mouthful. 4. Feed through the mouth to the healthy side, choosing foods with uniform density, appropriate粘性, not easily loose and deformed, and not easily left on the mucosa, such as egg custard and rice porridge. | 1/8 Caregivers have mastered the knowledge of nasogastric care for the patient and the method of thickened meal preparation. |

Nursing Issue 4 - Erythroderma

Nursing Measures: 1. Immediately communicate with the doctor to discontinue low-right intravenous infusion. Administer antiallergic medication as prescribed. 2. Avoid spicy foods and alcohol during the illness, avoid scratching, teach the patient to take a warm shower with a soft towel to dry, and prohibit the use of alkaline bath products and soaps. 3. Gently apply calamine lotion to the entire body 4 times a day. 4. Provide successful case examples of dermatitis symptoms, actively encourage the patient, and eliminate their anxiety and fear.

Discharge Plan
1. Strengthen nutrition, instruct the family to prepare a nutritionally comprehensive high-protein diet according to the patient's taste, with small and frequent meals.
2. Swallowing function exercise guidance and follow-up, encourage self-feeding as much as possible, and only remove the tube after there is no coughing when drinking and eating.
3. Wear non-slip canvas shoes when going out to prevent falls, provide a follow-up path card, and regularly review various indicators.
