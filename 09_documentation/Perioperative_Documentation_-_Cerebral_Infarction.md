Background---Large Area Cerebral Infarction

Cerebral infarction with an area diameter greater than 5cm or involving two or more lobes, or with an infarct area greater than 1/2 or 2/3 of the same side. It is usually a complete stroke of the main trunk of the internal carotid artery, the main trunk of the middle cerebral artery, or the cortical branches, or an anterior cerebral artery infarction, or a basilar artery trunk infarction.

Background---Glossoptosis

Due to the severe suppression of the cerebral cortex and subcortical structures, it leads to complete or incomplete paralysis of the pharyngeal muscle group, causing decreased muscle tension in the larynx and tongue, resulting in glossoptosis and affecting ventilation function, aggravating cerebral hypoxia, and severely endangering life.

4. Cough stimulation by finger pressure on the trachea, 3-4 times a day;
5. Tongue extension training: massage from the root of the tongue to the tip and gently pull outward (BID before meals);
6. Breathing training: use breathing training tools.
