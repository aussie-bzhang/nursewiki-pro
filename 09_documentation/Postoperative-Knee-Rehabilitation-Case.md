Summary

David H Kim et al. summarized in "Arthroscopic Treatment of Knee Adhesions":

In 1919, Bennett used a cut-open quadriceps tendon lengthening procedure, which was unsuccessful.

In 1944, Thompson used a cut-open quadriceps muscle shaping procedure, which had some success.

In 1963, Nicolle used multiple incisions and release procedures at the adhesion site, with poor results.

In 1927, O'Connor innovatively used arthroscopic adhesion release, but due to the lagging arthroscopic technology, its promotion was limited.

David h kim,Thomas J Gill.Arthroscopic treatment of the arthrofibrotic Knee ArthroscopicArthroscopy,2004,20(6):187-194

Treatment (Non-surgical Treatment)

For mild adhesions, mild lesions, and within 3 months of time, the rehabilitation strategy for joint stiffness is: "No pain, more gain!"

Manual release under anesthesia: If the rehabilitation treatment reaches 3 months and the effect is poor, the decision is made based on the patient's condition.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->

Surgical Treatment

Indications

Adhesions for more than 6 months

Poor results from rehabilitation treatment

Patients' expectations, which are also an important reference for whether to undergo surgery

The surgery mainly targets fibrous rigidity, not bony rigidity

●

Difficulties in manual release under anesthesia (easily fractured or ligament rupture)

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->

Surgical Treatment

Contraindications

Decreased knee stability
Non-union of fractures
Traumatic joint cartilage degeneration
Infection
No joint space
Flap transplantation after surgery
Severe osteoporosis

Traditional Surgical Methods

Open release surgery and quadriceps muscle shaping
Large trauma, severe pain, and excessive bleeding
Unable to perform effective early rehabilitation training
Postoperative adhesions and additional injuries are likely to occur
Postoperative complications: infection, quadriceps muscle weakness
Poor treatment results and long rehabilitation time

**Stage 1**: Inflammatory response phase (postoperative 0-2 weeks)

**Goals**

Basic treatment

Control swelling, analgesia, prevent infection, reduce inflammation

Compression bandaging, cold compress, elevation of the affected limb, ankle pump exercise to control swelling; cold compress, medication, and femoral nerve block to control pain

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->

**Stage 1**: Inflammatory response phase (postoperative 0-2 weeks)

ROM Training

CPM Training

Full range of knee joint movement, main active movement (without increasing pain and drainage volume), analgesics (taken before training); patellar movement exercise.

Start using on the first postoperative day, 3 times a day, 60 minutes per session, use for one week or longer

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->

**Stage 1**: Inflammatory response phase (postoperative 0-2 weeks)

Weight bearing

Muscle strength training

Different opinions, we generally use crutches for 1-2 weeks to avoid exacerbating inflammation and swelling

Start lower limb isometric contraction training immediately after surgery; perform flexion and extension muscle isometric contraction training at different angles; do not increase pain, reduce repetitive training

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->

**Stage 2**: Fibrosis formation phase (postoperative 2-6 weeks)

. **Goals**:
Reduce pain and swelling, active functional movement of the knee joint, mild functional daily activities, promote somatosensory recovery, a key stage for determining the final outcome
. Basic treatment: continue the treatment of stage 1;
o ROM training:
Continue joint mobility training, strive to achieve greater joint mobility, the focus of treatment is "self-passive stretching",配合使用重量牵引 (long-term, low load); maintain and restore patellar movement.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->

**Stage 2: Fibrosis Formation Period (2-6 weeks postoperatively)**

**Load-bearing: Gradually transition to crutch-free, full weight-bearing**

**Muscle Strength Training: Not to be ignored, as it not only helps restore muscle strength but also maximizes ROM improvement**

**ADL Training: Encourage patients to use the affected limb more in ADLs, aiming for as much independence as possible in daily activities**

**Brace Training: Dynamic/Static brace progression, brace use is most effective in this stage for obtaining ROM**

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->

**Stage 3: Scar Formation Period (postoperative > 6 weeks)**

**Goals:**

- Comprehensive recovery of knee function and functional training
- Ability to perform occupational and non-occupational activities
- Maintenance or achievement of postoperative ROM

**Basic Treatment:**

- Continue treatment from stages 1 and 2
- Pre-training heat敷, post-training ice敷
- Persistent physical therapy addressing specific issues
- **ROM Training: Continue active/active-assisted/passive joint range of motion training**
- **Muscle Strength Training: Resistance training should not be ignored, as it plays a role in increasing ROM**

<!-- Slide number: 23 -->

**Stage 3: Scar Formation Period (postoperative > 6 weeks)**

**Brace Training: The application of braces can be moderately increased in intensity, still emphasizing long-term, regular brace wear to obtain maximum soft tissue stretching**

**Proprioception Training: Backward walking, sideways walking, balance board, correcting gait**
