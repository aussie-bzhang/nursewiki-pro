# Introduction and Development History
Fast-Track Surgery (FTS), also known as "Enhanced Recovery After Surgery" (ERAS), was first proposed by Danish surgeon Kehlet in 2001. It refers to a series of evidence-based measures (mainly including surgical techniques, anesthesia methods, pain control, and nursing care) taken during the perioperative period to effectively reduce surgical trauma, stress, and promote rapid recovery in patients, achieving the purpose of shortening hospital stay and reducing hospital costs. Perioperative nursing plays an indispensable role in the concept of fast-track surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Introduction and Development History - Origin and Application
2001 - Proposed by Danish surgeon Henrik Kehlet, explored in various surgical patients
2001 - Promoted in Europe and America, shortened hospital stay, early postoperative recovery, and changed treatment models
2004 - First introduced and applied by Professor Li Jieshou of the General Hospital of the Nanjing Military Region
2006 - The Department of Gastroenterology at West China Hospital reported the first related paper
2010 - The International Society for Enhanced Recovery was established in Sweden

Practical proof - Safety and effectiveness of fast-track recovery

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Introduction and Development History - Application in Gastroenterology
Inclusion criteria: 60 elderly patients with colon cancer, with concurrent organ dysfunction
Research results: Average hospital stay of 2 days, 57 patients recovered bowel movement within 2 days after surgery
                            Off-bed activity time of 5-6 hours within 24-48 hours after surgery
                            Off-bed activity time of more than 10 hours at discharge
Complications: Infection rate and gastrointestinal bleeding 3%, anastomotic fistula 3%

Conclusion: Fast-track surgery treatment is safe and effective

# Introduction and Development History - Application in Orthopedics
Denmark: 13,800 unilateral hip replacement surgeries
                  (Hospital stay: 12 days in 2000 - 4 days in 2009)
Netherlands: Hip joint hospital stay: 1 day (carried out in day care units)
USA: Hip joint hospital stay: 3-4 days
China: Hip joint hospital stay: 7-16 days

Conclusion: Fast-track recovery is safe and effective in the application of orthopedics

# Implementation Details
Preoperative: - No bowel preparation
                   - No overnight fasting  1500ml of glucose water orally 10h and 2h before surgery
Intraoperative: - Use thoracic epidural anesthesia
                   - Place epidural catheter for analgesia
                   - Intraoperative warming  controlled fluid administration
Postoperative: - No routine placement of nasogastric tube for decompression
                   - No placement or early removal of abdominal drainage tubes and catheters
                   - Early drinking and eating
                   - Early ambulation

# 4. Preemptive Analgesia
ERAS recommends preemptive analgesia before surgery to actively control pain
         To prevent the occurrence of pain hypersensitivity, take analgesic measures before surgery to slow down the occurrence of postoperative pain, that is, "preemptive analgesia and anti-inflammatory"
Once pain becomes chronic, treatment will be more difficult. Early treatment is essential
For postoperative pain, preemptive analgesia is advocated, that is, to provide analgesic treatment before the occurrence of injury stimulation

# 2. Anaesthetic Techniques (ERAS Optimized Anaesthetic Techniques)
Use anaesthetics with rapid onset and short duration of action to ensure that the patient can recover quickly after anaesthesia, which is conducive to early postoperative activity
Nerve block is the most effective method of postoperative analgesia, which can reduce the stress response of the nervous and endocrine metabolism caused by surgery
Continuous use of epidural analgesia for 24-48h after surgery can effectively reduce the stress response
Local anaesthetic techniques can provide analgesia and are conducive to protecting lung function, reducing cardiac burden

# 4. Placement of Drainage Tubes
The 2011 CDC guidelines recommend that drainage tubes not be placed at the incision site

Placing drainage tubes at the incision site increases the infection rate
Closed drainage can effectively drain the fluid in the larger potential dead space, but it cannot prevent infection

# 3. Early Mobilization

Encourage patients to get out of bed and exercise as soon as possible (provided: effective pain control)

# 3. Early Mobilization
ERAS recommendations for early postoperative mobilization:
- Provide an independent environment
- Mobilize for 2 hours on the day of surgery
- Mobilize for 6 hours daily thereafter
- Alleviate postoperative fatigue
- Improve sleep quality
- Reduce complications
- Enable earlier return to daily activities
Benefits of early mobilization

# 4. Nutritional Support
ERAS postoperative nutritional support plan:
- Encourage patients to start oral intake as soon as possible (Grade A recommendation)
- Oral nutritional supplementation (approximately 200ml, high-energy food, 2-3 times daily) should be initiated from the day of surgery
- Recommend that patients continue oral nutritional supplementation at home for several weeks after discharge (Grade A recommendation)

# 5. Postoperative Exercise
Early: Accurately assess the patient's basic condition, exercise appropriately, and begin functional exercises without exacerbating local symptoms. A large number of studies advocate for the initiation of functional exercises on the day of surgery
- Select the correct exercise programs, with initial focus on muscle strength training to quickly restore muscle strength and ensure the smooth progression of subsequent exercises
- Exercises should be professionally guided, easy to learn, and easy to understand
- The amount of exercise should not be excessive, with the general exercise time not exceeding 45-60 minutes, progressing gradually

# 6. Sleep Care
Good sleep is an important factor in promoting postoperative recovery
Postoperative patients may experience sleep disturbances

- Comfortable sleep environment
- Pain relief
- Avoid disturbances

# 7. Discharge Instructions
Discharge readiness
- Estimation and judgment of the patient's and family's ability to leave acute care facilities
- Perception of whether they are ready for discharge
- Indicator that the patient has fully recovered and can be safely discharged

# 8. Prospects and Outlook
The concept of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) is to control perioperative pathophysiological changes through multi-modal interventions, improving the prognosis of surgical patients. Perioperative nursing is an indispensable and crucial part of the ERAS concept, and the quality of nursing directly affects whether the patient can recover quickly. However, the realization of the ERAS theory is not the result of a single method, but rather the reasonable combination of various optimized approaches to produce a good effect.
Nursing work has evolved from traditional nursing to evidence-based nursing to precision nursing
