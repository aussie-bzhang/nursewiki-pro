## Case Introduction
Patient: Gao, Female, 75 years old
Medical History: Admitted on October 14, 2015, at 15:48 due to recurrent abdominal distension for two years, worsening for over forty days.
Diagnosis: Liver cirrhosis decompensation, ascites, umbilical hernia.
Past medical history includes coronary heart disease, diabetes mellitus (blood glucose control not specified), schistosomiasis, and history of cholecystectomy.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
## Case Introduction
No history of hypertension or hepatitis.
Abdominal distension, visible varicose veins of the abdominal wall, and surgical scars. The umbilicus is prominently bulging with surface skin ulceration and bleeding.

## Initial Wound Assessment

Liver cirrhosis with ascites complicated by umbilical hernia rupture, marked abdominal distension
Umbilical hernia protrusion, size 8.5cm×9cm×5cm
Partial loss of epidermis in the wound, size 4.1cm×6.5cm
Wound without sinus tract, no sinus, wound appearance consisting of 25% red tissue and 75% yellow tissue, wound exudate is large, yellow, clear, no odor
No redness, swelling, or pain around the wound skin

## Nursing Experience
The use of a one-time ostomy bag and drainage bag to collect exudate can accurately observe and record the color, quantity, and nature of the drainage fluid and exudate, providing effective reference materials for clinical treatment.
The use of new types of dressings for wound healing is effective and the method is simple and easy to master, worthy of promotion.
For liver cirrhosis with ascites and umbilical hernia rupture, both exudate management and wound treatment need to be done well, as well as the prevention of pressure ulcers, which puts higher requirements on our nursing work.
## Shortcomings
Insufficient standardization of image material collection, insufficient skill in operation.
