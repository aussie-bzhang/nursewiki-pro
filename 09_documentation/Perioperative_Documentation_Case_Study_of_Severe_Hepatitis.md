Nursing Measures
Part Four

Rational Diet

Bed Rest

Emotional Stability
Psychological care should be provided to alleviate tension and maintain a cheerful mood.
Absolute bed rest is required until jaundice subsides and symptoms are largely resolved.
A rational diet can promote the recovery and regeneration of liver cells to some extent and is conducive to the early recovery of the disease.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
Nursing Measures
Part Four

01
Nursing Issue
Risk of asphyxia
Related to laryngeal spasm, increased airway secretions during epilepsy seizures

Nursing Measures

Prevent asphyxia
Loosen the collar and belt
Lower the patient's head to one side, allowing saliva and respiratory secretions to drain from the corner of the mouth
Prevent tongue prolapse causing airway obstruction: lift the chin and pull the tongue out
Keep a suction device at the bedside and promptly remove sputum
Do not force feeding

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Nursing Measures
Part Four

02
Nursing Issue
Risk of injury
Related to generalized muscle spasms and sudden loss of consciousness during epilepsy seizures

Nursing Measures

Prevent injury during seizures
Avoid falls: place the patient on the ground if a seizure is imminent
Avoid abrasions: remove glasses, dentures, place soft items under the patient's head, remove dangerous items nearby to prevent collisions
Prevent biting: place a mouth guard between the upper and lower molars, but do not force it in
Do not apply force to limbs during seizures to prevent fractures, muscle tears, and joint dislocation
After seizures, the patient may have short-term confusion, and oral temperature measurement is prohibited for patients with excited motor seizures to prevent self-harm, harm to others, or wandering

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
Nursing Measures
Part Four

03
Nursing Issue
Disorder of self-esteem
Related to the embarrassing appearance during seizures, which damages the patient's self-esteem

Nursing Measures

Psychological Care
Care, understand, and respect the patient

Help the child and family correct their attitude towards the disease, establish a healthy psychology, encourage the child to express their feelings, communicate more with family and medical staff, and provide emotional support.

Eliminate the patient's loneliness, anxiety, and fear, reduce or eliminate feelings of inferiority

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
Nursing Measures
Part Four

04
Nursing Issue
Knowledge Deficiency

Nursing Measures

Health Guidance
Introduce the basic knowledge of the disease to the family.
Adhere to medication, perform blood routine tests monthly, and liver and kidney function tests quarterly.
Encourage the child to participate in beneficial social activities.
Prohibit engaging in dangerous activities such as climbing high or swimming to avoid life-threatening risks during seizures.
Carry a brief medical card with the patient's name, address, medical history, and contact information at all times for timely and effective treatment during emergencies.
