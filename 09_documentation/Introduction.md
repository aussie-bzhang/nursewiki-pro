| 16/11 | Dysphagia | Interdisciplinary collaboration, the patient can eat normally by mouth without aspiration | Nurse | Increase feeding training on the basis of the original measures. 1. Feeding position: have the patient take a trunk flexed 30° supine position, the head forward flexed, the shoulder of the hemiplegic side elevated with a pillow, the head tilted to the healthy side. 2. Food form: choose according to the principle of easy to difficult, easy to swallow food characteristics are uniform density, appropriate viscosity, not easy to disperse, easy to deform, not easy to remain on the mucosa, such as mashed vegetables, jelly, custard, etc., while also considering the color, smell, taste, and temperature of the food. 3. One-bite amount: the most suitable amount of food for swallowing for each bite, normally about 20 ml, but for patients with dysphagia, start with 3-4 ml and then increase accordingly. 4. Rate: guide the patient to eat, chew, and swallow at a uniform speed. 5. Swallowing residue food removal training includes swallowing training, multiple swallowing training, nodding swallowing training, and lateral swallowing training. 7. Selection of utensils: the spoon face should be small, not easy to stick to food, the handle should be thick, and the length should be appropriate. | 24/11 the patient's Wada Drinking Test 2, the patient can eat normally by mouth without aspiration |

Why does the patient develop false diarrhea?

<!-- Slide number: 36 -->
Reasons for the patient's false diarrhea
01
The infarction affects the defecation reflex, and the patient's sensory receptors for rectal distension decrease
Since the patient is constipated rather than suffering from diarrhea, the administration of antidiarrheal medication orally will worsen constipation
03

A large amount of fecal mass accumulates in the anal canal, pushing against the intestinal wall and stimulating increased secretion and exudation of water into the rectum. The water cannot soften the hard feces, and it flows along the intestinal wall, resulting in false diarrhea

# Reflections and Gains
Gains
For false diarrhea in patients with cerebral infarction, it should be analyzed and assessed from multiple perspectives. Without thinking, treating it as diarrhea will ultimately lead to more severe constipation in the patient; and in the process of clinical nursing, the effectiveness of nursing should be assessed in a timely manner. If the effect is not significant, it should be timely to cooperate with relevant departments to formulate a treatment and nursing plan.
Impressions
If we can integrate clinical thinking into the clinical nursing process, we are not just nurses who only execute medical orders, but rather nurses who use clinical thinking to discuss the patient's condition with doctors, formulate nursing plans, and reflect the value of nursing.
