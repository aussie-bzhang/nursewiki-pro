I. Preoperative Preparation

  1. Why is it not immediate surgery when admitted to the hospital?
     Surgery is an important means of treating surgical diseases, however, the trauma of surgery, anesthesia, and the stimulation of the disease itself can cause a series of neuroendocrine reactions, leading to disorders of physiological function and varying degrees of psychological stress in the human body, thereby weakening the body's defense ability and tolerance to surgery, which directly affects the prognosis of surgery.
      Therefore, it is necessary to prepare in advance, correct the existing and potential physiological and psychological problems of the patient, improve the patient's tolerance to surgery and anesthesia, and minimize the risk of surgery.

I. Preoperative Preparation
2. How to prepare well before surgery?

1) Adjust your mindset and actively cooperate.
2) Truthfully report your condition to the medical staff and answer related questions. Such as age? What diseases have you had in the past? What medications have you taken? Have you had any surgeries? Menstrual history?
3) Complete relevant examinations: blood tests, urine tests, X-rays, ultrasounds, CT scans, electrocardiograms, gastroscopy, colonoscopy, etc.
4) Family support.

4) Digestive Tract Preparation: Fast and No Fluid Intake
① Fast and no fluid intake: adults should start fasting 12 hours before surgery, and no fluid intake 4 hours before surgery.
   To prevent vomiting during anesthesia or surgery that may cause asphyxia or aspiration pneumonia
② For gastrointestinal surgery: start eating a nutritious low-fiber diet 1-2 days before surgery.
   Prompt the patient to defecate before surgery, and if necessary, perform enema, use glycerin suppositories, to prevent the relaxation of the anal sphincter after anesthesia, which may lead to fecal excretion and increase the risk of contamination.

## 2. Nutrition and Diet

What should one eat after surgery? Should one take good care to replenish nutrients?
Generally (non-gastrointestinal surgery): After the patient is fully awake from anesthesia, they can start eating according to their condition and medical advice (high in protein and rich in vitamin C);
Local anesthesia: If there is no discomfort after surgery, the patient can start eating;
Abdominal surgery (gastrointestinal surgery): Patients need to fast for 1-3 days, wait for the anal排气, and then start with liquid food as advised by the doctor.

## 2. Nutrition and Diet

How should one eat specifically after gastrointestinal surgery?
Gastrointestinal surgery: The initial intake should be small, and filtered vegetable soup, thin rice porridge, lotus root starch, fish soup, etc., can be consumed. By the 5th to 6th day, semi-liquid food can be introduced, and soft food can be transitioned to between the 7th to 9th day. General food can be started 10-12 days after surgery.

## 3. Rest and Activity

Is it not allowed to move after surgery? Should one rest in bed?
Maintain a quiet ward, reduce interference with the patient, and ensure they have a quiet rest;
Encourage early bed activities after the condition is stable;
Bed activities: deep breathing, effective coughing, active limb movement, turning over, patting the back.
Increase lung capacity
Improve blood circulation
Prevent deep vein thrombosis
Promote peristalsis
Reduce urinary retention

## 3. Rest and Activity

Is it a concern that one feels very cold after surgery?
This is a common postoperative reaction and is considered normal.
Pay attention to keeping warm. However, avoid placing a hot water bottle close to the body for warmth to prevent burns.

## 4. Common Postoperative Symptoms
1) PAIN - Most severe within the first 24 hours, can be relieved after 2-3 days.

Methods to alleviate:
        Press on the incision site during coughing and turning over
        Administration of analgesic pump
        Wound physiotherapy
        Distract attention

<!-- Slide number: 32 -->
## 4. Common Postoperative Symptoms
2) FEVER - Most common postoperatively

Cause: Reaction to surgical trauma

Symptoms: Slight increase in body temperature, generally not exceeding 38.5℃, and gradually returns to normal after 3 days, which is considered normal postoperative fever.

<!-- Slide number: 33 -->
## 4. Common Postoperative Symptoms
3) NAUSEA AND VOMITING - Due to anesthetic reaction

Cause: Anesthetic drug reaction

Treatment: Stops naturally as the anesthetic effect wears off

Note: Tilt the head to one side during vomiting

<!-- Slide number: 34 -->
## 4. Common Postoperative Symptoms
4) BLOATING

Cause: Inhibition of gastrointestinal motility, inability to expel intestinal gas

Treatment: After排气, bloating can be relieved

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->
## 4. Common Postoperative Symptoms
5) URINARY RETENTION - More common in the elderly

Cause: Inhibition of micturition reflex after anesthesia
       Incision pain causes reflex spasm of the post-urethral sphincter
       Patients are not accustomed to using bed pans
Treatment: Heat application, listening to water sounds, massage
       For those who have not had a catheter inserted or have had the catheter removed, and if the condition permits, they should urinate in bed or get out of bed as soon as possible

<!-- Slide number: 36 -->
## 4. Common Postoperative Symptoms
6) DRY MOUTH - Common postoperatively

**Reason:** Preoperative medication-induced, restricting glandular secretion

**Management:** At this time, a clean handkerchief or cotton swab dipped in a small amount of boiled water can be used to moisten the lips and tongue to alleviate discomfort. However, it is important not to drink water to avoid related complications.

## Support from the Family
What should the family do as caregivers?

1. Assist with preoperative preparation
2. Material support: living arrangements, financial preparation
3. Emotional support: encouragement, understanding, support
4. Wait in the waiting area outside the operating room after surgery
5. Assist with postoperative rehabilitation training: such as turning over, back patting

**Turning:** Every two hours, pay attention to the drainage tube
**Patting the Back:** Hands in an arch shape   from bottom to top, from outside to inside
