# Surgical Specimens
      Any tissue, organ, or secretion or foreign body related to the patient's disease that is removed during surgery in the operating room is considered a surgical specimen. Modern medicine has many experimental techniques and diagnostic methods, but live tissue pathological diagnosis remains the most reliable and direct "first diagnosis" for surgical diseases and is the "gold standard".

# Pathological Specimen Submission Procedure
 1. The operating doctor hands over the specimen to the scrub nurse for proper care. If there are 2 or more specimens, they should be placed separately.
2. The scrub nurse confirms the specimen with the doctor.

① The scrub nurse confirms whether the tissue taken is to be submitted for testing, and if so, confirms the specimen name with the doctor.
② After confirmation, the specimen should be properly stored. Larger specimens should be wrapped in saline gauze, and smaller ones should be placed in a specimen bag to prevent drying and dehydration, which may affect the test results.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Pathological Specimen Submission Procedure

① The circulating nurse fills in the relevant information on the specimen bag according to the doctor's order using a blue marker (specimen name, patient name, department, hospital number, bed number, etc.)
② After filling in the information, the circulating nurse should double-check with the operating doctor and scrub nurse to ensure accuracy.

3. Circulating nurse fills in the specimen bag

# Pathological Specimen Submission Procedure

The pathological specimen bag, made of plastic, has the following advantages:
① Information about the specimen and patient can be filled in on the bag, making it easily identifiable.
② The bag mouth has a parent-child groove sealing strip, and the bottom is flat and perpendicular to the specimen bag. Its characteristics are a tight seal that does not leak, stands upright without tilting, and the specimen can be completely immersed in the fixative; it overcomes the disadvantages of traditional glass specimen bottles, such as being heavy and easily broken, making storage, transportation, observation, and use more convenient.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Pathological Specimen Submission Procedure

4. Scrub and circulating nurses jointly check

① After the scrub nurse confirms that the information on the specimen bag is correct, the specimen is promptly placed in the bag.
② In operations with a large number of specimens, such as total hysterectomy with pelvic lymph node dissection, each tissue and lymph node cut by the doctor during the operation should be confirmed and immediately placed in the corresponding specimen bag using a flat forceps. Only one specimen should be placed in a single specimen bag to avoid confusion of specimens. If there are two or more specimens, they should be stapled together or placed in a larger specimen bag to prevent scattered specimens from being lost.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Pathological Specimen Submission Procedure
5. Doctor fills out the pathology report form

     The doctor fills out the pathology report form at the end of the operation and double-checks the specimen name and quantity with the doctor. If there are many specimens, the doctor should note the number of specimens on the pathology report form and sign.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Pathological Specimen Submission Procedure
6. Scrub nurse registers, immerses specimens, and places them in the specimen cabinet

The scrub nurse takes the specimen and pathology report form to the pathology room and clearly and neatly registers in the pathology register (date, patient name, department, specimen name, etc.). Note: Recheck the information on the pathology report form and the information on the specimen bag before registration. Place the pathology report form in a folder, immerse the specimen in 10% formalin completely, seal the mouth of the specimen bag, and place it in the corresponding department specimen box. Finally, lock the specimen cabinet.

# Frozen Specimen Submission Procedure

The physician fills out the frozen section pathology report and the informed consent form for frozen section specimens, and the family signs the form. The circulating nurse follows the physician's instructions to fill in the relevant information (specimen name, patient name, department, bed number, etc.) on the specimen bag with a marking pen and verifies it with the physician. Afterward, the frozen section specimens, pathology report, and informed consent form are handed over to the designated worker for timely delivery to the pathology department without the need for formalin solution fixation.

# Importance of Proper Specimen Handling

Firstly, proper handling of surgical pathology specimens is essential to provide material for pathological diagnosis and to provide a basis for clinical diagnosis of disease nature, determination of treatment plans, and prognosis assessment.

Secondly, surgical excised pathological specimens have characteristics of specificity and uniqueness in material collection. If the management and handling of surgical specimens are not properly done (such as specimen deterioration, loss, confusion, etc.), it directly affects the定性 of the disease and treatment, and may even delay the disease, leading to misdiagnosis, causing serious harm to the patient's physical and mental health, and causing medical disputes.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Summary

The specimens excised during surgery play a crucial role in the treatment of the patient's disease and the prognosis of the disease. Errors in specimen management not only cause irreparable harm to the patient but also directly affect the定性 of the disease and treatment, and may also trigger medical disputes and legal issues.
Therefore, medical staff in the operating room must enhance their awareness of specimen management, strengthen their sense of responsibility, and strictly implement various management systems to ensure the safety of surgical specimens and prevent errors and accidents from occurring.
