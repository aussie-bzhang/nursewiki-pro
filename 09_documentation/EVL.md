What is Esophageal-Gastric Variceal Hemorrhage?
     Due to causes such as liver cirrhosis and Budd-Chiari syndrome, portal vein pressure increases, normal hepatic portal vein return is obstructed, the portal vein has no valves, and the pressure continues to increase, causing the following four groups of communicating branches to open, expand, and twist.

Why is the first group the most important?
    The esophageal-gastric varices are close to the superior vena cava, with a large pressure difference. Moreover, the action of gastric acid and other factors leads to the earliest and most significant symptoms.

EVL Surgery
Endoscopic Esophageal Variceal Ligation (EVL)   In the endoscopy, the prominent esophageal varices are ligated, causing them to necrose and fall off, leaving superficial ulcers locally, forming inflammation and scars under the mucosa, and the submucosal variceal veins disappear, thereby achieving the therapeutic goal.

Indications:

                                                                           : Contraindications

1 Patients with esophageal varices and/or gastric varices bleeding or those who fail to respond to drug hemostasis;
2. Patients with recurrent esophageal varices bleeding, poor general condition, unable to tolerate surgical treatment;
3. Preventing recurrent bleeding, elective surgery

Severe dysfunction of heart, lung, brain, and kidney, unable to tolerate surgery; severe hemorrhage, hemodynamic instability; unable to cooperate with surgery.

EVL Surgery
Ligation Method:
First, perform a routine electronic gastroscopy, carefully check the range and degree of esophageal varices. Install the ligation device and re-enter the endoscope, starting from 1.75 cm (controversial) from the distance to the cardia, showing the varices to be ligated, and starting the endoscopic aspiration device. After several seconds of continuous aspiration, the varices can be seen to be aspirated into the inner cylinder under the endoscope, and the field turns red, indicating that the cylinder is full of varices. At this time, pull the traction line with force, and during the retraction of the inner cylinder, due to the lower edge resistance of the outer cylinder, the rubber ring will safely fit around the base of the ligation site. Stop negative pressure aspiration, slowly inject air, and the ligation varices at the base can be seen to be tightly grasped by the rubber ring in the form of a polyp, and the color gradually turns purple. Ligation should be performed around the esophagus in turn, avoiding direct aspiration of varices with ulcers, erosions, and obvious red signs. Generally, all varices should be ligated at one time to prevent bleeding from the unligated veins due to increased pressure.

EVL Surgery
Postoperative Complications:
1. Bleeding: Generally within 1 week after esophageal variceal ligation, the ligated tissue falls off, leaving a superficial ulcer, which is a high-risk period for early recurrence of bleeding. First, avoid various triggers: improper diet is the main trigger, any action that increases abdominal pressure can cause rebleeding, and gastroesophageal reflux is also one of the factors.

2. Pharyngeal pain: Patients report pharyngeal pain 24-48 hours after surgery, which is caused by repeated insertion of the fiberoptic gastroscopy. Explain the cause of the pain to the patient, eliminate anxiety, and gargle with physiological saline or boric acid solution, 2-3 days after which the pain disappears.

3. Post-sternotomy discomfort: All patients have varying degrees of post-sternotomy discomfort, which is caused by ligation. Generally, it disappears 1-2 weeks after surgery. Severe symptoms may require the use of small doses of sedatives and analgesics.

4. Esophageal stricture: Caused by scars and contractions at the base of the ligated tissue.

EVL Preoperative Nursing
1. Psychological Nursing: Communicate warmly with the patient and family, comfort the patient to eliminate their fear and anxiety. Explain to the patient the purpose, method, operation process, and intraoperative cooperation, as well as postoperative注意事项. Inform them that EVL is a minimally invasive surgery, without surgery, less pain, simple and easy to operate, safe, and reliable in efficacy, so that the patient can relax and maintain a good mental state to accept the surgery;

2. Prepare for preoperative care: Fast for 12 hours before the operation, inject diazepam intramuscularly 30 minutes before the operation, and 10mg of drotaverine hydrochloride tablets to reduce the patient's anxiety and reduce secretions from the glands during the operation, establish a venous access, and take 10 minutes before the operation to take the gastroscopy gelatin.

3. Check the performance of various instruments, and prepare all rescue drugs.

EVL Intraoperative Nursing

Patient Care: The nurse stands on the left side of the patient, holds the bite pad tightly with the left hand, instructs the patient to maintain the supine position and relax the whole body, and guide the patient to perform swallowing movements when the gastroscopy is inserted into the pharynx, breathe through the mouth, and facilitate the smooth passage of the gastroscopy through the esophagus. If there is any secretion, let it flow into the bend plate next to the corner of the mouth.

Cooperate closely with the doctor: Assist the doctor in taking the operation part of the gastroscopy during the ligation process.

Closely observe the patient's condition: If possible, perform electrocardiographic monitoring, closely observe the patient's vital signs, facial expressions, and gestures, and inform the doctor of any abnormalities in a timely manner to take measures. After the ligation is completed, help the patient remove the bite pad, wipe away the secretions and bloodstains, observe that the vital signs are stable, and send them back to the ward.

EVL Postoperative Nursing
Dietary Care: Dietary care is very important in the first week after surgery, as it is a peak period for recurrent bleeding. Fast for 72 hours after surgery, and if there is no active bleeding 72 hours later, drink cool water, and if there is no discomfort, eat congee, milk, and other foods. Gradually transition to non-fibrous, low-salt semi-liquid diet. Avoid rough, cold, hard, spicy, and fibrous foods, do not drink alcohol, strong tea, or coffee, chew food slowly, and avoid overeating.

Position and Activity: Instruct the patient to rest in bed for 24-48 hours after surgery, maintain a head elevation of 40° in the supine position, avoid gastroesophageal reflux, and perform gentle movements when turning over. Avoid strenuous exercise within 2 weeks, as EVL postoperative 7-10 days is the descaling period, and random movement may cause rebleeding.
