Thoracic Surgery Perioperative Nursing
Surgery Department

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
Perioperative care is a comprehensive process surrounding surgery, starting from the patient's decision to undergo surgical treatment and continuing until they are basically recovered. This includes preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative periods, specifically from the time of determining surgical treatment until the basic treatment related to this surgery is essentially completed. The duration is approximately 5-7 days before surgery to 7-12 days after surgery.

Preoperative Assessment

Dietary Guidance
To enhance physical fitness, increase tissue repair and anti-infection ability, preoperative patients should consume high-calorie, high-protein, high-fiber, vitamin-rich, and easily digestible foods, such as lean meat and fish, eggs, fresh vegetables and fruits, and soy products. Nutritional support via intravenous infusion may be necessary if required.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Assistance with Various Examinations
Laboratory preoperative examinations: blood and urine routine, liver and kidney function, blood coagulation four items, six items of immune tests for infectious diseases, blood gas analysis
X-ray chest, abdominal ultrasound if necessary
Electrocardiogram
Patients over 65 years of age, with abnormal electrocardiogram or a history of hypertension, coronary heart disease, or diabetes mellitus, must undergo cardiac color Doppler ultrasound
Patients over 65 years of age with a history of emphysema, chronic bronchitis, asthma, or other respiratory system diseases, or with other systemic diseases affecting lung function, must undergo pulmonary function tests

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
Psychological Nursing
Patients have varying degrees of anxiety before surgery, worrying about the safety of the surgery and the postoperative condition. Therefore, nursing staff should guide patients to maintain a good mental state and correctly treat the disease. Patients should understand the necessity of surgery and have some psychological preparation for possible complications. If patients have any questions or concerns, they should ask the doctor and nursing staff in a timely manner, and approach the surgery with a positive attitude.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
Respiratory Preparation
Smoking increases bronchial secretions and exacerbates respiratory symptoms. Patients should quit smoking 2 weeks before surgery.
Consciously perform deep breathing and coughing exercises to facilitate lung expansion after surgery. Patients should practice abdominal deep breathing and effective sputum expectoration training.
Prevent respiratory tract infections

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
Gastrointestinal Preparation
To prevent vomiting during anesthesia or surgery that may lead to asphyxia or aspiration pneumonia, patients should eat soft foods the night before surgery. Generally, fasting and no drinking are recommended after 10 pm the night before surgery.
Since most patients are not accustomed to defecating in bed, especially after surgery due to surgical trauma and the effects of anesthesia, urinary retention and constipation are easily occurred. Therefore, patients should be instructed on bed-based defecation before surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
Skin Preparation
Maintain personal hygiene, take a bath, cut nails, change into clean surgical attire.
Prepare the surgical area according to requirements (for thoracotomy, shave the hair on the chest skin and axillary hair on the surgical side).

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Handover Process on the Day of Surgery and in the Operating Room
Cross-check surgical orders, medical records, imaging data, and jointly verify patient information and wristband identification.
Patient's basic condition, vital signs, whether there are movable dentures, prosthetics, skin preparation, allergies, etc.
Empty the bladder, apply preoperative medication as ordered.
Prepare the required medical records, radiographic films, CT films, MRI films, and medications, and jointly verify with operating room staff.
If there are valuable items, hand them over to the family for safekeeping.
Complete the handover according to the surgery handover form, record, and sign.
Prepare the bed unit and various monitoring instruments according to the surgery situation, such as oxygen, drainage bags, vacuum devices, electrocardiogram monitors, etc.

Postoperative Nursing

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
Observation of Vital Signs
The surgical trauma and anesthesia have a significant impact on respiratory and circulatory function, and patients may experience unstable respiratory and circulatory function. Therefore, it is very important to monitor blood pressure, pulse, and respiration regularly after surgery. Therefore, postoperative electrocardiogram monitoring is provided for 48-72 hours.
Measure body temperature four times a day for the first three days after surgery. Apply physical降温 if the temperature exceeds 38.5℃.
There are many opportunities for fluid balance disorders in surgical patients, which can include dehydration or fluid overload, blood electrolyte disorders, or acid-base disturbances. Therefore, it is often necessary to record the patient's intake and output after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
Positional Care
After returning to the ward, assist with bed transfer, and position the patient according to the anesthetic and surgical methods. For thoracotomy, the head of the bed should be elevated 30° and the foot of the bed 15°; patients with spinal or epidural anesthesia should remain supine for 6 hours after surgery, and then take a semi-recumbent or sitting position when the patient recovers from anesthesia and blood pressure is stable.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
Closed Chest Drainage Nursing
Maintain the integrity and sterility of the管道
Encourage patients to perform coughing and deep breathing exercises to facilitate the drainage of effusion, restore pleural cavity negative pressure, and allow lung re-expansion
Maintain unobstructed drainage, squeeze the drainage tube every 30-60 minutes to prevent it from being compressed, kinked, or blocked
Securely fix
The liquid level in the water-seal bottle should be 60 cm below the chest outlet level of the drainage tube. Under no circumstances should the drainage bottle be higher than the patient's chest, to prevent the backflow of drainage fluid into the pleural cavity and cause infection
Observe and record the color, quantity, shape, and range of fluctuation of the drainage, change the water-seal bottle daily, make a mark, and record the drainage volume.
If the drainage volume exceeds 100 ml/h, continuously observe for 4-6 hours and no reduction is seen, and the bedside chest X-ray shows a shadow of a clotted hemothorax, with respiratory and circulatory obstruction, pulse over 120/min, and respiration over 30/min, then it is diagnosed as active bleeding within the chest cavity, and reoperation is required to stop the bleeding. Therefore, if the chest drainage volume exceeds 100 ml per hour, it should be reported to the doctor immediately.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
Respiratory Care
Administer oxygen at a flow rate of 3-4 L/min.
Maintain a clear airway, encourage patients to cough after surgery, and inform patients that postoperative coughing and expectoration are important measures to prevent postoperative respiratory tract infections.
Assist patients with back percussion.
For sputum that is thick and difficult to cough up, nebulizer inhalation can be administered to dilute the sputum, reduce sputum resistance, and facilitate sputum expectoration. This also allows the aerosol to enter the alveoli, reducing the risk of infection.
Encourage patients to blow balloons. Deep breathing by the patient causes the diaphragm to descend, improving ineffective alveolar ventilation, facilitating the drainage of pleural effusion and fluid, ensuring effective ventilation, preventing lung infection, and preventing atelectasis.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
Coughing and Percussion Techniques
Effective coughing technique: when coughing, press the surgical side of the chest with both hands, take a deep breath, relax the hands, and cough while pressing the chest tightly. This can reduce the vibration of the chest caused by coughing, alleviate incision pain. Perform abdominal breathing 3-5 times first, then slowly take a deep breath and hold the breath for 2 seconds before coughing forcefully twice. Before expectoration, drink a small amount of warm water or follow the doctor's instructions for nebulizer inhalation.

Technique: The patient takes a semi-recumbent position, and the operator stands on the patient's affected side. The palm is in a ring shape, and the opposite lung is percussed with the wrist strength, from the bottom up, from the outside in, 1 time/1-2h.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Dietary Guidance
Water can be consumed 3 hours after surgery, and light, fluid foods such as congee can be eaten after 6 hours postoperatively.
In the early postoperative period, it is advisable to consume light, easily digestible foods, gradually increasing high-protein, high-calorie, and high-vitamin diets. At the same time, attention should be given to consuming more coarse fiber foods to maintain smooth bowel movements.
Avoid high-fat foods and prevent vomiting and weight gain.
For the first three months postoperatively, it is not advisable to consume cold water, coffee, tea, alcohol, and other stimulating foods.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
Activity Guidance
Early postoperative activity is beneficial for the recovery of the entire body's functions, such as preventing pulmonary complications, promoting blood circulation, facilitating wound healing, preventing the formation of lower limb venous thrombosis, and promoting the recovery of urinary function. When getting out of bed, it is recommended to sit on the edge of the bed for 30 seconds first.
