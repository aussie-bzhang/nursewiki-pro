## Overview
 1. Person-centred nursing: It refers to observing, judging, and dealing with patients' responses to pain during diagnosis and treatment, and trying to meet the individual needs and changes in the overall aspects of patients' emotions, psychology, and function during the process of injury or treatment.
2. With the continuous development of medical levels, people have higher requirements for the quality of nursing. Patients need not only excellent diagnostic and treatment techniques but also high-quality nursing services. The service宗旨 is for the patients, thereby improving the satisfaction of surgical patients. We will explore high-quality nursing services in the operating room from several aspects.

## Preoperative Visit
1. The preoperative visit is generally conducted in the afternoon one day before the surgery, with the visiting nurse being the circulator nurse who will assist in the patient's surgery. It is advisable to avoid the patient's mealtime or other treatments, and the visit should be kept between 10 to 15 minutes, with an attempt to provide a quiet and comfortable visiting environment to reduce noise.
2. Before the visit, carefully read the medical and nursing records to understand the surgery and anesthesia methods, the patient's condition, and various biochemical test results. Understand the patient's general condition, mental and emotional state, sensory status, motor nerve status, defecation, respiration, circulation, body temperature, skin, and electrolyte balance status, and conduct preoperative assessment. From the perspective of nursing, anticipate the potential problems during surgery, and take proactive and effective measures for prevention, ensuring that the entire process of surgery is well-prepared.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
## Preoperative Visit
3. Upon entering the ward, make an active self-introduction to eliminate the unfamiliarity between the nurse and the patient, then explain the purpose of the visit. Briefly introduce the environment of the operating room, the medical facilities for surgery; introduce the general situation of the surgical doctor and anesthesiologist.
4. Encourage the patient to express their wishes and concerns, inform them of the position during surgery, possible discomforts, necessary cooperation, and pre-admission注意事项: fasting and drinking time; changing to clean clothes on the surgery day; if there are valuable items, jewelry, and dentures, please entrust them to the family; female patients should not wear makeup, etc.
5. According to the patient's different psychological state, acceptance ability, living habits, and family background, use kind and warm words to comfort and encourage them, carry out targeted psychological nursing, and pay attention to protecting the patient's privacy, enhancing the patient's confidence.

## Intraoperative Nursing
1. On the day of the surgery, the nurse who conducted the preoperative visit will go to the ward to pick up the patient, reducing the patient's sense of unfamiliarity, and hand over to the ward nurse. After checking and confirming that there are no discrepancies, lead the patient into the operating room, change into special slippers, a maternity hat, and a special surgical gown, completing the surgery with the most beautiful appearance and best condition.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
## Intraoperative Nursing
2. Support the patient into the operating room, assist the patient in lying on the operating bed, cover the blanket, protect the patient's privacy, and conduct a three-party check by the circulator nurse, the surgeon, and the anesthesiologist to verify the patient's information.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
## Intraoperative Nursing
3. After verifying the information, conduct an anesthesia assessment, perform venipuncture. When performing the puncture, instruct the patient not to move, distract the patient's attention during the puncture (such as throwing out a sentence unrelated to the surgery, where do you live), and insert the needle quickly to reduce pain. After the puncture, re-educate the patient on the benefits of the catheter.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
## Intraoperative Nursing
4. Assist the doctor in positioning the patient, at the same time, explain to the patient the importance of the correct position for surgery, anesthesia, and preventing postoperative complications. Prevent incorrect positioning from affecting respiration and circulation or causing pressure sores on the limbs. Make full use of position pads to make the surgical patient more comfortable and the exposure clearer, maintaining the movement of the chest and the functional position of the limbs.

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
## Intraoperative Nursing
5. Pay attention to psychological nursing, understand the patient's needs in a timely manner, and meet their needs. Pay special attention to several time periods: such as during anesthesia, when the surgery begins, when the patient feels uncomfortable, when vital signs change. The circulator nurse must be by the patient's side, providing comfort and encouragement.
6. For anxious mothers, provide hand-holding services. Communicate with the patient, hold the patient's hand. For example, before the anesthesia is administered, the circulator nurse will say to the patient, "Please hold my hand, I will accompany you through this special moment. If you are afraid, please tell me, and we will share the burden together."

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
## Intraoperative Nursing
7. During the surgery, pay attention to adjusting the room temperature. Wrap a scarf around the patient's neck, cover the outstretched arms with warm blankets, and only remove the blanket covering the patient when the doctor is ready to disinfect; after the surgery is over, when sending the patient out of the operating room, help the patient dress properly, protect the patient's privacy, and pay attention to keeping warm. In cold weather, heat the infused fluids and abdominal lavage solutions to prevent intraoperative hypothermia.
8. Closely monitor the patient's vital signs changes, pay attention to ECG monitoring, and ensure the smooth flow of intravenous fluids. Pay close attention to the patient's facial expressions and the operation of various instruments to ensure the smooth progress of the surgery and the safety of the patient. Report the birth of the baby to the family as soon as the baby is born, inform them that the surgery has gone smoothly so far, and it will take about half an hour for the mother and baby to return to the ward. Please do not worry or be anxious.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Intraoperative Nursing
9. Strictly follow all nursing operation procedures in the operating room. Carefully maintain various nursing records. Requirements: all nursing tasks should be stable, accurate, gentle, and quick. During surgery, do not discuss topics unrelated to the operation, avoid loud talking, and demonstrate compassionate care.
10. Establish a patient transportation and reception register, a rapid pathology register, and improve the handover process and the writing of handover registers. Carefully fill out the surgical nursing record, self-check for potential safety hazards, and ensure the safety of surgical patient care.
11. After the operation, inform the patient that the surgery went smoothly, thank them for their cooperation, and inform them that they will be sent back to the ward shortly. A detailed handover with the ward nurse is to be conducted, advising the patient to rest well and to contact the doctor or nurse immediately if they feel unwell. I will visit you again tomorrow.

# Postoperative Visit
1. Upon entering the ward, greet the patient, introduce yourself, and explain the purpose of the visit. Through conversation with the patient, understand the patient's and family's attitude and psychological state towards the surgery, as well as their understanding and coping with the surgery. Answer the patient's questions in a more easily understandable manner. If unable to answer, seek assistance from the doctor for a detailed explanation.
2. Address the nursing issues raised by the patient, provide comfort and explanation, and for issues that cannot be resolved promptly, communicate with the nurse manager for timely resolution and improvement.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Postoperative Follow-up
3. Follow up on postoperative patients, patiently answer their questions, offer encouraging words, send sincere blessings to the patients; understand the patient's satisfaction with the operating room nursing staff's work and services.
4. Provide feedback to the surgeons to understand the intraoperative cooperation and areas of service that need improvement to enhance the synergy between medical staff;
5. Inquire with the ward nurses about the projects that need improvement in the transfer and handover process. Through these feedbacks, understand the shortcomings in the operating room nursing work, carefully complete the postoperative follow-up record, and formulate corrective measures to better serve patients.
