# Lumbar Spondylolisthesis

A condition caused by abnormal bony fusion between adjacent vertebral bodies due to congenital malformation, trauma, or wear and tear, resulting in partial or complete displacement of the superior vertebral body relative to the inferior vertebral body. In a normal lumbar spine, the vertebrae are aligned neatly. If, due to congenital or acquired reasons, one lumbar vertebral body slips forward relative to the adjacent lumbar vertebra.

# Psychological Care

Most patients experience varying degrees of tension and fear. In response to this state of mind, we not only comfort and encourage the patients but also use easily understandable language when communicating with patients and their families. We explain the principles of the surgery and successful case examples to eliminate the patients' concerns and enhance their confidence in overcoming the disease.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Preoperative Preparation

Limit activity to prevent further slippage;
Prepare for skin preparation 1 day before surgery, ensuring not to damage the skin;
Complete relevant examinations, and prepare 400-800ml of blood if necessary;
Follow routine preoperative fasting and water restriction, and perform a cleansing enema the night before surgery;
Administer antibiotics to prevent infection in the morning of surgery;
Practice bed toilet before surgery.

# General Postoperative Care

Transfer the patient with 3-4 people, keeping the spine in a horizontal position and supporting it to the bed;
Closely monitor vital signs;
Observe the lower limb sensation, motor function, and sphincter function;
Observe the wound drainage, pain, limb sensation, and motor function;
Properly secure all drainage tubes, observe the color, nature, and amount of drainage, and frequently squeeze the drainage tubes to ensure unobstructed flow.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Positional Care

Follow the routine care for continuous epidural anesthesia or general anesthesia, lying flat on the back with no pillow for 6 hours, and turning the head of the patient under general anesthesia to one side. Start turning every 2 hours after 6 hours, paying attention to gentle movements and observing the wound bleeding.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Observation and Prevention of Early Postoperative Complications

The main early postoperative complication is infection. Therefore, dynamically observe the patient's body temperature and local wound condition (including redness, swelling, heat, and pain) after surgery, and prevent deep vein thrombosis formation about a week later by performing more lower limb movements to promote blood circulation.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Inpatient Rehabilitation Guidance

Encourage early functional exercise:
① Guide the patient to perform straight leg elevation from 3 to 5 days postoperatively, mainly to exercise the quadriceps muscle;
② Perform exercises to relax the piriformis muscle;
③ Perform bridge-type back extension exercises (including three-point support and five-point support) and prone back extension exercises 3 weeks after surgery to enhance lumbar stability;
④ One month after surgery, wear a lumbar corset to get out of bed and move around, paying attention to protective measures, the range of movement, and intensity should increase gradually.

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
# Discharge Instructions

Instruct the patient not to perform excessive bending and weight-bearing activities early on, try to reduce spinal curvature, and gradually return to normal life. Explain the knowledge of preventing recurrence and loss of spondylolisthesis reduction to the patient.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Summary

The perioperative nursing of lumbar spondylolisthesis patients is an indispensable condition for the success of surgery;
Nurses should correctly assess the patient's psychology and mental state, identify existing or potential nursing problems, develop detailed nursing plans, and revise them in accordance with changes in the condition. Good preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative care, especially postoperative condition observation and rehabilitation training, play a very important role in preventing incision infection, preventing nerve root and dura mater adhesion, improving local blood circulation, preventing deep vein thrombosis, and promoting the reduction and absorption of local inflammation and edema.
