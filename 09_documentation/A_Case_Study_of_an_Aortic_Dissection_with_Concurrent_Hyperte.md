# Introduction
Aortic Dissection:
Aortic dissection originates from the tear of the intimal and middle layers of the aorta, forming an intimal tear. This allows the middle layer to be directly exposed to the lumen. Under the driving force of pulse pressure, the aortic blood flows through the intimal tear directly into the diseased middle layer, separating the middle layer to form a dissection.

The male-to-female incidence ratio is 3:1.
The highest incidence age group is 50-60 years old.

# Case Description
Admission Complaint: The patient was admitted to the hospital at 23:47 on October 24, 2017, due to "sudden chest pain with profuse sweating for 4 hours"
Current Medical History: The patient suddenly developed chest pain around 19:00 on October 24, 2017, without a clear cause. The pain was tearing-like, without radiation pain. The chest pain persisted and could not be relieved, accompanied by profuse sweating. There was no headache, dizziness, occasional cough, and sputum. The patient was then referred to our hospital for further diagnosis and treatment, and was admitted to the Department of Cardiology for further treatment. Since the onset of the disease, the patient has been tired, with a general appetite and poor sleep. Bowel movements and urination were normal, and there was no significant weight change. The patient was given multi-parameter cardiac monitoring, nasal oxygen inhalation, and symptomatic treatment for blood pressure control, anti-inflammatory, and myocardial nutrition.

# Discharge Plan
Guide the patient to focus on rest after discharge, gradually increase activity levels, and pay attention to balancing work and rest

Instruct the patient to follow a low-salt, low-fat diet, quit smoking and alcohol, eat more fresh fruits and vegetables, and consume foods rich in dietary fiber to maintain smooth bowel movements
Guide the patient to learn to adjust their psychological state, regulate negative emotions, maintain a pleasant mood, and avoid emotional excitement

Follow the doctor's instructions to continue taking medication, control blood pressure, and do not adjust the dosage arbitrarily
The change in the patient's lifestyle after the illness requires the active cooperation and support of family members, guide the patient's family to create a good environment for physical and mental修养
Regular follow-up visits, seek medical attention promptly if chest, abdominal, or lumbar pain symptoms occur
Teach the patient to self-measure heart rate and pulse, and keep a blood pressure monitor at home, measuring regularly

# Post-discharge Follow-up
15/11: Telephone follow-up, the family reported that the patient lives in Shenzhen after discharge, has a blood oxygen monitor and a blood pressure monitor at home, quit smoking and alcohol during this period of discharge, developed the habit of early to bed and early to rise, walks with the elderly in the park in the morning and evening, checks blood pressure and pulse in the morning and evening, takes medication regularly, blood pressure is basically fluctuating between 100-140/60-90mmHg
22/11: Telephone follow-up, the family reported that the patient often plays Mahjong with friends recently, often stays up until 1 or 2 in the morning, does not take medication regularly, does not exercise, and has a slightly elevated blood pressure (about 160/100mmHg) in the past two days without chest, abdominal, or lumbar pain symptoms.
1/12: Telephone follow-up, the family reported that after persuasion, the patient can develop good habits of regular rest, take medication regularly, and the aortic CT showed that the stent had not shifted, and there was no new occurrence of aortic dissection
