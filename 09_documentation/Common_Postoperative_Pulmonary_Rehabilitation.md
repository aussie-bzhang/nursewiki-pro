# I. Overview
Perioperative Period:
         The period from the time the patient decides to undergo surgical treatment to the time when the treatment related to this surgery is basically completed. The duration varies depending on the size and duration of the surgery.

Perioperative Management:
         The preparation for the patient's surgery and the promotion of postoperative recovery.
         Centered around surgical treatment, including preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative stages of management.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
I. Overview
Pulmonary Rehabilitation

Based on a comprehensive assessment of the patient's health status, with the aim of improving the patient's respiratory function, a personalized comprehensive management plan is established through various non-pharmacological means, including exercise, psychological education, common disease prevention and treatment knowledge education, removal of etiology or exacerbating factors of the disease, etc.

   Target population includes: various pathological conditions that may lead to respiratory function impairment: respiratory failure, heart failure, neurological and spinal cord diseases, limited mobility, aspiration, chronic airway diseases, psychological disorders, perioperative period, etc.

# II. Physical Therapy Plan During the Perioperative Period
Preoperative Assessment Content:
Preoperative assessment content includes past medical history, smoking status, physical examination, to inspect whether the patient has symptoms of respiratory system diseases. If chest X-ray findings suggest, it is recommended that the patient undergo pulmonary function tests, arterial blood gas tests, etc.

Purpose

01
Screen for patients who may develop pulmonary complications
02
Provide general postoperative care instructions, including the surgery itself, wound location, use of respirators, and注意事项 when there is an endotracheal tube in place, such as inability to speak
03
Provide guidance on chest physical therapy, instructing patients to strengthen deep breathing and coughing exercises before surgery to enhance lung function and place them in the best lung function state

# Three: Precautions in Common Surgical Procedures and Treatments
## Precautions:

1. Respiratory training should strengthen the expansion of remaining lung tissue. If a unilateral lung resection is performed, strengthen diaphragmatic breathing and unilateral breathing, and enhance chest wall movement.
2. During cough training, place a pillow under the armpits and perform cough training according to the different patients, starting from light coughs to continuous heavy coughs.
3. During postural drainage, ensure that the chest drainage tube is not obstructed and the patient can walk around with assistance. When expectorating, keep the affected side up, with the therapist using one hand to tap and the other to stabilize the wound.
4. Pay attention to the range of motion of the limbs on the affected side, the training of muscle strength, and the correction of posture during physical therapy.
5. Begin turning and standing training as soon as possible, progressing to walking.
6. For moderate to severe air leak phenomena, rest more in bed, perform postural drainage and percussion, and do not emphasize deep breathing or the use of pulmonary function instruments.
7. Patients who have had thymectomy can perform standard drainage positions if necessary.
8. For patients with pectus excavatum or pectus carinatum, protect the chest wall for the first three months after surgery, avoid pressure or collisions, and pay attention to posture correction. Begin deep breathing exercises one month after surgery, and increase chest expansion exercises after three months.

## (II) Cardiac Surgery
Generally, cardiac surgery is performed for congenital heart disease patients to correct congenital heart malformations or for rheumatic heart disease patients to perform valve整形 and valve replacement surgery or for ischemic heart disease patients to perform coronary artery bypass surgery.

## (III) Abdominal Surgery
In recent years, laparoscopic surgery has increased in indications and is the main method for abdominal surgery. The therapist should pay attention to the position of the abdominal surgical incision and assess its potential impact on the respiratory tract. Patients often wear abdominal binders, and they should be reminded not to wear them too tightly as it may affect breathing.

## (IV) Esophageal Surgery
Esophageal surgery wounds may be located differently, with the wound extending to three locations: the lower neck, the lateral and posterior chest, and the abdomen. The wound is extensive, usually painful, and the patient is weak, so great attention should be paid to pulmonary function rehabilitation training.

## (V) Neurosurgical Surgery
Understanding the pathophysiology of the patient's brain nervous system in neurosurgical surgery can help understand respiratory issues. Brain surgery and postoperative care aim to restore brain function, striving to maintain central nervous system perfusion and oxygenation, reduce brain metabolism, and prevent secondary brain injury.

1. Airway patency
2. Sufficient oxygen to the brain
3. Sputum removal

## (VI) Head and Neck Surgery
Mostly for the treatment of cancer, common diagnoses include oral cancer, laryngeal cancer, etc. When treating such patients, attention should be paid to the social and psychological status of the patients, as well as their concerns about appearance, recovery, and worries.

## Precautions:

1. Keep the head of the bed at about 30° to reduce swelling in the head and neck. During postural drainage, the bed can be tilted flat, and after treatment, return to the original position. When turning, keep the head in the middle or slightly forward.
2. Avoid performing oral or nasal suctioning unless necessary, as it may injure the esophageal suture site.
3. Do not guide diaphragmatic breathing, as the diaphragm may have been injured during surgery, and the patient should only maintain deep breathing to expand the lungs and maintain airway hygiene.

## (VII) Neurosurgical Surgery
## Precautions:

1. If there is epidural monitoring, the intracranial pressure should not exceed 20 mmHg at any time, and the cerebral perfusion pressure should be less than 50 mmHg. When transitioning from a semi-sitting position to a flat position or to a higher position, intracranial pressure increases, so the patient's condition should be considered when selecting the appropriate position for sputum drainage. When the patient can breathe spontaneously but is unconscious, stimulation of the trachea can assist with spontaneous coughing, or the chest wall can be stimulated to induce spontaneous deep breathing movements.
2. For patients with spinal cord surgery, use the whole-body rotation method when turning, and pay attention to cervical traction to avoid being affected by turning. If the abdominal muscles are weak, abdominal pushing techniques can be provided from the acute phase to assist with effective sputum expectoration.
3. For patients with multiple injuries, adjust the force and frequency according to the patient's tolerance, or use vibration to increase the depth and stimulate coughing. If the patient can breathe spontaneously, respiratory exercises and techniques to assist with coughing are generally sufficient, and techniques such as postural drainage and manual percussion or vibration are not necessary.

## (VIII) Head and Neck Surgery
## Precautions:

1. Keep the head of the bed at about 30° to reduce swelling in the head and neck. During postural drainage, the bed can be tilted flat, and after treatment, return to the original position. When turning, use the whole-body rotation method to avoid twisting the skin flaps. After turning, check the integrity of the skin flaps, and if there are any abnormalities, reset them promptly. Avoid percussion for sputum removal near the skin flaps.
2. Pay attention to the hygiene and humidity of the airway in tracheostomy patients (sterile normal saline or复苏 bag can be dripped into the tracheostomy incision to stimulate coughing and chest wall vibration during exhalation, which can help cough out sputum from deeper areas), and perform preventive pulmonary rehabilitation.
