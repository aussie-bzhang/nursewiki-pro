Cerebral Infarction
       Cerebral infarction is a condition caused by various reasons leading to local cerebral tissue ischemia and hypoxia, resulting in necrosis of cerebral tissue and subsequent clinical manifestations of neurological dysfunction.

Smoking, diet
Lack of physical activity, obesity
Excessive mental stress and depression
Underlying cardiovascular disease
High-risk factors
Etiology: The most common cause of cerebral infarction is the presence of atherosclerosis, which is a factor in the development of cerebral infarction.
High-risk factors

3. Maintain a safe environment to prevent accidents.
4. Promote effective communication and collaboration with the healthcare team.

# 护理措施
1. Assess the patient's mobility status and identify any limitations or risks.
2. Develop and implement a personalized care plan to address mobility issues.
3. Provide education on fall prevention strategies and encourage the use of assistive devices when necessary.
4. Collaborate with the physical therapy team to ensure appropriate rehabilitation interventions.
5. Monitor the patient's progress and adjust the care plan as needed.

# Urinary Reflex

Urination is a spinal reflex and is controlled by the brain's higher centers, which can be promoted or inhibited by consciousness. Normal urinary control includes controllable bladder storage and timely, complete bladder emptying.

## Spinal Cord (Sacral Segment)

### Urinary Physiology:

**Bladder Filling**
- Stimulated pressure receptors
-传入 sympathetic nerve
- Spinal cord urinary lower center

**Cerebral Cortex Produces Urge to Urinate**
- Reflexive inhibition of pudendal nerve of the body, relaxing the external urethral sphincter
- Parasympathetic nerve excitation
- Spinal cord center
- Efferent nerve
- Bladder detrusor muscle contraction, bladder internal sphincter relaxation
- Urine production

# Neurogenic Bladder
**Treatment:**
- **I. Conservative Treatment:**
  1. Behavioral training (timed voiding, prompting voiding)
  2. Medication
  3. Catheterization therapy (intermittent catheterization)
  4. External collector
  5. Intralesional drug perfusion therapy
- **II. Surgical Treatment:**
  - Management of storage and voiding disorders
- **III. Neural Regulation and Neural Stimulation**
- **IV. Others: Neural bridging, nerve regeneration, tissue engineering, bioengineering, etc.**

# Summary
**Gains**
- Learned nursing knowledge for elderly patients with stroke (dysphagia, mobility impairment, urinary and fecal incontinence, etc.)
- Applied GCS, Iwata Drinking Test, MMSE, BADL, and nutritional screening assessment forms
- Practiced abdominal acupoint massage, finger strength anal dilation, passive exercise, active exercise, and bladder pressure measurement
- Accumulated experience in individual case care

**Shortcomings**
1. Lack of specialized knowledge in rehabilitation, insufficient understanding of neurological dysfunction due to cerebral infarction, and failure to conduct a comprehensive multidisciplinary assessment and early nursing intervention upon admission.
2. Health education for caregivers is inadequate.
