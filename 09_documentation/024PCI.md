01 Purpose and Background

The purpose and the background

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
Background
Acute onset

Severe condition

PCI surgery is currently the preferred treatment for acute myocardial infarction, which can effectively improve myocardial perfusion and achieve vascular recanalization [3]. The application of PCI surgery has reduced the in-hospital mortality rate of acute myocardial infarction to about 4%.

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is a severe coronary heart disease that occurs on the basis of coronary artery lesions, with acute and severe reduction or occlusion of coronary blood supply, leading to severe and persistent acute ischemia of myocardium and myocardial necrosis [1]. Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) has an acute onset and is a life-threatening condition [2].

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
Cardiac Rehabilitation

Cardiac rehabilitation refers to the application of various coordinated and purposeful interventions, including rehabilitation assessment, exercise training, dietary guidance, lifestyle guidance, medication adherence, regular monitoring of indicators, and health education, to improve the quality of life of patients, return to normal social life, and prevent cardiovascular events [4]. Cardiac rehabilitation is an essential (preventive) measure, (health) knowledge, and behavioral patterns for patients with myocardial infarction to completely overcome cardiovascular death. Exercise therapy is an important component of secondary prevention in modern cardiac rehabilitation programs [5]. Exercise therapy is beneficial in reducing the risk of death from cardiovascular diseases [6].

### Nursing Diagnosis 1 - Chest tightness, chest pain - Related to myocardial ischemia and necrosis
| Nursing Rationale | Chest tightness, scapular pain, tingling sensation in both upper limbs |
| --- | --- |
| Nursing Goal | Disappearance of symptoms of chest tightness, scapular pain, and tingling sensation in both upper limbs |
| Nursing Measures | Oxygen therapy: Administered medium flow continuous oxygen 3L/min; Follow medical orders for dilation, antiplatelet aggregation, lipid regulation, etc.; Maintain a quiet ward environment, avoid adverse stimuli, stabilize emotions, understand the reasons for emotional excitement, counsel patients, and communicate with family members to reduce myocardial oxygen consumption; Closely observe the location, nature, and duration of chest tightness, chest pain, and radiation pain, monitor with electrocardiogram, and accurately record intake and output; Strengthen nursing care and rounds, implement fall prevention measures, prevent accidents; |
| Nursing Evaluation | No chest tightness, scapular pain, or tingling sensation in both upper limbs reported on 25/10 |

### Nursing Diagnosis 2 - Inability to tolerate activity - Related to reduced cardiac output

| Nursing Rationale | Absolute bed rest post-PCI on 24/10, muscle strength assessment: Grade 4 |
| --- | --- |
| Nursing Goals | Muscle strength assessment: Grade 5 |
| Nursing Measures | During the 24/10 absolute bed rest period, instruct the patient in turning over in bed, placing daily items in easily accessible locations; assist with assuming semi-recumbent or upright positions for washing or eating, 20-30 minutes per session, 3 times a day; instruct ankle pump exercises: plantar flexion and dorsiflexion, each movement held for 10 seconds, 20 repetitions per session, 3 times a day, perform more voluntary movements to prevent DVT formation; on 25/10 in the afternoon, transferred to a general ward, remote monitoring, instruct bedside activities, 15-20 minutes per session, 3 times a day; on 26/10, the patient moves around in the ward and ward, mainly with slow walking, 5-10 minutes per session, intermittent exercise, remote monitoring, heart rate controlled at 70-80 beats per minute; instruct the patient in "three half-minutes" to strengthen fall prevention; |
| Nursing Evaluation | On 26/10, muscle strength assessment: Grade 5 |
