**Introduction**

Introduction

1. Stroke is one of the most serious diseases threatening human life and health in the world today. In this patient group, infection is one of the most common complications, with an incidence rate of 7% to 22% during the acute phase of stroke. This represents a large patient population. Moreover, pneumonia is an important risk factor for stroke mortality; in the United States, the average additional cost for patients with pneumonia after stroke is $14,836 per case.[1]
2. Dysphagia, as a common complication of cerebrovascular diseases, is increasingly prevalent with the rising incidence of cerebrovascular diseases year by year. The complications caused by dysphagia, such as aspiration pneumonia, impaired intake of water and nutrients, asphyxia, and psychological disorders, seriously affect the quality of life of patients, increase the rate of disability and mortality.[2]

**Palliative Care Model**

1. World Health Organization definition of palliative care: To help patients and their families face the problems brought about by life-threatening diseases, and to identify pain and other problems, including psychological, social, and spiritual issues, early on. This involves careful assessment to prevent and alleviate suffering, and improve the quality of life.
2. Palliative care: Life care, improving patient comfort, providing psychological care and support to patients and their families, and providing comprehensive care for patients' physical, mental, social, and spiritual well-being, thereby alleviating patient suffering and improving the quality of life.
3. Physical: Pain relief care, alleviating discomfort; Mental: Social support, comprehensive care; Social: Positive emotions, positive life; Spiritual: Spiritual care, facing life calmly.
