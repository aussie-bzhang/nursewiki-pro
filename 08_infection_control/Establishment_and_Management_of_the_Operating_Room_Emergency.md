# CONCEPT
Cardiac arrest is a series of manifestations caused by various reasons, leading to the sudden cessation of normal contraction and blood supply function of the heart, causing a complete interruption of systemic circulation, severe hypoxia and metabolic disorders in various tissues and organs.
Cardiac arrest can be primary or secondary.
Respiratory and cardiac arrest in surgical patients can occur in various situations, such as coronary heart disease, myocardial infarction, rheumatic heart disease, myocarditis, congenital heart disease, cerebrovascular accidents, severe trauma, poisoning, water, electrolyte, and acid-base imbalance, anesthesia or surgical accidents, hypothermia, shock, etc. Once respiratory and cardiac arrest occurs in surgical patients, immediate rescue should be organized to actively save the patient's life.

② Absence of pulse in major arteries, disappearance of carotid and femoral artery pulsations;

⑥ No bleeding from surgical incisions, dark red blood color in the surgical field;

# Emergency Measures - General Measures

Keep the airway open and perform artificial respiration promptly. Execute medical orders promptly and administer medication accurately; oral orders must be repeated once before execution. Label the syringes used for adding medication with the type; the liquid packaging bags should indicate the name and dosage of the medication contained. Retain the medication bags, ampoules, etc., until the rescue is completed for verification and statistics.

# Emergency Measures - Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)
     External cardiac massage: ① Position: The patient lies flat on their back without a pillow on a firm surface. In adults: Open the airway using the head-tilt, chin-lift method or the jaw-thrust maneuver (suitable for patients with cervical spine injuries), tilting the head back 90°, clearing the oral cavity of foreign objects to maintain an open airway; external cardiac massage: The operator places the palm root of the left hand at the midpoint of the two nipples, with the hands' palm roots overlapping, and the arms pressing vertically on the sternum, with a compression depth of ≥5cm, and a compression rate of not less than 100 times/minute. ② Compression site: The lower half of the sternum, that is, the midpoint of the sternum along the line of the two nipples. ③ Compression precautions: Do not be too forceful or too strong to avoid rib fractures. The compression and relaxation time each account for 50%; the pressure must be uniform. After each compression, the pressure must be completely released to ensure that the chest wall fully rebounds; the palm root cannot be lifted off the chest during compression and relaxation; minimize interruptions during compression, with no interruptions exceeding 10 seconds. While performing external cardiac massage, artificial respiration should be given at a ratio of 30:2. The compression should be paused during artificial respiration.

# II. Emergency Response Plan for Surgical Patients with Shock

<!-- Slide number: 41 -->
2026/5/11
# CONCEPT
  Shock (shock) is a metabolic disorder and cellular damage caused by insufficient perfusion of effective circulating blood volume.
Shock can be classified into hypovolemic shock, septic shock, cardiogenic shock, neurogenic shock, and anaphylactic shock. Surgical shock is mainly the first two.
Hemorrhagic shock and traumatic shock both belong to hypovolemic shock. Hemorrhagic shock is more common in esophageal variceal rupture, ulcer bleeding, liver and spleen rupture, ectopic pregnancy, or excessive blood loss during surgery.
Traumatic shock is more common in fractures, crush injuries, etc., where blood loss occurs to the outside, body cavity, or plasma, and blood渗ates into tissue spaces, causing a sharp decrease in circulating blood volume.
Nurses should carefully and dynamically observe blood loss and vital signs during surgery to actively prevent and respond to the occurrence of shock.

<!-- Slide number: 42 -->
2026/5/11
# CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS
Early manifestations: The patient is anxious or restless, pale, cold and wet hands and feet, rapid heartbeat, and increased blood pressure.
Late manifestations: The patient's skin is cold and wet, urine output decreases, pulse is rapid, heart rate increases, responsiveness is dull, and consciousness is blurred.

<!-- Slide number: 43 -->
2026/5/11
# Emergency Measures
The patient lies flat on their back in a supine position or with the head and feet elevated by 30 degrees, taking a semiconcave position to increase return blood volume and reduce respiratory burden.
Oxygen therapy and maintaining an open airway, establish an artificial airway according to the condition, use an artificial respirator and face mask to provide oxygen, maintain arterial blood oxygen tension at 85-100mmHg, and oxygen saturation above 95%.
Continuous electrocardiogram monitoring, radial artery catheterization to monitor blood pressure changes, maintain systolic blood pressure at 90-100mmHg.
Timely volume resuscitation: Open venous access, at least one venous access connected to a blood transfusion set (16-18# catheter two accesses or central venous catheter), start with plasma volume expanders such as plasma, and immediately start rapid blood transfusion after cross-matching.

<!-- Slide number: 44 -->
2026/5/11
# Emergency Measures
Execute medical orders quickly and accurately, repeat oral orders twice to confirm accuracy before administration, and keep empty ampoules or drug bottles for review after rescue.
Actively manage the primary disease,
Indwelling urinary catheter: Monitor urine output, color, and characteristics, maintain an hourly urine output of more than 30ml.
Strictly implement the "three checks and seven matches" system, implement sterile technique operation procedures.
Pay attention to warmth, maintain room temperature at 22-26°C, use a warming blanket if necessary, to prevent hypothermia and decreased metabolism rate in the patient.

# Overflow of Water
Overflow of water in the operating room can occur due to aging, damage, or improper use of water supply equipment or pipelines. Severe overflow not only damages the floor, walls, and equipment but can also cause electrical leakage or even fires. The destructive power of natural disasters such as earthquakes is extremely strong. When such emergencies occur in the operating room, staff should remain calm and composed, correctly perform first aid according to the emergency plan, actively respond, and effectively evacuate.

# Emergency Plan for Patients in the Operating Room Suffering from Pressure Ulcers, Burns, or Falls

# CONCEPT
Accidental incidents such as pressure ulcers, burns, or falls may occur during the period when surgical patients are in the operating room, posing safety risks. The occurrence of pressure ulcers is mainly due to improper positioning of the body, and it is also related to the quality and hardness of the positioning pads, the duration of the surgery, the points and areas of force, the patient's age, nutritional status, skin elasticity, peripheral blood circulation, and many other factors. The improper use of electrosurgical equipment in the operating room can cause burns or scalds. Falls can occur if surgical patients are not properly attended to or restrained. These accidental incidents not only threaten patient safety, increase patient suffering, but also increase hospitalization days and medical costs. Operating room nurses should place surgical positions correctly, apply appropriate restraints, and safely use electrosurgical equipment to ensure the safety of surgical patients. In the event of an accident, staff should actively and correctly handle the situation to minimize risks and hazards as much as possible.
