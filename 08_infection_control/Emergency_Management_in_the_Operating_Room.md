# Emergency Plans
Emergency plans, also known as emergency plans, refer to pre-planned action plans or schemes formulated in advance to ensure rapid, orderly, and effective emergency and rescue operations, reduce accident losses, and respond to major accidents or disasters.
Emergency plans can be divided into three types: comprehensive emergency plans, special emergency plans, and on-site emergency response plans.

# Emergency Management of Operating Room Accidents - Special Infection Surgery
Patients suspected or diagnosed with prion infection should use disposable medical instruments, equipment, and items. After use, they should be double-sealed and incinerated.
Reusable contaminated instruments, equipment, and items should be soaked in a 1mol/L sodium hydroxide solution for 60 minutes before cleaning.
The surface of the operating room environment can be treated with a chlorine disinfectant solution containing 500-1000mg/L.

<!-- Slide number: 30 -->
# Emergency Management of Operating Room Accidents - Special Infection Surgery
For instruments, equipment, and items contaminated with gas gangrene, soak in a chlorine disinfectant solution containing 1000-2000mg/L for 30-45 minutes. In case of obvious contamination, use a chlorine disinfectant solution containing 5000-10000mg/L to disinfect for ≥60 minutes before cleaning, disinfection, and sterilization. The surface of the operating room environment can be wiped with a chlorine disinfectant solution containing 1000mg/L or an equivalent amount of other disinfectant.

<!-- Slide number: 31 -->
# Emergency Management of Operating Room Accidents - Special Infection Surgery
For instruments and items contaminated with unknown infectious agents, they should comply with the national regulations issued at that time.

<!-- Slide number: 32 -->
# Emergency Management of Operating Room Accidents - Special Infection Surgery
After special infection surgery, if the exhaust air unit is contaminated, first treat the outer surface of the exhaust (return) air outlet with a chlorine disinfectant solution, then replace the return air filter. It is recommended to use a safe and easily removable filter unit. The removed filter should be sealed and transported out as medical waste.

# Hospital Fire Incident
Around 4:30 pm on December 15, 2005, a major fire broke out at the Central Hospital of Liaoyuan City, Jilin Province, resulting in 39 deaths, 95 injuries, and direct economic losses of 8.21 million yuan.
The direct cause of the accident was a short-circuit fault in the cable trench of the power distribution room of the central hospital, which ignited combustible materials.
The indirect cause was that the construction quality was unqualified during the renovation of the power distribution room and some electrical equipment of the central hospital, which laid a major hidden danger. The alarm was late, delaying the fire-fighting time. The central hospital did not seriously implement the fire safety responsibility system and fire safety measures.

# Emergency Response Procedure for Fire in the Operating Room - Precautions
Foam fire extinguishers are regularly placed in the operating room, and each staff member should know the exact location of the fire exit and fire extinguishers and be able to use them correctly.
Each shift should carefully check for safety at all locations to ensure that electrical equipment is turned off after surgery.
Smoke detectors and water sprays are installed on the ceiling of the corridor outside the operating room.
During evacuation, follow the instructions of the operating room safety exit signs.
Staff must adhere to the principle of "patients first, critically ill patients first, and medical staff last" when evacuating surgical patients to a safe location.
Do not use elevators during evacuation to prevent evacuation failure due to power outages.

# Emergency Response for Respiratory/Heartbeat Arrest

Clinical manifestations of cardiac arrest
1. Loss of consciousness
2. Absence of pulse in the great arteries, disappearance of carotid and femoral artery pulsation
3. Absence of spontaneous respiration
4. Cardiac arrest, disappearance of heart sounds
5. Pupils dilated, loss of light reflex
6. No bleeding from surgical incisions, dark red blood color in the surgical field
7. Electrocardiogram and electroencephalogram show straight lines

<!-- Slide number: 50 -->
# Emergency Response for Respiratory/Heartbeat Arrest

<!-- Slide number: 51 -->
# Emergency Response for Respiratory/Heartbeat Arrest
Immediate resuscitation → External cardiac massage → Tracheal intubation → Rapid fluid administration → Administer medication as ordered → Close collaboration → Symptomatic treatment → Timely documentation

Precautions
① Ensure that the operating room is equipped with commonly used resuscitation drugs.
② Perform cardiopulmonary cerebral resuscitation simultaneously, and place a urinary catheter if conditions permit.
③ Pay attention to raising the room temperature and keeping warm during resuscitation.
④ Place ice caps at designated locations and notify promptly for ice block collection and delivery.
⑤ Master the resuscitation process and the safe use of defibrillators and other resuscitation equipment.

# Emergency Response for Respiratory/Heartbeat Arrest
2015 American CPR and ECC Guidelines Update
Survival Chain: Split in Two
Compression depth should be at least 5CM, but should not exceed 6CM
Compression rate is specified as 100-120 times per minute
The rescuer's hands should be removed from the patient's chest and arms during the interval of compression
Whether the cardiac arrest is caused by heart failure or not, medical staff should provide external cardiac massage and ventilation
Use defibrillators as soon as possible
Each interruption should be less than 10S, and the compression operation should not be less than 60% of the entire CPR process
Advanced airway ventilation frequency is 10 times per minute
The three-step assessment of vital signs becomes two steps: assess consciousness while assessing respiration and pulse

# Training and Learning of Emergency Plans---Simulation Exercises
Purpose
 Simulation exercises can improve the emergency team's treatment level and response speed. Various emergency plans can only be implemented smoothly during actual operations through repeated simulations.
Through exercises, not only can everyone understand and master the plans, but also test whether the plans are reasonable, scientific, and comprehensive, so that they can be modified and improved in a timely manner.
Emergency simulation exercises are conducted regularly each year to continuously improve the emergency response capabilities of medical personnel.

# Summary
            To respond to emergencies, we must develop a set of emergency response plans and management systems tailored to the actual situation of our hospital's operating room. However, systems can be a double-edged sword; the successful implementation of systems depends on effective implementation. If the implementation is not in place, even the best system becomes mere talk. Therefore, the operating room should improve its emergency system, perfect emergency measures, and maintain constant readiness and regular drills, so that in the event of an emergency, we can take timely, rapid, efficient, and orderly measures to actively respond, and minimize the harm caused by the emergency.
