# Introduction

       Intracerebral hemorrhage, also known as cerebral hemorrhage, accounts for 18.8%-47.6% of all cerebral strokes in China. The mortality and disability rates are the highest among all strokes, with an in-hospital mortality rate of up to 20%-30% within the first 3 months after onset, which is 2-6 times higher than that of ischemic stroke. Thirty percent of survivors have residual neurological deficits.

Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Intracerebral Hemorrhage in China, Chinese Journal of Neurology, 2015, 48(6): 435-444

# Clinical Manifestations
Basal ganglia hemorrhage: The putamen and thalamus are the two most common sites of hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage.

Typical symptoms include hemiparesis (hemiparesis, hemisensory loss, and hemianopia on the opposite side of the lesion), and consciousness disorders can occur with massive hemorrhage.

# Disadvantages of Long-term Tracheostomy Tube Insertion

3. THREE
Tracheal mucosa necrosis due to long-term compression, promoting scar formation and airway stenosis

1. ONE
Insufficient lower respiratory tract humidification, causing damage to tracheal and bronchial mucosa, increased sputum production

2. TWO
The potential gap between the outer tube and the tracheal wall forms a dead corner for bacterial growth, becoming the source of recurrent tracheal inflammation

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
# Indications for Tracheostomy Tube Removal
Good swallowing function
Normal body temperature, stable breathing
Clear consciousness or severe impairment improving to mild impairment
Reduced sputum volume (for 2 days or more)
No pulmonary infection
Autonomous coughing
Reflex recovery
No pharyngeal obstruction

Indications for tube removal
Literature suggests: When the primary cause of tracheostomy is resolved, it is necessary to actively create conditions for early tube removal
Liu Lihong, Wang Qiuxia, Nursing of 56 cases of severe craniocerebral injury patients with tracheostomy, Journal of Modern Nursing, 2015, 12: 1432-1433
