# Perioperative Nursing for Endometrial Polyps

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# Overview of Endometrial Polyps
1. Endometrial polyps (EP) are a common gynecological disease, resulting from local hyperplasia of the endometrium. They manifest as single or multiple smooth tumors protruding into the uterus with varying lengths of pedicles. They can cause irregular vaginal bleeding and infertility.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# High-risk Population:
Women of childbearing age and postmenopausal women are all high-risk populations for endometrial polyps.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Etiology
1. Endocrine Factors
Estrogen promotes endometrial hyperplasia and menstrual flow, thus the formation of endometrial polyps is closely related to high estrogen levels.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Etiology
2. Inflammatory Factors
Long-term gynecological inflammation, intrauterine foreign bodies (such as intrauterine devices), delivery, abortion, puerperal infection, surgical procedures, or mechanical stimulation may all cause the occurrence of endometrial polyps, and long-term inflammatory stimulation can cause the polyps to grow larger.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Etiology
3. Other
Ageing, hypertension, obesity, diabetes, and long-term use of tamoxifen after breast cancer surgery are all high-risk factors for endometrial polyps.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Clinical Manifestations
1. Mainly menorrhagia and prolonged menstrual periods or abnormal uterine bleeding before menopause. However, the severity of symptoms is not related to the number, diameter, or location of the polyps.

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
# Clinical Manifestations
2. Abdominal Pain
Commonly starts in the second half of the menstrual cycle, worsening progressively, and gradually disappearing after the menstrual period. It is mainly due to the obstruction of endometrial polyps, leading to poor menstrual excretion.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Clinical Manifestations
Abnormal Vaginal Discharge
A few patients with large polyps may experience blood in the vaginal discharge, also known as contact bleeding, especially after sexual intercourse and when straining during defecation.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Clinical Manifestations
Infertility
If endometrial polyps grow in the cervical canal, they may obstruct sperm from entering the uterine cavity; if they grow in the uterine cavity, they may hinder implantation of the fertilized egg or affect embryo development, leading to infertility.

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Examination
1. Gynecological examination
2. Ultrasound examination
3. Hysteroscopy
4. Pathological examination

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Treatment
1. Conservative Treatment
For small, asymptomatic polyps, no intervention may be required temporarily, and some polyps may regress spontaneously.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
# Treatment
2. Hysteroscopic resection
Patients with obvious clinical symptoms, with the presence of a mass in the uterine cavity detected by ultrasound, or where the mass in the uterine cavity cannot be completely ruled out as malignant, are recommended for surgical treatment. Patients with a desire to conceive are also recommended to undergo surgery before attempting pregnancy.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
# Patient Preparation
Generally, surgery is performed 3-7 days after the menstrual period is clean; sexual activity is prohibited 3 days before surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# Preoperative Nursing
1. Psychological Nursing: As hysteroscopy is a new surgical method, most patients are unfamiliar with it. Explain to the patient that hysteroscopy is a safe, effective, minimally invasive, and quick-recovery surgical method to alleviate their anxiety and cooperate with treatment.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# Preoperative Nursing
2. Skin Preparation: Prepare the skin the day before surgery, the range is from 10 cm above the pubic symphysis to 5 cm below the perineum, and 1/2 of the inner thigh.
3. Dietary Guidance: Provide easily digestible food the night before surgery; fasting from food and water 12 hours before surgery.
4. Vaginal Preparation: Perform iodine vinegar disinfection of the vagina once a day before surgery; place a seaweed rod in the cervix at 18:00 the night before surgery and fill the vagina with gauze.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# Preoperative Nursing
5. Sleep Guidance: Provide a good sleep environment to keep the patient in a stable mental state; follow medical advice to give sedative-hypnotic drugs before surgery;
6. Measure body temperature and blood pressure on the night before surgery and in the morning on the day of surgery; report any abnormalities to the doctor immediately;
7. Hand over the patient to the operating room nurse and sign off after confirming the information.

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
# Postoperative Nursing
1. Hand over the patient to the operating room nurse, and inquire about the intraoperative situation in detail to facilitate targeted postoperative nursing;
2. Closely observe vital signs and changes in the condition, closely monitor for abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding, and other emergencies;

<!-- Slide number: 22 -->
# Postoperative Nursing
3. Postoperative fasting from food and water for 4 hours, then can be given a small amount of fluid or regular food in small and frequent meals, continue low-flow oxygen inhalation for 2 hours. Keep the patient in a supine position with the head turned to one side to prevent vomiting and asphyxiation; after the patient becomes conscious, they can place a pillow and turn regularly, instruct the patient to perform leg flexion exercises and ankle exercises. They can get out of bed to move around as soon as possible after 4 hours.

<!-- Slide number: 23 -->
# Postoperative Nursing
4. Uterine Cavity Balloon Drainage Tube: Securely fix the drainage tube, ensure the drainage bag is below the outlet level and change it daily to avoid pressure and twisting of the uterine cavity drainage tube, and maintain unobstructed drainage. At the same time, provide health education for the patient and their family. Observe and record the color, nature, and amount of the drainage fluid regularly.

<!-- Slide number: 24 -->
# Postoperative Nursing
5. Keep the perineum clean, perform perineal cleaning twice a day.
6. Pay attention to the patient's urination, and if there is difficulty urinating, handle it promptly to avoid bladder distension affecting uterine contraction and increasing vaginal bleeding.

<!-- Slide number: 25 -->
# Discharge Instructions
1. Rest, avoid sexual activity and bath for 1 month, and keep the perineum clean.
2. Follow up on vaginal abnormal bleeding.
3. This condition is prone to recurrence, and it should be reviewed in the outpatient department every 3 months. If asymptomatic, there is no need for repeated surgical treatment.

<!-- Slide number: 26 -->
# Summary
    Hysteroscopic resection is a new minimally invasive technique widely used in clinical practice. Nurses should be familiar with and master the preoperative and postoperative nursing of hysteroscopic surgery to improve the success rate of surgery and reduce the occurrence of postoperative complications.
