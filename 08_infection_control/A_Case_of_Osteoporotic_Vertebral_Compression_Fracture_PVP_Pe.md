# Introduction
World Population Aging: The current elderly population is 130 million, expected to reach 418 million by 2035 [1]
Osteoporosis is common and prevalent: The incidence rate of osteoporosis in men over 60 is about 60.72%, and in women over 60 is as high as 90.84% [1]
OVCF: Vertebral compression fractures caused by osteoporosis, with an incidence rate of 10% in people over 60, and up to 30% in women over 60 [1]

[1]  Yin Ping, Ma Yuanzheng, Ma Xun, et al. Treatment Guidelines for Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fractures. Chinese Journal of Osteoporosis, 2015, 6: Vol. 6, No. 2

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Pathogenesis of OVCF
Reduced bone mass and sparse trabeculae lead to an increased risk of vertebral脆性fractures
Due to the longitudinal force, the height of the vertebrae is lost, the vertebrae are "flattened", the kyphosis of the spine increases, and "hunchback" appears
The strength and stability of the vertebrae decrease, unable to meet the normal biomechanical requirements, stimulating the nerves and causing back pain

[1]  Yin Ping, Ma Yuanzheng, Ma Xun, et al. Treatment Guidelines for Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fractures. Chinese Journal of Osteoporosis, 2015, 6: Vol. 6, No. 2

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Clinical Manifestations and Hazards of OVCF
Back pain: Aggravated when sitting, standing, and active, relieved when lying flat or on the side, leading to chronic back pain later [1]
Complications of long-term bed rest: For elderly patients, long-term bed rest can lead to complications such as pulmonary infection, venous thrombosis, and bedsores, which can be life-threatening in severe cases [1]
Limited pulmonary and cardiac function: Due to the compression of the vertebrae, the kyphosis of the spine affects the chest cavity, leading to impaired pulmonary and cardiac function [1]
Increased mortality

[1]  Yin Ping, Ma Yuanzheng, Ma Xun, et al. Treatment Guidelines for Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fractures. Chinese Journal of Osteoporosis, 2015, 6: Vol. 6, No. 2

# Treatment Methods for OVCF    Percutaneous Vertebroplasty (PVP)
"Flattened" vertebrae

PVP: Economical, quick, and the most commonly used
90% of patients experience significant pain relief, and they can get out of bed the first day after surgery with a lumbar corset
[1]  Huang Gang, Chen Ha Yun, Liu Zhuo, et al. Percutaneous Vertebroplasty and Percutaneous Vertebroplasty for Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fractures. Journal of Southern Medical University. 2010, 12-2729-04

# Case Introduction
Basic Information: Bed 16, Department of Spinal Surgery, Zou XX, male, 76 years old, hospital number 489024
Chief Complaint: Back pain and limited activity due to a fall for 6 hours.
Current Medical History: 6 hours before, a fall on the ground led to back pain and significant limited activity, more pronounced when standing or sitting up, relieved when lying flat and resting. For further treatment, came to our hospital for an X-ray examination, indicating a compression fracture of the T10 vertebra. For further treatment, the outpatient department was admitted with "compression fracture of the thoracic vertebra."
Past Medical History: Long-standing history of hypertension, chronic bronchitis, no history of diabetes or coronary heart disease.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Admission Information
Special physical examination: Admitted by wheelchair, alert, limited activity of the thoracic and lumbar spine, paravertebral tenderness at the level of the T10 vertebra, and percussion pain at the level of the T10 vertebra.
Auxiliary Examinations: X-ray indicated a compression fracture of the T10 vertebra, and the MRI indicated a fresh compression fracture of the T10 vertebra
Diagnosis: 1. Fresh compression fracture of the T10 vertebra; 2. Severe senile osteoporosis

# Treatment Process

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
# Surgical Process
The patient was placed in a prone position, the G-arm was used for surface localization and marking (clear G-arm fluoroscopy is very important for intraoperative localization and cement injection)
After satisfactory local anesthesia, the G-arm was monitored to puncture at the "eagle eye" 2 or 10 o'clock position of the pedicle root shadow,

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Surgical Process
Combined with intraoperative G-arm fluoroscopic localization, the puncture needle entered the anterior 1/3 of the vertebra
During the operation, bone cement was mixed, the powder and liquid were mixed and shaken outside for 1 minute, and the syringe aspirated and injected into the plunger
Under G-arm monitoring, the bone cement was gradually pushed into the plunger using the plunger, and attention was paid to whether there was cement leakage, and the patient's vital signs during the operation were observed

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# The Role of Bone Cement and Related Complications
The main component of bone cement is polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA), which is divided into two parts: white powder and liquid, mixed when used
Bone cement generates heat during solidification in the operation, which can reach up to 65°C, and the thermal effect can relieve pain and increase the strength and stability of the vertebrae
Complications: Due to the local high pressure during the injection filling of bone cement, bone cement or fat droplets can enter the blood vessels, causing embolism (pulmonary embolism is common)
Monomer toxicity (vasodilation)

Vasodilation leads to decreased blood pressure and increased heart rate
White powder
Liquid part

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Postoperative Effects
PVP surgery can significantly enhance the strength and stability of the vertebrae, and the patient can get out of bed to move around on the first day after surgery, promoting recovery
They can start by sitting up at the bedside and gradually transition to walking around
Wear a lumbar corset (within 4 weeks after surgery)
