### Notes:
Maintaining body temperature is an important task. In normal physiological conditions, the body's core temperature is precisely controlled at 37°±0.2°C, and this narrow range of 0.4°C is known as the temperature threshold range.
When body temperature deviates from this range due to various reasons, the body adjusts body temperature through different physiological responses according to the degree of deviation.
When heat loss exceeds the body's needs, that is, the core temperature is low, according to the degree of hypothermia from mild to severe, the body's responses are as follows:
Vasoconstriction: The small terminal vessels of the superficial blood vessels contract, blood flow decreases, and heat loss is reduced.
Non-shivering thermogenesis (NST): Some metabolic heat production methods from non-muscle sources
Shivering: Includes involuntary muscle contraction for heat production, which is considered as the last resort when other strategies fail due to its high energy consumption.

When heat loss is less than the body's needs, the body's response is:
Vasodilation: The small blood vessels near the skin dilate, blood flow increases, and excessive heat is dissipated.
Sweating: The body exudes fluid and evaporates, reducing the temperature of the skin surface.

### Notes:
Let's continue to look at the curve of body temperature changes in patients undergoing general anesthesia, which can be roughly divided into three stages.
Stage I: Core temperature rapidly decreases due to heat redistribution.
Stage II: Temperature decreases relatively slowly and linearly compared to Stage I, around 2-3 hours after induction of anesthesia, at which point heat redistribution is basically completed.
Stage III: After 3-5 hours of anesthesia induction, temperature enters a plateau phase, and heat loss caused by the environment continues. The temperature eventually decreases to the lower limit of the threshold range, at which point the body begins to counteract the decrease in temperature through vasoconstriction.

# Regional Anesthesia
Temperature receptors

Similar hypothermic process as general anesthesia
Central and peripheral temperature regulation functions are impaired
The skin temperature in the blocked area is misjudged by the affected temperature receptors
Hypothermia in patients under regional anesthesia is often not detected
Core temperature is usually not monitored
Patients do not particularly express discomfort from heat

### Notes:
The name "regional anesthesia" needs standardization.
The process of hypothermia caused by regional anesthesia is similar.
In spinal and epidural anesthesia, both central and peripheral temperature regulation functions are impaired, hindering the normal temperature regulation response of the body and disrupting more than 50% of nerve conduction.
The skin temperature in the blocked area is misjudged by the affected temperature receptors as abnormally high, so that the temperature regulation system causes the temperature to drop below normal without a cold response.
Ultimately, severe hypothermia patients may experience shivering, unless sedatives are administered to suppress their reaction.
