Concept
Desktop Sterilizer: A device that uses high-pressure steam sterilization to sterilize physically using saturated steam.
Our department uses the German MELAG Euroklav 23V-S fully automatic high-pressure steam sterilizer.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->

Basic Principle
High-pressure steam sterilization: Utilizes the heat effect and latent heat of high-pressure steam to destroy the proteins, nucleic acids, cell walls, and cell membranes of microorganisms, causing them to denature and die, thereby achieving sterilization.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

Basic Principle
Pre-vacuum sterilization: Refers to the process of first extracting the air inside the sterilization container before introducing steam, and then starting the sterilization process.
Pulsed vacuum sterilization: Refers to the process of first extracting the air inside the sterilization container before introducing steam, followed by completely emptying the sterilization container of all gases. This cycle is repeated several times before starting the sterilization process. Pulsed vacuum is currently the most effective step in the best sterilization cycle, and three or more cycles can achieve the removal of 99.99% of the air inside the sterilization container, allowing steam to quickly cover the items to be sterilized.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->

Advantages
Precise sterilization program monitoring system to ensure safety.
B级 sterilization program with three pre-vacuum cycles.
No need for water supply and drainage: internal circulation cooling saves water; easy installation and operation.
Multiple disinfection programs designed for clinical convenience.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->

Disadvantages
Small capacity
Unwrapped items after sterilization must be used immediately on the table and cannot be stored; wrapped items can only be stored for up to 4 hours and must be used as soon as possible.
The desktop sterilizer cannot be used as a routine sterilization method and can only be used for emergency sterilization of instruments on the table.

Daily Maintenance
1. Cleaning and Sanitization
Turn off the power switch of the sterilizer and unplug the power cord from the wall.
Ensure the chamber is not hot
Use soft, lint-free cloths
Do not use cleaning agents containing chlorine or acids
First, wet the cloth with alcohol, then wipe away the stains

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 41 -->

Daily Maintenance
For stubborn stains on the chamber, tray rack, and chamber sealing surface, use stainless steel cleaner with a pH of 5-8
Clean the door seal with a neutral cleaner
Do not allow the cleaner to flow through the chamber into the pipeline

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 42 -->

Daily Maintenance
Do not use rough objects such as metal or stainless steel brushes
The screws on the door lock (right side) and the door hinges (right side) must be lubricated with silicon resin lubricant regularly to ensure easy opening and closing of the door

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 43 -->

Daily Maintenance
2. Distilled Water Replacement
Change the distilled water in the water tank once a week
Wipe the dirt and impurities in the water tank with a cloth, leaving no residue. Otherwise, when emptying the waste water tank, it may enter the filter in the drain pipe, affecting the life of the fine filter.

Precautions
1. Ensure there is sufficient distilled water in the water tank
2. Maximum loading capacity: textiles 1.1Kg, instruments 4Kg
3. Do not forcibly interrupt the sterilizer program before the drying process starts
4. Be careful of residual steam burns when opening the sterilizer chamber door after the sterilization program is completed

Precautions
6. When placing sterilized items, attention should be paid to:
Items should be clean and dry
Do not oil them, as this may block the exhaust holes
Textiles should be placed parallelly, items should not be too close to each other to allow steam to pass through
Avoid direct contact between textiles and the chamber to prevent absorption of condensate, which may damage the sterilizer and hinder the penetration of steam into the textiles, leading to incomplete sterilization and poor drying.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 48 -->

7. Unwrapped sterilized items should be used immediately and stored in sterile containers, with a shelf life of no more than 4 hours. Wrapped sterilized items have a shelf life of 4 hours. The drying program must be completed for wrapped sterilization.
8. Weekly biological monitoring: If the test is positive or the sterilizer is overhauled, or not used for a long time (more than 2 weeks), normal use can only be resumed after three consecutive negative cultures.

Discussion
3. How to Perform Instrument Sterilization During Ophthalmic Surgery When There Are Many Procedures
If there are too many instruments, do not use the sterilizer for rapid sterilization.
Coordinate the surgical schedule with the Chief, and try to use plasma sterilization as much as possible.
If it is necessary to use the sterilizer, pay attention to the protective covers for sharp and delicate instruments, remove them during sterilization, and handle them gently.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 53 -->

4. Can Externally Supplied Instruments Be Sterilized in the Sterilizer? Why?
No. Externally supplied instruments must be sent to the supply department for sterilization.
The supply department has a complete sterilization process,完善的 infection management and detection system, with biological monitoring and fifth-generation chemical knowledge cards. Instruments are only released upon passing the inspection, and can be traced for management.

5. What Sterilization Method Should Be Chosen for Endoscopic Instruments That Can Withstand High-Temperature and High-Pressure Sterilization?
The preferred method is plasma sterilization, as plasma sterilization causes less damage to instruments than high-temperature and high-pressure sterilization.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 54 -->

6. Can Instrument Sterilization Be Performed in the Sterilizer When There Are Many Instruments?
When there are many instruments, place the trays in layers to ensure that instruments on each layer do not cross or overlap, leaving gaps to allow steam to pass through smoothly. If there is no space, wait for the next batch to be disinfected or send them to the supply department for processing. Do not force the instruments to be stacked together to achieve speed, as this may affect the quality of sterilization.
