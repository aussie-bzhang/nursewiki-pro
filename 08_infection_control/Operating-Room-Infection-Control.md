Pathological Specimen

Any tissue, organ, or object related to the patient's disease or foreign bodies removed during surgery in the operating room are considered surgical specimens.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->

Importance of Specimens

Live tissue pathological diagnosis is the first diagnosis for surgical diseases and is the "gold standard"【1】.
Specimens removed during surgery must not be handled by anyone without the doctor's permission.

【1】Zhou Aiqin, Jia Chunxiang. Standardized Pathway Management of Surgical Specimens【J】. Chinese Journal of Modern Nursing. 2013, 19 (11): 1338-1339.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->

Valueless Specimens

Any tissue, organ, or limb without pathological or retention value should be shown to the family after the operation and a record of the surgical specimen should be made. A special person should send it for incineration.
For valueless intra-body foreign bodies, internal fixation materials, etc., the family of the patient should make a record after seeing them and dispose of them as medical waste.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->

Valuable Specimens

The scrub nurse is responsible for specimen management on the operating table and should prevent drying or loss. The specimen should be personally handed to the doctor after the operation.

Intraoperative Management

1. Larger specimens are stored in a bent plate.
2. Smaller specimens are stored in a small gauze, and clamped with an Allis forceps.
3. No one is allowed to take specimens on the operating table without permission.
4. Pay attention to aseptic and tumor-free principles when storing specimens.
5. Specimens should be placed in a specimen bag as soon as there are many of them.
