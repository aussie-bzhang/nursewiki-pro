Technical indicators requirements for clean rooms:
The minimum static pressure difference between different clean rooms with interconnection should be ≥5Pa, and the maximum static pressure difference should be less than 20Pa.
Seriously contaminated rooms should maintain negative pressure to the adjacent rooms, with a minimum static pressure difference of ≥5Pa.
Clean areas should maintain positive pressure to adjacent non-clean areas, with a minimum static pressure difference of ≥5Pa.
The number of days with temperature and humidity not meeting the standards should not exceed 5 days/year, and the number of consecutive days not meeting the standards should not exceed 2 days/year.

8.2.1 Monitoring Frequency (Biological Detection) - Air
       The hospital should carry out monitoring every quarter in departments with a high risk of infection; in clean operating departments (rooms) and other clean places, at the time of acceptance of new construction and reconstruction, and after the replacement of high-efficiency filters; when suspected hospital infections are suspected to be related to air pollution, monitoring should be carried out at any time, and corresponding pathogenic microorganism detection should be carried out.

# Common Issues in Hospital Infection Management
1. The operating room nurse manager lacks knowledge training in hospital infection management, is not familiar with relevant infection control regulations and requirements, and does not know how to do operating room infection control work.
2. Hospital leaders have insufficient understanding of hospital infection, insufficient investment in the operating room, and insufficient basic facilities, such as the inability to control the temperature in the operating room, especially in winter, where surgeons may wear sweaters and cotton shoes during surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 55 -->
# Common Issues in Hospital Infection Management
 3. There are irregularities in the packaging materials and methods of sterile items, and the items in the storage room are placed in disarray; the environment, facilities, and equipment for self-cleaning surgical instruments do not meet the requirements, and the relevant personnel have not received standardized training. The sterilization methods do not meet the requirements, such as using immersion methods to disinfect surgical instruments.

# Weaknesses in Current Operating Room Infection Management
1. Operating room managers lack knowledge of preventing hospital infections in operating rooms, do not fully understand relevant regulations and hospital infection control quality requirements, and cannot correctly guide operating room nurses.
2. Training is insufficient, and medical staff lack basic knowledge of preventing and controlling hospital infections and iatrogenic infections, such as hand hygiene, safe injection, disinfection and sterilization, isolation techniques, medical waste management, and occupational exposure protection.
3. Hospital infection control personnel do not provide sufficient guidance and supervision for operating room infection control work.

# Case Analysis
From April 3, 1998, to May 27, 1998, Shenzhen Women and Children's Hospital performed a total of 292 surgeries. As of August 20, 1998, there were 166 cases of incisional infection, with an infection rate of 56.85%, which was a severe hospital infection event.
From October 9, 2009, to December 27, 2009, among 38 cesarean section patients at the Gaorao Central Hospital of Chao'an District, Shantou City, Guangdong Province, 18 cases of incisional infection occurred, with an infection rate of 47.36%.

# Case Analysis
Main Causes of Incisional Infection in Surgery
Unqualified sterilization of surgical instruments, using glutaraldehyde at an ineffective concentration to disinfect surgical instruments, and the immersion time not reaching the specified requirements
Inadequate cleaning of instruments
Relevant departments did not implement effective monitoring of the sterilization effect of surgical instruments and items in a timely manner
Surgical hand antisepsis agents do not meet hygiene requirements
Serious hospital infection risks exist in many links, such as inadequate and unimplemented regulations and systems.

# Standards for Primary-level Hospitals
1. Independently set up, clearly partitioned, standardized processes, clear identification, clean and hygienic. Clean and disinfect in a timely manner between consecutive surgeries and after all surgeries are completed on the same day (1 point).
Partitioning: restricted area, semi-restricted area, unrestricted area
Clear identification in each area

<!-- Slide number: 64 -->
# Standards for Primary-level Hospitals
2. All personnel entering the operating room should change into specialized clothing, hats, disposable surgical masks, and shoes for the operating room. Non-infectious surgeries and infectious surgeries should be carried out in separate rooms. If they are carried out in the same operating room, non-infectious surgeries should be arranged first, followed by infectious surgeries (1 point).

<!-- Slide number: 65 -->
# Standards for Primary-level Hospitals
3. Surgical instruments and items should be cleaned as soon as possible after use, and instruments must be sterilized after each use. Cleaning, packaging, and sterilization should comply with national regulations. Heat-resistant and high-temperature-resistant instruments and items should be sterilized using pressure steam sterilization. After sterilization, the surgical instrument package should be stored in a clean and dry storage cabinet. Anesthetic equipment should be cleaned and disinfected regularly. Reusable laryngoscopes,螺纹管, face masks, oropharyngeal passages, and simple ventilators must be "one person, one use, one disinfection", and should be kept clean, dry, and sealed (1 point).

<!-- Slide number: 66 -->
# Standards for Primary-level Hospitals
 4. The operating room should establish hospital infection management regulations such as pre-job training systems for medical staff infection control basic knowledge, hand hygiene systems for surgical personnel, management systems for infectious surgeries, occupational safety systems for medical staff, follow-up systems for postoperative incisional infections, operating room cleaning and disinfection isolation systems, and management systems for incoming surgical instruments (1 point).

<!-- Slide number: 67 -->
# Standards for Primary-level Hospitals
 5. Each operating room should only have one operating table; surgical hand washing facilities should meet the requirements of WS/T313-2009. Isolation measures and postoperative disinfection measures for patients with special infections such as open pulmonary tuberculosis and anthrax should be in place (1 point).
