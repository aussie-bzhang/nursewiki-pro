**One. Blood-borne Infection**

• Main Pathogens: HBV, HCV, HIV

• Main Contaminants: Blood, body fluids, secretions, excreta, tissues and organs containing HBV, HCV, HIV, and other infectious materials.

• Main Modes of Transmission: Through broken skin and mucous membranes

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->

**Risk of Infection**

Medical personnel with broken skin and mucous membranes exposed to contaminated blood, body fluids, and contaminated surfaces have a risk of HBV, HCV, and HIV infection of 30%, 6%, and 0.3%, respectively.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->

**Main Risk Factors for Blood-borne Infection in Medical Personnel**

— Sharp Object Injuries

The degree of risk depends on:

• Frequency of exposure, nature of injury

• Presence and amount of visible blood

• Presence of virus

• Depth of injury

• Standard precautions
• Objects: Blood, all body fluids, incomplete skin, mucous membranes

**Basic Requirements for Isolation Techniques**

• Emphasize thorough hand hygiene before and after each patient contact

• Advise the use of gloves

• Use high-efficiency filter masks made of peroxide hexene fiber: N 95 masks

**(III) Correct Operation, Reduce Injury and Contamination**

Do not bend or pull out contaminated needles and sharp objects with hands; use mechanical methods.

Do not install surgical blades by hand.

Emergency management for medical staff exposed to contaminated blood

Sharp object injury:
1. Remain calm
2. Rapidly and deftly remove gloves according to standard procedure
3. Immediately apply pressure with the non-injured hand from the proximal to the distal end of the injured site to expel some blood, thereby reducing the degree of contamination
4. Simultaneously, rinse with running water
5. Disinfect the injured area with iodine alcohol.

Emergency management for medical staff exposed to contaminated blood

Contamination due to splashing or immersion:
• Rapidly and deftly remove hats, masks, and surgical gowns according to standard procedure

• Rinse the contaminated area with running water

II. Air Pollution

Air Pollution: Carbon dioxide, anesthetic waste, surgical smoke, dust

Preventive Measures: Keep doors closed, no smoking, wet cleaning, waste gas collection, etc.

III. Chemical Injury

Chemical Injury: Glutaraldehyde, ethylene oxide, chemotherapy drugs

Preventive Measures: Cover containers of chemical disinfectants in use, store concentrated solutions properly. Ethylene oxide should be fully decomposed, and protective measures should be taken for the preparation of chemotherapy drugs.
