**Introduction**
    ICU-Acquired Weakness (ICU-AW), also known as ICU-Acquired Myasthenia, refers to the onset of unexplained weakness in critically ill patients in the ICU, which may appear several hours after mechanical ventilation. The clinical manifestations mainly include difficulty in weaning, paresis or quadriplegia, decreased reflexes, and muscle atrophy [1].
      ICU-Acquired Myasthenia (ICU-AW) is a secondary neuro-muscular complication in critically ill patients [2]. It generally occurs in patients with complications such as sepsis, multiple organ failure, and hyperglycemia [3]. ICU-Acquired Myasthenia increases the mortality rate, prolongs the duration of mechanical ventilation, affects the ability to perform activities of daily living, and increases the incidence of complications such as decubitus ulcers.
      Currently, there is no effective treatment for ICU-AW, and early mobilization is particularly important for the prevention of ICU-AW [4].
