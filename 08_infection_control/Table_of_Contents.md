## Background

Acute lower respiratory tract infection has a high incidence and a variety of complications, making it one of the more common diseases in the elderly population. In the elderly, the prognosis of acute lower respiratory tract infection is poor, often leading to structural and functional damage to multiple organ systems, with a relatively high mortality rate.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
## Background
A survey was conducted on 11,245 elderly patients
556 cases of hospital-acquired infection occurred, with an infection rate of 4.94%
The most common site of infection was the lower respiratory tract, accounting for 66% of the infection cases
The departments with the highest incidence of lower respiratory tract infection were mainly the neurology department
All susceptible individuals had one or more underlying diseases, mainly including cerebrovascular diseases, tumors, and pulmonary diseases
Pathogens causing hospital-acquired lower respiratory tract infections in elderly patients
Gram-negative bacilli were predominant, accounting for 62.68%
The most common pathogens were Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa
Gram-negative bacilli showed resistance to ampicillin, piperacillin, cefazolin, and ceftriaxone with resistance rates all greater than 70%

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
## Background
Reasons for the occurrence of lower respiratory tract infection during hospitalization
Comorbid underlying diseases
Nutritional disorders and impaired immune function in patients with malignant tumors
Patients with brain nerve function damage or pharyngeal muscle function damage may experience swallowing dysfunction or
  impaired coughing and sputum clearance
Patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease have excessive and thick respiratory secretions that are difficult to cough up, easily remaining in the respiratory tract, providing opportunities for the growth and reproduction of pathogens
Patients with bronchial asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and autoimmune diseases often use corticosteroids for treatment, which can suppress immune function and cause opportunistic infections

## Background
Reasons for the occurrence of lower respiratory tract infection during hospitalization·
Structural and physiological degenerative changes in the respiratory system of the elderly

Pulmonary function decline
Decreased mucociliary clearance function
Decreased cough and sputum clearance, even loss of function
Decreased SIgA secretion
Antibiotic use
Inhibition of the growth of sensitive bacteria
Reproduction of non-sensitive bacteria and resistant bacteria, leading to infection
Commonly leads to fungal growth and reproduction, causing secondary infection

# Key Points of Swallowing Function Management Education
Oral exercises and swallowing function training
Food selection - Paste meal
Requirements: Uniform density, appropriate粘性, not easily loose, easily deformable through the pharynx and esophagus, and rarely remain on the mucosa.
Do not feed water, liquid food, or un-thickened paste food by mouth
Judgment and management of aspiration
If the following symptoms occur: coughing during or after eating, hoarseness when speaking, food residue in the mouth and nose, inability to speak, inability to cough despite wanting to, difficulty breathing or breathing with a杂声, cyanosis of the lips, blue face, asphyxia, etc., immediately stop feeding and inform the medical staff

# Experience
