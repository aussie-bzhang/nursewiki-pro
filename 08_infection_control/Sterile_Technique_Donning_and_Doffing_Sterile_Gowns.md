# Role of Sterile Gowns
Sterile gowns play a crucial role during surgical procedures. They effectively prevent the transmission of microorganisms between surgical personnel and patients, reducing the risk of surgical infections. A qualified sterile gown is made of special medical material, which has good barrier properties and can prevent the entry of bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens. It creates a relatively sterile environment for surgery, ensuring patient safety during surgery, improving the success rate of surgery, and is an indispensable protective equipment for surgical operations.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Material and Design of Sterile Gowns
Material Characteristics
Design Principles
Sterile gowns are usually made of special medical materials, such as breathable non-woven fabrics, which allow surgical personnel to maintain comfort during long-duration surgeries and reduce feelings of stuffiness. The surface of the material is treated with anti-static properties to prevent the adhesion of dust and microorganisms, ensuring its sterile performance. The design is ergonomically suitable, allowing it to flexibly follow the movements of surgical personnel without hindering operations. The collar and sleeve openings are designed to be tight, effectively preventing bacteria from entering the surgical area and providing comprehensive protection for surgery.

Steps for Donning Sterile Gowns

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Preparations
Before donning a sterile gown, thorough preparations should be made. First, select a sterile gown of the appropriate size to ensure comfort and not hinder surgical operations. Then, check the integrity of the gown for any defects such as tears or holes. Prepare auxiliary items such as sterile gloves. Surgical personnel must wash their hands, wear masks and hats, and make personal protective measures to avoid carrying microorganisms that could contaminate the gown. These preparations are the foundation for correctly donning the gown and ensuring its sterile protective function.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Picking Up the Sterile Gown
Correct Grip
Position Adjustment
Surgical personnel should pick up the ends of the gown collar with both hands, ensuring not to touch any other part of the gown to prevent contamination. At this point, the inside of the gown should face the person, and the outside should face out, maintaining the gown's sterile surface untouched.
Gently shake out the gown to allow the shoulder and sleeve openings to naturally unfold, making it easier to don. At the same time, ensure that the gown is positioned correctly, avoiding excessive length or shortness that could affect surgical operations.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Donning the Sterile Gown
Arm Insertion
Align the sleeve opening of one side of the gown with the arm, then quickly insert the arm into the sleeve, straightening the arm as much as possible to ensure the sleeve completely covers the arm.
Tie the Collar
Next, grasp the collar of the gown with the hand, tie the collar, ensuring the neck is completely covered to prevent bacteria from entering through the collar.
Tidy the Gown
Tidy the back and waist of the gown to make it fit the body, avoiding wrinkles that could affect the sterile effect.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Tying the Belt
Belt Position
Locate the two belts at the waist of the gown and cross them in front of the body.
Tie the Belt
Then, thread the ends of the belts through the crossing point and tie them tightly, ensuring the waist of the gown fits closely to prevent the gown from slipping during surgery.
Donning Completed
After these steps, the sterile gown is correctly donned, and surgical personnel can begin the surgical operation.

Steps for Doffing Sterile Gowns
03

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Unfastening the Belt
After the surgery, the first step is to unfasten the belt around the waist. Gently pull the knot of the belt to release it, allowing the belt to naturally loosen. This step is to prepare for the subsequent easy removal of the gown, avoiding any hindrance to operations or contamination of the gown during the doffing process. When unfastening the belt, be careful to move gently to prevent the gown from being pulled too hard, which could cause unnecessary contamination.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Doffing the Gown
Shoulder Movement
Grasp the shoulder of the gown with the fingers and slowly pull the gown upwards to separate the shoulder from the body.
Arm Removal
Then, remove the sleeve openings of the gown one by one from the arms, gradually pulling the arms out of the gown.
Back Tidying
Finally, gently pull the gown down from the back and tidy the gown for the next step of handling.

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Handling the Gown
Placement
Place the gown inside out in the designated container or on the instrument table.
Prevent Contamination
Be careful not to let the outside of the gown touch other items to avoid contamination of the surrounding environment.
Subsequent Handling
After the gown is removed, it will be handled according to hospital regulations, such as disinfection and cleaning, to ensure that its sterile properties do not pose a risk to the environment.

# Importance of Sterile Technique
Sterile technique is of great importance in surgery and is a key link in preventing surgical infections and ensuring patient safety. Adhering strictly to sterile technique can effectively reduce the infection rate at the surgical site and lower the risk of postoperative complications. A sterile surgical environment creates favorable conditions for the smooth progress of surgery, improves the success rate of surgery, and promotes patient recovery. Conversely, if sterile technique is not properly executed, it may lead to serious consequences such as incisional infection, deep tissue infection, and others, extending the patient's hospital stay, increasing their pain and medical expenses, and even posing a risk to their life. Therefore, every surgical personnel must attach great importance to sterile technique and operate strictly in accordance with the specifications.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
# Precautions for Donning and Doffing Sterile Gowns
Handling After Doffing
After doffing the gown, it is important to handle the gown correctly to prevent the spread of contamination and to clean oneself to prepare for the next surgery or other work.
Post-Operation Handling
During the donning and doffing process, actions should be quick and accurate, avoiding contact with surrounding items that could contaminate the gown.
Key Points During Operation
Pre-Operation Attention
Surgical personnel must ensure that their hand hygiene meets the requirements before donning and doffing sterile gowns to prevent bacteria on their hands from contaminating the gown.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# Common Misconceptions in Sterile Technique Operations
Environmental Contact Issues
If the surrounding environment is not tidy during the donning and doffing of the gown, it is easy to cause the gown to be contaminated.
Hand Contact Misconceptions
Surgical personnel may inadvertently touch the non-sterile parts of the gown with their hands, leading to contamination.
Improper Actions
Inadequate speed and accuracy in donning and doffing actions can also affect the sterile effect of the sterile gown.

# Measures to Maintain a Sterile Environment
Regular Cleaning of Operating Rooms: Operating rooms should be cleaned and disinfected regularly to maintain cleanliness and sterility of surfaces such as floors, walls, and ceilings.
Standardized Personnel Flow: Strictly control the entry and exit of personnel in operating rooms to minimize dust and microorganisms brought about by personnel movement.
Proper Use of Sterile Items: All sterile items should be properly stored and used within their validity period, avoiding expiration or contamination.
