Perioperative Blood Glucose Management

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
Definition of Perioperative Period
Refers to the entire process surrounding surgery. It starts from the patient's decision to undergo surgical treatment and continues until the patient is basically recovered, including preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative periods. Specifically, it refers from the time of determining surgical treatment to the time when the treatment related to this surgery is basically completed.

Time: Approximately 5-7 days before surgery to 7-15 days after surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Perioperative Blood Glucose Impact and Prognosis
(One) Preoperative Assessment
1. Assess the surgical risk in patients with diagnosed diabetes: The surgical treatment for diabetic patients should be based on the principle of safety and simplicity. A comprehensive preoperative assessment of the patient's tolerance to surgery and anesthesia is an important link in ensuring surgical safety.
2. Identify undiagnosed diabetic patients to reduce surgical risk: A complete preoperative assessment for all patients undergoing surgical treatment should include a glucose metabolism level test.
3. Risk assessment for diabetic patients: Potential complications and blood glucose control before admission.

#
(Second) Impact of Hyperglycemia on the Perioperative Body
1. Postoperative infection: On the first postoperative day, a simple blood glucose level >12.2mmol/l is a sensitive indicator for postoperative infection.
2. Mortality: The mortality rate increases 8 times in patients with fasting blood glucose >7mmol/l or random blood glucose >11.1mmol/l, and there is a direct relationship between mortality rate and blood glucose elevation in critically ill patients with blood glucose >5mmol/l.

# Two, Perioperative Blood Glucose Control Goals
(One) Determining the Blood Glucose Control Goals during the Surgical Period
Preoperative: Fasting blood glucose <7.8mmol/l, postprandial blood glucose <10mmol/l.
Intraoperative: Blood glucose 5.0~11.0mmol/l.
Postoperative: Intensive care: blood glucose 7.8mmol/l~10.0mmol/l
Small and medium-sized surgery: fasting blood glucose <7.8mmol/l, postprandial blood glucose <10mmol/l.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
#
(Second) Preoperative Blood Glucose Control Goals
1. Elective surgery: Fasting blood glucose 7~10mmol/l.
2. Ophthalmic surgery: 6~7mmol/l.
3. Emergency surgery: Random blood glucose <14mmol/l, and maintain vital signs stable.
4. Critically ill patients: 4.4~6.1mmol/l.
5. Non-critically ill patients: Pre-meal <6.1mmol/l, random blood glucose <10mmol/l.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
Purpose of Blood Glucose Control
Does not affect fat and protein metabolism
Does not affect the phagocytic ability of white blood cells, reduce the incidence of infection
Favors wound healing
Try to control within the renal glucose threshold, reduce water and electrolyte loss
Avoid hypoglycemia when using anesthetics and intraoperative medications

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
Postoperative Monitoring
Small surgery patients:
Monitor blood glucose, urine glucose, urine ketone bodies, electrolytes
Large and medium-sized surgery patients:
Monitor blood glucose (every 2-4 hours)
Monitor urine glucose, urine ketone bodies (about every 2-4 hours on the first postoperative day, and gradually reduce the frequency of monitoring)
Monitor electrolytes, liver and kidney function, blood gas analysis, electrocardiogram

Dietary Management
Start eating after the patient's intestinal peristalsis recovers, and develop an individualized dietary plan for the patient

Prevention and Treatment of Diabetes Complications after Surgery
Wound healing difficulty treatment:
           —Strengthen perioperative protein supplementation, improve glucose metabolism
           —Extend the suture removal time appropriately

Hypoglycemia prevention:
        —Blood glucose control should not be pursued to normal
        —Monitor blood glucose
        —Detect hypoglycemia symptoms in a timely manner
        —Supplement glucose

Precautions for Perioperative Blood Glucose Management
Perioperative blood glucose monitoring is the basis for the good use of insulin
Insulin infusion should maintain blood glucose at 7-10mmo/L, the ratio of glucose and insulin is individualized
Adjust the insulin dose in a timely manner during the patient's recovery period of eating

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
Postoperative Management of Elderly Patients
The principle of blood glucose management in elderly patients after surgery is the same as before, and blood glucose should not be reduced too low or reduced rapidly in a short period of time.
Once symptoms such as drowsiness, loss of consciousness, and convulsions occur after surgery, consider severe hypoglycemia-induced coma or cerebral edema

### Notes:
The increase in cell apoptosis around the wound, the decrease in cell proliferation rate, the delayed appearance of muscle fiber phenotype, the lack of toughness in newly synthesized fibers, and the slow growth of capillaries will all lead to wound healing difficulties

Preoperative Nursing Assessment and Health Education
Health education:
The target of health education should be the patient and their family;
Increase the awareness of patients and their families about hypoglycemia reactions;
Obtain information about the patient's past acute complications to make predictions, guide the patient and their family to observe the key points;
Educate patients on the importance of blood glucose monitoring;
Educate patients and their families on the significance and key points of dietary control.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
Preoperative Nursing Assessment and Health Education
Nursing measures:
Monitor blood glucose for patients receiving insulin injection and planning for large and medium-sized surgery;
Pay attention to the occurrence of hypoglycemia in patients undergoing fasting examination before surgery;
Prevent hypoglycemia;
Sugar-free diet control and appropriate exercise, especially attention to lung function.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
Postoperative Nursing Assessment and Health Education
Assessment Points:
Blood glucose levels during and after surgery;
Recovery of gastrointestinal function after surgery;
Whether acute complications have occurred;
Infection status at the surgical site.

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Postoperative Nursing Assessment and Health Education
Nursing Measures:
During the fasting period, monitor blood glucose every 4 hours;
Discuss the goals of blood glucose control and formulate a plan;
Accurately administer insulin intravenously or by injection;
Closely monitor for the occurrence of hypoglycemic reactions;

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
Postoperative Nursing Assessment and Health Education
Nursing Measures:
For patients who inject insulin into the abdomen and undergo abdominal surgery, it is contraindicated to inject insulin near the wound;
Skin, tubes, and incision care should be performed under sterile conditions;
Pay attention to hand hygiene;
During the transitional period after surgery, consult the nutrition department for dietary plans;
