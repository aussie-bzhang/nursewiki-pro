# Hip Osteoarthritis – Perioperative Nursing for Total Hip Arthroplasty

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
The hip joint is the largest weight-bearing joint in the human body. It consists of two main parts: the spherical part of the proximal femur—the femoral head—is contained within the acetabulum of the pelvis. A ligament—the round ligament—connects the acetabulum and the femoral head, providing joint stability.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
Hip arthritis is a type of osteoarthritis disease characterized by degeneration of joint cartilage or changes in bone structure due to long-term uneven load on the hip joint surface.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
Symptoms of Hip Arthritis
Pain in the lateral buttock, inguinal region, etc., which may radiate to the knee
Hip pain, swelling, internal effusion, cartilage wear, osteophyte hyperplasia
Joint deformity, limited internal rotation and extension of the hip, 
Unable to walk, even bedridden

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
Etiology
Obesity
The increase in body weight is proportional to the incidence of hip arthritis
Cartilage structure
When the cartilage becomes thin and rigid, its tolerance to pressure decreases
Trauma and external force
When the joint bears unbalanced muscle force and local pressure
Genetic factors
More common in whites and females

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
Clinical Manifestations
70% of patients have a history of infection and trauma in other parts of the body before onset
1. General manifestations
Acute onset, chills, high fever, typical symptoms of acute infectious intoxication
2. Local manifestations
Pain in the affected hip, flexion, abduction, and external rotation in a passive position.
Children may have knee pain, high fever, rapid pulse, and tenderness, percussion tenderness, positive Thomas sign in front of the hip.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Radiographic Findings
1. Early stage: Periarticular soft tissue swelling; increased soft tissue density, widened joint space. The epiphysis of children may be displaced outside the joint.
2. Middle stage, joint cartilage destruction → narrowing of the joint space → erosion and destruction of the joint surface
3. Marginal erosion and destruction of the bone edge outside the joint surface → bone destruction, floating bone fragments, joint collapse → narrowing or disappearance of the joint space → joint dislocation, head neck defect, bony fusion.

General Information: Patient ***, *** bed, female, *** years old,
Chief Complaint: More than four months before admission, the patient experienced numbness and tingling in the left lower limb, slight pain in the left buttock, with numbness as the main symptom. Numbness radiates to the posterior side of the calf. There is no numbness in the sole and toes. The symptoms worsen when walking and can be relieved by lying flat and resting. The patient was diagnosed with "cystic degeneration of the left femoral head and osteoarthritis of both hips" in an external hospital. The patient had received acupuncture, physical therapy, traction, and other treatments, but the symptoms did not improve significantly.
Medical History: Previous diseases: lumbar disc herniation
Physical Examination: T: 37℃, P: 80 beats/min, R: 20 beats/min, BP: 146/86mmHg, general condition good, active position, cooperative during physical examination.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->

Special Physical Examination: The left lower limb is 4cm longer than the right lower limb, the right hip has normal range of motion, there are no sensory abnormalities in the right lower limb, the left hip four-square test is suspicious, significant limitation in internal rotation, marked pain in the left buttock during internal rotation, negative tenderness in both inguinal groins, positive Thomas sign on the left, negative straight leg raise test, negative strengthening test, negative lumbar percussion tenderness, positive tenderness at the sciatic notch of the left buttock, marked radiation pain to the posterior side of the left calf.
Auxiliary Examinations: Pelvic CT three-dimensional imaging: OA of both hips.
Diagnosis: Bilateral Hip Osteoarthritis

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->

Left total hip arthroplasty was performed under general anesthesia on 3-4.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
Dietary Education
Preoperative guidance: No food or water after the evening before surgery
Postoperative guidance: No food or water (within 6H) – light diet (6H outside) – normal diet, high-protein, fruits and vegetables, blood-building foods (second day)

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
Functional Exercise
Preoperative guidance:
Dorsiflexion, plantar flexion, quadriceps muscle training, turning over the affected side, lifting the buttocks
Auxiliary devices
Postoperative guidance:
Maintain the affected limb in abduction and neutral position on the first day, place a soft pillow under the knee, once the lower limb sensation recovers, the patient can perform the dorsal flexion and toe extension activities of the affected limb ankle joint, and isometric contractions of the quadriceps muscle.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
Ankle Extension and Flexion:
1. Gently curl the toes upwards
2. 5-10 times every hour
3. Each action lasts for 3 seconds;
then extend the foot to straighten the foot surface.
Start immediately after surgery until complete recovery.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->

      Guide the patient to perform isometric contractions of the quadriceps muscle and toe movements. Take a semi-recumbent position (the bed head should not exceed 90° to avoid dislocation of the prosthesis), perform gentle hip flexion and extension activities, pay attention to flexion <45°, the heel should not leave the bed, and at the same time, move the knee and ankle joints.

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->

The patient sits on the edge of the bed, the patient performs the exercise of moving the lower leg, the purpose of which is to restore the function of the quadriceps muscle, increase leg strength, and prepare for walking on the ground.

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
# How to Get Out of Bed

Place the walker next to the surgical side
Move the body towards the bedside
Move the surgical side leg to the bed
Prevent external rotation of the surgical side hip
Move the healthy side leg down as the other leg
Straighten the body
Support the walker to stand

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
# How to Walk with a Walker

Place the walker 10cm in front of the body
Step with the healthy side leg first
Then follow with the surgical side leg.

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
# How to Sit

Before sitting down, prepare with a chair that has a back and armrests, add a cushion, step back, check the position, hold with both hands, and sit down slowly (hip flexion should not exceed 90°, and a higher chair should be used).

<!-- Slide number: 21 -->
# How to Stand

To stand up from the chair, first move the body to the side of the chair, place the affected limb in front, and have the non-affected leg bear most of the weight.

# Nursing Diagnoses and Nursing Measures
**P** Fear and anxiety - Related to the lack of understanding of the disease and the unknown about surgery
**I**: Encourage patients to express themselves, meet their needs promptly, establish a good nurse-patient relationship, and build the patient's confidence
**O**: Patient's anxiety is relieved (3-3)

**P** Knowledge deficit
**I**: Strengthen preoperative and postoperative education
**O**: Patient understands the preoperative and postoperative precautions (3-3)

**P** Pain - Related to tissue damage caused by surgery
**I**: Pain pump, administer Tramadol 1 tablet bid as ordered
**O**: Patient's pain is relieved, p: 5—1, suitable for sleep (3-4)

**P** Hypoperfusion - Related to preoperative and postoperative fasting, excessive blood and fluid loss during surgery
**I**: Establish a venous access, infuse fluids as ordered, adjust the infusion rate, and transfuse blood
**O**: Correct hypovolemia, hemoglobin level rises (3-5)

**P** Impaired physical mobility - Related to surgical trauma and pain
**I**: Help with correct and reasonable turning, or moving the body, and guide functional exercise correctly
**O**: Hip flexion is less than 90 degrees, gait practice is correct (3-6)

<!-- Slide number: 26 -->

**P** Fever - Related to surgical absorption heat
**I**: Instruct the patient to drink more water
**O**: Patient's body temperature returns to the normal range (3-13)

<!-- Slide number: 27 -->
### Potential Complications

**P** Risk of infection - Related to surgery and tissue damage
**I**: Administer Cefotetan injection as ordered, observe the wound drainage, and change dressings promptly
**O**: Patient's wound heals well, no infection occurs (3-13)

**P** Risk of skin integrity damage - Related to postoperative bed rest
**I**: Keep the bed unit clean and dry, turn regularly, massage the skin, and promote blood circulation
**O**: Patient's skin is intact, no pressure sores (3-13)

**P** Risk of deep vein thrombosis
**I**: Functional exercise, observe for edema, and administer 1 tablet of Xarelto qd as ordered
**O**: Patient has no deep vein thrombosis (3-13)

<!-- Slide number: 29 -->

**P** Risk of dislocation - Related to improper positioning
**I**: Keep the affected limb in 15~30° abduction and neutral position
   Do not perform straight-leg elevation activities after surgery
   Place the commode from the non-affected side to avoid moving the hip joint
   Gradually guide and demonstrate the posture to avoid dislocation during rehabilitation, do not cross the feet, do not bend forward or stoop to pick up things, do not sit on low stools, etc.
   Closely observe the patient's condition, if the affected limb is severely painful, the lower limbs show unequal length or excessive external rotation, then it is highly suspected of dislocation, and the doctor should be reported immediately for emergency treatment
**O**: Patient has no dislocation (3-13)
