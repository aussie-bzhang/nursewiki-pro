# Hip fractures are common, with high rates of disability and mortality, placing a heavy burden on families and society. Internationally, it has been listed as a disease of equal importance to cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Determining the best treatment strategy for hip fractures is of great significance in reducing patient disability.

Epidemiology Statistics show that hip fractures account for more than 20% of all fractures. Epidemiological data indicate that the number of people suffering from hip fractures worldwide will increase from 1.3 million in 1990 to 2.6 million in 2025, and to 4.5 million by 2050. In the United States, 937.5 people per 10,000 suffer from hip fractures each year, with more than half of the annual incidence of fractures occurring in Asia.

# Treatment Principles
Conservative treatment has many complications, and early surgery has become a consensus.

Treatment Principles:
Robust fixation
Early ambulation
Avoid long-term bed rest and its various complications
Robust internal fixation of intertrochanteric fractures and early patient activity should be considered as standard treatment methods.

| Diagnosis on Admission | 1. Closed right femoral neck fracture (subcapital type, Garden IV type); 2. Hypertensive disease (grade 3, high risk); 3. Senile osteoporosis |

The outpatient X-ray showed: right femoral neck fracture with slight displacement, neck shaft angle approximately normal. Admitted to the orthopedic department, after admission, right lower limb skin traction was applied for immobilization, and on 20/11, the patient was sent to the operating room for right total hip arthroplasty. Currently, the incision on the right hip is slightly swollen, with a few ecchymoses around, good incision approximation, and slight erythema. After getting out of bed with assistance, the patient felt less weakness in the limb compared to before, with no limb numbness or discomfort. |
| Past Medical History | Has a history of hypertension for over 50 years, has been taking medication to control blood pressure, and blood pressure is well controlled. Has over 50 years of rheumatic heart disease, which has not recurred after treatment. |

# III. Risk of Prosthesis Dislocation

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 21/11 | Observe whether there is a sudden onset of severe pain in the affected limb, unequal length of lower limbs | No prosthesis dislocation occurred | 1. # Discharge Education
1. Regular lifestyle, happy mood, sufficient sleep, avoid catching a cold
2. Reasonable and healthy diet
3. According to individual conditions, perform continuous functional exercise; gradually increase activity, not to feel tired
4. Within the first six months after surgery, avoid adduction and external rotation of the affected limb to prevent prosthesis dislocation; if dislocation occurs, immobilize immediately and seek medical attention promptly
5. Take care of the prosthesis, avoid overloading, reduce wear and tear to extend the lifespan of the prosthesis

<!-- Slide number: 29 -->

Six Don'ts

Note

# Reflection
1. In clinical practice, applying evidence-based nursing to care for patients not only helps nurses accurately inform or advise patients but also strengthens the consistency between nursing and medical care; it also reduces waste of time and money due to inappropriate nursing, enabling patients to receive high-quality care.
2. Due to the patient's inability to get out of bed after fracture surgery, eating, drinking, defecating, and urinating are all on the bed, strengthening life care is particularly important; elderly people have thin skin, frequent turning over, preventing dampness, and preventing pressure ulcers are also the focus of handover.
3. Pay attention to the assessment of caregivers' care skills, provide patient guidance, and communicate with the family in a timely manner to gain support and recognition;
4. Pay attention to the cooperation and communication among multi-disciplinary teams.
