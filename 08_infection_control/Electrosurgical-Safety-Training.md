Overview > Purpose

Standardize the operation procedures for three types of electrosurgical energy equipment: monopolar electrotome, bipolar coagulator, and ultrasonic scalpel. Guide operating room nurses in correctly assessing, using, and maintaining electrosurgical equipment to reduce safety hazards during operation and maximize the safety of patients and healthcare professionals during surgery.

Terminology > Electrosurgery

Electrosurgery (electrosurgery) is a high-frequency current surgical system applied in operating rooms. Electrosurgery integrates a variety of high-frequency current surgical equipment such as high-frequency electrotomes, large vessel closure systems, ultrasonic scalpels, argon beams, LEEP knives, and endoscopic electrosurgical knives, and controls the cutting depth and hemostasis speed during the surgical process through computers to achieve hemostasis and coagulation effects.

### Notes:
**Flat Description**

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
Terminology > Monopolar Electrotome

Monopolar electrotome (monopolar electrotome) is an electrosurgical device that uses high-frequency current (greater than 200KHz) in a single circuit to produce thermal energy and discharge to cut and hemostasize tissues.

Working Principle of High-Frequency Electrotome

### Notes:
**Flat Description**

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
Terminology > Bipolar Coagulator

Bipolar coagulator (bipolar electrotome)
is a high-frequency current generator that, when the bipolar coagulator instrument is in good contact with the tissue, produces thermal energy between the two poles of the bipolar forceps, which is used for electrocoagulation and hemostasis of human tissues.

Working Principle of Bipolar Coagulator

### Notes:
**Flat Description**

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
Terminology > Ultrasonic Scalpel

Ultrasonic scalpel (ultrasonic scalpel)
is a generator that can produce ultrasonic energy and mechanical vibration. By acting on the metal probe (blade) through the ultrasonic frequency generator, it oscillates mechanically at a frequency of 55.5kHz through the blade (50-100μm), converting electrical energy into mechanical energy, causing the liquid in the tissue to vaporize, the hydrogen bonds of proteins to break, cells to disintegrate, proteins to coagulate, and blood vessels to close, achieving the effects of incision and hemostasis.

Working Principle of Ultrasonic Scalpel

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
Terminology > Return Electrode

The return electrode (return electrode) is used in conjunction with the high-frequency electrotome main unit during electrosurgical surgery to provide a safe return path for the electrosurgical current. The use of the return electrode can effectively reduce current density, increase heat dissipation, disperse the current, and prevent thermal injury.

### Notes:
**Flat Description**

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Terminology > Coupling Effect

Coupling effect (coupling effect) refers to the phenomenon where there is a close match and mutual influence between the input and output of two or more circuit components or electrical networks, and energy is transmitted from one side to the other through interaction. In electrosurgical applications, it manifests as the transmission of energy from the working cable (electrotome pen or hook) to adjacent cables or metal instruments.

Common Equipment > Monopolar Scalpel > Key Operations

Connect the high-frequency scalpel pen to the main unit. When fixing the scalpel connection line, do not twist it with other wires to prevent coupling effects; place the scalpel pen in an insulating protective cover when not in use; to avoid equipment leakage or short-circuit, do not wrap the wire around metal items; if there is a grounding device, it should be properly connected.
Test the output power of the scalpel pen using hand control or foot control.
Clear the carbon deposits on the scalpel pen in a timely manner; replace the scalpel tip promptly if it is found to be malfunctioning.
After the surgery is completed, turn the output power to the lowest setting, turn off the main unit power, disconnect the monopolar scalpel connection line, remove the circuit negative pole plate, and disconnect the power line.
After the operation, use the registration form, clean and organize the scalpel equipment.

Common Equipment > Monopolar Scalpel > Negative Pole Plate Use

It is recommended to use high-quality soft negative pole plates with double loops, and disposable negative pole plates are strictly prohibited from reuse and cutting.
Select a negative pole plate of the appropriate size, with dedicated negative pole plates for adults and children.
For patients with burns, newborns, and those with metal implants, it is recommended to use bipolar coagulation, or choose capacitive circuit pad.
Infants or children should select the appropriate negative pole plate according to their weight, and it is strictly prohibited to cut the negative pole plate. The negative pole plate should have strong adhesion and be easy to tear off.
Before use, check its validity period, integrity, defects, discoloration, adhesions, and dryness; expired, damaged, or dried-out hydrogel negative pole plates are prohibited from use; negative pole plates should not be stacked, and they should be used immediately after unpacking.

Strictly follow the instructions for use

Common Equipment > Monopolar Scalpel > Precautions
Precautions
Patients with pacemakers or metal implants should avoid or use high-frequency scalpels (can be used under the guidance of the manufacturer or cardiologist), or switch to bipolar coagulation.
If monopolar scalpel is necessary, use the minimum effective power and the shortest time
The position of the negative pole plate of the circuit should be close to the surgical site; when selecting the application site of the negative pole plate, let the main current loop avoid metal implants
Strengthen monitoring, closely observe the patient's condition. For patients with pacemakers, prioritize the use of bipolar coagulation and low-power operation under the guidance of professionals, to avoid the current loop passing through the heart and pacemaker, and try to keep the wire away from the pacemaker and its lead

<!-- Slide number: 28 -->
Common Equipment > Monopolar Scalpel > Precautions
### Precautions
It is generally recommended to avoid prolonged continuous operation of the monopolar scalpel, as the negative pole plate of the circuit cannot dissipate current in time, which may cause skin burns.
The output power should be selected according to the type of tissue being cut or coagulated to achieve the desired surgical outcome, and should be adjusted gradually from low to high.
When using alcohol-based disinfectant to disinfect the skin, it should be avoided that the disinfectant accumulates on the operating table. After disinfection, the monopolar scalpel should be activated only after the alcohol has evaporated to prevent skin burns in the patient due to electric sparks encountering flammable liquids. When using an electric scalpel or coagulator during airway surgery, prevent airway burns. It is contraindicated to use mannitol enema in intestinal surgery, and caution should be exercised when using an electric scalpel in patients with intestinal obstruction.

### Notes:
【Flat Description】

<!-- Slide number: 29 -->
Common Equipment > Monopolar Scalpel > Precautions
### Precautions
The electric scalpel pen cable should not be wrapped around metal objects to prevent electric leakage and accidents.
The working prompt sound should be adjusted to a volume that can be clearly heard by the staff.
The negative pole plate should be as close as possible to the surgical incision site (but not less than 15 cm), avoiding crossing lines over the body to ensure the shortest path for current flow.
Before using instruments with coagulation function in laparoscopic surgery, check the integrity of the insulation layer to prevent electric leakage and damage to adjacent organs.
The equipment should be regularly inspected and maintained.

<!-- Slide number: 35 -->
Common Equipment > Bipolar Coagulation > Precautions
### Precautions
Select the appropriate coagulation instrument and output power according to the surgical site and tissue characteristics.
When using bipolar coagulation, apply physiological saline for intermittent flushing or drip irrigation to keep the tissue moist, tension-free, and the surgical field clean. Avoid high temperature affecting the important tissues and structures around the coagulation, reducing tissue char and adhesion to the bipolar forceps or clamp.
It is recommended to use intermittent coagulation, with each coagulation time of about 0.5 seconds, which can be repeated multiple times until the coagulation effect is achieved, avoiding excessive coagulation.

<!-- Slide number: 36 -->
Common Equipment > Bipolar Coagulation > Precautions
### Precautions
Protection of bipolar coagulation instruments or forceps tips:
When coagulating, remove the charred material from the bipolar coagulation instruments or forceps with a wet gauze or professional non-injury cloth in a timely manner. Do not use sharp objects to scrape it off, as this may damage the alloy material at the tip or forceps tip.
Operate the bipolar coagulation instruments gently. When fixing the bipolar forceps tip, maintain a certain distance between the two tips to avoid contact and form a current short circuit or poor tip closure due to external force, affecting the coagulation effect.
After cleaning, the bipolar coagulation instruments should be covered with a protective cap on the tip or forceps tip.
Equipment maintenance and care:
Pay attention to the compatibility between the bipolar coagulation instruments and the main unit
The foot control panel should be covered with a waterproof protective cover before use, which is convenient for cleaning and prevents circuit failure or short circuit

<!-- Slide number: 42 -->
Common Equipment > Ultrasonic Scalpel > Precautions
### Precautions
Use strictly according to the manufacturer's instructions and install the accessories in accordance with the specifications.
Ultrasonic Scalpel Alarm:
When the ultrasonic scalpel self-check detects a fault, the main unit screen will display a fault code, and it is necessary to have the equipment technical personnel repair or replace the components in a timely manner.
If two foot switches are pressed at the same time during use, the main unit will have an alarm, but no fault code will be displayed.
When the ultrasonic scalpel is used for an extended period of time and the temperature is too high, the machine will automatically alarm. The ultrasonic scalpel blade should be immersed in physiological saline, and then used after the blade has cooled down.

<!-- Slide number: 43 -->
Common Equipment > Ultrasonic Scalpel > Precautions
Precautions
Contraindications for the use of ultrasonic scalpel:
Do not touch the ultrasonic scalpel with hands while in operation, and avoid continuous long-term overload operation; do not close the blade head by stepping on the foot pedal or holding metal items and bone tissue with the ultrasonic scalpel head
Since the closure of the lumen by the ultrasonic scalpel is permanent, it is necessary to confirm whether the type of tissue closed is suitable

### Notes:
【Flat Description】

<!-- Slide number: 44 -->
Common Equipment > Ultrasonic Scalpel > Precautions
Precautions
Maintenance and care of ultrasonic scalpel:
Handle the ultrasonic scalpel head gently to avoid heavy pressure, do not collide with hard objects or drop it to the ground
After use, wipe the handle line clean with a damp cloth, do not rinse with water, and keep the 15-20cm diameter coil in place according to its arc
For patients with blood, body fluids, isolation, or special infections, use chlorine disinfectant or acidified water to wipe and disinfect, or handle them according to the postoperative treatment methods for special infection patients
Avoid collision or forceful throwing during cleaning
The handle line must be sterilized according to the manufacturer's instructions or use a single-use sterile protective cover to meet the sterile requirements
