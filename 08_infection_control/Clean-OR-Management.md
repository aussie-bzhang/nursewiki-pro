Clean Operating Departments

1. Clean operating room: An operating room that uses air purification technology to reduce the microbial particles and total particulate matter in the surgical environment air to an allowable level.

Air purification technology: Controls indoor dust content through three levels of filtration (primary, medium, and high efficiency), selects certain air flow patterns and air changes, and achieves a certain level of air purification.

2. Clean auxiliary rooms: Rooms that require air cleanliness.

3. Non-clean auxiliary rooms: Rooms that do not require air cleanliness.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->

Purification Air Conditioning System

An air conditioning system that uses filtration and sterilization as the main measure to control the concentration of suspended dust and microorganisms in the controlled area to the required level.

	 Air processor
	 Primary, medium, and high-efficiency filters
	 Pressure fan
	 Air heater
	 Return air and supply air outlets

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->

Purification of Air Conditioning System

	 The purification effect is achieved by continuously replacing the polluted air in the room with clean air processed by the purification air conditioning system.

	 Self-purification: The practice of pre-activating the purification air conditioning system for different levels of operating rooms.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->

Self-purification Time

The time required to reduce the dust concentration in the operating room air to approximately 90% or to the upper limit concentration of the design cleanliness level after the postoperative waste has been cleared under normal air change conditions.

For consecutive surgeries, a certain time must be allowed to meet the self-purification requirements before the next surgery can be performed.

Common Terms

	 Surgical area: The area that requires special protection, including the operating table and a certain distance outside the four sides of the operating table.
	 Surrounding area: All other areas except the surgical area.
	 Cleanliness level 5: Equivalent to the original 100 level
	 Cleanliness level 6: Equivalent to the original 1000 level       	 Cleanliness level 7: Equivalent to the original 10000 level      	 Cleanliness level 8: Equivalent to the original 100000 level     	 Cleanliness level 8.5: Equivalent to the original 300000 level

	 Static state: The state in which the indoor purification air conditioning facilities and functions are complete and ready for operation, but there are no medical equipment and personnel in the room.
	 Static state: The state in which the indoor purification air conditioning facilities and functions are complete and operational, and if there are medical equipment, the medical equipment is installed and ready for operation, but there are no staff.

	 Floating bacteria concentration: Abbreviated as floating bacteria concentration. The number of colony-forming units per unit volume of air obtained by random sampling in the air with a floating bacteria sampler and culture, representing the number of floating bacteria in the air, cfu/m3
	 Sedimentation bacteria concentration: Abbreviated as sedimentation bacteria concentration. Sedimentation method is also known as the plate exposure method. The number of colony-forming units obtained by exposing a culture dish to the air and covering it after sampling, and then culturing, representing the number of bacteria that can be settled in the air, cfu/plate

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->

	 Scale of clean operating departments
One operating room for every 20 to 25 beds
Large area: 50 to 60 m2, medium operating room area: 30 to 40 m2, small operating room area: 20 to 30 m2
	 Environment of clean operating departments
It is advisable to be far away from the source of pollution within or around the hospital, in the upper windward direction
It is not advisable to be located on the first floor or the top floor of high-rise buildings
The clean operating department should be an independent area
It should be conveniently connected with departments such as radiology, pathology, central sterile supply, and blood transfusion.

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->

	 Plan layout of clean operating departments
Divided into clean and non-clean areas, and the communication between them must be equipped with a buffer room or a transfer window

Easy to evacuate, reasonable functional flow, and clear distinction between clean and dirty areas

5. Strict Dress Code Management
	 Surgical suite clothing, pants, shoes, hats, and masks must be worn.
	 The bottom of the shirt is tied inside the washing pants, and hair must not be visible.
	 The mask covers the mouth and nose.
	 Change clothes and shoes when going out.

	 Change surgical patients to clean patient clothing and wear isolation hats.

<!-- Slide number: 30 -->

6. Strengthen Suite Door Management
	 Keep the front and back doors closed during surgery.
	 Allocate surgical suites by specialty and keep them relatively fixed.
	 All items are fixed in number and placed in designated locations to reduce the number of times they are brought in and out.
	 Avoid frequent door opening and closing to change the pressure gradient, affecting the purification effect.

<!-- Slide number: 31 -->

4. Strictly Control Personnel Entry and Exit
	 Dedicated management staff
	 No more than 2 people per case
	 Refuse visitors for transplant, joint replacement, heart, special infection, and other surgeries.
	 External visitors must be approved by the medical department.
	 New surgery, academic exchange, and surgical demonstrations can be arranged in surgical suites with live broadcast systems.

	 Item Access
	 Open the outer packaging of instruments and equipment first, wipe them with wet wipes, and then enter.
	 Disposable items are removed from the packaging and sent to the surgical suite and sterile room.

	 Item Location Management in the Surgical Suite

Thirdly, Cleaning Management
	 Before and after each surgery, and at the end of each day's surgery: Wet cleaning
	 During the operation of the purification air conditioning system.
	 After cleaning, continue to run to achieve the self-cleaning time.
	 Cleaning tools require: Non-fibrous fabric; Different colors are distinguished; Disinfect and dry after use for standby.

	 Waste collection, classification, and standardized disposal are carried out in a timely manner to minimize ground contamination.

	 At the end of the day's surgery: Clean and disinfect the exposed ground and surfaces in the surgical suite.
	 Every week: Carry out a thorough cleaning of the surgical department, including all surfaces including ceilings, walls, and floors.

Fourthly, Operation Management
	 Maintenance and Maintenance
	 A dedicated maintenance administrator, follow the equipment's instructions for maintenance and maintenance.
	 Develop an operation manual, with checks and records.
	 Regularly inspect and maintain air handling units and fresh air handling units to keep them clean.
	 Fresh air handling unit: The coarse filter should be cleaned every 2 days.
	 Coarse filter should be replaced every 1~2 months.
	 Medium efficiency filter should be checked weekly and replaced every 3 months.
	 High-efficiency filter should be replaced annually.

<!-- Slide number: 39 -->

Fifthly, Safety Management
	 Exclusive safety officer, safety knowledge training, and various emergency plan drills.
	 Fire facilities must be in good condition.
	 Safety doors must be kept open at all times, and safety corridors must have clear signage, no clutter, and ensure they are unobstructed.
	 Conduct monthly inspections of the surgical department.
	 Activate the emergency plan in the event of a fire.

<!-- Slide number: 40 -->

Terminal High-Efficiency Particulate Air (HEPA) Filters: Check annually and replace when resistance exceeds the design resistance by 160 Pa or after 3 years of use.

Medium-Efficiency Filters in Exhaust Fan Units: Replace annually and replace promptly if contamination or blockage is detected. Regularly inspect the return air filter grill and clean it weekly. Replace it annually. In case of special contamination, even if replaced, wipe the inner surface of the return air inlet with a disinfectant.
