## One: Concept of Hypothermia:
   Body temperature refers to the internal temperature of the body, which is the result of metabolism and skeletal muscle movement in the body, and is also an important condition for human life activity. When the central body temperature is below 36°C, it is called hypothermia.

There are reports that 50%-70% of surgical patients experience hypothermia, which is a common complication during the perioperative period and can lead to severe physiological disorders, even threatening the patient's life.

## Effects on Coagulation Function
Platelet function is weakened, reducing the activity of coagulation factors, leading to prolonged bleeding time and increased blood loss. Hypothermia also causes venous blood stasis, which is prone to lead to deep vein thrombosis over a long period of time.

## Metabolic Disorders
# Increased Incidence of Wound Infection
. Hypothermia can reduce the metabolic rate of the body, with a reduction of 6% in metabolic rate for every 1°C decrease in body temperature. At a body temperature of 28°C, the metabolic rate is 50% of normal.

Experiments have shown that when the body temperature is between 32-35°C, there can be a redistribution of blood to various organs. Extracellular potassium ions transfer to the cell interior during hypothermia, leading to hypokalemia.
  The decrease in body temperature can directly damage the body's immune function, reduce skin blood oxygen supply, and decrease protein and osteogenic protein synthesis, which lead to an increased incidence of wound infection in patients. It has been reported that the incidence of wound infection in elective intestinal surgery increases by about 1 times. The average hospital stay is extended by 20%.

## Delayed Awakening
# Shivering
Hypothermia can suppress sympathetic nervous activity, reduce the production of catecholamines, weaken the body's response to external stimuli, and at the same time, the metabolism of anesthetic drugs in the body slows down, leading to a relative prolongation of awakening and extubation time.
  Hypothermic shivering can increase oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production. The incidence of shivering in patients who have not been effectively warmed during the recovery from anesthesia is about 40%, and shivering causes muscle contraction, increasing oxygen consumption by 48.6%, and increases patient fear, causing wound pain.

## Three: Causes of Hypothermia

Anesthetic Effects on Body Temperature
1. Anesthetics have an inhibitory effect on body temperature regulation.
Under general anesthesia, the central body temperature of the human body increases from the original 0.2°C fluctuation to 4°C, anesthetics inhibit vasoconstriction and shivering. Muscle relaxants cause skeletal muscle relaxation, leading to a decrease in body temperature.
2. The effect of spinal anesthesia
Spinal anesthesia blocks nerve conduction, interfering with temperature receptors while inhibiting sweating, vasoconstriction, and shivering, leading to a decrease in temperature.

## Three: Causes of Hypothermia

Age Effects on Body Temperature
Children have an underdeveloped temperature regulation center, and their body temperature is prone to decrease with the room temperature. For example, a 1-year-old infant's body temperature can decrease by 0.5°C during 1 hour of surgery at normal temperature; it can decrease by 3°C-4°C after 2 hours of surgery. The basal metabolic rate of the elderly is low, and the rate of temperature decrease is also high.
Environmental Temperature and Humidity Effects on Body Temperature
There is a difference in the perception of room temperature between patients and medical staff. It has been reported that 67% of patients experience shivering in winter, indicating that low temperatures are prone to increase heat loss. Low humidity in the operating room can lead to increased evaporation during preoperative skin disinfection and incision irrigation, increasing heat loss.

## Four: Prevention of Perioperative Hypothermia
1. Control of indoor temperature and humidity: Maintaining appropriate temperature and humidity can reduce skin heat loss and is an effective method for preventing hypothermia.
Appropriate temperature and humidity:
 Temperature 22…25℃; relative humidity 45％RH…60％RH.

# Four. Prevention of Perioperative Hypothermia
2. Reducing Heat Loss from the Body Surface: 
   ① Prevent redistribution of body temperature: Use a heated blanket on the operating table, ensure adequate warmth for the limbs, perform preoperative disinfection with appropriate dosage and rapid movement. A single layer of covering can reduce 30% of heat loss. 
   ② Surface warming: Use an inflatable thermal blanket at 36-40°C to cover the non-surgical area, which can effectively prevent the occurrence of intraoperative hypothermia and is currently one of the more ideal methods.
