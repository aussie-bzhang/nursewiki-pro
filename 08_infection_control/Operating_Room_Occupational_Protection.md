#
Hand Hygiene Protocol
Hand hygiene is the simplest, most effective, and most important measure to prevent infection, yet it is also the most difficult to perform well. It must be frequently and repeatedly propagated and educated to nursing staff: in order to protect patients and ourselves, we must seriously adhere to the hand hygiene protocol.

Company Logo

#
Indications for Hand Hygiene
Before and after contact with confirmed or suspected patients
After contact with blood, body fluids, and contaminated items
Before handling clean or sterile items
Before and after aseptic procedures
Before and after entering and leaving isolation wards, donning and doffing isolation gowns
Between contaminated and clean procedures on the same patient
Before putting on and after removing gloves

#
Occupational Protection and Hand Hygiene
Microorganisms can colonize on the hands of healthcare workers and be transmitted to patients, other healthcare workers, and their family members through contact. Hand hygiene can break the main route of hospital transmission and is the most economical and effective method to reduce hospital infections. Healthcare workers adhering to high-quality hand hygiene can reduce the incidence of hospital infections by 25%-50%. Hand hygiene is also the most effective method for healthcare workers to protect themselves.
In the operating room, it is not only surgeons and nurses who need to pay attention to the execution of hand hygiene, but also anesthesiologists and workers.

Company Logo

#
Occupational Exposure Protection
Proper use of protective isolation equipment. Gloves must be worn when handling the blood, body fluids, and contaminated items of patients. Replace them promptly if they become contaminated; wear isolation gowns, masks, and protective goggles during operations where there is a risk of blood or body fluid splashes. Use aprons and shoe covers if necessary.

Company Logo

#
Standard Protective Measures for Staff
1. Wear gloves when contacting contaminated substances and items contaminated by them.
2. Wash hands immediately after removing gloves.
3. Wash hands immediately after contacting contaminated items and their contaminated items.
4. Wear protective masks, goggles, or face shields if there is a risk of blood, body fluids, or secretions splashing onto the face and eyes during work. Wear isolation gowns.
5. Prevent needlestick injuries when handling sharp objects.
6. Take proper disinfection measures for medical devices and instruments used by patients.

Company Logo

#
Occupational Exposure Reporting Process for Healthcare Workers
Healthcare workers who sustain needlestick injuries should report to the department head or nurse manager immediately. During normal working hours, report to the hospital infection control department within 2 hours. If the source patient is HIV-positive or suspected to be HIV-positive, report immediately after exposure and fill out the "Dongguan Third People's Hospital Medical Needlestick Injury Registration Form" to be sent to the hospital infection control department. During holidays and non-normal working hours, notify the on-call medical staff for assessment and treatment immediately.

#
Occupational Exposure Management Process
                                             Needlestick Injury
If gloves are worn, quickly remove the gloves
Gently squeeze from the proximal to the distal end to expel as much blood as possible
Wash the wound with soap and running water for about 10 minutes
Disinfect with 75% alcohol or 0.5% iodophor
Bandage the wound as needed
Mucosal Exposure: 1. Mucous membranes - Rinse repeatedly with large amounts of normal saline and disinfect with 0.05% iodophor. 2. Eyes - Rinse repeatedly with large amounts of normal saline or use an eyewash station for further treatment by a specialist.

#
Intraoperative Protection
Personnel Control:
1. Visitors should be restricted for such surgeries; all participants entering the operating room should not enter and exit at will, and efforts should be made to minimize the flow of personnel in the operating room.
2. Special observation surgery personnel must wear isolation gowns, change shoes or shoe covers, wear masks and hats according to regulations; visitors should be strictly limited and prohibited from moving between operating rooms.

Company Logo

#
Intraoperative Protection
1. Wear N95 face masks for procedures involving droplet and airborne transmission.
2. If the patient is a contact infectious case or has bleeding and exudative conditions: spread a large sheet on the trolley to wrap the patient completely. Set up an infection card to remind staff to take isolation measures.
3. Surgeons should wear shoe covers, double gloves, and wear disposable surgical gowns if necessary. Two circulating nurses should be set up inside and outside the room, and all items needed should be passed by the outside nurse. Do not enter the operating room at will.

#
Postoperative Management
Human waste generated during surgery should be disposed of strictly as pathological waste, as infectious waste, and placed in double yellow bags, marked, and sealed promptly. The medical waste should be handed over to the hospital's medical waste disposal personnel for timely removal and centralized processing. The operating room floor, chairs, instrument tables, operating beds, etc., should be wiped with high-efficiency disinfectants.

#
General Methods of Radiation Protection
1. Short exposure time.
2. Increase the distance from the X-ray source and use shielding protection.
3. Prioritize inherent protection and personal protection.
4. The inherent safety protection of the X-ray machine itself and the fixed protective facilities in the operating room.
5. Consider the protection of both operating room staff and patients.
6. Take full care of all staff in the area without neglecting others' protection.

#
Particle Surgery Protection
1. Radioactive implants should be placed in lead barrier containers.
2. Wear masks and gloves when handling radioactive materials.
3. Wear masks when working around radioactive materials to prevent inhalation.
4. Use lead barriers when exposed to high-frequency radiation.
5. Individuals should maintain a safe distance from the necessary protective layers as much as possible.
6. Limit access to areas with ionizing radiation.
