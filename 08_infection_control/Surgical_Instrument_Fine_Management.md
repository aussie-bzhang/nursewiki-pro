# The issue of medical device disinfection is not a trivial matter

From October 9, 2009, to December 27, 2009, among the 38 cesarean section patients at the Gaojiao Central Hospital in Chaoshan District, Shantou City, Guangdong Province, 18 had surgical site infections, with the pathogen being rapidly growing nontuberculous mycobacteria. The main reason for these pregnant women's infections was that the hospital's surgical instrument sterilization was not up to standard, and the medical staff had a weak sense of infection prevention and control. The hospital's surgical instruments were not cleaned thoroughly, and there was blood residue. The surgical knives, scissors, suture needles, and disposable surgical scissors used were soaked in glutaraldehyde, which could not achieve sterilization. The surgical hand antiseptic agent did not meet the standard. After the clinical occurrence of multiple cesarean section patient surgical incision infection cases, the medical staff were slow to respond, failed to timely identify problems, report them, and take effective prevention and control measures.

The "surgical knives, scissors, suture needles, and disposable surgical scissors" mentioned in the Shantou maternal infection incident are classified as highly hazardous medical devices according to the Seldin classification, and should be sterilized before use. According to the principles of choosing disinfection and sterilization methods, medical devices that are wet and heat-resistant should be首选 pressure steam sterilization. However, the hospital chose to use glutaraldehyde immersion sterilization, which was obviously not standardized.

After the incident, the hospital was ordered to make rectifications within a specified time and the relevant responsible persons were held accountable. The hospital director was administratively demoted, the deputy director in charge was administratively demoted, the head of the Nursing Department and the head of the Obstetrics and Gynecology Department and the head nurse were removed from their posts.

# Materials and Manufacturing Processes

Surface Treatment—Sandblasting, Brushing

Appearance—Matte, Semi-matte

Reprocessing Elements:
1. Prior to the first cleaning, de-oiling treatment should be carried out.
2. Since the product surface does not have an electroplating layer, new products are sensitive to chlorinated substances and iodophors, and are relatively prone to rust. It is necessary to minimize the contact time between the product and chlorinated substances and iodophors. After surgery or use, the surface residues should be washed off with tap water as soon as possible, and the speed of reprocessing should be accelerated. Pay attention to moisture preservation for instruments left overnight;
3. Due to the special surface manufacturing process of this type of product, the cleanliness requirements for sterilization steam are higher. If the content of impurities in the steam is higher than the standard requirements, the surface of the product after sterilization will produce spots.

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Materials and Manufacturing Processes

Surface Treatment—Physical Coating, Chemical Oxidation

Appearance—Black, Gold, Blue, Grey, etc.

Reprocessing Elements:
1. Avoid using ultrasonic cleaning machines during cleaning.
2. Prohibit the use of hard cleaning tools such as steel wool, scrub pads, and degreasing powders.
3. It is recommended to use neutral cleaning agents during cleaning and avoid adding hydrogen peroxide or using strong alkaline cleaning agents.
4. Avoid using strong alkaline disinfectants such as sodium hydroxide during disinfection.
5. If rust remover is used, the concentration and soaking time should be strictly controlled.
6. After cleaning, it is recommended to use a quick-drying lubricant for secondary lubrication on the joint parts of this type of product.

# Surgical Instrument Spots

Spots are water spots that appear on the surface of instruments after high-temperature steam sterilization.

Main Causes: 1. Impurities in the sterilization steam remain on the surface of the instrument, forming spots.
2. If there is non-water-soluble oil film on the surface of the instrument before sterilization, it will also lead to the formation of spots after sterilization.

# Electrically Charged Surgical Instruments
Why is it necessary to perform insulation performance testing on electrically charged surgical instruments?
Taking monopolar electrosurgical instruments as an example:

Under normal circumstances, the end of the laparoscope contacts the lesion for cutting or coagulation operations. However, if there is a defect in the insulation layer of the laparoscope body, the leaking part may come into contact with healthy internal organs, and the high-frequency high-voltage leakage current may cause severe electrical burns to healthy tissues, resulting in irreversible serious accidents. If there is leakage at the puncture area, it will cause skin burns to the patient. If there is leakage at the handle, it will cause electric shock injuries to medical staff.

The leaking surgical instrument is burning the patient's healthy organs

# Insulation Testing
Adverse Events Related to Insulation Layer Damage

In a study, visual inspection and insulation performance testing were conducted on 81 robotic surgical instruments and 299 laparoscopic instruments. Insulation failure was detected in 72.8% of robotic surgical instruments and 35.1% of laparoscopic instruments, respectively. Most of the insulation damage in robotic surgical instruments was located at the front end of the instruments, which is the area entering the abdominal cavity, while the insulation damage in laparoscopic instruments was located at the rear end, which is the area outside the abdominal cavity [1]. Similar adverse event reports have also been made domestically, including a case where a woman was burned with a high-frequency electrosurgical wire during cesarean section, resulting in third-degree burns and requiring skin grafting surgery [1].

## Instrument Hydration after Surgery

After surgery, it is often difficult to completely remove the biofilm or dried bloodstains from the instruments. This means that the instruments contaminated after surgery are more likely to fail the first round of cleaning, thereby affecting the final sterilization effect, which can easily lead to nosocomial infection.

In the process of handling, hydration is now widely practiced. Before transporting contaminated instruments to various departments, instruments with obvious contaminants or bloodstains are hydrated. So, can the hydration process of surgical contaminated instruments effectively control contamination?

<!-- Slide number: 32 -->
## Instrument Hydration after Surgery

A domestic experiment was conducted to analyze the effect of instrument hydration on improving the cleaning effect of instruments, ensuring surgical safety, and thus to study whether the hydration process of instruments after surgery can reduce the intensity of cleaning work, shorten the processing time of contaminated instruments, and play an important role in improving the frequency of instrument use and promoting infection control.

### Methods: 

Randomly select 1200 pieces of contaminated medical instruments, divided into hydration group and non-hydration group, each with 600 pieces. By practicing the technical specifications in the process of handling surgical contaminated instruments, the importance of instrument hydration on improving the cleaning effect of instruments was analyzed. The sample rates of the two groups were compared, and the cleanliness after cleaning of contaminated instruments with and without hydration was compared.

The results show: under visual inspection and 5x magnification, 600 pieces of hydrated group instruments are all qualified under visual inspection, and 597 pieces are qualified under 5x magnification, with a pass rate of 99.5%; 592 pieces of instruments in the 600-piece non-hydration group are qualified under visual inspection, and 587 pieces are qualified under 5x magnification, with a pass rate of 97.8%. The P value of this experiment is less than 0.05, indicating a statistically significant difference.

The results of this study show that the purpose of instrument hydration is to create favorable conditions for instrument sterilization. Hydration of instruments after surgery is very necessary for the removal of biofilm, and is a key link in controlling infection caused by incomplete instrument cleaning. At the same time, appropriate hydration treatment can improve the pass rate of instrument cleaning, ensure the quality of cleaning, and play an important role in the infection control of surgical contaminated instruments.

Chang Xianyuan, Hao Shuqin. Comparative analysis of the effects of different methods of instrument hydration cleaning [J]. Journal of Hospital Infection Control, 2012, 22(2): 338-338.

### Water Quality
1. Requirements for instrument cleaning water?
According to "Part 1: Management Specifications of Hospital Disinfection Supply Center", the cleaning water should have tap water, hot water, soft water, and purified water. The quality of tap water should meet the provisions of GB5749; the conductivity of the final rinsing water should be ≤15μS/cm (25℃).
2. Impact of conductivity on instrument cleaning? High conductivity indicates a high mineral content. After long-term cleaning, minerals will form on the surface of the instruments, which can increase the resistance of bacteria and may lead to sterilization failure. High conductivity, after frequent cleaning of instruments, is prone to rust. High conductivity can cause the surface of instruments to produce mineral spots, which are a type of white spots.

## Steam Quality
Steam condensate

Zhang Qing and others investigated the status of steam supply for large-scale steam sterilizers in 2583 domestic hospitals. The survey results showed that a large proportion of hospitals use external steam boilers and steam pipelines made of carbon steel, with long gas supply pipelines (over 100m accounted for 83.95%). The external steam source is delivered to the CSSD through the boiler room, and carbon steel pipelines can lead to a large amount of rust and other impurities in the steam. Long steam pipelines, many bends, and long service life can lead to the shedding of insulation layers and other hidden dangers affecting steam quality, a large amount of condensate, and poor drying effects, even leading to sterilization failure due to wet packages. Appendix B1 proposes that purified water with an electrical conductivity of ≤5μs/cm (at 25℃) should be used for steam supply water, but the situation of external steam supply water is not optimistic, with 82.76% using soft water or tap water. Tap water contains chloride ions, soluble salts, microorganisms, and other impurities. Soft water only contains or contains a small amount of soluble calcium and magnesium compounds, which do not meet the quality requirements for steam supply water, leading to impure steam.

Zhang Qing, Wu Ke Ping, Qian Liming, Yang Zexia, Sun Yibo, Yu Xiaomei. Survey of the Current Status of Steam Supply for Large-scale Steam Sterilizers in 2583 Domestic Hospitals [J]. Journal of Chinese Antiseptics, 2020, 37(01): 30-32.

# Workflow for Disinfection Pre-treatment

1. Disinfection Location
Used reusable medical devices are disinfected locally in isolation wards or fever clinics.
2. Disinfection Method
★ Use 1000mg/L chlorine-containing disinfectant for immersion for 30 minutes; for heavily contaminated items, use 2000mg/L chlorine-containing disinfectant for immersion for 30 minutes.
★ For items that are not resistant to moisture, use a 1000mg/L chlorine-containing disinfectant spray disinfection method, with an action time of 30 minutes.
3. Sealed Packaging
After disinfection pre-treatment, the equipment and items are sealed with double-layer waterproof collection bags, and the outer packaging is marked with "COVID-19" identification.

## Non-wet and Non-heat-resistant Items
1. Disinfection: Immersion in 2000mg/L chlorine-containing disinfectant for 30 minutes; change the disinfectant once used.
2. Rinse: Remove the surface residual disinfectant with running water.
3. Wash: Brush under the liquid surface with an enzymatic detergent, change the detergent once used, and disinfect the cleaning tools.
