Terminology > Electrosurgery

Electrosurgery is a high-frequency current surgical system applied in operating rooms. Electrosurgery integrates a variety of surgical high-frequency current equipment such as high-frequency electrotomes, large vessel closure systems, ultrasonic knives, argon knives, LEEP knives, and endoscopic electrotomes, and controls the cutting depth and hemostasis speed during the surgical process through computers to achieve hemostasis and coagulation effects.

Terminology > Monopolar Electrotome

The monopolar electrotome is an electrosurgical device that uses high-frequency current (greater than 200KHz) in a single circuit to produce thermal energy and discharge to cut and hemostasize tissue in the human body.

# Why High-Frequency Current is Needed?

Faraday Effect: When low-frequency alternating current passes through human tissue, it can cause neural and muscular stimulation, even ventricular fibrillation, which is life-threatening.

Neural and Muscular Stimulation

Frequency

### Notes:
Low-frequency alternating current can produce neural and muscular stimulation in biological tissue. Only when the frequency is greater than 100KHZ will the stimulation disappear.
Therefore, for electrosurgical products, we choose high-frequency alternating current, which is why we often call it "high-frequency electrotome".

**Assessment**
Cochlear Implant Devices

When performing neck and head surgery on patients with cochlear implants, it is not advisable to use monopolar electrosurgical mode.

For patients with cochlear implants undergoing neck and head surgery, if they do not have an external reference electrode, bipolar electrosurgical mode can be used.

Prohibit the bipolar electrode from contacting the implant.

If the cochlear implant has an external reference electrode, bipolar electrosurgical mode can also be used, but the electrode must be at least 10 centimeters away from the external reference electrode.

The patient needs to undergo relevant checks separately after surgery.

### Notes:
When performing electrosurgical procedures, it is extremely important that the electrosurgical accessory does not make unintended direct contact with any metal devices since direct coupling of energy will occur.

Use caution by covering braces with teeth guards (silicone or rubber) or use bipolar energy when possible. Metal braces are generally in contact with the tooth enamel only, which happens to be the hardest material in the body.  Current will not transfer from the braces, through the teeth to the gums because of the high impedance of the tooth enamel. However, if the braces are accidentally touched by an activated electrosurgical instrument, sparking could occur and cause injury to adjacent tissue.

A palate spreader is a metal device glued to the roof (soft palate) of a patient’s mouth prior to the application of braces.  It is applied by an orthodontist and can be removed if necessary.  The use of electrosurgery on a patient with a palate spreader does pose a greater risk if the surgical procedure is in the immediate area of the device. Because the metal palate spreader is in contact with soft tissue, electrosurgical current may be transferred to the device by unintended direct contact, capacitive coupling or insulation failure and could burn the tissue. Valleylab recommends removing palate spreaders prior to surgery if at all possible.

As a general rule it is always prudent to remove any metal within the electrosurgical circuit when possible.  If the metal can’t be removed, consider the use of bipolar electrosurgery.  If the use of monopolar electrosurgery is required, isolate the metal as recommended above to prevent any direct coupling with the active accessory, capacitive coupling, or insulation failure. It is always advisable to use the lowest power settings as well as short activations when possible for the desired tissue effect.

Valleylab Clinical Hotline Form Letter concerning use of dental appliances, June 8, 2007

### Notes:
Coupling Effect

The coupling effect refers to the phenomenon where two or more circuit components or electrical networks have a close match and mutual influence between their inputs and outputs, and energy is transmitted from one side to the other through interaction. In the application of electrosurgery, this is manifested as the transmission of energy from the working cable (electrocautery pen or hook) to adjacent cables or metallic instruments.

### (V) Notes

Select the appropriate coagulation instrument and output power according to the surgical site and tissue characteristics.
When using bipolar coagulation, apply physiological saline for intermittent irrigation or drip to keep the tissue moist, tension-free, and the surgical field clean. Avoid high temperature affecting the important tissues and structures around the coagulation, reducing tissue charring and adhesion to the bipolar forceps or clamp.
It is recommended to use intermittent coagulation, with each coagulation time of about 0.5 seconds, which can be repeated multiple times until the coagulation effect is achieved, avoiding excessive coagulation.
44

<!-- Slide number: 45 -->
### (V) Notes

**Protection of Bipolar Coagulation Instruments or Forceps Tips:**
During coagulation, the charred residue on bipolar coagulation instruments or forceps should be promptly wiped off with a wet gauze or professional non-traumatic fabric. Sharp instruments should not be used to scrape off the residue to avoid damaging the alloy material at the tip or forceps尖端.

The bipolar coagulation instrument should be operated gently. When fixing the bipolar forceps tip, maintain a certain distance between the two tips to prevent them from contacting each other, which could lead to a short circuit of the current or poor closure of the tips due to external force, affecting the coagulation effect.

After cleaning the bipolar coagulation instrument, a protective cover should be placed over the tip or head.

**Equipment Maintenance and Care:**
Pay attention to the compatibility between the bipolar coagulation instrument brand and the main unit.
The foot control panel should be covered with a waterproof protective cover before use for ease of cleaning and to avoid circuit faults or short circuits.

# Three: Safe Use of Ultrasonic Scalpel

5/11/2026

**Terminology > Ultrasonic Scalpel**

**Ultrasonic Scalpel (ultrasonic scalpel)**
is a generator that can produce ultrasonic energy and mechanical vibration. It acts on the metal probe (blade) through the ultrasonic frequency generator at a frequency of 55.5kHz, causing mechanical oscillation (50～100μm) through the blade, converting electrical energy into mechanical energy, which then causes the vaporization of tissue fluid, breaking of protein hydrogen bonds, cell lysis, protein coagulation, and vascular closure, achieving the effects of incision and hemostasis.

Ultrasonic Scalpel Maintenance and Care:
