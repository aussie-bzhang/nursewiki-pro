Case Introduction
On August 16, 2015, the patient felt chest tightness, palpitations, and shortness of breath during activity, lasting about 30 minutes each time, admitted to the First Hospital of Nanjing for hospitalization, diagnosed with rheumatic heart disease (severe mitral stenosis), arrhythmia (atrial fibrillation, heart function III level), and immediately underwent mitral valve replacement + tricuspid valve plasty + atrial fibrillation maze ablation.

Within the first six months after surgery, the patient was repeatedly admitted due to heart failure symptoms, and was treated with anticoagulation, anti-infection, diuretics, improvement of heart function, and control of ventricular rate, etc., and finally discharged on February 5, 2016.

Two days later, the patient had no obvious cause for symptoms to worsen and was admitted to our department (refused routine examination).

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->

Case Introduction

Anticoagulation: Clopidogrel, Warfarin, Low molecular weight heparin

Control of ventricular rate: Metoprolol

Treatment Plan

Cardiac strengthening: Digoxin

Diuretics: Furosemide + Dopamine intravenous infusion

Nutrition of myocardium: Cyclic adenosine monophosphate
Discharged on March 15, 2016 after improvement

Vital Signs Monitoring
On February 10, 2016, at 13:10 in the afternoon and at 23:04 on the same day, the electrocardiogram showed ventricular fibrillation (electrical storm)

“Electrical storm”: A clinical syndrome that requires emergency treatment, characterized by spontaneous occurrence of 2 or more episodes of ventricular tachycardia or ventricular fibrillation within 24 hours [1].
Clinical manifestations:
          Eyeballs rolled up, limbs convulsed, unconscious
Emergency measures:
          200J biphasic wave non-synchronous cardioversion
         Cardiopulmonary resuscitation
         Open venous access
         Administer medication as prescribed

Activity Guidance

New Concepts
     Metabolic Equivalent  [1 METS = 3.5mlO2/(kg·min)]
Concept: Based on the energy expenditure at rest and sitting, it expresses the relative energy metabolic level during various activities.
Activities include personal life, eating, bedside urination, simple passive and active exercises of the upper and lower limbs, and sitting on the bedside chair.

    Note: During activities, do not cause changes in hemodynamics, heart rate should not be below 50 beats/min or above 120 beats/min.

In April 2011, the journal Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise published the latest version of the energy expenditure coding table for physical activities.
