# Perioperative Nurse's Psychological Care for Surgical Patients
Eastern Operating Room     Zhao Hui

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# Understanding the Psychological State of Surgical Patients
The psychological state of surgical patients mainly manifests as tension, anxiety, worry, and fear. Some surgical patients often find themselves in a state of high tension, anxiety, and fear, which can directly lead to changes in vital signs such as body temperature, pulse, respiration, and blood pressure. If these emotions are too intense, they can directly interfere with the smooth implementation of surgery and anesthesia.

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# Psychological Care Demonstrates Humanistic Care

During surgery, providing reasonable and appropriate psychological care to patients can significantly improve their anxiety, fear, and other psychological states, allowing them to actively cooperate with treatment and ensure the smooth progress of surgery. This also enhances patient satisfaction, reflecting the humanistic care of operating room nurses and the provision of high-quality nursing services.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# How to Provide Effective Psychological Care for Surgical Patients?
1. Warm Visit
2. Smile Welcome
3. Care for Patients
4. Love and Escort

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Preoperative Visit

Warm Visit
Carefully conduct preoperative health education
1. Visit the patient's bedside, introduce yourself in simple and understandable language, verify the patient's identity and surgery, and understand information related to the surgery. Answer patient questions patiently to alleviate preoperative tension.
2. Inform the patient of the surgical precautions.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Psychological Care for Patients Entering the Operating Room
Smile Welcome
1. Greet patients with a smile, a friendly and amiable attitude, and gentle actions.
    At this moment, patients also pay great attention to the behavior and speech of operating room medical staff, which requires that the tone of their conversation be soft and harmonious.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Psychological Care for Patients Entering the Operating Room
2. Verify the patient, introduce the environment
      The circulating nurse introduces the environment while pushing the operating bed, allowing the patient to have a clear idea. Since patients enter the operating room alone without family members, the active introduction by the operating room nurse gives them a sense of security, reduces fear, eliminates doubts, and improves their tolerance to surgery and anesthesia.

#

Use non-verbal communication methods correctly, such as good facial expressions, caring eyes, and appropriate touches, to show concern for the patient and psychological comfort.

# Psychological Care for Conscious Patients During Anesthesia
Guide patients to cooperate with anesthesia and assist in positioning the patient.

For example: During epidural anesthesia, the nurse supports the patient's legs with both hands to increase physical contact and body language, using encouraging and comforting language to communicate. Instruct the patient not to move around. Engage in conversation with the patient to distract their attention. This not only alleviates the fear, unease, and anxiety caused by surgery but also helps normalize blood pressure and heart rate, enhancing the patient's confidence and cooperation with anesthesia and surgery.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Psychological Care for Conscious Patients During Surgery

Care and Accompany
After anesthesia and during surgery, cover the patient to minimize body exposure. Protect the patient's privacy.

Pay attention to keeping the patient warm to create a comfortable and safe environment

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
# Psychological Care for Conscious Patients During Surgery

Constant attention and timely communication. During surgery, patients may experience nausea and vomiting due to reasons such as organ traction. The nurse should comfort the patient, instruct them to take deep breaths, tilt their head to one side to prevent aspiration, and hold the patient's hand to provide encouragement and psychological cues.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Psychological Care for Conscious Patients During Surgery
Intraoperative Psychological Support
   1. Provide necessary information to patients who need to know the progress of the surgery, explaining that the surgery is going smoothly to reassure them.
   2. Reassure patients who feel lonely and afraid, such as holding their hand to increase comfort and eliminate concerns; explain each step of the operation and the measures taken (such as applying pressure restraints) patiently to obtain the patient's cooperation.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Psychological Care for Conscious Patients During Surgery
Keep the operating room quiet and do not talk about matters unrelated to the surgery.

When discussing surgical methods, avoid mentioning the patient's condition that they should not know to avoid increasing their psychological burden.

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Psychological Care After Surgery
Love and Escort
    After the surgery is completed, wipe the patient's skin of bloodstains and disinfectant with warm water, cover the patient with a blanket, pay attention to keeping them warm; when moving the patient, protect the incision and various drainage tubes, intravenous infusion, and fix restless patients appropriately to transport them smoothly to the ward.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Conclusion

    Centered on the patient, integrate humanistic care into nursing services, focus on the psychological care of surgical patients, and eliminate the tension and fear caused by surgery through the professional nursing and careful care of operating room nurses. Make patients feel respected and loved, confident, and in the best state for surgery, ensuring the smooth progress of surgery. Satisfy patients and promote the harmony between doctors and patients.
